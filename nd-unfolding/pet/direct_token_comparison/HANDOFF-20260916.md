## How to report the result, and how not to

Joseph's stated endpoint: *"a recommendation supported by matched learning results and
compute costs, with uncertainty and scope stated clearly"*, and *"don't force a winner
if the evidence is inconclusive"*. His practical question is **which approach gives
better unfolding closure, stability, and compute cost on the same task.**

- Closure and stability come from the frozen criteria: the paired 95% interval,
  `>5%` lower bound, `>=7/8` favourable seeds, plus the normalization, ESS, tail-ESS,
  projection and cap safeguards.
- **Compute cost** is now reported by `summarize_runs.py` in its `compute` block:
  per-arm total and median fit seconds, the paired direct/pooled ratio, and total wall
  seconds. It is **reported, never gated** — five controls pin that, including one
  where a 10× cheaper arm with no closure gain still returns `NO_PASS`.
- `NO_PASS` may be inconclusive **or** a safeguard failure, and is never a statement of
  inferiority. Say which it is.
- Scope the claim explicitly to **uniform low multiplicity**: the fixture gives every
  event exactly 4 objects, so the matrix never tests variable multiplicity, which is
  arguably where individual tokens would most plausibly help. Joseph chose to run the
  frozen matrix and flag this gap rather than change the fixture.
- Three things must stay separate, on his explicit instruction: **information
  retention** (an established implementation property), **synthetic learning
  performance**, and **compute cost**. Numerical preflight agreement and information
  retention are not evidence of better learning.
- The cross-device preflight discrepancy is **not** evidence that individual tokens are
  scientifically worse.
