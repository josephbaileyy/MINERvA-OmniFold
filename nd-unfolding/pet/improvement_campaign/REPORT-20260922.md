# PET recovery: diagnosis and improvement campaign — scientific report

**Status: IN PROGRESS.** Sections 1–4 and 8 are written from completed, committed measurements; sections 5–7
are filled as their experiments land. Every number here is traceable through
`CLAIM_EVIDENCE_INDEX-20260922.md` to a committed receipt. PET is diagnostic method development: nothing in this
report is a publication adoption, an uncertainty product, a central-value change, or a Gate-6 action, and nothing
here changes the historical comparison's thresholds or its `NEITHER_ELIGIBLE / NO_SELECTION` verdict.

Authorization: `docs/orchestration/AUTHORIZATION-20260922-pet-improvement-campaign.md`. Scope:
`SCOPE-HANDOFF-20260922.md`. Design: `PROTOCOL-20260922.md` (+ amendments).

## 1. The question

The completed comparison measured, on simulated pseudodata with a known injected truth `E_avail` tilt, that our
production PET arm recovered `0.3037` of the injected seven-bin displacement and Gregor's pretrained PET2-small arm
`0.4165`, against an adequacy floor of `0.5560` (`0.8 ×` an acceptance reference `0.6950` built from `1−(1−a)^k` at
k = 3). Both arms failed; the difference `ours − theirs = −0.1129`, 95 % interval `[−0.1471, −0.0786]` over eight
seed pairs, favoured his arm in every pair. This campaign asks what caused the shortfall, what fixes it, whether
more events help, whether the adequacy reference is appropriate, and what limits remain.

## 2. Was the comparison running what it declared? (Phase A)

Two source-level suspicions were raised before this campaign; **both are confirmed at runtime**, by capturing the
executed objects while the historical code at commit `68cf9d29` ran unmodified through the OI-136 guard, and by
mining the 336 fits the full-scale campaign left behind. Evidence:
`phase_a/INTENDED_VS_EXECUTED-20260922.md`, `phase_a/receipts/runtime_audit_{ours,theirs}.json`,
`phase_a/receipts/historical_receipts.json` (jobs 58742194, 58742195).

1. **The declared optimizer for Gregor's arm never ran.** `frozen_design.THEIRS_COMPLETE` specifies TorchAdamW with
   weight decay 0.01, warmup/cosine scheduling and global-norm clipping at 1.0. What executed at step 1 was the
   engine's Horovod-wrapped Keras `Adam`: `weight_decay = None`, `clipnorm = global_clipnorm = clipvalue = None`,
   no schedule object, `epsilon = 1e-7` rather than torch's `1e-8`. The intended clip was not merely absent but
   would have bound on **100 % of updates** (measured global gradient norms up to 1474). `training_recipe.py`'s
   optimizer and schedule were never reached from the driver.
2. **The step declared identical across arms was not identical.** Both arms build the same truth-side PET on the
   same truth inputs — the input arrays are byte-identical across arms — but the engine applied the driver's arm
   batch size to *both* steps: step 2 ran at batch 2048 for his arm and 512 for ours (`586` vs `2344` engine steps
   at gen, i.e. 4× fewer optimizer updates per epoch), at iteration-0 learning rates of 1e-4 vs 4e-4, and on a
   *different validation subset*, because the engine's train/validation cut is computed in batches. The realized
   step-2 initial weights also differ between arms at equal seed, because building his step-1 model consumes the
   global RNG first.
3. **Other executed facts that were not declared anywhere.** Every fit after iteration 0 ran at a forced learning
   rate of 1e-5 (`annealed_estimator.py:71` → `omnifold.py:376-377`), so the tuned rate governed iteration 0 only.
   Early stopping was inert (patience 10 against 8 epochs), so `restore_best_weights` never restored and each fit
   handed on its **last-epoch** weights — while the validation-loss minimum was the last epoch in only 7 of 48
   final-stage fits for his arm and 30 of 48 for ours. Step 1 normalizes each class to 1e6, discarding the physical
   class ratio. The step-2 truth cloud feeds **raw PDG codes** (−3122 … 2.0e9) as a continuous column into Dense
   layers and the k-NN features.
4. **What was NOT defective:** his pretrained weights survive cloning exactly (176/176 tensors at the first
   optimizer step); his token features are the converted `eta/phi/log pT/log E` set, not the historical
   raw-momentum defect (a stale comment, not a live bug); no non-finite inputs; the three declared differences from
   his source are what ran, and only the MINOS-match one is resolvable from existing sources.

**These are defects in the comparison's fidelity to its own design, not established causes of the recovery
shortfall.** They bias the between-arm comparison (his arm ran a recipe nobody chose, and the "shared" step differed),
which is why the campaign rebuilt the path with explicit per-step recipes rather than reusing it. The repaired driver
refuses to train when the executed optimizer differs from the declared one, and its regression tests assert on the
executed objects: 31 pass on Perlmutter, 7 xfail marking the historical behaviour (job 58748758).

## 3. Is the shortfall specific to PET? (Phase B, scalar references)

`phase_b/scalar/SCALAR_REFERENCES-20260922.md` reproduces the historical closure bit-exactly (31/31 checks, incl.
half sizes 600,130 / 600,111 with 32 dropped, and the reference `0.6949731569`) and then measures what simple,
response-aware estimators achieve on the *same* endpoint.

| estimator at k = 3 (the historical iteration count) | recovery |
|---|---:|
| binned IBU, muon kinematics only | 0.164 |
| binned IBU, muon + reconstructed `E_avail` | 0.472 |
| GBDT OmniFold, muon only | 0.140 |
| GBDT OmniFold, + reconstructed `E_avail` | 0.412 |
| MLP OmniFold, + all reco hadronic summaries | 0.486 |
| **historical PET, ours** | **0.304** |
| **historical PET, Gregor's arm** | **0.417** |

**No response-aware scalar estimator reaches the 0.556 floor at k = 3 either.** Gregor's arm scores where a
gradient-boosted tree given reconstructed `E_avail` scores. The shortfall is therefore not PET-specific, and the
comparison's floor was not attainable at three iterations by anything tested.

Two further measurements locate the loss:

- **Iterations.** Recovery is still climbing at k = 3: binned IBU with reco `E_avail` reaches 0.648 at k = 10 and
  0.683 at k = 15; GBDT OmniFold reaches 0.524 at k = 20. The historical three iterations sit on the steep part of
  the curve. This matches the published neutrino experience (arXiv:2504.06857 uses 50 iterations and states that
  including efficiency inside the loop "considerably slows the rate of χ² convergence, since this increases the
  bias towards the prior in regions of low efficiency").
- **What the truth side must extract.** A learner given true `E_avail` recovers the known tilt to 0.999 on held-out
  events; given only true muon pT and p‖ — the historical truth PET's only explicit event-level inputs — it reaches
  0.33, and 0.05 in the low-acceptance region. The truth-side network must therefore reconstruct `E_avail` from its
  particle cloud to do better than ≈ 0.33, while that cloud carries raw PDG codes as a continuous feature (§2.3).

## 4. Is the adequacy reference appropriate? (Phase E, first part)

The reference is `1−(1−a)^k` at k = 3 with a per-cell acceptance `a`, and the floor is `0.8 ×` it. Measured facts:

- **The reference is computed on a different quantity from the one scored.** It is built on the (pT, p‖) cell
  displacement; the score is the seven-bin `E_avail` marginal. Applying the reference model's own assumptions to the
  scored spectrum gives **0.578** at k = 3, not 0.695; evaluated on the actual halves with the historical pseudodata
  normalization it gives **0.523** — *below the 0.556 floor derived from it*.
- **It is not an attainability bound in either direction.** Textbook efficiency-corrected IBU with reco `E_avail`
  exceeds it (0.923 at k = 2) before overshooting and going unstable; and the low-acceptance region, whose
  reference is 0.014, is recovered at 0.56–0.76 by that estimator — but only by dividing by acceptance, which the
  engine's handling of misses never does.
- **The gap is definitional, not statistical** (`phase_e/results/reference_assessment.json`, pool S). Rebuilt on
  fresh events the historical reference is again 0.6950; the response-aware IBU at k = 3 gives 0.472 at the
  historical size and 0.480 at 8× the events, so eight times the statistics closes only 0.008 of the 0.223 gap —
  at least 96 % of it comes from what the reference assumes, not from sample size. The reference model realized
  on the scored object gives 0.523 (1×) and 0.528 (8×), below its own floor at both sizes.
- **A known-function toy confirms it is neither a bound nor a match** (`phase_e/results/toy_reference.json`):
  with no smearing, `1−(1−a)^k` at k = 3 gives 0.788 where the engine's miss rule attains 0.659 (0.025 of the gap
  from the score's renormalization, 0.104 from the engine's normalization), while efficiency correction reaches
  0.991, above it.
- **Prospective recommendation (not adopted, changes no verdict):** a reference must be computed for the
  estimator's actual miss rule and normalization, on the scored seven-bin spectrum — e.g. the response-aware IBU
  at the same k — and a floor derived from it should be checked on a known-function toy before use.
- The historical thresholds are nonetheless retained unchanged for every like-for-like verdict in this campaign
  (`PROTOCOL-20260922.md` §4). Any recalibrated reference is a prospective recommendation only.

## 5. Where PET loses recovery, and what improves it (Phase B/C, PET)

All from `phase_b/pet/PET_DIAGNOSTICS-20260922.md`, on the DEV halves (development evidence), through the repaired
driver.

**The driver reproduces the historical result.** The historical as-executed `ours` recipe through the new driver
gives 0.311 ± 0.028 over 4 seeds against the historical 0.304 ± 0.014 over 8 (Welch difference +0.008,
95 % [−0.040, +0.055]); the gate and its fallback were written before any result. The scorer reproduces the
historical seed-127 score to 1e-15. The 4-seed spread is twice the historical one, so effects below ≈ 0.03 are
reported as unresolved at 2 seeds, not as nulls.

**The truth-side PET is not the bottleneck.** Trained on the known tilt alone, the executed truth PET learns it to
0.898 ± 0.012 on held-out events (raw PDG codes), 0.952 ± 0.037 with a one-hot PDG encoding and 0.973 with true
`E_avail`/`q3` as globals — against 0.33 for any learner restricted to its explicit globals. It extracts the
available-energy information from the hadron cloud. At the historical learning rate the fit moves by 0.05–0.2
between consecutive epochs, so handing on the last of eight epochs is a noisy draw.

**Step 1 is not the bottleneck either.** Inside the unfolding (historical recipe, K = 10), the pulled weights
remove 66 % of the pseudodata–prior difference in reconstructed `E_avail` after one iteration and 94 % by k = 10.

**The loss is in how the correction reaches events that fail reconstruction, and in stopping at k = 3.**

| k | truth recovery (push) | pulled weights | reco `E_avail` closure | accepted events | missed events |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.164 | 0.191 | 0.656 | 0.313 | 0.098 |
| 3 | 0.334 | 0.363 | 0.824 | 0.554 | 0.212 |
| 10 | 0.506 | 0.512 | 0.940 | 0.774 | 0.378 |

Under the engine's carry-misses rule, every missed event (58 % of the truth sample) enters step 2 carrying its
previous weight unchanged, so the classifier learns a correction diluted by events that carry none: over accepted
events the pull holds 0.59 of the displacement at k = 1 but the step-2 output keeps 0.31. Recovery is still rising at
k = 10.

**What changes it (K = 10, paired against the historical recipe, 2 seeds):**

| factor | recovery at k = 3 | at k = 10 | paired Δ at k = 10 |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| historical recipe (carry-misses, anneal to 1e-5, last epoch) | 0.334 | 0.506 | — |
| no forced 1e-5 after iteration 0 | 0.326 | 0.496 | −0.010, not resolved |
| best-validation epoch handed on | 0.290 | 0.452 | **−0.054** (both seeds) |
| **efficiency-corrected step 2** | **0.608** | **0.817** | **+0.310** (both seeds) |

Iterations and the miss rule are the two levers. Efficiency correction clears the historical floor already at
k = 3 and lifts the low-acceptance region from 0.17 to 0.67 at k = 10 — the PET-level reproduction of the scalar
result (§8). It is also exactly the estimator §7 shows failing when a distortion changes the event mix inside a
truth bin; it is a model-dependence choice, and it is tested under those distortions with the PET before any
statement about it is made. Selecting the best-validation epoch *hurts*: the validation loss of a weighted
classifier does not pick the epoch whose ratio recovers best.

*(The feature arms C1–C4 — reco energy summaries at step 1, truth summaries at step 2 — are running in the
carry-misses mode; their results and the frozen confirmatory comparison follow.)*

## 6. Does more data help? (Phase D)

The confirmatory study runs on fresh pool-S draws; what exists now is **development-stage** evidence at scalar
level on the DEV halves (`phase_d/SCALAR_SCALING-20260922.md`, 222 runs, 3 overlapping draws per size, with a
per-subset oracle anchor because subsampling the prior also moves the evaluation population).

| axis (aggregate recovery) | 75k | 150k | 300k | 600k |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| prior MC size — OmniFold carry-misses, k = 3 | 0.337 | 0.369 | 0.411 | 0.416 |
| prior MC size — OmniFold carry-misses, k = 20 | 0.447 | 0.524 | 0.582 | 0.548 |
| prior MC size — OmniFold efficiency-corrected, k = 20 | 0.794 | 0.791 | 0.862 | 0.813 |
| pseudodata size — OmniFold carry-misses, k = 20 | 0.541 | 0.540 | 0.551 | — |
| oracle anchor (exact tilt on that subset) | 0.969 | 0.974 | 0.984 | 0.984 |

- **Prior-MC statistics buy recovery in the historical miss mode** (+0.079 at k = 3 and +0.101 at k = 20 from
  75k to 600k, against draw spreads of 0.005–0.06) and the curve has not flattened by 300k. Whether it continues
  past 600k is not measured here.
- **Pseudodata statistics are not the binding constraint** over 75k–300k: nothing moves by more than its spread.
- **Miss handling outweighs every statistical axis tested** (≈ +0.3 at every size).
- The efficiency-corrected and AUSSIE curves are flat in prior size, the signature of an estimator limited by its
  extrapolation assumption rather than by statistics.

## 7. Robustness and coverage (Phase E)

Everything here is from `phase_e/PHASE_E_SCALAR-20260922.md`: the predeclared distortions of protocol amendment 1,
drawn on fresh pool-T events, 3 independent replicates per case, scalar yardsticks (the PET candidates are scored
against the same cases when frozen).

**Identifiability first.** A reco-level two-sample classifier at the historical pseudodata size (null from 20
equal-model splits: mean +0.0002, 95 % band ±0.002) separates **every** predeclared truth distortion, by 2.6× to
78× the threshold; the neutron-multiplicity test D4d is the weakest (+0.0126 / +0.0055). The ±5 % hadronic scale
(R1) is distinguishable; the ±1 % muon scale (R2) sits at or below threshold and the 10 % cluster smearing (R3) is
not distinguishable — those are **unprobed at this size**, not harmless.

**The miss-handling trade-off is the central robustness result.** At k = 3:

| distortion | IBU, misses carried | IBU, efficiency-corrected | GBDT OmniFold (carried) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| D1 `E_avail` tilt +0.35 (development point) | 0.483 ± 0.012 | 0.932 ± 0.021 | 0.414 ± 0.004 |
| D1 `E_avail` tilt −0.35 | 0.382 | 0.913 | 0.340 |
| D5 NuWro generator reweighting | 0.475 | **−0.20 ± 0.17** | 0.430 |
| D4c proton multiplicity ×1.3 | 0.197 | 0.314 | 0.112 |

Efficiency correction is far ahead whenever the distortion is a function of the variables the unfolder sees
(the `E_avail` tilts, pion multiplicities: 0.84–0.96), and it **fails — moves away from the target — when the
distortion changes the event mix inside a truth bin**, because it then extrapolates an acceptance that no longer
holds. The engine's carry-misses rule is slower but does not fail that way. Neither mode is dominant; the choice is
a model-dependence choice and must be stated as one. The D1 +0.35 yardsticks on fresh events match B1's on the
historical halves (0.472 / 0.412), so the development numbers transfer.

**Hidden variables.** Under D4d (neutron multiplicity ×1.3 and ×1/1.3 — invisible in `E_avail`, visible to the
detector) every estimator ends farther from the target than the untouched prior, pushing the wrong way in 14 of 18
estimator × replicate cells (0 of 9 under D1). This is the hidden-variable limit any unfolding in these observables
carries; that efficiency correction fails *specifically* worse here is **not established** at this sample size.

**Detector response.** A ±5 % hadronic energy-scale error combined with the development tilt moves measured
recovery by ±0.06 — three times the historical 0.02 non-inferiority margin. Same-response closure cannot see this;
any candidate's recovery statement is conditional on the simulated response.

**Coverage** is deliberately not run yet: protocol §7 reserves it for a candidate that is adequate or best-in-class
on FINAL.

## 8. Conditional alternatives (Phase F)

A bounded scalar benchmark of **AUSSIE** (arXiv:2602.24282), the non-iterative replacement for OmniFold's pull step,
was run because the diagnosis pointed at iteration limits (`phase_f/AUSSIE_SCALAR_BENCHMARK-20260922.md`). Its first
result — 0.791 aggregate against 0.412 for OmniFold at k = 3 — was **confounded**, and the ablation says how:

| estimator | miss handling | setting | aggregate recovery |
|---|---|---|---:|
| scalar OmniFold (HGB) | carry-misses (the engine's rule) | k = 50 | 0.582 |
| scalar OmniFold (HGB) | efficiency-corrected | k = 50 | 0.783 |
| AUSSIE | misses pinned to the prior (λ = 1000) | — | 0.648 |
| AUSSIE | unconstrained (λ = 0) | — | 0.849 |

**Most of the apparent gain is miss handling, not the non-iterative formulation.** Letting the learned truth-level
ratio extrapolate to events that fail reconstruction is worth ≈ +0.20 in recovery here, while AUSSIE retains a
modest edge (≈ +0.07) at matched miss handling. A PET-backbone AUSSIE evaluation is therefore **not justified on
this evidence**; the efficiency-correction lever is tested directly in the PET experiments instead.

**Efficiency correction is not free, and the binned and learned versions behave differently.** Binned IBU with the
acceptance division peaks at 0.923 (k = 2) and then destabilizes — at k = 20 it is back to 0.559 with a
low-acceptance recovery of **−0.424**, i.e. it overshoots the target — while the learned-function version inside
OmniFold sits at 0.783 at k = 50 (`phase_b/scalar/results/summary.json`, `phase_f/results/aussie_ablation.json`).
Dividing by a small acceptance amplifies whatever the estimator gets wrong there, so any recommendation involving
efficiency correction has to carry a stopping rule and a variance statement, not just a mean recovery.

Two cautions travel with that number, and both are tested rather than assumed: efficiency correction assumes the
selection efficiency depends only on the truth variables the unfolder sees, and this closure's tilt is an exact
function of true `E_avail`, which those unfolders receive. Protocol amendment 1's hidden-variable distortion (D4d,
neutron multiplicity, which changes the detector response at fixed truth `E_avail`) is the predeclared test of that
assumption.

## 9. Published evidence this campaign relies on

Every quotation below was checked against the paper's own text (downloaded full text, string-matched); claims that
could not be matched were discarded. A first-pass literature memo produced by a delegate is **not** used and is not
committed: two of its quotations do not appear in the paper it attributed them to, and its ranked recommendation
rested on a `ParticleViT` comparison that the paper does not contain (the string does not occur in it).

- **Iteration count.** *A Practical Guide to Unbinned Unfolding* (arXiv:2507.09582): "The default choice in the
  OmniFold software is set to 3 iterations." *Machine Learning Assisted Unfolding for Neutrino Cross-Section
  Measurements* (arXiv:2504.06857) runs "50 iterations of each of the unfolding procedures, so that their
  convergence rates can be compared", and chooses "a cutoff of 20 iterations for IBU-UniFold and 45 iterations for
  the OmniFold methods". Our measurement that recovery is still climbing at k = 3 (§3) is consistent with that
  practice, and the historical comparison's three iterations are the software default rather than a measured
  convergence point.
- **Efficiency inside the loop.** arXiv:2504.06857: "We observe that including efficiency effects during unfolding
  considerably slows the rate of χ2 convergence, since this increases the bias towards the prior in regions of low
  efficiency. Correspondingly, the rest of the results in this paper are all obtained using the approach of an
  efficiency correction after unfolding." This is the published counterpart of our §8 ablation, in which the same
  choice is worth ≈ +0.20 recovery at k = 50 on our endpoint.
- **Supplying the relevant observable.** arXiv:2504.06857 compares giving the network the derived observables
  against making it learn them, and finds the former "converges both faster and to better values", concluding that
  "directly providing the most relevant observables yields better performance". This is the hypothesis our Phase C
  feature arms test for `E_avail`.
- **AUSSIE.** arXiv:2602.24282 reports that its non-iterative objective "consistently finds better solutions than
  ten OmniFold iterations" and "displays drastically reduced dependence on the reference simulation". Our bounded
  benchmark (§8) reproduces an advantage only at matched miss handling, and a modest one.
- **Response mismodeling.** arXiv:2512.07074: "Without a correctly specified response kernel, the solution by
  OmniFold and other machine learning methods will be biased." This motivates the predeclared detector-response
  distortions R1–R3, which are kept separate from truth-model changes.

## 10. What a terminal result here cannot authorize

`PROTOCOL-20260922.md` §1: no estimator adoption, no change to the historical verdict or thresholds, no
`C_stat`/`C_ML`, no real-data unfolding. Recalibrated references and recommended configurations are prospective.
