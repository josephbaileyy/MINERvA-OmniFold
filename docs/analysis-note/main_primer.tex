This plain-language guide describes a study of OmniFold, a machine-learning
unfolding method (correcting data for detector blurring),
on public MINERvA neutrino data. It asks how well the method works when
several quantities are unfolded at once. It reproduces the overall rate of a
published MINERvA cross section to about one percent, with a shape
difference. Unfolding five related quantities gives best estimates only:
their totals agree with simpler results, but simulated tests show biases
when the truth changes by as much as simulations differ, statistical error bars fail a
direct test, and no tested way of reporting the result meets our adopted 10\%
precision target under the study's prescription. As an illustration, tests
calibrated by simulating the whole analysis reject hybrid versions of five
simulations under stated conditions. These rejections are not
physical exclusions, give no evidence of gain over matched simpler
projections, and, for NuWro's overall test, are not robust to a
hydrocarbon-target correction.
\end{abstract}
