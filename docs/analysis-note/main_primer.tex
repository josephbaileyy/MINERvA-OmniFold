\begin{abstract}
This is a short, jargon-light guide to a measurement of how often
muon-neutrinos interact in the MINERvA detector, and to the machine-learning
method (``unbinned OmniFold'') used to make it. It explains what the method
% plain-language pass, director ruling on base defect F2, F12 (DECISION-20261018-joseph-plain-language-pass; PLAIN-LANGUAGE.md C7)
does in everyday terms, why it makes measuring several quantities at once more
practical than the traditional method does, and what we found --- including a
close reproduction of a published result, an established low-recoil discrepancy
recovered with a different unfolding method, and a study of how far the signal
definition (the set of interactions the measurement counts) can be extended
beyond the published limits on the muon's angle and momentum. Part of that
extension is supported by the detector's data and part is dominated by the
simulation; the two kinds of region are kept separate.
\end{abstract}
