\title{Unbinned Five-Observable Unfolding of MINERvA Charged-Current
Neutrino Data and Calibrated Joint Tests of Generator Predictions}

\author{Joseph Bailey}
\email{josephrb@stanford.edu}
\affiliation{Department of Physics, Stanford University, Stanford, California 94305, USA}
\author{Benjamin Nachman}
\email{nachman@stanford.edu}

\begin{abstract}
Can neutrino-interaction generators be tested jointly in five observables when
no qualified five-dimensional cross-section uncertainty exists?  We apply
unbinned machine-learning unfolding, to our knowledge for the first time on
neutrino-scattering data, to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ charged-current
inclusive data from the MINERvA open-data release.  A simultaneous
two-observable unfold reproduces the published
$d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$ measurement.  Adding available energy,
three-momentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass, without an explicit
five-dimensional response tensor, gives central values that pass marginal
anchors and injected-shape closure tests; the estimator's measured model
dependence prevents a qualified total uncertainty at useful precision, so no
five-dimensional cross
section with uncertainties is reported.  Joint total and shape
statistics on the five-dimensional result are calibrated with pseudo-experiments
that rerun the full unfolding under a declared nuisance model.  Under that
model, all ten simple hypotheses formed from five predictions (MINERvA
Tune~v1, GENIE~2.12.10 with and without MEC, NuWro~21.09, GiBUU~2019) are
rejected at familywise level 0.05; pseudo-experiments first lost to scheduler
limits were recovered, report-only, without changing a decision.  The
rejections are conditional on the declared model, whose principal limitations
are the absence of a calorimetric recoil-response uncertainty and an
unconverged fine-grid null; an exploratory $\pm4\%$ recoil-response variation
of the data unfold, not an uncertainty estimate, removes no rejection.  They
are statements about the specified predictions, not a measured cross section,
and no claim is made that the joint information adds discrimination beyond
matched coarse projections.
\end{abstract}
