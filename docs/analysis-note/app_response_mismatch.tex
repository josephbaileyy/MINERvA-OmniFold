\section{Detector-response-mismatch closure (protocol implemented; not yet run)}
\label{app:response-mismatch}

The truth-shape and hidden-variable closures in \S\ref{sec:validation} test recovery of deliberately modified event populations. They do not directly test truth-distribution bias from a mismatch between the pseudo-data detector response and OmniFold's nominal response. Kevin McFarland suggested this distinction and proposed stressing the hadronic record, especially for quasielastic events, to inspect low-$W$ bias (private communication).

\paragraph{Reconstruction-only test implemented in the pipeline.} The first-stage diagnostic uses the ordinary five-dimensional closure sample. After copying pseudo-data arrays, before background mixing or classifier training, it changes only the reconstructed pseudo-data record
\begin{equation}
  \Eavail^{\mathrm{pseudo}} \longrightarrow
  (1+\epsilon)\Eavail^{\mathrm{pseudo}} .
\end{equation}
The nominal reconstructed simulation, all truth coordinates, event weights, and event membership stay fixed. The pseudo-data therefore have a deliberately incompatible conditional response; OmniFold still uses the nominal response. The protocol requires both signs of $\epsilon$, at an amplitude fixed before the results are examined. That amplitude should be justified by a documented hadronic-response variation or a quantified assumption, not by the inherited factor in reconstructed $\Eavail$ alone.

Outputs include the full five-dimensional unfolded/reference residual, one-dimensional $\Eavail$ and $W$ projections, and an $(\Eavail,W)$ ratio map. The primary observable is signed bias versus $W$, reporting $W<\SI{1.1}{GeV}$ separately from the high-$W$ tail. The global integral is a control rather than the decision statistic. The implementation runs only as a closure on pseudo-data and only on the five observables, cannot be combined with truth-reweight injection or bootstrapping, and labels every output as a diagnostic that must not be quoted.

\paragraph{Physically correlated follow-up.} The reconstruction-only arm is intentionally cheap and adversarial, not a detector model. A more faithful follow-up would select quasielastic events, shift leading-nucleon energy up and down, and recompute reconstructed $\Eavail$, $q_{3}$, $W$, and any affected selection from the altered low-level record. The current scalar event-loop outputs lack the information for that recomputation, so this arm needs dedicated diagnostic output from the event loop. It is deferred unless the reconstruction-only test shows material bias or the collaboration asks for the stronger response model.

\paragraph{Status and interpretation.} This appendix records a validation protocol, not a result or an uncertainty component. No response-mismatch output has been produced and no pass threshold has been set. The test leaves the quoted central value and the current covariance construction unchanged. A small observed bias would support stability of the $(\Eavail,W)$ interpretation only within the tested response envelope. A large bias would require a decision on the response model or an uncertainty component, and would not be absorbed silently into a non-closure error.
