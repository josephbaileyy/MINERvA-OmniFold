\section{Detector-response-mismatch closure test}
\label{app:response-mismatch}

The truth-shape and hidden-variable closures in \S\ref{sec:validation} test recovery of deliberately modified event populations. They do not directly test truth-distribution bias from a mismatch between the pseudo-data detector response and OmniFold's nominal response. Kevin McFarland suggested this distinction and proposed stressing the hadronic record, especially for quasielastic events, to inspect low-$W$ bias (private communication). This appendix gives a validation protocol for such a test, not a result or an uncertainty component. Its first stage, the reconstruction-only test below, has been implemented but not yet run: no output of it has been produced and no pass threshold has been set. Closures of the same kind, from the shifted response samples of $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$, have been run (below).

\paragraph{Reconstruction-only test.} The test uses the ordinary five-dimensional closure sample. After the pseudo-data arrays are copied, and before background mixing or classifier training, it changes only the reconstructed pseudo-data record
\begin{equation}
  \Eavail^{\mathrm{pseudo}} \longrightarrow
  (1+\epsilon)\Eavail^{\mathrm{pseudo}} .
\end{equation}
The nominal reconstructed simulation, all truth coordinates, event weights, and event membership stay fixed. The pseudo-data therefore have a deliberately incompatible conditional response; OmniFold still uses the nominal response. Both signs of $\epsilon$ are required, at an amplitude fixed before the results are examined. A documented hadronic-response variation or an explicit quantitative assumption should justify that amplitude; the overall factor in the reconstructed-$\Eavail$ definition, Eq.~\eqref{eq:eavail-reco-implemented}, is not by itself a sufficient basis.

Outputs include the full five-dimensional unfolded/reference residual, one-dimensional $\Eavail$ and $W$ projections, and an $(\Eavail,W)$ ratio map. The primary observable is the signed bias versus $W$, with $W<\SI{1.1}{GeV}$ reported separately from the high-$W$ tail. The global integral is a control rather than the decision statistic. The implementation runs only as a pseudo-data closure and only on the five observables, and cannot be combined with truth-reweight injection or bootstrapping. It labels every output as a diagnostic that must not be quoted.

\paragraph{Closures from the shifted response samples.} Noise-free, signal-only closures at the nominal truth take pseudo-data from simulation whose reconstructed recoil response is shifted, either for each deposit category of $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ (\S\ref{sec:fived16-cov}) at its $\pm1\sigma$ or as a $\pm4\,\%$ scale of the whole recoil energy, and unfold them with configuration~(e) on the unshifted response. Each closure's bias, less that of the unshifted closure, is compared on the sixteen cells with the per-cell $\sigma$ of $C_{\mathrm{total}}+C_{\mathrm{resp}}$. For the deposit categories the median $|\Delta|/\sigma$ is \fvval{ct3b-species-med-lo}--\fvval{ct3b-species-med-hi} and the largest \fvval{ct3b-species-max}; for the whole-recoil scale the median is at most \fvval{ct3b-w2b-med} and the largest \fvval{ct3b-w2b-max}. The closures are descriptive and carry no noise estimate: no pass threshold is set, because the closure settings were deferred. They do not establish the bias under the true detector response, validate $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ or its category sizes, or describe response variants other than those tested.

\paragraph{Physically correlated follow-up.} The reconstruction-only test is by design a computationally inexpensive adversarial stress test; it is not a detector model. A more faithful follow-up would select quasielastic events, shift the leading-nucleon energy up and down, and recompute reconstructed $\Eavail$, $q_{3}$, $W$, and any affected selection from the altered low-level record. The current per-event outputs hold only scalar observables, not that low-level record, so the follow-up requires dedicated diagnostic output from the event processing. It is deferred unless the reconstruction-only test shows material bias or the collaboration asks for the stronger response model.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The test leaves the quoted central value and the current covariance construction unchanged. A small observed bias would support stability of the $(\Eavail,W)$ interpretation only within the tested response envelope. A large bias would require a decision on the response model or an uncertainty component, and would not be absorbed silently into a non-closure error.
