\label{sec:part-intro}

\subsection{Motivation and previous measurements}

Accelerator-based neutrino oscillation experiments infer oscillation parameters from the
energy dependence of event rates. Both the probability that a neutrino interacts and the
relation between its energy and the observed final state depend on neutrino--nucleus
scattering. At energies of a few GeV, quasielastic scattering, resonance production, and
deep-inelastic scattering populate overlapping regions of observed phase space. Nuclear
effects further modify the particles available for energy reconstruction. Uncertainties
in these processes affect the interpretation of data from T2K, NOvA, Hyper-Kamiokande,
and DUNE~\cite{T2K:2023osc,NOvA:2021nfi,HyperK:2018design,DUNE:2020jqi}. Differential
cross sections constrain the interaction models used to predict these rates and
reconstruct neutrino energies.

MINERvA measured neutrino--nucleus scattering on several targets in the low- and
medium-energy configurations of Fermilab's NuMI
beam~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz,MINERvA:2016iqn}. Using the medium-energy neutrino exposure,
with a mean neutrino energy of about $6\,\mathrm{GeV}$, Ruterbories \emph{et al.}
reported the inclusive charged-current (CC) $\nu_\mu$ cross section on hydrocarbon,
differential in the muon momentum transverse ($p_T$) and longitudinal ($p_\parallel$) to
the beam~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. That measurement used D'Agostini iterative Bayesian
unfolding (IBU) with a binned detector-response matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx}.

Binned implementations of IBU, singular-value decomposition, and TUnfold require a
response defined on a grid whose size grows with the number of
observables~\cite{Adye:2011gm,Hocker:1995kb,Schmitt:2012kp}. Machine-learning approaches
to unbinned unfolding use individual events~\cite{Arratia:2021otl,Huetsch:2024quz,Canelli:2025guide},
either by reweighting a reference simulation or by generating unfolded
samples~\cite{Datta:2018mwd,Bellagente:2019uyp,Bellagente:2020piv,Backes:2022vmn}.
OmniFold iteratively reweights simulated events using likelihood ratios estimated by
classifiers~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk,Chan:2023tbf}. Additional
observables enter as classifier inputs, and the resulting event weights can be used to
form different projections or reporting bins without repeating the unfolding.

H1 published the first applications to collider data, unfolding eight-dimensional
lepton--jet correlations and later jet substructure at high $Q^2$ in deep-inelastic
scattering~\cite{H1:2021wkz,H1:2023fzk}. LHCb measured identified-hadron distributions
in $Z$-tagged jets~\cite{LHCb:2022rky}, and ATLAS unfolded 24 $Z$+jets observables
simultaneously and subsequently reported a measurement of the full charged-particle
phase space~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets,ATLAS:2026fullps}. Recent work has also addressed
background treatment in reweighting and generative
approaches~\cite{Falcao:2025backgrounds,Butter:2025genfold}.

Huang \emph{et al.} gave the first neutrino cross-section application of OmniFold,
comparing an unfolding of muon kinematics in simulated T2K near-detector data with
traditional binned methods~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.

\subsection{Objectives and scope}

Here we apply OmniFold to MINERvA's public data.
The first objective is to benchmark OmniFold against MINERvA's published
double-differential cross section $d^2\sigma/(dp_T\,dp_\parallel)$ in the same signal
phase space. The benchmark compares the integrated normalization and the differential
shape. The two estimates share data and sources of systematic uncertainty, and their
cross-covariance is unavailable. Two analysis choices were made after the comparison
with the publication was known. The comparison is therefore descriptive and is not a
blind validation. The second objective
is to test the extension to five observables by adding, in sequence, the available
hadronic energy $E_{\mathrm{avail}}$, the magnitude of the three-momentum transfer
$q_3$, and the hadronic invariant mass $W$. The extension tests whether an estimator can
recover correlations among the observables and support uncertainties on the resulting
joint distribution.

The two-dimensional OmniFold result reproduces the published integrated cross section
within about $1\%$, with a correlated shape difference of unidentified origin. Its
covariance procedure under-covers at both held-out simulation tunes. The
three- and four-dimensional central values give integrals consistent with the
lower-dimensional results and are reported without covariances. Residual bias,
numerical instability, and interval under-coverage at held-out simulation tunes
limit the five-dimensional
outputs to illustrative use.
