\subsection{Motivation and previous measurements}

Accelerator-based long-baseline experiments study neutrino oscillations by comparing the flavor content of a neutrino beam near its source with that observed hundreds of kilometers away. The oscillation probabilities depend on the neutrino energy. Each experiment reconstructs that energy from the observed particles, which requires a model of neutrino--nucleus scattering. At a few GeV, quasielastic scattering, resonance production, and deep-inelastic scattering can produce similar detector signatures, and interactions within the nucleus can change which particles emerge. Differential cross-section measurements constrain the interaction models that experiments such as T2K, NOvA, Hyper-Kamiokande, and DUNE use to predict event rates and reconstruct neutrino energies~\cite{T2K:2023osc,NOvA:2021nfi,HyperK:2018design,DUNE:2020jqi}.

The MINERvA detector~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz} measured neutrino--nucleus scattering on several targets in the low- and medium-energy configurations of Fermilab's NuMI beam~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn}. Using the medium-energy neutrino exposure, with a mean neutrino energy of about $6\,\mathrm{GeV}$, Ruterbories \emph{et al.} reported the inclusive charged-current (CC) $\nu_\mu$ cross section on hydrocarbon, differential in the muon momentum transverse ($p_T$) and longitudinal ($p_\parallel$) to the beam~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. That measurement used D'Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU) with a binned detector-response matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx}.

Binned implementations of IBU, singular-value decomposition, and TUnfold require a response grid that grows with the number of observables~\cite{Adye:2011gm,Hocker:1995kb,Schmitt:2012kp}. Unbinned machine-learning methods use individual events~\cite{Arratia:2021otl,Huetsch:2024quz,Canelli:2025guide} to reweight a reference simulation or generate unfolded samples~\cite{Datta:2018mwd,Bellagente:2019uyp,Bellagente:2020piv,Backes:2022vmn}. OmniFold uses classifiers to estimate the likelihood ratios that iteratively reweight simulated events~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}. Adding observables changes the classifier inputs, and the resulting event weights can be used for different projections and reporting bins.

H1 published the first applications to collider data, unfolding eight-dimensional lepton--jet correlations and later jet substructure at high $Q^2$ in deep-inelastic scattering~\cite{H1:2021wkz,H1:2023fzk}. LHCb measured identified-hadron distributions in $Z$-tagged jets~\cite{LHCb:2022rky}, and ATLAS unfolded 24 $Z$+jets observables simultaneously and subsequently reported a measurement of the full charged-particle phase space~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets,ATLAS:2026fullps}. Recent work has also addressed background treatment in reweighting and generative approaches~\cite{Falcao:2025backgrounds,Butter:2025genfold}.

Huang \emph{et al.} gave the first neutrino cross-section application of OmniFold, comparing an unfolding of muon kinematics in simulated T2K near-detector data with traditional binned methods~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.

\subsection{Objectives and scope}

We apply OmniFold to MINERvA's public data. The two-dimensional (2D) muon cross section, $d^2\sigma/(dp_T\,dp_\parallel)$, agrees in its integral with MINERvA's published measurement~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} within about $1\%$ and shows a correlated shape difference of undetermined origin (Sec.~\ref{sec:muon-cross-section-comparison}). We then add available hadronic energy $E_{\mathrm{avail}}$, the magnitude of the three-momentum transfer $q_3$, and hadronic invariant mass $W$ to test how well the data constrain their distributions and correlations (Sec.~\ref{sec:part-higher-dimensional}).

We report the five-observable cross section in sixteen regions. All three hydrocarbon generator predictions lie below the unfolded integrals in every high-$p_T$ cell (Sec.~\ref{sec:sixteen-cell-generator-comparison}). Agreement in the tested projections provides a consistency check against the lower-dimensional results. The held-out interval shortfalls, dependence on the starting distribution and unfolding algorithm, sensitivity to rounding-level input changes, and unexplained run-period difference restrict the use of these results for model constraints (Sec.~\ref{sec:result-validity}).
