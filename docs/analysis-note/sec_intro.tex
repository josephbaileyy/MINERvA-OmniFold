This note asks three questions of MINERvA's public medium-energy neutrino data, unfolded without binning by OmniFold.

\paragraph{Does unbinned OmniFold reproduce MINERvA's published two-dimensional muon cross section?}
In the muon's transverse and longitudinal momenta, the unbinned result reproduces the published integrated cross section within about one percent, with a correlated shape difference whose source has not been identified (\S\ref{sec:part-q1}).
Whether it is ready for publication as a measurement has not been established.
Two analysis choices were made after the comparison with the publication was known (\S\ref{sec:method-config}, App.~\ref{app:devhistory}).
The comparison is therefore descriptive and not an independent test of the method.

\paragraph{What does extending the unfolding to five observables give, and where does it stop being reliable?}
Adding the available hadronic energy, the momentum transfer and the hadronic invariant mass gives central values in three, four and five dimensions.
Integrated over the added observables, they agree with the lower-dimensional results.
A cross section on sixteen five-observable cells is given only as an illustration (\S\ref{sec:part-q2}).
It stops being reliable where the tests find residual bias at two fake-data truths, sensitivity to rounding-level changes of the inputs and an unexplained disagreement between run periods (\S\ref{sec:part-q3}).
Calibrated tests of five generator predictions reject all ten hybrid null hypotheses under six stated conditions.
The rejections are not physical exclusions.

\paragraph{What do the validation tests show about the method's bias, stability and uncertainty?}
No reported interval has validated coverage.
The procedure's two-observable covariance under-covers jointly at both held-out tunes.
A five-dimensional coverage test failed its futility criterion, and neither coverage test of the sixteen cells gives a validated result (\S\ref{sec:part-q3}).
Section~\ref{sec:part-validity} gives, for each result, what is reported, what is validated and its main limit.

\paragraph{The data and the published measurement.}
MINERvA (Main Injector Experiment for $\nu$-A) is a dedicated neutrino--nucleus scattering experiment in Fermilab's NuMI beamline, immediately upstream of the MINOS near detector~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}. During roughly a decade of operation ending in 2019, it recorded high-statistics samples in low-energy and medium-energy (ME) beam configurations~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn} and in neutrino-dominated (forward horn current, FHC) and antineutrino-dominated modes. It measures neutrino--nucleus cross sections in the few-GeV region most relevant to accelerator-based oscillation experiments. In that region, these cross sections and their nuclear effects are a leading systematic uncertainty for T2K~\cite{T2K:2023osc}, NOvA~\cite{NOvA:2021nfi}, Hyper-Kamiokande~\cite{HyperK:2018design}, and DUNE~\cite{DUNE:2020jqi}. The few-GeV regime combines quasielastic, resonant, and deep-inelastic processes that no single model describes well. This analysis uses the public ME FHC CC-inclusive data product (\S\ref{sec:experiment}).

The MINERvA ME FHC charged-current (CC) inclusive double-differential cross section in muon transverse and longitudinal momentum, $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$, was published by Ruterbories \emph{et al.} \cite{MINERvA:2021owq} using D'Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU)~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} with a binned response matrix. We reproduce it with \emph{unbinned} OmniFold~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw, Andreassen:2021zzk}, which iteratively reweights events using machine-learned likelihood ratios on per-event observables rather than on a fixed histogram binning.

Applied to the data, the five-observable estimator gives a cross section on the sixteen cells of \S\ref{sec:fived16} in $\pT$, $\pPar$, $\Eavail$, $q_{3}$ and $W$, as illustrative output, not for fitting or for constraining models.

The five observables are simultaneous, not independent: reconstructed $W$ is fixed by $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, apart from a clamp at $W^{2}=0$ that affects \SI{1.5}{\percent} of simulated events (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-definitions}).

\paragraph{Unbinned unfolding and its applications.}
Machine-learning advances have enabled unbinned unfolding (overviews in Refs.~\cite{Arratia:2021otl,Huetsch:2024quz}; cross-experiment guidance in Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide}). One broad family iteratively reweights a starting simulation. OmniFold~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk,Chan:2023tbf} is its most widely adopted method. The other generates new samples directly with a neural network~\cite{Datta:2018mwd,Bellagente:2019uyp,Bellagente:2020piv, Backes:2022vmn}. Both lift the binned-response limitations of widely-used classical methods~\cite{Adye:2011gm} such as IBU, the SVD method~\cite{Hocker:1995kb}, and TUnfold~\cite{Schmitt:2012kp}.

H1 published the first real-data applications in deep-inelastic scattering: lepton--jet correlations unfolded simultaneously in eight dimensions~\cite{H1:2021wkz}, followed by jet substructure at high $Q^{2}$~\cite{H1:2023fzk}. LHCb unfolded identified-hadron distributions in $Z$-tagged jets~\cite{LHCb:2022rky}. ATLAS performed a simultaneous unbinned measurement of twenty-four $Z+$jets observables~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}. Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} collects these and other applications and distills cross-experiment practice. Three of the practices it audits are applied here: the ensemble-mean cross-check of the central value and the bootstrap statistical covariance (\S\ref{sec:uq-boot}), and the joint-perturbation (unified-throw) check, used in the five-axis covariance (\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}).

H1 cut backgrounds to a negligible level. LHCb corrected per track before unfolding. ATLAS left its less-than-\SI{0.25}{\percent} background in place and assigned it as an uncertainty, while identifying negatively-weighted simulated events as the natural in-method subtraction~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets,Andreassen:2021zzk}. Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} recommends this in-method subtraction for nontrivial backgrounds. Recent methodological work explicitly treats backgrounds, fakes, acceptance, and inefficiency in OmniFold-HI~\cite{Falcao:2025backgrounds} and generative unfolding~\cite{Butter:2025genfold}. These constructions address related problems. Their subtraction prescriptions can differ.

Closest to this analysis is the study of Huang \emph{et al.}~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}, the first application of OmniFold to a neutrino cross section: a mock-data unfolding of muon kinematics on the public T2K near-detector simulated dataset, benchmarked against traditional binned approaches. Two of its findings bear on neutrino applications. The first is its iteration count, which we do not adopt (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). The second is an ensemble of reweighting trials with different train/test splits that controls the ML stochastic component. We include that component explicitly, as the training-seed variation band (\S\ref{sec:systematics}).

The present work differs from that study in three ways. It unfolds \emph{real} MINERvA data to an absolutely normalized cross section, with a 2D uncertainty budget whose covariance components are all built with the central value's own learner (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). Its gradient-boosted decision-tree (GBDT) learners suit a low-dimensional tabular feature set. It compares the result directly with a published binned measurement of the same data (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Why unbinned.}
First, OmniFold learns event weights in the full observable space and histograms only the final result. Reported bins can be chosen or refined after unfolding without rerunning it. Background subtraction is unbinned too in the 2D result and its covariance, in the 3D and 4D central values and in the sixteen-cell five-dimensional result (\S\ref{sec:fived16}), which use refined negative-weight subtraction (\S\ref{sec:method-algo}). The configuration-(a) five-dimensional central value (Table~\ref{tab:config-key}) and its five-axis covariance, and the extended-fiducial value, use the binned purity weight, a per-reco-bin $(D-B)/D$ weight fixed \emph{before} training.

Second, additional observables enter as input features, without multiplying response-matrix bins. The available-energy extension (\S\ref{sec:3d}) is built this way; a binned method such as IBU would need a correspondingly larger multidimensional response matrix to include it.

Third, OmniFold regularizes through model capacity and through an iteration count, which it shares with IBU. Comparing the two methods on the same data probes the result's method dependence (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Scope and organization.}
The results hold for one implementation of unbinned OmniFold, applied to one data set, and for the configurations, observables and deformations tested. The generator tests of \S\ref{sec:joint5d} evaluate hybrid null hypotheses and add no reportable uncertainty. Full-event unfolding with point-cloud transformer (PET) representations is diagnostic method development, documented in a companion note (App.~\ref{sec:pet}). No result in this note depends on it.
