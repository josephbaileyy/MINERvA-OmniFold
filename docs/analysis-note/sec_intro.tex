\section{Introduction and motivation}
\label{sec:intro}



MINERvA (Main Injector Experiment for $\nu$-A) is a dedicated neutrino--nucleus scattering experiment in Fermilab's NuMI beamline, immediately upstream of the MINOS near detector~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}. During roughly a decade of operation ending in 2019, it recorded high-statistics samples in low-energy and medium-energy (ME) beam configurations~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn} and in neutrino-dominated (forward horn current, FHC) and antineutrino-dominated modes. MINERvA measures neutrino--nucleus cross sections on a range of nuclear targets in the few-GeV region most relevant to accelerator-based oscillation experiments. These cross sections and their nuclear effects are a leading systematic uncertainty for T2K~\cite{T2K:2023osc}, NOvA~\cite{NOvA:2021nfi}, Hyper-Kamiokande~\cite{HyperK:2018design}, and DUNE~\cite{DUNE:2020jqi}. The few-GeV regime combines quasielastic, resonant, and deep-inelastic processes that no single model describes well. This analysis uses the public ME FHC CC-inclusive data product (detector details in \S\ref{sec:experiment}).

The MINERvA ME FHC charged-current (CC) inclusive double-differential cross section in muon transverse and longitudinal momentum, $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$, was published by Ruterbories \emph{et al.} \cite{MINERvA:2021owq} using D'Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU)~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} with a binned response matrix. We reproduce it with \emph{unbinned} OmniFold~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw, Andreassen:2021zzk}, which iteratively reweights events using machine-learned likelihood ratios on per-event observables rather than on a fixed histogram binning.

Machine-learning advances have enabled unbinned unfolding (overviews in Refs.~\cite{Arratia:2021otl,Huetsch:2024quz}; cross-experiment guidance in Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide}). One broad family iteratively reweights a starting simulation; OmniFold~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk,Chan:2023tbf} is its most widely adopted method. The other generates new samples directly with a neural network~\cite{Datta:2018mwd,Bellagente:2019uyp,Bellagente:2020piv, Backes:2022vmn}. Both lift the binned-response limitations of widely-used classical methods~\cite{Adye:2011gm} such as IBU, the SVD method~\cite{Hocker:1995kb}, and TUnfold~\cite{Schmitt:2012kp}.

\paragraph{Prior OmniFold applications.} Over the last few years OmniFold has moved from a method paper to production measurements. H1 published the first real-data applications in deep-inelastic scattering: lepton--jet correlations unfolded simultaneously in eight dimensions~\cite{H1:2021wkz}, followed by jet substructure at high $Q^{2}$~\cite{H1:2023fzk}. LHCb unfolded identified-hadron distributions in $Z$-tagged jets~\cite{LHCb:2022rky}. ATLAS performed a simultaneous unbinned measurement of twenty-four $Z+$jets observables~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}. Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} collects these and other applications and distills cross-experiment practice. Three of the practices it audits appear in our uncertainty budget: the ensemble-mean cross-check of the central value, the bootstrap statistical block, and the unified-throw covariance check of \S\ref{sec:uq-combine}.

H1 cut backgrounds to a negligible level; LHCb corrected per track before unfolding. ATLAS left its less-than-\SI{0.25}{\percent} background in place and assigned it as an uncertainty, while identifying negatively-weighted simulated events as the natural in-method subtraction~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets,Andreassen:2021zzk}. Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} recommends that route for nontrivial backgrounds. Recent methodological work explicitly treats backgrounds, fakes, acceptance, and inefficiency in OmniFold-HI~\cite{Falcao:2025backgrounds} and generative unfolding~\cite{Butter:2025genfold}. These constructions address related problems; their subtraction prescriptions can differ.

Closest to this analysis is the study of Huang \emph{et al.}~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}, the first application of OmniFold to a neutrino cross section: a mock-data unfolding of muon kinematics on the public T2K near-detector simulated dataset, benchmarked against traditional binned approaches. Two of its findings bear on neutrino applications. The first is its iteration count, which we do not adopt (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). The second is an ensemble of reweighting trials with different train/test splits that controls the ML stochastic component; we budget that component explicitly as the training-seed variation band (\S\ref{sec:systematics}). The present work differs from that study in three ways. It unfolds \emph{real} MINERvA data to an absolutely normalized cross section, with a 2D uncertainty budget whose blocks are all built with the central value's own learner (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). It uses GBDT learners suited to a low-dimensional tabular feature set. It validates the method directly against a published binned measurement of the same data (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Why unbinned:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item OmniFold learns event weights in the full observable space and histograms only the final result, so reported bins can be chosen or refined after unfolding without rerunning it. Background subtraction is unbinned too in the 2D result and its covariance and in the 3D and 4D central values, which use refined negative-weight subtraction (\S\ref{sec:method-algo}). The five-dimensional central value and its covariance, and the extended-fiducial value, use the binned purity weight, a per-reco-bin $(D-B)/D$ weight fixed \emph{before} training.

  \item Additional observables enter as input features, without multiplying response-matrix bins. The available-energy extension (\S\ref{sec:3d}) is built this way; a binned method such as IBU would need a correspondingly larger multidimensional response matrix to include it.
  \item OmniFold regularizes through model capacity and through an iteration count, which it shares with IBU. Comparing the two methods on the same data probes the result's method dependence (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Analysis strategy.} The published 2D cross section provides a quantitative validation of unbinned OmniFold on MINERvA data and a size comparison for its uncertainty budget (\S\S\ref{sec:experiment}--\ref{sec:results}). The same pipeline then adds $\Eavail$, $q_{3}$ (\S\ref{sec:3d}) and $W$ (\S\ref{sec:eavailw}) as classifier inputs, tested by marginalization to each lower-dimensional result, closure, coverage and classifier-family comparisons. The validation and uncertainty of the five-dimensional estimator (\S\ref{sec:5d}) and the calibrated generator tests built on it (\S\ref{sec:joint5d}) follow these central values. A scalar study with relaxed truth-muon cuts (\S\ref{sec:fps}) probes an alternative signal definition.

\paragraph{Scope.} The results hold for one implementation of unbinned OmniFold, applied to one data set, and for the configurations, observables and deformations tested. The two-dimensional result has an uncertainty budget whose coverage has not been tested (\S\ref{sec:uq-boot}, \S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}, \S\ref{sec:coverage}). Above two dimensions the results are central values without intervals of validated coverage (\S\S\ref{sec:3d}--\ref{sec:5d}). The generator tests of \S\ref{sec:joint5d} evaluate hybrid null hypotheses and add no reportable uncertainty. Point-cloud (PET) unfolding of the full event is future work, under development and documented separately (App.~\ref{sec:pet}). No result in this note depends on it.
