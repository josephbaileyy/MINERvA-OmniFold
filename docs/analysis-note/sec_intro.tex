\subsection{Motivation and previous measurements}

Accelerator-based neutrino oscillation experiments infer oscillation parameters from the energy dependence of event rates. Both the probability that a neutrino interacts and the relation between its energy and the observed final state depend on neutrino--nucleus scattering. At energies of a few GeV, quasielastic scattering, resonance production, and deep-inelastic scattering populate overlapping regions of observed phase space. Nuclear effects further modify the particles available for energy reconstruction. Uncertainties in these processes affect the interpretation of data from T2K, NOvA, Hyper-Kamiokande, and DUNE~\cite{T2K:2023osc,NOvA:2021nfi,HyperK:2018design,DUNE:2020jqi}. Differential cross sections constrain the interaction models used to predict these rates and reconstruct neutrino energies.

MINERvA measured neutrino--nucleus scattering on several targets in the low- and medium-energy configurations of Fermilab's NuMI beam~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz,MINERvA:2016iqn}. Using the medium-energy neutrino exposure, with a mean neutrino energy of about $6\,\mathrm{GeV}$, Ruterbories \emph{et al.} reported the inclusive charged-current (CC) $\nu_\mu$ cross section on hydrocarbon, differential in the muon momentum transverse ($p_T$) and longitudinal ($p_\parallel$) to the beam~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. That measurement used D'Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU) with a binned detector-response matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx}.

Binned implementations of IBU, singular-value decomposition, and TUnfold require a response grid that grows with the number of observables~\cite{Adye:2011gm,Hocker:1995kb,Schmitt:2012kp}. Unbinned machine-learning methods use individual events~\cite{Arratia:2021otl,Huetsch:2024quz,Canelli:2025guide} to reweight a reference simulation or generate unfolded samples~\cite{Datta:2018mwd,Bellagente:2019uyp,Bellagente:2020piv,Backes:2022vmn}. OmniFold iteratively reweights simulated events using classifier estimates of likelihood ratios~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk,Chan:2023tbf}. It takes additional observables as classifier inputs. The same event weights can then be used for different projections and reporting bins.

H1 published the first applications to collider data, unfolding eight-dimensional lepton--jet correlations and later jet substructure at high $Q^2$ in deep-inelastic scattering~\cite{H1:2021wkz,H1:2023fzk}. LHCb measured identified-hadron distributions in $Z$-tagged jets~\cite{LHCb:2022rky}, and ATLAS unfolded 24 $Z$+jets observables simultaneously and subsequently reported a measurement of the full charged-particle phase space~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets,ATLAS:2026fullps}. Recent work has also addressed background treatment in reweighting and generative approaches~\cite{Falcao:2025backgrounds,Butter:2025genfold}.

Huang \emph{et al.} gave the first neutrino cross-section application of OmniFold, comparing an unfolding of muon kinematics in simulated T2K near-detector data with traditional binned methods~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.

\subsection{Objectives and scope}

We apply OmniFold to MINERvA's public data to measure the cross section in five observables. We add the available hadronic energy $E_{\mathrm{avail}}$, the magnitude of the three-momentum transfer $q_3$, and the hadronic invariant mass $W$ in sequence to test recovery of correlations and the uncertainties on the joint distribution. We benchmark the two-dimensional central values against MINERvA's published $d^2\sigma/(dp_T\,dp_\parallel)$, comparing the normalization and shape in the same signal phase space. The estimates share data and systematic uncertainties, and their cross-covariance is unavailable. Two analysis choices were made after the comparison with the publication was known. The benchmark is therefore descriptive.

The two-dimensional result reproduces the published integral within about $1\%$. A correlated shape difference of unidentified origin remains. The covariance procedure under-covers at both held-out simulation tunes. The higher-dimensional unfoldings preserve normalization and agree with the lower-dimensional results in the tested projections. We report the three- and four-dimensional central values without covariances and the five-observable cross section in sixteen cells for descriptive comparisons with generator predictions. The reported sixteen-cell covariance is too small for the deviations from truth in held-out-tune pseudo-experiments, even though it includes terms those pseudo-experiments do not vary. Model tuning or constraints would require resolving this shortfall, residual bias, algorithm and rounding dependence, and the unexplained run-period difference. A post hoc check finds each joint generator-test rejection also visible in a matched coarse projection, so the five-observable tests show no evidence of discrimination beyond those projections.
