\section{Statistical methods}
\label{app:statmethods}
\label{app:methods}

This appendix collects the statistical methods of the measurement: covariance
inversion; the training-seed, bootstrap and universe estimators, the
MAT-conformant construction with the $1/\Phi$ flux normalization, and their
combination; the comparison conventions ($\chi^{2}$, pulls, shape-only); the
ill-conditioning/rank analysis; the tension anatomy; the closure tests; and the
full-event $C_{\rm stat}$ construction. The 2D pull diagnostics and the open
coverage test are in \S\ref{sec:coverage}. Only the analysis-specific
conventions are documented, not a general statistics primer; the $\chi^{2}$
convention referenced throughout is Eq.~\eqref{eq:chi2} in \S\ref{sec:chisq}.
The variance source this note calls \emph{training-seed variation} is named \texttt{seedscan} in the scripts, figure files and stored artifacts; the code name is retained there as provenance and the two refer to the same object.


\subsection{Numerical conventions: inverting a covariance (Cholesky, ridge jitter, pseudo-inverse)}
\label{sec:invert}

Equation~\eqref{eq:chi2} requires inverting $\mathbf{C}$.

\paragraph{Cholesky and ridge jitter.}
$\mathbf{C}$ is positive-definite (PD) iff $\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}\T$
for a lower-triangular $\mathbf{L}$ (the Cholesky factorization); if it
succeeds, Eq.~\eqref{eq:chi2} is well-posed. A PD matrix left barely non-PD by
numerical noise is repaired with an isotropic ridge $\varepsilon\,\mathbf{I}$,
which \texttt{uq/analyze\_uq.py} sets to
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon \;=\; 10^{-12} \,\frac{\Tr\mathbf{C}}{n},
\end{equation}
twelve orders of magnitude below the mean diagonal: far below any real signal
in $\mathbf{C}$ and safely above floating-point round-off.

\paragraph{Rank deficiency at $N \le n$.}
A sample covariance from $N\le n$ replicas in $n$ bins has rank at most $N-1$.
This was the regime of the Stage-1 bootstrap
($N=50$ replicas, $n=205$ bins): Cholesky failed without jitter, and
the required jitter, $1.6\times10^{-94}$, was a numerical
artifact of the rank deficiency rather than a real near-singularity.
The Stage-2 bootstrap ($N=300 > 205$) is full-rank. The
\emph{systematic} covariance has a more stubborn version of this
problem that survives at any universe count (\S\ref{sec:rank}).

\paragraph{Pseudo-inverse.}
For the $\chi^{2}$ against the paper covariance,
\texttt{compare\_to\_paper\_fullcov.py} replaces $\mathbf{C}^{-1}$
with the Moore--Penrose pseudo-inverse computed via SVD,
$\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{U}\Sigma\mathbf{V}\T$ with
$\Sigma=\diag(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{+} \;=\; \mathbf{V}\,\Sigma^{+}\,\mathbf{U}\T,
\qquad
\Sigma^{+}_{ii} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
1/\sigma_{i} & \sigma_{i} > \tau,\\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
It calls \texttt{np.linalg.pinv} with no \texttt{rcond} argument (lines
\texttt{103} and \texttt{124}), so the operative cutoff is \textbf{NumPy's
documented default for that function}, $\tau = \texttt{rcond}\cdot\sigma_{\max}$
with $\texttt{rcond}=10^{-15}$ --- \emph{not} the
$n\,\sigma_{\max}\varepsilon_{\text{machine}}$ convention of
\texttt{np.linalg.lstsq} and MATLAB, which at $n=205$ would be about
$45\times$ looser ($205\times2.22\times10^{-16}=4.6\times10^{-14}$); an earlier
version of this appendix quoted that formula for \texttt{pinv} and was wrong.
Singular modes below the cutoff are set to $0$ in the inverse rather than
$\infty$, so the quadratic form is stable even when $\mathbf{C}$ has zero or
near-zero eigenvalues.

\paragraph{Training-seed variation.}
\label{sec:seedscan}
For each reported bin $i$, the $n$ trial values $X_{i}^{(1)},\dots,X_{i}^{(n)}$
give the unbiased sample standard deviation
\begin{equation}
\sigma^{\ML}_{i}
\;=\;
\sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(X^{(k)}_{i}-\bar X_{i}\big)^{2}},
\qquad
\bar X_{i} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k} X^{(k)}_{i},
\end{equation}
summarized by the median of $\sigma^{\ML}_{i}/\bar X_{i}$ over the $205$
reported bins. With $n=10$ \texttt{lgbm} trials the total-$\sigma$ relative
spread is $\boxed{0.0055\%}$, the per-bin median relative spread
$\boxed{0.166\%}$, and $\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}^{\ML}} = 5.061\times10^{-41}$.
Against the paper's per-bin total-uncertainty median of $6.86\%$ the ML noise
is $\approx 2.4\%$ of the quoted uncertainty, far subdominant to data-stat and
systematics. Going from $n=4$ to $n=10$ trials moved these numbers within
rounding, so the envelope is converged.

\paragraph{Poisson bootstrap.}
\label{sec:bootstrap}
\emph{Coherent} means that an up-weighted data event flows through both the
step-1 classifier training and the final cross-section construction, and that
an up-weighted MC event carries the same factor on its reco-tree and
truth-tree copies, which keeps the migration matrix self-consistent. The two
sub-RNGs make the data and MC sources uncorrelated by construction, and pinning
the GBDT seed isolates the bootstrap from the ML noise of \S\ref{sec:seedscan}.
A Poisson(1) factor can be zero, so some events are dropped from each replica,
mimicking a resample with replacement; Poisson(1) is the large-$N$ limit of
the multinomial counts (\S\ref{app:poisson}). Each replica's $14\times16$
histogram is flattened into a $205$-vector $\mathbf{X}^{(k)}$ over reported
bins (row-major over $(\pT,\pPar)$), and the covariance is the unbiased sample
covariance
\begin{equation}
\big(\mathbf{C}^{\boot}\big)_{ij}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}
\big(X_{i}^{(k)} - \bar X_{i}\big)\big(X_{j}^{(k)} - \bar X_{j}\big),
\qquad
\bar X_{i} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k} X_{i}^{(k)},
\label{eq:bootcov}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ \texttt{np.cov(Xrep, rowvar=False, ddof=1)}, checked with the
Cholesky-plus-jitter pattern of \S\ref{sec:invert}.
\emph{Seed pinning:} the quoted $N=300$ block pins \texttt{-{}-seed 1} and varies
only \texttt{-{}-bootstrap-seed}, so it carries no training-seed variation. It
superseded an earlier set whose replicas varied both seeds and so leaked ML
stochasticity into the statistical block (\S\ref{sec:combine}).

\paragraph{Universes.}
\label{sec:universes}
Where the bootstrap shakes the \emph{events}, the universes shake the
\emph{model}. The Stage-2 sweep has \textbf{187 universes across 44 bands}. The
dominant ensemble is the $100$-universe PPFX flux band (hadron-production
sampling); almost all other bands are $\pm1\sigma$ pairs ($N_{u}=2$), e.g.\
\texttt{Muon\_Energy\_MINOS} and the lateral \texttt{Muon\_Energy\_MINERvA}
(muon energy scale), \texttt{MinosEfficiency} (MINOS muon-match efficiency),
the GENIE knobs \texttt{MaRES}, \texttt{MvRES}, \texttt{MaCCQE}, and the
lateral FSI response \texttt{GEANT\_\{Neutron,Pion,Proton\}}. The five
genuinely kinematic bands (BeamAngleX/Y, MuonResolution,
Muon\_Energy\_MINERvA/MINOS) require selection-complete
\texttt{MNV101\_ACTIVE\_UNIVERSE} event loops when migration across the event
selection matters. MinosEfficiency and GEANT are weight-only; that classification places them outside the kinematic replacement and does not by itself establish hadronic-response completeness (\S\ref{sec:systematics}).

MINERvA's MAT framework
(\texttt{PlotUtils/\allowbreak MnvVertErrorBand::\allowbreak CalcCovMx}, \S\ref{sec:minerva})
builds the per-band covariance of the $205$-vectors $\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)}$ as the
\emph{mean-centered, biased} sample covariance, referenced to the
\textbf{universe mean} (not the CV) and normalized by $1/N_{u}$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\mathbf{C}^{B}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{N_{u}}\sum_{u=1}^{N_{u}}
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)} - \langle\mathbf{X}\rangle_{B}\big)
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)} - \langle\mathbf{X}\rangle_{B}\big)\T,
\qquad
\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle_{B} = \frac{1}{N_{u}}\sum_{u}\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)}.
\;}
\label{eq:matcov}
\end{equation}
It is applied \emph{uniformly} to all $N_{u}\ge 2$, with \textbf{no
special-case $\pm1\sigma$ pair formula}. At $N_{u}=2$ it collapses to the rank-1
$\tfrac14(\mathbf{X}_{+}-\mathbf{X}_{-})(\mathbf{X}_{+}-\mathbf{X}_{-})\T$;
mean-centering kills the symmetric component, so a one-sided or asymmetric pair
correctly contributes near-zero variance rather than the rank-2 contribution a
naive CV-centered formula would give (\S\ref{app:pair}). The $1.4\%$ overall
target-nucleon normalization uncertainty (Aliaga NIM, arXiv:1305.5199), which
lies outside the \texttt{MnvVertErrorBand} machinery, is added as a
fully-correlated rank-1 outer product, and bands are assumed independent
(different physics sources, different RNGs):
\begin{align}
\mathbf{C}^{\text{norm}} \;&=\; (\sigma_{N}\,\mathbf{X}^{\CV})(\sigma_{N}\,\mathbf{X}^{\CV})\T,
\qquad \sigma_{N} = 0.014,
\label{eq:normband}
\\
\mathbf{C}^{\syst}
\;&=\;
\sum_{B}\mathbf{C}^{B}\;+\;\mathbf{C}^{\text{norm}}.
\label{eq:systblocksum}
\end{align}
Independence is a modeling assumption with one known correlated exception --- the
Bashyal flux\,$\leftrightarrow$\,Muon\_Energy\_MINOS joint block
(\S\ref{sec:rank}), which we have since rederived ($\rho=0.900$,
\texttt{uq/flux\_muonE\_cross.root}); it is real but rank-preserving, so for the
2D budget the block sum is retained as the headline and the cross term is
carried as a diagnostic.

A second and larger correction is omitted by Eq.~\eqref{eq:systblocksum}: the
band sum is linear in the per-band covariances, whereas the OmniFold reweighter
is \emph{nonlinear} in the input perturbations, so jointly perturbing all bands
and re-unfolding produces a cross term beyond any pairwise correlation. A
\emph{unified throw} measures this directly --- $N$ joint re-unfolds in which
every reweightable band and the flux multisim are perturbed simultaneously per
event ($w_{\CV}\prod_B\rho_B^{g_B}$, $g_B\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$) and the cross
section re-extracted. The corrected implementation (asymmetric $\pm1\sigma$
endpoint interpolation, one fixed estimator seed, covariance about the throw
mean, mean shift from CV stored separately) is described in
\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}; it does not subtract a scalar run-to-run jitter floor
(\texttt{nd-unfolding/unified\_throw\_cov.py}), and the 5D result uses its
strict joint ensemble and matched-endpoint comparator. No corrected 4D or
extended-fiducial covariance is reported. A single-throw superposition probe is
insufficient to test the nonlinear response. Any adoption must document the
low-rank treatment, mean shift, and selection-support bound before transferring
information onto a sweep block,
$\mathbf{C}^{\syst}\!\to\!(\mathbf{C}^{\syst}-\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{vert}})+\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{vert}}\mathbf{G}$
($\mathbf{G}=\operatorname{diag}g$), which is positive-semidefinite by construction
(a direct rank-$N$ swap is not) and never falls below the block baseline.

\paragraph{How MINERvA builds its published covariance.}
\label{sec:minerva}
The construction is not spelled out in the paper; it is defined in the MAT C++
framework, and \textbf{MAT samples per-band exactly as we do}, using the
mean-centered $1/N$ sample covariance and summing bands element-wise.
\emph{What the Ruterbories 2021 paper says (Section V):}
three uncertainty categories --- flux, detector response,
neutrino-interaction model --- evaluated by ``re-extracting the cross
section using modified simulations'', i.e.\ the universe method, but
with \emph{no covariance formula given}. Specifics quoted:
flux below $4\%$ across most of phase space thanks to the
$\nu$-$e$-scattering constraint (Valencia, arXiv:1906.00111);
$1.4\%$ target-nucleon normalization; in-situ test-beam hadron
response with per-particle GEANT inelastic variations; muon energy
scale $1\%$ constrained by the low-recoil fit of Bashyal
(JINST 16 P08068), with the explicit statement that
\emph{``the fit causes the flux and muon energy scale uncertainties to
be correlated and that correlation is propagated to the final
result''} --- the only non-block-diagonal piece the paper admits.
The only covariance in its equations is $V$ in the model-test
$\chi^{2}$ (Eq.~1, ``the measurement covariance matrix''), with $205$
degrees of freedom (Table I), plus a log-normal $\chi^{2}$ variant
motivated by the highly-correlated flux normalization (Peelle's
Pertinent Puzzle).
\emph{What the paper does not say:} no per-band covariance construction, no
$\pm1\sigma$ pair formula, no band-independence/block-sum statement; nothing
about rank, condition number, or regularization of $V$; no bootstrap or
coverage validation (the paper uses D'Agostini iterative unfolding). All of
this is delegated to MAT or simply unstated.

\emph{Per band (\texttt{MnvVertErrorBand::CalcCovMx}).} The MAT source fixes
the three properties encoded in Eq.~\eqref{eq:matcov}: the reference is the
\textbf{universe mean} $\langle x\rangle_{u}$, not the central value $X_{\CV}$;
the normalization is \textbf{$1/N$} (biased), not Bessel's $1/(N-1)$ --- a $1\%$
offset for PPFX ($N=100$); and there is \textbf{no special case for $N=2$}.
The ``MINERvA-101 $\pm1\sigma$ pair'' formula
$\tfrac12(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{+}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{+}\T +
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-}\T)$ is a folk re-derivation that
agrees only for CV-symmetric shifts. We verified our rollup against MAT
byte-for-byte: populating a \texttt{PlotUtils::MnvH1D} with the same $44$ bands
/ $188$ universes and comparing \texttt{MnvH1D::GetTotalErrorMatrix} against our
\texttt{-{}-add-norm 0} rollup gives a max element-wise relative difference of
$5.5\times10^{-17}$ (machine $\varepsilon$), with $\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}}$
matching to seven digits.
\emph{Total (\texttt{MnvH1D::GetTotalErrorMatrix}).} A straight element-wise sum
across bands plus the statistical matrix. Band-independence is assumed;
cross-band correlations exist only if encoded inside a single combined band
(e.g.\ the Bashyal joint fit, handled outside this loop). MAT does \emph{no}
ridge / shrinkage / SVD truncation on the published total cov and no special
handling of rank deficiency --- the published $V$ is full-rank because it
includes the \emph{statistical} block, not because it sums richer systematics
(its systematic-only rank is $120<140$; see \S\ref{sec:rank}).
\emph{Upstream references that set each band's $\pm1\sigma$ amplitude:}
Flux / PPFX (100 universes), Aliaga, PRD 94 092005 (2016), arXiv:1607.00704;
$\nu$-$e$ flux constraint ($<4\%$), Valencia, PRD 100 092001 (2019),
arXiv:1906.00111; muon-E scale $\leftrightarrow$ flux fit, Bashyal, JINST 16
(2021) P08068; test-beam / GEANT hadron response, Aliaga, NIM A 789 28 (2015),
arXiv:1501.06431; GENIE reweight knobs, Andreopoulos, NIM A 614 87 (2010), and
the GENIE manual arXiv:1510.05494; $1.4\%$ normalization (detector mass),
Aliaga, NIM A 743 130 (2014), arXiv:1305.5199.

\paragraph{Block-sum combination.}
\label{sec:combine}
The bootstrap samples \emph{events} (with two distinct sub-RNGs for data and
MC), the universes vary \emph{model weights} with the GBDT seed pinned, and the
training-seed variation scans the \emph{GBDT seed} with all weights fixed. The
three Monte Carlos are independent by construction, so
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{\tot}
\;=\;
\mathbf{C}^{\ML} \,+\, \mathbf{C}^{\stat} \,+\, \mathbf{C}^{\syst}.
\label{eq:blocksum}
\end{equation}
The combined-cov hook in \texttt{compare\_to\_paper\_fullcov.py}
(\texttt{-{}-omnifold-cov ROOT:HIST}, repeatable) adds OmniFold-derived
covariances on top of the paper's \texttt{TotalCovariance} by exactly this
rule. $\mathbf{C}^{\ML}$ is more than an order of magnitude below the other
two; the headline includes it but it is numerically negligible.

\subsubsection{Covariance-aware $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$}
\label{sec:chisq}

With $\boldsymbol{\Delta}\equiv\mathbf{x}^{\ours}-\mathbf{x}^{\paper}$
on the $205$-bin set and $\mathbf{C}$ the chosen covariance,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chi2}
\chi^{2}
\;=\;
\boldsymbol{\Delta}\T\,\mathbf{C}^{+}\,\boldsymbol{\Delta},
\qquad
\mathrm{ndf} \;=\; n_{\text{reported}} = 205,
\qquad
\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf} \;=\; \chi^{2}/205.
\end{equation}
\texttt{chi2\_with\_cov} in \texttt{compare\_to\_paper\_fullcov.py} builds the
$205\times205$ block, inverts it with \texttt{np.linalg.pinv}
(\S\ref{sec:invert}), and forms the quadratic form;
\texttt{-{}-omnifold-cov ROOT:HIST} (repeatable) adds each OmniFold covariance
via Eq.~\eqref{eq:blocksum}, and \texttt{-{}-subtract-stat} removes the paper's
stat block to avoid double-counting the bootstrap (\S\ref{sec:rank}).

\paragraph{This $\mathrm{ndf}$ is dimension-conditional, and is justified here by the truncation
scan --- not by convention.} Setting $\mathrm{ndf}=n_{\text{reported}}$ presumes the covariance has
close to $n_{\text{reported}}$ well-determined directions. On the $205$-bin 2D problem that
presumption is \emph{tested and holds}: the rank-truncation scan rises smoothly
$0.69\,(r{=}50)\to2.35\,(100)\to3.30\,(180)\to3.66\,(205)$ with no plateau or late jump, the
signature of a spectrum whose effective rank is comparable to its dimension. The scan is the
evidence; $205$ is not assumed.

\textbf{It does not transfer to the N-D covariances.} The standard-P4 5D candidate covariance has
$10\,694$ reported bins and a measured effective positive rank of $\mathbf{263}$ (numerical
condition number infinite), and its $4825$-bin 5D$\to$4D projection preserves that rank exactly at
$263$. A sum of ${\sim}45$ two-endpoint MAT bands plus statistical and ML blocks cannot span
$10\,694$ directions, so this is a property of the construction, not a defect. Quoting
$\chi^{2}/n_{\text{reported}}$ against such a covariance divides by a number roughly $40\times$
(5D) or $18\times$ (4D) larger than the count of directions actually contributing to the quadratic
form, and would understate any tension by that factor.

\textbf{Therefore $\mathrm{ndf}=n_{\text{reported}}$ must not be used for an N-D covariance.} Any
N-D $\chi^{2}$ shall instead quote (i) the inverse actually used --- pseudo-inverse with its
\texttt{rcond}, or the truncation rank --- stated at the point of quotation rather than by
reference; (ii) the \emph{retained rank} as $\mathrm{ndf}$; (iii) the rank-truncation scan for that
covariance, which is expected \emph{not} to be smooth, and whose smoothness is precisely what
licensed the 2D choice above; (iv) which covariance is meant, since the 5D candidate, its 4D
projection and the published 2D block have different ranks; and (v) \emph{for every
sample-covariance block entering the sum}, its ensemble size $N$, its normalization convention
(biased $1/N$ or unbiased $1/(N-1)$), the effective dimension $p$ actually inverted after
truncation, and the finite-ensemble treatment applied --- or the explicit statement that none was.
Measurements and derivation: App.~\ref{app:provenance} (\texttt{rank-inversion}); the
predeclared rank cut itself is a statistics decision recorded in
\texttt{docs/STATVAL\_REPAIR.md}.

\paragraph{Why declaration (v), and why it does not yet mandate a correction.}
Requirements (i)--(iv) can all be satisfied while a quoted $\mathrm{ndf}$ silently inherits an
uncorrected finite-ensemble bias from the sample-covariance blocks inside the sum, because none
of them is the ensemble size of any such block. The inverse of a noisy sample covariance is biased
\emph{upward}: for an unbiased $1/(N-1)$ estimator over $N$ independent Gaussian realizations the
standard debiasing factor is $(N-p-2)/(N-1)$, and for this analysis's biased $1/N$ production
convention (\texttt{nd-unfolding/uq\_math.py}, ``universe-mean centered, biased 1/N'') it is
$(N-p-2)/N$. On the unified-throw 5D candidate, with $N=160$ joint throws and a truncated
$p=100$, that is $58/160\simeq0.363$ --- the uncorrected precision matrix is too large by a
factor $\simeq2.76$. \textbf{The consequence runs the opposite way to the intuition:} a
$\chi^{2}$ evaluated on a fixed residual is \emph{inflated}, not flattered, so tension is
overstated rather than hidden; what becomes over-optimistic is any confidence region derived from
the same over-tight covariance.
\textbf{No generic correction is mandated here, deliberately.} The quoted covariance is a
\emph{sum} of about 45 deterministic rank-one MAT band outer products plus statistical and ML
blocks, which has no single debiasing factor; the standard factor also assumes independent
Gaussian realizations and a truncation dimension chosen independently of the data, neither of
which is established for a data-dependent rank cut. The treatment is therefore to be decided
\emph{for the covariance and inverse actually adopted}, once (v) makes its operands exist. The
cheap half is unconditional: record $N$ beside each block in the construction receipts.

\paragraph{What protects the \emph{quoted} combined $\chi^{2}$: an external statistical floor.}
The headline $\chiCombined$ inverts not our blocks alone but the paper$+$ours \emph{sum}, which
carries the paper's \texttt{StatOnlyCov}: rank $205$ and near-diagonal (\S\ref{sec:rank}), it puts a
variance floor under \emph{every} eigendirection of the combined matrix. It is also the larger of the
two statistical contributions, by $\simeq2.5$ in \emph{square-root} trace and so by
$2.55^{2}\simeq6.5$ in \emph{trace} (operands in \S\ref{sec:rank}; the power matters, since the two
figures describe one comparison and differ by more than a factor of two). The small-eigenvalue
directions on which a finite-$N$ debiasing factor would act are therefore set by an external,
published block rather than by any ensemble of ours, which bounds this quoted number's exposure to
declaration~(v). \emph{This is deliberately not an argument from trace share:} a trace weights
eigenvalues by $\lambda$ and a precision matrix by $1/\lambda$, so a block with a negligible trace
fraction can still dominate an inverse. The ours-only construction demonstrates it: without the
external floor our finite-$N$ blocks fill the null space of $\mathbf{C}^{\syst}$ (rank $140\to201$)
and the pseudo-inverse returns $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=252$ (\S\ref{sec:rank}). The protection
concerns \emph{where the smallest retained eigenvalues come from}, not how much trace our blocks
hold. It does not make $\chiCombined$ a calibrated goodness-of-fit --- it is not one, for the
separate shared-systematics reason given with the worked example below.

Three things have to be said with it, and none of them is repaired by a
disclaimer placed elsewhere.

\emph{(i) It cannot be calibrated, because the cross-covariance is missing.}
For two estimates $x$ and $y$ built from the same data,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:diffcov}
  \operatorname{Cov}(x-y) \;=\; C_{x} + C_{y} - C_{xy} - C_{yx},
\end{equation}
and the published (IBU-era) result and this OmniFold result share the same
MINERvA data and the same flux, detector and GENIE systematics. The
$C_{xy}$ terms are unavailable, so the summed covariance $C_{x}+C_{y}$ is the
wrong matrix by an unknown amount: it double-counts the shared systematics and
omits their correlation, and the sign of the net error is not determined. No
value of this statistic --- $1.5$, $1$, or anything else --- can therefore be
converted into a goodness-of-fit or a $p$-value.

\emph{(ii) ``Correct uncertainties give exactly one'' is false and has been
removed from this appendix.} Under the usual calibrated assumptions
$\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ has \emph{expectation} $1$ and a spread of order
$\sqrt{2/\mathrm{ndf}}$, which at $\mathrm{ndf}=205$ is about $0.10$; a single
realization near $1$ is not evidence of correct uncertainties and a
realization at $1.5$ is not by itself evidence against them. An earlier version
of this paragraph said that a perfectly correct model with correct
uncertainties gives exactly $1$, and then used $\approx1.5$ as a tolerance
against it. Both halves of that inference are withdrawn.

\emph{(iii) The coverage question is untouched by it.} The 2D toy ensemble
provides no independent-truth coverage test (\S\ref{sec:coverage}), so nothing
here validates the interval this covariance implies. We quote the number as a
distance and a ratio to the paper; the reported agreement claims are the
per-bin standardized differences and the ratios of \S\ref{sec:pull}, not this
scalar. The log-normal variant (matching the paper's Table I treatment of the
correlated flux normalization) gives $\chiCombinedLog$, essentially identical.

\subsubsection{Pull distribution}
\label{sec:pull}

For each of the $205$ reported bins,

\;=\;

\qquad
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{pull}_{i}] = 0,\quad
\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{pull}_{i}] = 1
\;\text{under the null},
\end{equation}
summarized by the pull mean, RMS, the $14\times16$ pull heatmap and a pull
histogram (\texttt{compare\_to\_paper\_fullcov.py}). With the combined
covariance (paper TotalCov $+$ flux-fixed matcorr universe $+$ bootstrap-300

\subsubsection{Shape-only $\chi^{2}$ with Jacobian propagation}
\label{sec:shape}

This tests whether the \emph{shape} of the $(\pT,\pPar)$ cross section matches
the paper independently of absolute normalization, which depends on the flux
integral, target nucleon count, and POT (each can drift by $\mathcal{O}(\%)$);
the shape is a pure unfolding diagnostic. With bin widths
$w_{j} = (\Delta\pT)_{j}\,(\Delta\pPar)_{j}$ and total
$T(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{j\in\text{mask}} w_{j}x_{j}$, the unit-area shape is
$s_{i}(\mathbf{x}) = x_{i}/T(\mathbf{x})$, with $\sum_{i} w_{i} s_{i} = 1$ by
construction, and (\texttt{normalize\_xsec\_shape.py}: \texttt{shape\_jacobian},
\texttt{shape\_chi2})
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial s_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{T}\big(\delta_{ij} - s_{i}\,w_{j}\big),
\qquad
\mathbf{J} \;=\; \frac{1}{T}\big(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{s}\,\mathbf{w}\T\big),
\qquad
\mathbf{C}^{\text{shape}} \;=\; \mathbf{J}\,\mathbf{C}^{\paper}\,\mathbf{J}\T.
\end{equation}
The unit-area constraint kills one degree of freedom: $\mathbf{w}\T\mathbf{J} =
\mathbf{0}\T$, so the ``all-bins-up'' direction is the kernel of $\mathbf{J}$,
and $\mathbf{C}^{\text{shape}}$ has one zero eigenvalue and rank $n-1$.
Accordingly $\chi^{2}_{\text{shape}} = (\mathbf{s}^{\ours}-\mathbf{s}^{\paper})\T
(\mathbf{C}^{\text{shape}})^{+} (\mathbf{s}^{\ours}-\mathbf{s}^{\paper})$, the
pseudo-inverse dropping the constrained direction, with
$\mathrm{ndf} = n_{\text{bins}} - 1 = 204$ on the $205$-bin set.
With the paper's \texttt{TotalCovariance} (Jacobian-propagated) the shape
$\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ on $205$ bins is $\boxed{3.596}$: essentially the
absolute-normalization $3.661$ \emph{minus} the contribution of overall scale.
The total-$\sigma$ ratio $\sigma_{\tot}^{\ours}/\sigma_{\tot}^{\paper} = 1.0111$
is a $1\%$ scale difference; shape disagreement at the $\sim 1\%$ level (median
bin ratio $1.0064$, $94\%$ of bins within $10\%$ of the paper) accounts for the
remaining shape residual.

\subsection{Inference from unfolded events and selected projections}
\label{sec:unbinned-inference}
Unfolding induces correlations among weighted events, so treating an unfolded
sample as independent observations in a downstream likelihood can
underestimate parameter uncertainties~\cite{Desai:2025correlated}; nominal
event weights alone do not specify that uncertainty. One possible approach
is to propagate coherent unfolding replicas through the downstream fit;
independently resampling already-unfolded rows is not equivalent to resampling
the original inputs and repeating the learned estimator. Classifier and
permutation goodness-of-fit tests likewise need a null calibration appropriate
to their fitting procedure and any reuse of data. This is a requirement on
inference, not a new validation result for the tests reported here.

The uncertainty target must also be specified: coverage for each bin or
projection separately does not imply simultaneous coverage for their joint
collection. For a fixed linear projection $y=P x$, covariance propagation is
$C_y=P C_x P^{\top}$ (for densities, $P$ includes the integrated bin widths);
this identity propagates a covariance but does not establish its calibration
or account for choosing a region after inspecting the data.

Recent work constructs calibrated intervals for selected functionals in
constrained inverse problems~\cite{Stanley:2025functionals} and joint confidence
regions for multiple functionals~\cite{Batlle:2025simultaneous}, which
could provide independent benchmarks for specified integrals or contrasts.
Their guarantees depend on the forward model, noise law, and parameter
constraints and do not transfer automatically to a learned response with
finite simulation and detector nuisances. A rank-deficient forward operator
is also distinct from a rank-deficient sample covariance; neither truncating
the latter nor validating a few projections certifies the full distribution.
Any such benchmark would supplement the required scalar-5D uncertainty
construction, not replace its adoption or supply coverage for PET.

\subsection{Ill-conditioning and rank deficiency}
\label{sec:rank}

\paragraph{The obstruction.}
An \emph{ours-only} $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ --- the data-to-model agreement
evaluated with our own covariance
$\mathbf{C}^{\ours} = \mathbf{C}^{\syst} + \mathbf{C}^{\boot} +
\mathbf{C}^{\ML}$ rather than the paper's $V$ --- requires inverting
$\mathbf{C}^{\ours}$, and it is rank-deficient:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}^{\syst}_{\text{sample}}) = 140 / 205,
\qquad
\mathrm{cond}(\mathbf{C}^{\syst}) = 8.7\times10^{8}.
\end{equation}
The deficiency is set by \textbf{band count, not universe count}:
under Eq.~\eqref{eq:matcov} each $\pm1\sigma$ pair is
rank $1$ and the $100$-universe PPFX band is rank $\le 99$ (and
\emph{effectively} rank-$1$: a single normalization mode holds
\SI{99.6}{\percent} of its variance, \S\ref{sec:uq-universes}), so the
$44$-band sum cannot fill the $205$-dimensional bin space. \emph{This is
not a missing-bands problem.} The paper's own systematic-only covariance
($\texttt{TotalCov}-\texttt{StatOnlyCov}$) is rank $120$ --- \emph{lower}
than our $140$ --- so we propagate at least as many independent systematic
directions as the published analysis (we use the same
\texttt{GetStandardSystematics}). The paper's $V$ is full-rank
($\mathrm{rank}=204$, $\mathrm{cond}=1.5\times10^{12}$) purely because it
\textbf{includes the statistical block} ($\texttt{StatOnlyCov}$, rank
$205$, near-diagonal), not because it sums richer systematics. Adding our
$300$-replica bootstrap does the same for us:
$\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}^{\syst}+\mathbf{C}^{\boot})=201$,
$\mathrm{cond}=3.8\times10^{14}$.

\paragraph{The real obstruction is statistical, not rank.}
Even at rank $201$ a defensible single ours-only $\chi^{2}$ is out of
reach, for a \emph{statistical-precision} reason. The bootstrap block is
smaller in \emph{global scale} by a factor $2.55$ in
square-root trace ($\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}^{\boot}}=1.817\times10^{-40}$ vs
$\sqrt{\Tr\,\texttt{StatOnlyCov}}=4.639\times10^{-40}$), equivalently about
$6.5$ in trace. The paper's full-rank, near-diagonal \texttt{StatOnlyCov} supplies a
variance floor in every reported eigendirection; because the covariance structures
differ, this should \emph{not} be read as a uniform $6.5$-fold per-mode effect. The
naive pseudo-inverse gives $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=252$ for
$\mathbf{C}^{\syst}+\mathbf{C}^{\boot}$, dominated by poorly-determined
directions, against the paper's $3.66$ with its larger stat floor, so the lever
toward an ours-only $\chi^{2}$ is the \emph{statistical} block, not more
systematic bands. The gap is \emph{not} a missing MC-statistical term: our
bootstrap already draws independent Poisson($1$) multipliers on \emph{both} data
and MC per replica (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap}; Practical Guide 2507.09582 \S5.1).
The remaining candidates are a genuine difference in statistical efficiency
between the unbinned reweighting and the published binned D'Agostini unfold, a
difference in what the paper's \texttt{StatOnlyCov} encompasses, or a
regularization effect in our own estimator that suppresses dispersion without
improving information. The split-bootstrap test below separates the first two
from an MC-statistics omission; \textbf{it does not separate them from the
third}.

\paragraph{Resolution (data/MC split bootstrap).}
A $200{+}200$-replica \emph{split} bootstrap Poisson-fluctuates the
data weights only (\texttt{boot\_data}) or the MC weights only (\texttt{boot\_mc})
with independent RNGs, so in expectation
$\mathbf{C}^{\boot}=\mathbf{C}^{\boot}_{\mathrm{data}}+\mathbf{C}^{\boot}_{\mathrm{mc}}$.
(i) \emph{Closure holds:}
$\sqrt{\Tr(\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{data}}+\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{mc}})}/
\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}^{\boot}}=1.07$, with the variance split $77\%$ data /
$23\%$ MC. (ii) The \emph{data stream alone} gives
$\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{data}}}/\sqrt{\Tr\,\texttt{StatOnly}}=0.356$
($\mathbf{C}^{\boot}/\texttt{StatOnly}=0.392=1/2.55$): the $2.5\times$ deficit is
present in the data-only stat error, so it is \emph{not} an MC-statistics
omission. \textbf{What that does and does not license.} It locates the
difference in the measured leg rather than in the simulated one. It does
\emph{not} show that our estimator extracts more information from the same
data: a smaller bootstrap dispersion is equally consistent with regularization
bias --- an estimator that responds less to resampling because it is pulled
toward its prior --- and with an interval that simply undercovers. Telling
those apart needs a coverage test against an independently stored truth, which
this analysis does not have (\S\ref{sec:coverage}). The measured statement is
the ratio; ``higher statistical efficiency'' is one of at least three
explanations of it and is not established here. (iii) The \emph{structure} also
differs: our stat block is correlated (mean
$|{\rm off\text{-}diag}\,\rho|=0.108$, leading-eigenvector overlap with
\texttt{StatOnly} $0.46$) whereas the paper's is near-diagonal ($0.009$); scaling
our block to the paper trace still leaves $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=76.7$, so the gap
is shape as well as magnitude. \textbf{Consequence:} the correct ours-only
$\chi^{2}$ uses our \emph{own} $\mathbf{C}^{\ours}$, not the paper's stat floor.
The truncation-dependence below remains (the inversion is still regularizer-set),
so we lead with per-bin pulls and the scan; the ours-only goodness-of-fit
is reported on $\mathbf{C}^{\ours}$
via a truncated-spectral $\chi^{2}$ (retain $\lambda>10^{-10}\lambda_{\max}$), the
same convention carried into the 3D generator comparison (\S\ref{sec:rank} is the
2D anchor; the 3D comparison applies it on all $1431$ reported bins).

On $\mathbf{C}^{\ours}$ over the $205$ reported bins, no regularizer
gives a stable single number:

$\texttt{TotalCov}-\texttt{StatOnlyCov}$ before adding our covariances,
driving $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ $\chiCombined\to \chiCombinedSubStat$. That \emph{over}-corrects:
our $\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}^{\boot}}=1.83\times10^{-40}$ is far smaller
than the paper's published stat block ($\sim$ the $2.7\times10^{-39}$
scale of \texttt{TotalCov}), so subtracting the latter without a
comparable replacement removes too much. The headline therefore keeps
the double-counted $\chiCombined$ as the well-defined ``what does OmniFold UQ
add on top of the published $V$'' statement, with the de-double-counted
number on file as a secondary diagnostic.

\paragraph{Remaining refinements and adopted conventions.}
The Bashyal flux\,$\leftrightarrow$\,muon-energy joint block is rederived

\subsection{Anatomy of the paper-covariance tension}
\label{sec:tension}

The short statement is in \S\ref{sec:results-tension}; this subsection gives
the derivation.

\paragraph{The paradox.}
The central values agree well ($\sigma_{\tot}^{\ours}/\sigma_{\tot}^{\paper}
= 1.011$, median bin ratio $1.006$), and the per-bin pull RMS against the paper
covariance is only $0.598$ (\S\ref{sec:pull}). A diagonal-only $\chi^{2}$,
ignoring all off-diagonal correlations, is therefore
\begin{equation}
\chi^{2}_{\diag}/\mathrm{ndf}
= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\frac{\Delta_{i}^{2}}{C_{ii}}
= \mathrm{RMS}(\mathrm{pull})^{2} + \mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{pull})^{2}
\approx 0.366,
\end{equation}
yet the full-covariance $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=3.661$: a
$10\times$ inflation that lives \emph{entirely} in the off-diagonal /
small-eigenvalue structure of the published \texttt{TotalCov}
(\texttt{diagnose\_tension.py}).

\paragraph{Eigenmode decomposition.}
Diagonalizing the reported-block paper covariance,
$\mathbf{C}=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\T$
($\lambda_{1}\!\ge\!\cdots\!\ge\!\lambda_{n}$), and projecting
$c_{k}=\mathbf{u}_{k}\T\boldsymbol{\Delta}$,
\begin{equation}
\chi^{2}=\boldsymbol{\Delta}\T\mathbf{C}^{+}\boldsymbol{\Delta}
=\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k}^{2}}{\lambda_{k}},
\qquad
\text{``eigen-pull''}_{k}\equiv\frac{c_{k}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}},
\end{equation}
verified to reproduce the \texttt{pinv} $\chi^{2}=750.49$ to $3\times10^{-9}$.
The tension is \textbf{neither a few pathological null directions nor the
absolute smallest eigenvalues}. The top contributing modes sit at
$\lambda_{k}/\lambda_{\max}\sim10^{-4}$--$10^{-5}$ --- the lower-middle of the
spectrum by magnitude, but still $\sim7$--$8$ decades above the cond-floor
($\lambda/\lambda_{\max}\sim10^{-12}$) --- with standardized-component
magnitudes $5$--$6$; these are descriptive coordinates in the published
covariance basis, not calibrated significances for two independent results.
The ten \emph{smallest}-$\lambda$ modes carry only $3\%$ of the $\chi^{2}$ and
about $90$ modes are needed to reach $90\%$: the $\chi^{2}$ is broadly
distributed, not localized to the ill-conditioned tail. A \texttt{pinv}-rcond
scan confirms it is not a numerical artifact: retaining only the $73$
best-determined directions ($\lambda_{k}/\lambda_{\max}>10^{-4}$) still gives
$\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=1.42$, and $139$ directions ($>10^{-6}$) give $2.79$;
the rank-truncation scan of \S\ref{sec:chisq} rises smoothly with no late jump,
so the tension does not ride on the last few least-determined modes.

\paragraph{It is shape, not normalization.}
Splitting $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ into a component along the measured
spectrum $\mathbf{x}^{\paper}$ (an overall scale) and the orthogonal
remainder, the scale piece contributes $\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{norm}}=0.10$ and the
shape piece $\chi^{2}_{\text{shape}}=750.1$. The $+1.1\%$ normalization
offset is statistically irrelevant to the tension; it is a correlated
\emph{shape} difference (consistent with the Jacobian shape-only
$\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=3.60$, \S\ref{sec:shape}).

\paragraph{Where it lives.}
Mapping the dominant eigenvectors and the per-bin contribution
$\Delta_{i}(\mathbf{C}^{+}\boldsymbol{\Delta})_{i}$ back onto the
$14\times16$ grid (\texttt{tension\_mode\_maps.png},
\texttt{tension\_chi2\_map.png}) localizes the tension to the
\textbf{low-$\pT$ cross-section peak ridge} ($\pT\lesssim0.4$ GeV/c,
$\pT$-bins 2/7/10 carry $16/11/12\%$) and the low-$\pPar$ near-edge
cells ($\pPar$-bins 0/1/8). The carrier modes are sign-alternating
\emph{shape} oscillations across the peak, not vertical $\pT$-columns
(which a flux $1/\Phi(\pT)$ normalization error would produce) and not
the $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$ acceptance boundary. This is exactly where
the flux and Muon\_Energy bands --- the two largest, and the only named
cross-band correlation (Bashyal, \S\ref{sec:rank}) --- dominate the
covariance: tight bin-to-bin correlation there leaves little freedom to
absorb a coherent $\sim1$--$2\%$ shape difference, so a small
OmniFold-vs-IBU shape difference becomes a large $\chi^{2}$.%
\footnote{The pipeline applies one global $\mathrm{POT}_{\mathrm{data}}/\mathrm{POT}_{\mathrm{MC}}$
ratio rather than a per-playlist ratio (KNOWN\_ISSUES J36), and the per-playlist ratios span
$0.1707$--$0.2371$. Because this statement asserts sensitivity at the $1$--$2\%$ level, the
mixture error was measured directly against it: comparing $R_{\mathrm{glob}}\sum_p \mathrm{MC}_p$
with $\sum_p R_p\,\mathrm{MC}_p$ over the twelve playlists changes the reco shape by at most
$0.07\%$ in $\pT$ and $0.14\%$ in $\pPar$ (and $0.03\%$ across the low-$\pT$ ridge), with the
residual $+0.12\%$ appearing as normalization rather than shape. The twelve playlists are nearly
shape-identical, so a large reweighting of them is almost pure normalization. The statement above
is therefore unaffected at the $14$--$30\times$ level.}

\paragraph{A methodological component (estimator and iteration).}
Part of the tension is regularization-dependent: the same data and
normalization ($\Sigma\sigma$ within $0.1\%$), unfolded with a different
GBDT backend, move the paper-cov $\chi^{2}$ by $\sim1$ unit while central
values barely change (iteration count, by contrast, is negligible):
\begin{center}

\toprule
OmniFold configuration & paper-cov $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ & pull RMS \\
\midrule
exact-GBT, 5 iter (frozen production) & $3.66$ & $0.598$ \\
HistGBT, 5 iter (10-seed mean) & $2.70\pm0.04$ & $0.56$ \\

(i) \textbf{Iteration count is negligible}: LightGBM $5\to10$ iters moves the
$\chi^{2}$ by $+0.01$ ($2.645\to2.657$), so OmniFold is well-converged at $5$
iters and the tension is \emph{not} an under-iteration artifact. (ii)
\textbf{The GBDT estimator carries a $\sim1$-unit band}: the frozen exact-GBT
production ($3.66$) is the outlier, while both histogram-binned backends agree
at $2.65$--$2.70$ (HistGBT is a $10$-seed mean, range $2.63$--$2.74$; the
$\pm0.04$ ML-seed jitter is $\sim25\times$ smaller than the $0.96$
exact$\to$hist gap, so that gap is genuinely the estimator, not seed noise).
The exact-gradient trees fit finer shape structure (less implicit smoothing) and
so deviate more from the paper's IBU-regularized result. This is direct evidence
that $\sim1$ $\chi^{2}$-unit of the tension is OmniFold's implicit
regularization rather than a physical data--MC disagreement
(\texttt{tension\_iter/}).

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The tension is (i) a genuine, broadly-distributed, \emph{correlated shape}
difference --- not a normalization offset and not a small-$\lambda$
inversion artifact --- concentrated in the low-$\pT$ peak where the
flux/Muon\_Energy bands dominate, plus (ii) a $\sim1$-$\chi^{2}$-unit
methodological band from the GBDT estimator/iteration regularization.
Both point to the same structure: the Bashyal
flux\,$\leftrightarrow$\,Muon\_Energy joint block (rederived at
$\rho=\rhoFluxMuon$, \S\ref{sec:rank}) is exactly the covariance structure
governing the region that carries the tension.

\subsection{Closure tests: detecting bias}
\label{sec:closure}

The variance methods of \S\ref{sec:variance} measure how much the unfold
wobbles; closure tests ask whether it is biased --- whether it returns a value
consistently offset from the right answer when a known truth is injected, which
the variance procedures cannot show. Each of the three tests injects a known
deformation into the MC, unfolds, and checks that the answer reproduces the
deformation, against an acceptance threshold on the median per-bin
$|\text{residual}|$ set comfortably above the ML-noise floor of $0.166\%$. All
three use 1A lgbm 5-iteration unfolds; their thresholds and results are listed
in \S\ref{sec:validation}, and the procedures and the values not given there
follow.

\paragraph{Truth-reweight closure.}
\label{sec:closure-tr}
For a known shape $f(\pT,\pPar)$, both the MC reco weights \emph{and} the MC
truth weights are multiplied by $f$ at the event level; the reweighted
pseudo-data are unfolded against the unchanged (CV) response and compared with
the reweighted truth marginal (\texttt{uq/closure/closure\_truth\_reweight.py}).
Two shapes are tested: \texttt{gauss\_pt},
$f = 1 + A\exp\!\big[-((\pT - p_{T}^{0})/\sigma)^{2}\big]$ with $A=0.2$,
$p_{T}^{0}=0.4$ GeV/c, $\sigma=0.1$ GeV/c; and \texttt{tilt\_pz},
$f = 1 + \alpha(\pPar - \pPar^{\text{ref}})$ with $\alpha=0.1$,
$\pPar^{\text{ref}}=5$ GeV/c. Both residuals are $\sim 100\times$ tighter than
the threshold; OmniFold recovers the injected truth shape essentially perfectly.

\paragraph{Hidden-variable closure.}
\label{sec:closure-hv}
The OmniFold feature set sees $(\pT,\pPar)$ on reco and truth but \emph{not}
the resolution variable $\dpT \equiv \pT^{\text{reco}}-\pT^{\text{truth}}$, so a
bias injected on $\dpT$ only --- an axis the network cannot see --- measures how
much leaks into the measured $(\pT,\pPar)$ marginal. The pseudo-data weight
bump is
\begin{equation}
w^{\text{reco}}
\;\to\;
w^{\text{reco}}\cdot
\Big(1 + A\exp\!\big[-((\dpT - c)/\sigma_{\dpT})^{2}\big]\Big),
\end{equation}
with $c=+0.1$ GeV/c, $\sigma_{\dpT}=0.05$ GeV/c, and amplitude scanned over
$A\in\{0.05,0.10,0.30\}$. Truth weights are \emph{unchanged}, so the reference
is the unaltered CV truth marginal (\texttt{uq/closure/closure\_hidden\_var.py}).
With $\bar r(A)$ the median of the per-bin signed residual at amplitude $A$, the
leakage coefficient is the slope of a linear fit,
$\bar r(A) \approx \kappa_{\text{leak}} A$, i.e.\
$\kappa_{\text{leak}} = \partial \bar r/\partial A$: the fraction of an unseen
$\dpT$-shaped bias that contaminates the visible answer. The acceptance bound,
$3.0\%$, is looser than for the truth reweight because $\dpT$ and $\pT$ are
weakly correlated, so the unfolding can never fully kill the leak.

On the $205$ reported bins the per-amplitude median residuals,
$0.57\% / 1.14\% / 3.43\%$ for $A = 0.05/0.10/0.30$, are linear in $A$, as
expected for small perturbations, with fitted slope
$\kappa_{\text{leak}} = \boxed{0.113}$: about $11\%$ of a hidden $\dpT$ bias
leaks into the measured $(\pT,\pPar)$ marginal. \textbf{Against the $3.0\%$
acceptance bound, two of the three arms pass and the largest does not:}
$A=0.30$ gives $3.43\%$, which \emph{exceeds} $3.0\%$. \S\ref{sec:validation}
records that arm as exceeding the threshold and this appendix agrees with it;
the earlier reading here --- that $A=0.30$ was under threshold and the leakage
was ``small enough to be safely ignored'' --- was an arithmetic error and is
withdrawn. What the scan establishes is the linearity and the coefficient: the
acceptance bound is satisfied for hidden-$\dpT$ deformations up to roughly
$A\simeq0.26$ and is violated above it. No non-closure uncertainty is assigned
from this scan either way (\S\ref{sec:validation}); a $\dpT$-shaped mismodeling
at the $30\%$ level would leak more than the analysis declared tolerable, and
that is recorded as an open sensitivity rather than as a passing test.

\paragraph{Alternative-model closure.}
\label{sec:closure-alt}
The OmniFold response is trained on the CV weights and the pseudo-data are built
from an alternative-universe reweighting: \texttt{MaCCQE:0} ($+1\sigma$), which
probes an interaction-model shape change, or \texttt{Flux:50} (PPFX index $50$),
which probes flux reweighting --- two distinct kinds of model dependence. The
reference is the in-acceptance-extrapolated alternative truth
(\texttt{uq/closure/closure\_alt\_model.py}),
\begin{equation}
\tilde X^{\text{ref}}_{i}
\;=\;
X^{\CV,\text{truth}}_{i}\cdot
\frac{X^{\text{alt},\text{in-acc}}_{i}}{X^{\CV,\text{in-acc}}_{i}},
\end{equation}
which scales the CV truth marginal by the alternative-to-CV ratio of
in-acceptance counts, isolating the unfolding's response to a model-shape
change from acceptance/efficiency effects. The in-acceptance biases are
$-1.68\%$ for \texttt{MaCCQE:0} (folded out by the extrapolation) and $-7.32\%$
for \texttt{Flux:50}. Both residuals pass the $2.0\%$ bound with $\gg 5\times$
headroom: the unfolding is robust to model variations on the order of
$\pm 1\sigma$ of GENIE and full PPFX flux universes.

\subsubsection*{Standing assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Gaussian approximation.} Pulls and $\chi^{2}$ treat
        each bin as Gaussian, justified because per-bin entries are
        weighted sums over $\mathcal{O}(10^{4})$ MC events (central
        limit theorem).
  \item \textbf{Independence of error sources.}
        Eq.~\eqref{eq:blocksum} requires no cross-correlations between
        ML, statistical, and systematic. The bootstrap pins the GBDT
        seed (so ML noise cancels at first order); universes pin the
        seed too; the training-seed variation pins the bootstrap. Cross-terms are
        higher-order. (The quoted $N=300$ bootstrap pins the GBDT seed;
        only the superseded seed-varying set leaked, \S\ref{sec:bootstrap}.)
  \item \textbf{Bootstrap diagnostic.} The
        same-ensemble $68.71\%$ fraction of \S\ref{sec:coverage} is a Gaussianity check, not a
        half-percent certification of $\mathbf{C}^{\stat}$.
  \item \textbf{Band independence in $\mathbf{C}^{\syst}$.}
        Different physics sources are taken as uncorrelated; the one
        known exception, the Bashyal flux\,$\leftrightarrow$\,muon-E
        joint block, is rederived and carried as a diagnostic --- adding
        it leaves the systematic rank unchanged (\S\ref{sec:rank}).
  \item \textbf{Combined-$\chi^{2}$ double-count.} The headline shares
        systematics with the paper's $V$ and is the ``what OmniFold UQ
        adds on top of $V$'' statement, not an independent ours-only
        test (\S\ref{sec:rank}).
  \item \textbf{Closure passes are valid for the perturbations
        tested.} Nothing guarantees they pass for unphysical
        deformations far outside the training distribution.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Multinomial $\to$ independent Poisson bootstrap limit}
\label{app:poisson}

A non-parametric bootstrap with replacement draws $N$ events from the
original $N$-event sample, so the draw counts satisfy
$(n_{1},\dots,n_{N}) \sim \mathrm{Multinomial}(N,\mathbf{p})$ with
$p_{i}=1/N$ uniform and $\sum_{i}n_{i}=N$. Each marginal
$n_{i}\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(N,1/N)$ has mean $1$ and variance
$(N-1)/N \to 1$, and for large $N$ converges to $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$
(Poisson limit theorem: many trials, small success probability, fixed expected
count). The constraint $\sum n_{i}=N$ introduces $\mathcal{O}(1/N)$
correlations between the $n_{i}$, which vanish in the large-$N$ limit, leaving
\emph{independent} Poisson(1) variables: the multinomial is a Poisson process
\emph{conditioned} on hitting $N$ counts, and the unconditional version is
independent Poissons (Poissonization). We implement the independent-Poisson form
(one \texttt{np.random.default\_rng().poisson(1.0)} draw per event) and drop the
constraint; for $N \gtrsim 10^{6}$ (our regime) the resulting relative error is
$\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})$ on the total event count --- far smaller than the
per-bin Poisson variance.

\subsection{The $\pm 1\sigma$ pair covariance: mean-centered (MAT-conformant)}
\label{app:pair}

For a band with two universes $u=0,1$ at $\pm 1\sigma$, with value vectors
$\mathbf{X}_{0}$, $\mathbf{X}_{1}$ and universe mean
$\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle = \tfrac12(\mathbf{X}_{0}+\mathbf{X}_{1})$, the
MAT-conformant estimator Eq.~\eqref{eq:matcov} at $N_{u}=2$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{B}_{\text{mean}}
\;=\;
\tfrac12\sum_{u=0,1}(\mathbf{X}_{u}-\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle)(\mathbf{X}_{u}-\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle)\T
\;=\;
\tfrac14(\mathbf{X}_{0}-\mathbf{X}_{1})(\mathbf{X}_{0}-\mathbf{X}_{1})\T,
\end{equation}
a rank-1 outer product of the \emph{half-difference}; being mean-centered, it
gives nothing for a common offset of the pair away from $X_{\CV}$. A naive
alternative centers on the CV, with
$\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{u} = \mathbf{X}_{u}-\mathbf{X}_{\CV}$,
$\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{0}=\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{a}$,
$\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}=\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{a}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{B}_{\text{CV-centered}}
\;=\;
\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{0}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{0}\T
                + \boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}\T\big)
\;=\;
\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}\T + \mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}\T.
\end{equation}
The two agree \emph{only} for a CV-symmetric pair ($\mathbf{m}=0$),
where both reduce to $\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}\T$. For an asymmetric or
one-sided pair the CV-centered form adds a rank-1
$\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}\T$ term: it counts the pair's common offset from the
central value as a second variance direction, which mean-centering discharges.
\textbf{Every per-band block $C_{b}$ in this analysis is mean-centered, matching
MAT} (\S\ref{sec:minerva}); the distinction matters for the asymmetric bands
(BeamAngle, MuonResolution, and the one-sided NC band).

\paragraph{Scope, and why ``mean-centered throughout'' would be wrong.} The
statement above is confined to the $\pm1\sigma$ \emph{band} estimator.
\textbf{The adopted five-axis covariance is not
mean-centered at every step, and the CV-centered term is retained there on
purpose.} Its inflation factor, Eq.~\eqref{eq:zinflation}, takes as its
numerator the diagonal of the unified-throw covariance centered on the
\emph{central value}, and the two centerings of that object differ by exactly
the outer product of the measured joint mean shift, Eq.~\eqref{eq:zcentering}.
So the $\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}\T$-type term that a single asymmetric band should
not carry is, at the level of the joint throw ensemble, a measured displacement
of the throw mean from the central value that the adopted variant keeps rather
than discards. It is not ``spurious'' in that role; calling it so is a
statement about the two-universe band estimator only. The mean-centered sibling
is structurally identical and measures $\sqrt{\Tr C}$ \SI{7.13}{\percent}
lower, which is why the variant is read from the product's own metadata and
enforced in code rather than assumed
(\S\ref{sec:eavailw-construction}).

\subsection{The full-event $C_{\rm stat}$ construction: what it is, and what it is not}
\label{app:cstatlimit}

\noindent\textbf{If you are here for the published uncertainty, it is not this.}  The
uncertainty on the published result is constructed in \S\ref{sec:systematics} and
its adopted five-axis covariance is identified in \S\ref{sec:eavailw-adopted}.
The remainder of this section describes a \emph{different} object, for a
different estimator, and none of it enters a published number.

\noindent\textbf{Status boundary.}  This artifact exists and its construction is
recorded, but it has one builder, is not independently verified or adopted, and
is not paired with the full-event nominal.  Joseph declined that pairing on
2026-08-20; no PET total covariance is adopted for publication.  The details
below describe the construction and measured family dispersion, not coverage or
an adopted ``statistical uncertainty.''

\noindent\textbf{Why it is documented under statistical methods rather than with
the point-cloud results.}  It is the statistical method for the full-event
estimator, which is expected to return to the measurement once the defects
behind its declined pairing are understood; filing the method with the
diagnostic results would record a current status as a permanent one.  \emph{The
current status is the operative fact}: today the full-event track is diagnostic
and method development, it carries no adopted uncertainty, and nothing here
anticipates a result.

\subsubsection{Construction, stated as a method choice}

The artifact is built from a $50$-member replica family. Each member repeats the whole analysis
(background-subtracted target construction, estimator training, cross-section extraction) on an
independent \emph{coherent} $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$ bootstrap of \emph{all three} contributing
inventories: one global factor per data, signal-MC and background-MC event, drawn from distinct
reproducible streams before any training subset is taken and reused wherever that event appears
(\S\ref{app:poisson}). The streams sit at offsets $s$, $s+10^{7}$ and $s+2{\times}10^{7}$ on the
replica seed $s$; the signal stream uses the same primitive as the recoil-only replicas, so those two
contracts are bit-consistent and share their first two offsets. Checks against the code should
identify the streams \emph{by offset}: the full-event loader draws the background factor a second
time as a verification, so a count of draws in that file is right for the wrong reason, and the
signal draw is not in that file at all. The data and background factors rebuild each member's
background-subtracted target through the Stay-Positive refinement rather than rescaling the nominal
target; the signal factors follow the signal MC everywhere it is used. The background stream is a
\emph{third} stream relative to the scalar bootstrap of \S\ref{sec:bootstrap} (data and MC only),
because the full-event pipeline injects its background as a separate weighted inventory with
counting noise of its own. The construction therefore resamples data and MC counting variation
together; this is its intended scope and does not make it an adopted statistical uncertainty. The
covariance is the sample covariance of the $50$ cross sections about their own mean, normalised
$1/(N-1)$, on the density scale.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{This is not a data-statistical error and must not be compared with one.} Published
statistical uncertainties are data-statistics; this object is not, and the difference is not small.
It resamples three inventories --- $4{,}116{,}128$ data, $49{,}152{,}885$ signal-MC and $564{,}591$
background-MC rows --- with raw counting fractions $1/\sqrt{N}$ of $0.0493\%$, $0.0143\%$ and
$0.1331\%$: the smallest inventory, the background MC, carries by far the largest counting floor,
and the quadrature combination, $0.1426\%$, is $2.89\times$ the data stream's $0.0493\%$ alone.
\textbf{These are row-count fractions on inventories, not variance shares of the cross section.} An
earlier version reported from them that MC accounted for ${\sim}88\%$ of the variance and the
background MC alone for $87\%$; that step does not follow and is withdrawn. The streams enter with
different weights and leverage (the background as a negative injection through the Stay-Positive
refinement, the signal MC through the trained map, the data through both target and fit), so a
cross-section variance decomposition needs propagation weights this analysis has not computed. The
numbers support only the qualitative point: the object resamples simulated inventories alongside the
measured one, one of them is small, and the total cannot be read as a data-statistical error. The
\emph{set} of streams is certain; a decomposition over them is not available. The measured family
spread is far above every one of these floors, so they describe what the object resamples, not how
the observed band divides; neither reading makes the quoted band field-comparable.

For a \emph{learned}, non-linear estimator, a coherent $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$ bootstrap --- the
standard non-parametric stand-in for statistical uncertainty --- does not automatically inherit its
linear-case properties. We verified that the
family is what the construction claims: the multiplicity distribution reproduces the
$\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$ probability mass function for $k=0\ldots5$ to better than $0.5\%$, the
zero-weight fraction is $e^{-1}$ to $2.4\times10^{-4}$ pooled and within $1.9\sigma$ of $e^{-1}$ in
every one of the $18$ populated longitudinal-momentum columns, and the members' refined targets agree
with the nominal's to $0.068\%$ on the rows both retain.

\subsubsection{Why the two readings of the band are not a live fork}

The family disagrees with the central value by a factor of order three, coherently, in the
best-accepted region of the plane. Two readings are available a priori.
\textbf{(a) The spread is real:} the estimator is genuinely this sensitive to the measured
sample there, and the quoted uncertainties in $6<p_{\parallel}<\SI{20}{GeV/c}$ really are of
order $67\%$. \textbf{(b) The proxy is wrong:} $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$ resampling gives $36.8\%$
of measured rows zero weight, and a learned estimator facing that much removed support may
respond in a way that is a property of the perturbation rather than of the data. Both fit the
measurements above: an unstable estimator and an invalid perturbation both act during training,
concentrate where information is scarcest, and leave the extraction faithful.
The proposed binary was withdrawn on 2026-08-19 as a type mismatch: a covariance measures
dispersion \emph{about a centre} and does not establish finite-family \emph{containment} of a
separately trained nominal, so non-containment of the nominal by $50$ draws is not what this
covariance reports and \textbf{no choice between (a) and (b) follows from it}. The readings are
recorded because the measurements were gathered while they were live and a reader will meet them in
that form elsewhere; neither is asserted.

What follows concerns the resampling scheme alone and is offered as an argument rather than a
ratified result; it bears on reading (b) without adjudicating the retracted fork. The resampled
objects are individual reconstructed events. A $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$ multiplicity $k$ on a row
states that, were the data retaken, that event occurred $k$ times; zero states that it did not
occur, which is exactly what counting noise does to an event observed once, so the zero atom is not
an artifact of the method. Independent $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$ multiplicities also reproduce exact
Poisson fluctuations in every aggregate of those rows (\S\ref{app:poisson}), which variance-matched
continuous alternatives do not: they match the first two moments and get the discreteness wrong.
\textbf{That argument reaches counts, and it stops there.} Exact aggregation concerns unweighted sums
of resampled rows; this covariance disperses the output of a weighted, learned, iteratively refit
estimator evaluated on every replica, and nothing above shows that a perturbation with the right
counting behaviour induces the right \emph{sampling} distribution for that functional --- still less
that the resulting interval covers. An earlier version concluded that the scheme ``is the sampling
distribution rather than a stand-in that might be the wrong one''; that promotion is withdrawn. What
survives is narrower, and is what reading~(b) needs: the zero atom is what counting noise does to a
row observed once, so its presence is not by itself evidence that the proxy is invalid.

A discriminating contrast was designed --- $\mathrm{Exponential}(1)$ shares mean and variance
with $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$ and differs only in $P(X{=}0)$, so substituting it would hold the
perturbation's variance fixed while removing its zero-support --- and it will not be run, because
neither outcome would change what is published. \textbf{The reason is the ruling, not a publication
argument.} Joseph ruled on 2026-08-20 that this covariance is not paired with the full-event
nominal, that \emph{no} PET total covariance is adopted for publication, and that PET remains a
diagnostic and method-development result reconsiderable only on estimator-equivalence \emph{and}
coverage evidence (App.~\ref{app:provenance}, \texttt{pet-pairing}). No ``publish it as it stands''
branch exists for either outcome to select; the earlier framing here --- that an unimportant zero
atom would mean publishing the covariance as it stands --- described a posture the ruling had already
closed, and is withdrawn. What stands on its own is the other half: replacing $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$
with a zero-free scheme because it yields a smaller uncertainty would be choosing a noise model by
its answer, not a defensible basis for tightening an error bar. Nor would the contrast be coverage
evidence, which is the gate the ruling actually sets.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{What the band figure is.} The $67\%$ is the spread of a \emph{refit} estimator under
\emph{total-statistics} resampling (data, signal MC and background MC together, not the measured
leg alone), and so not a data-statistical error in the sense a published one is. Two consequences
travel with the number wherever it is quoted. First, it is the observed refit dispersion \emph{of
this estimator} --- a learned, iterative reweighting refit on every replica --- not a property of
the data alone; it includes the estimator's own sensitivity to which events are present. Second, it
is a property of \emph{this} estimator-plus-perturbation pair and \textbf{not} a bound on what the
data can support. An earlier version called it an ``upper bound on how poorly the data constrain the
cross section,'' reasoning that a better-regularised estimator could improve on it and none could do
worse for want of information. That is withdrawn: no information bound follows from an observed
refit dispersion without conditions on the estimator's bias and on the perturbation's validity as a
sampling proxy, and neither is established here; a more strongly regularised estimator can show
\emph{less} dispersion while constraining the cross section no better, exactly the direction that
breaks the claimed inequality. What is measured is a dispersion, under a stated perturbation, of a
stated estimator, on the statistical component only; it says nothing about systematics.
Ratification rests with the estimator's owner and the covariance-construction reviewer, the open
item recording the question is not closed by anything here, and nothing here licenses promoting the
central value, quoting the covariance as verified, or treating the region as settled.

\subsubsection{Limitations of the covariance object itself}

\emph{The covariance was built by one implementation and is therefore not independently
verified.} Its specification clauses are abort-on-failure assertions inside that same builder (a
self-check), and the regression against the in-tree recipe has power over arithmetic and ordering
only, not over domain or centring. No claim of independent construction or verification is made for
this object, and none should be inferred from the clause count.

Every estimated standard deviation carries a $10.1\%$ fractional uncertainty of its own from the family
size alone, $1/\sqrt{2(N-1)}=1/\sqrt{98}$, against the $7.11\%$ that $N=100$ would give. The design
document specifying $N=100$ labels a family of this size \texttt{INSUFFICIENT}, and because that
document's supersession is unratified, the label stands in the record as written (\texttt{VL132},
\texttt{CSTAT-R7}).

The reporting domain and the \emph{stably} reported domain differ, and quoting one for the other is an
error. The measurement is reported on $262$ cells (non-zero reconstruction acceptance) and the
covariance on the same domain, but only $257$ of those cells are reported by \emph{every} member:
five enter the reported set in some members and not others, so their apparent variance is partly the
reporting mask switching rather than the cross section moving, and one of the five is reported in
only $24$ of the $50$ members. Those five are published and flagged and are used in no inversion or
goodness-of-fit. \textbf{The $257$ are the quotable all-member domain, and they are not an
``invertible sub-block'' --- this appendix called them one, which is impossible at this rank, and the
name is corrected here.} The covariance's rank is $49=N-1$, so no sub-block larger than $49$ cells is
ordinarily invertible, $257$ and $262$ alike; any use requiring an inverse requires a declared
regularisation which this analysis does not silently supply (\S\ref{sec:rank}). Finally, the
covariance is on the density scale and centred on the replica mean rather than on the published
central value: integrating it requires bin areas, and the centring means it does not by itself
express the offset tabulated above.
