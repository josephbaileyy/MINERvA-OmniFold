\subsection{Numerical conventions: inverting a covariance (Cholesky, ridge jitter, pseudo-inverse)}
\label{sec:invert}

Equation~\eqref{eq:chi2} requires inverting $\mathbf{C}$.

\paragraph{Cholesky and ridge jitter.} $\mathbf{C}$ is positive-definite (PD) iff its Cholesky factorization $\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}\T$ exists with lower-triangular $\mathbf{L}$; then Eq.~\eqref{eq:chi2} is well-posed. Numerical noise that leaves a PD matrix barely non-PD is repaired with an isotropic ridge $\varepsilon\,\mathbf{I}$, set to
% set in \texttt{uq/analyze\_uq.py}
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon \;=\; 10^{-12} \,\frac{\Tr\mathbf{C}}{n},
\end{equation}
twelve orders below the mean diagonal, far below any real signal in $\mathbf{C}$ and safely above floating-point round-off.

\paragraph{Rank deficiency at $N \le n$.} A sample covariance from $N\le n$ replicas in $n$ bins has rank at most $N-1$. The 300-replica bootstrap ($N=300 > 205$) is full-rank. The rank of the systematic block is limited at any universe count (\S\ref{sec:rank}).

\paragraph{Pseudo-inverse.} For the $\chi^{2}$ against the paper covariance, the comparison replaces $\mathbf{C}^{-1}$ with the Moore--Penrose pseudo-inverse computed via SVD, $\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{U}\Sigma\mathbf{V}\T$ with $\Sigma=\diag(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})$:
% (\texttt{compare\_to\_paper\_fullcov.py})
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{+} \;=\; \mathbf{V}\,\Sigma^{+}\,\mathbf{U}\T,
\qquad
\Sigma^{+}_{ii} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
1/\sigma_{i} & \sigma_{i} > \tau,\\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The comparison calls \texttt{np.linalg.pinv} at its default, NumPy's documented cutoff $\tau = \texttt{rcond}\cdot\sigma_{\max}$ with $\texttt{rcond}=10^{-15}$; this is not the $n\,\sigma_{\max}\varepsilon_{\text{machine}}$ convention of \texttt{np.linalg.lstsq} and MATLAB. Modes below the cutoff receive inverse weight $0$ rather than $\infty$, keeping the quadratic form stable for zero or near-zero eigenvalues.

\subsubsection{Training-seed variation}
\label{sec:seedscan}
The training-seed block is built from five settings $s\in\{42,45,48,51,54\}$ of the GBDT seed triplet, each unfolded on the same $20$ bootstrap replicas (at $s=42$, the first $20$ of the $300$ of \S\ref{sec:bootstrap}). With $\bar{\mathbf X}^{(s)}$ the mean of the $20$ replica $205$-vectors at setting $s$ and $\bar{\bar{\mathbf X}}$ the mean over the five settings,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{\ML}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{4}\sum_{s}\big(\bar{\mathbf X}^{(s)}-\bar{\bar{\mathbf X}}\big)
\big(\bar{\mathbf X}^{(s)}-\bar{\bar{\mathbf X}}\big)\T ,
\end{equation}
a sample covariance of rank $4$ that includes the bootstrap\,$\times$\,seed interaction at its $1/20$ share. Its total-$\sigma$ relative spread is $\boxed{0.0010\%}$, the per-bin median relative spread $\boxed{0.048\%}$, and $\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}^{\ML}} = 1.351\times10^{-41}$.

\subsubsection{Poisson bootstrap}
\label{sec:bootstrap}
A coherent bootstrap carries each data factor through step-1 classifier training and final cross-section construction. Each MC factor is shared by the reco-tree and truth-tree copies, preserving migration consistency. The three sub-RNGs (data, signal MC, background MC; Table~\ref{tab:variance-sources}) make the sources uncorrelated by construction, and pinning the GBDT seed isolates the bootstrap from the ML noise of \S\ref{sec:seedscan}. A Poisson(1) factor can be zero, so some events are dropped from each replica, mimicking a resample with replacement; Poisson(1) is the large-$N$ limit of the multinomial counts (\S\ref{app:poisson}). Each replica's $14\times16$ histogram becomes a $205$-vector $\mathbf{X}^{(k)}$ of reported bins, row-major over $(\pT,\pPar)$, with unbiased sample covariance
\begin{equation}
\big(\mathbf{C}^{\boot}\big)_{ij}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}
\big(X_{i}^{(k)} - \bar X_{i}\big)\big(X_{j}^{(k)} - \bar X_{j}\big),
\qquad
\bar X_{i} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k} X_{i}^{(k)},
\label{eq:bootcov}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ \texttt{np.cov(Xrep, rowvar=False, ddof=1)}, checked with the Cholesky-plus-jitter pattern of \S\ref{sec:invert}. The quoted $N=300$ block varies only the bootstrap seed and carries no training-seed variation.
% (flags \texttt{-{}-seed 42} pinned, \texttt{-{}-bootstrap-seed} varied, \texttt{-{}-bootstrap-streams three};
% stageC/production/DESIGN.md §3.4, S1)

For these replicas (205 bins, ME~FHC LightGBM, seed~42) the relative standard deviation of the total cross section is $\boxed{0.061\%}$, the per-bin median relative spread $\boxed{0.676\%}$, and the per-bin $p_{84}$ $1.602\%$. With $300$ replicas the per-bin $\sigma$ has a relative standard error of about $4.1\%$ by the Gaussian formula $1/\sqrt{2(N-1)}$; $N=300$ is chosen for full rank.

\subsubsection{Multinomial $\to$ independent Poisson bootstrap limit}
\label{app:poisson}

Drawing $N$ events with replacement from an $N$-event sample gives counts $(n_{1},\dots,n_{N}) \sim \mathrm{Multinomial}(N,\mathbf{p})$ with uniform $p_{i}=1/N$ and $\sum_{i}n_{i}=N$. Each marginal $n_{i}\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(N,1/N)$ has mean $1$, variance $(N-1)/N \to 1$, and converges to $\mathrm{Poisson}(1)$ as $N$ grows (many trials, small success probability, fixed expected count). The $\sum n_{i}=N$ constraint induces $\mathcal{O}(1/N)$ correlations that vanish at large $N$. The multinomial is an independent Poisson process conditioned on $N$ counts; removing that constraint gives independent Poisson(1) variables (Poissonization). We draw one \texttt{np.random.default\_rng().poisson(1.0)} factor per event. For $N \gtrsim 10^{6}$, our regime, the relative total-count error is $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})$, far smaller than the per-bin Poisson variance.

\label{sec:universes}
The July model sweep used by the quoted budget has 187 universes across 44 bands, dominated by the $100$-universe PPFX flux band (hadron-production sampling); the quoted band sum drops Rvp1pi and holds $43$ bands plus the normalization. Almost all other bands are $\pm1\sigma$ pairs ($N_{u}=2$), including \texttt{Muon\_Energy\_MINOS} and lateral \texttt{Muon\_Energy\_MINERvA} (muon energy scale), \texttt{MinosEfficiency} (MINOS muon-match efficiency), GENIE knobs \texttt{MaRES}, \texttt{MvRES}, \texttt{MaCCQE}, and lateral FSI response \texttt{GEANT\_\{Neutron,Pion,Proton\}}. The quoted universe block takes these five bands from re-unfolds of re-selected universes.

MINERvA's MAT framework (\texttt{PlotUtils/\allowbreak MnvVertErrorBand::\allowbreak CalcCovMx}, \S\ref{sec:minerva}) uses the mean-centered, biased sample covariance of the $205$-vectors $\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)}$, referenced to the universe mean with normalization $1/N_{u}$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\mathbf{C}^{B}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{N_{u}}\sum_{u=1}^{N_{u}}
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)} - \langle\mathbf{X}\rangle_{B}\big)
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)} - \langle\mathbf{X}\rangle_{B}\big)\T,
\qquad
\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle_{B} = \frac{1}{N_{u}}\sum_{u}\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)}.
\;}
\label{eq:matcov}
\end{equation}
uniformly for all $N_{u}\ge 2$, with no special $\pm1\sigma$ pair formula. At $N_{u}=2$ it reduces to rank-1 $\tfrac14(\mathbf{X}_{+}-\mathbf{X}_{-})(\mathbf{X}_{+}-\mathbf{X}_{-})\T$. Mean-centering removes the symmetric component, correctly giving near-zero variance for a one-sided or asymmetric pair where naive CV-centering would give rank 2 (\S\ref{app:pair}). The $1.4\%$ target-nucleon normalization uncertainty (Aliaga NIM, arXiv:1305.5199) lies outside \texttt{MnvVertErrorBand} and is added as a fully-correlated rank-1 outer product. Bands are assumed independent, with different physics sources and RNGs,
\begin{align}
\mathbf{C}^{\text{norm}} \;&=\; (\sigma_{N}\,\mathbf{X}^{\CV})(\sigma_{N}\,\mathbf{X}^{\CV})\T,
\qquad \sigma_{N} = 0.014,
\label{eq:normband}
\\
\mathbf{C}^{\syst}
\;&=\;
\sum_{B}\mathbf{C}^{B}\;+\;\mathbf{C}^{\text{norm}}.
\label{eq:systblocksum}
\end{align}
Independence is a modeling assumption with one known correlated exception, the Bashyal flux\,$\leftrightarrow$\,Muon\_Energy\_MINOS joint block (\S\ref{sec:rank}).

Equation~\eqref{eq:systblocksum} omits a larger correction. The band sum is linear, but OmniFold responds nonlinearly to input perturbations, so joint re-unfolding produces a cross term beyond any pairwise correlation. A unified throw measures it through $N$ re-unfolds, simultaneously perturbing every reweightable band and the flux multisim per event ($w_{\CV}\prod_B\rho_B^{g_B}$, $g_B\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$), then re-extracting the cross section. The corrected construction is in \S\ref{sec:uq-combine}; its use in the five-axis covariance of this note is in \S\ref{sec:eavailw-adopted} (Eq.~\eqref{eq:zinflation}).

\subsubsection{The $\pm 1\sigma$ pair covariance: mean-centered (MAT-conformant)}
\label{app:pair}

For two universes $u=0,1$ at $\pm 1\sigma$, with vectors $\mathbf{X}_{0}$, $\mathbf{X}_{1}$ and mean $\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle = \tfrac12(\mathbf{X}_{0}+\mathbf{X}_{1})$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:matcov} at $N_{u}=2$ gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{B}_{\text{mean}}
\;=\;
\tfrac12\sum_{u=0,1}(\mathbf{X}_{u}-\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle)(\mathbf{X}_{u}-\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle)\T
\;=\;
\tfrac14(\mathbf{X}_{0}-\mathbf{X}_{1})(\mathbf{X}_{0}-\mathbf{X}_{1})\T,
\end{equation}
a rank-1 outer product of the half-difference. A common offset from $X_{\CV}$ contributes nothing. Naive CV-centering instead uses $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{u} = \mathbf{X}_{u}-\mathbf{X}_{\CV}$, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{0}=\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{a}$, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}=\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{a}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{B}_{\text{CV-centered}}
\;=\;
\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{0}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{0}\T
                + \boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}\T\big)
\;=\;
\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}\T + \mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}\T.
\end{equation}
The estimators agree only for CV-symmetric pairs ($\mathbf{m}=0$), both reducing to $\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}\T$. For asymmetric or one-sided pairs, CV-centering adds the rank-1 $\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}\T$ common-offset direction. Every per-band block $C_{b}$ here is mean-centered to match MAT (\S\ref{sec:minerva}); this matters for BeamAngle, MuonResolution and the one-sided NC band.

\paragraph{Scope of mean-centering.} Mean-centering applies to the $\pm1\sigma$ band estimator only. The five-axis covariance used in this note deliberately retains the CV-centered term in its inflation operand, the unified-throw covariance diagonal centered on the central value (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-construction}, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:zinflation} and~\eqref{eq:zcentering}).

\subsubsection{How MINERvA builds its published covariance}
\label{sec:minerva}
MAT's C++ framework defines the covariance construction that the published paper leaves unspecified. It is the mean-centered $1/N$ per-band sample covariance and element-wise band sum used here.

\paragraph{Ruterbories 2021, Section V.} Three uncertainty categories --- flux, detector response, neutrino-interaction model --- evaluated by ``re-extracting the cross section using modified simulations'', i.e.\ the universe method, but with \emph{no covariance formula given}. Specifics quoted: flux below $4\%$ across most of phase space thanks to the $\nu$-$e$-scattering constraint (Valencia, arXiv:1906.00111); $1.4\%$ target-nucleon normalization; in-situ test-beam hadron response with per-particle GEANT inelastic variations; muon energy scale $1\%$ constrained by the low-recoil fit of Bashyal (JINST 16 P08068), with the explicit statement that \emph{``the fit causes the flux and muon energy scale uncertainties to be correlated and that correlation is propagated to the final result''} --- the only non-block-diagonal piece the paper admits. The only covariance in its equations is $V$ in the model-test $\chi^{2}$ (Eq.~1, ``the measurement covariance matrix''), with $205$ degrees of freedom (Table I), plus a log-normal $\chi^{2}$ variant motivated by the highly-correlated flux normalization (Peelle's Pertinent Puzzle). \emph{What the paper does not say.} It leaves to MAT, or does not state, the per-band covariance construction, any $\pm1\sigma$ pair formula and any band-independence or block-sum statement; the rank, condition number and regularization of $V$; and any bootstrap or coverage validation (it uses D'Agostini iterative unfolding).

\paragraph{MAT per band and in total.} \texttt{MnvVertErrorBand::CalcCovMx} implements Eq.~\eqref{eq:matcov}, referenced to the universe mean $\langle x\rangle_{u}$ rather than $X_{\CV}$, normalized by biased $1/N$ rather than Bessel's $1/(N-1)$ (a $1\%$ offset for PPFX, $N=100$), with no special case for $N=2$. The folk ``MINERvA-101 $\pm1\sigma$ pair'' formula $\tfrac12(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{+}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{+}\T + \boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-}\T)$ agrees only for CV-symmetric shifts. The quoted universe block, filled into a \texttt{PlotUtils::MnvH1D} with MAT's universe weights on Flux, agrees with \texttt{MnvH1D::GetTotalErrorMatrix} to $1.3\times10^{-16}$ of its largest element; the quoted Flux band carries MAT's $\nu$--$e$ and inverse-muon-decay constraint weights. An earlier check compared \texttt{MnvH1D::GetTotalErrorMatrix} with our band sum without the normalization band (\texttt{-{}-add-norm 0}), over $44$ bands / $187$ universes, and gave a maximum difference of $5.5\times10^{-17}$ of the largest element (machine $\varepsilon$) and $\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}}$ matching to seven digits. That check could not detect that the Flux band omitted those constraint weights, because it filled the \texttt{MnvH1D} without MAT's per-universe weights (\texttt{SetUnivWgts}), so both sides were unweighted; it also ran on a sweep from before the flux fix, not on a quoted product.

\subsubsection{Covariance-aware $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$}
\label{sec:chisq}

With $\boldsymbol{\Delta}\equiv\mathbf{x}^{\ours}-\mathbf{x}^{\paper}$ on the $205$-bin set and $\mathbf{C}$ the chosen covariance,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chi2}
\chi^{2}
\;=\;
\boldsymbol{\Delta}\T\,\mathbf{C}^{+}\,\boldsymbol{\Delta},
\qquad
\mathrm{ndf} \;=\; n_{\text{reported}} = 205,
\qquad
\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf} \;=\; \chi^{2}/205.
\end{equation}
The comparison applies \texttt{np.linalg.pinv} to the $205\times205$ block (\S\ref{sec:invert}) and computes the quadratic form. Options add OmniFold covariances via Eq.~\eqref{eq:blocksum} or subtract the paper's stat block to avoid counting it alongside the bootstrap (\S\ref{sec:rank}).
% Code: \texttt{chi2\_with\_cov} in \texttt{compare\_to\_paper\_fullcov.py};
% options \texttt{-{}-omnifold-cov ROOT:HIST} (repeatable) and \texttt{-{}-subtract-stat}.

\paragraph{Dimension-conditional ndf.} Setting $\mathrm{ndf}=n_{\text{reported}}$ requires nearly that many well-determined directions. The choice rests on the $205$-bin 2D truncation scan rather than being assumed: the scan rises smoothly with the retained rank $r$, $0.39\,(r{=}50)\to1.04\,(100)\to1.76\,(180)\to2.27\,(205)$ (exact-split cross-check: $0.69\to2.35\to3.30\to3.66$), with no plateau or late jump, indicating an effective rank comparable to the dimension. This evidence is specific to 2D and does not transfer to N-D covariances (\S\ref{sec:nd-declarations}).

\emph{(i) Missing cross-covariance prevents calibration.} For estimates $x$ and $y$ from the same data,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:diffcov}
  \operatorname{Cov}(x-y) \;=\; C_{x} + C_{y} - C_{xy} - C_{yx},
\end{equation}
and the published IBU-era and OmniFold results share MINERvA data and flux, detector and GENIE systematics. Without $C_{xy}$, the summed $C_{x}+C_{y}$ double-counts shared systematics and omits their correlation. Its net error has unknown size and sign. No value of the statistic, including $1.5$ or $1$, can be converted to a goodness-of-fit probability or a $p$-value.

\emph{(ii) Near-one realizations are not evidence of correct uncertainties.} Under calibrated assumptions, $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ has expectation $1$ and spread of order $\sqrt{2/\mathrm{ndf}}$, about $0.10$ for $\mathrm{ndf}=205$. One realization near $1$, such as the quoted $\chiCombined$, is not evidence of correct uncertainties. Nor is the exact-split cross-check's $1.47$, computed with an earlier covariance built before the flux-band repair, evidence against them on its own (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, superseded values).

\emph{(iii) It does not address coverage} (\S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}, \S\ref{sec:coverage}). The covariance and the central value share the LightGBM estimator and, in the universe and statistical blocks, its training seed (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). Agreement claims use per-bin standardized differences and ratios (\S\ref{sec:pull}); this scalar is only a distance and ratio to the paper. The log-normal value $\chiCombinedLog$ depends on the definition given with the worked example.

\subsubsection{Shape-only $\chi^{2}$ with Jacobian propagation}
\label{sec:shape}

This tests whether the \emph{shape} of the $(\pT,\pPar)$ cross section matches the paper independently of absolute normalization, which depends on the flux integral, target nucleon count, and POT (each can drift by $\mathcal{O}(\%)$); the shape is a pure unfolding diagnostic. For bin widths $w_{j} = (\Delta\pT)_{j}\,(\Delta\pPar)_{j}$ and total $T(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{j\in\text{mask}} w_{j}x_{j}$, the unit-area shape is $s_{i}(\mathbf{x}) = x_{i}/T(\mathbf{x})$, satisfying $\sum_{i} w_{i} s_{i} = 1$, with
% (\texttt{normalize\_xsec\_shape.py}: \texttt{shape\_jacobian}, \texttt{shape\_chi2})
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial s_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{T}\big(\delta_{ij} - s_{i}\,w_{j}\big),
\qquad
\mathbf{J} \;=\; \frac{1}{T}\big(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{s}\,\mathbf{w}\T\big),
\qquad
\mathbf{C}^{\text{shape}} \;=\; \mathbf{J}\,\mathbf{C}^{\paper}\,\mathbf{J}\T.
\end{equation}
The unit-area constraint removes one degree of freedom: $\mathbf{w}\T\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{0}\T$. The ``all-bins-up'' direction is $\mathbf{J}$'s kernel, leaving $\mathbf{C}^{\text{shape}}$ one zero eigenvalue and rank $n-1$. Thus $\chi^{2}_{\text{shape}} = (\mathbf{s}^{\ours}-\mathbf{s}^{\paper})\T (\mathbf{C}^{\text{shape}})^{+} (\mathbf{s}^{\ours}-\mathbf{s}^{\paper})$ drops that direction, with $\mathrm{ndf} = n_{\text{bins}} - 1 = 204$ for $205$ bins. For the exact-split cross-check (not recomputed for the LightGBM central value), with the paper's \texttt{TotalCovariance} (Jacobian-propagated) the shape $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ on $205$ bins is $\boxed{3.596}$: essentially the absolute-normalization $3.661$ \emph{minus} the contribution of overall scale. The total-$\sigma$ ratio $\sigma_{\tot}^{\ours}/\sigma_{\tot}^{\paper} = 1.0111$ is a $1\%$ scale difference. The remaining residual comes from $\sim 1\%$ shape disagreement (median bin ratio $1.0064$; $94\%$ of bins within $10\%$ of the paper).

\subsection{Ill-conditioning and rank deficiency}
\label{sec:rank}

\paragraph{The obstruction.} An ours-only data-to-model $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ requires inverting $\mathbf{C}^{\ours} = \mathbf{C}^{\syst} + \mathbf{C}^{\boot} + \mathbf{C}^{\ML} + \mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{PL}}$ instead of the paper's $V$. Its universe block is rank-deficient,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}^{\syst}_{\text{sample}}) = 138 / 205,
\qquad
\mathrm{cond}(\mathbf{C}^{\syst}) = 9.78\times10^{11},
\end{equation}
counting eigenvalues of $(\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{C}\T)/2$ above $10^{-12}\lambda_{\max}$, with $\mathrm{cond}$ the ratio of $\lambda_{\max}$ to the smallest retained eigenvalue ($124$ and $9.45\times10^{9}$ above $10^{-10}\lambda_{\max}$). Every rank and condition number of our blocks quoted below uses this definition. Band count, not universe count, limits the rank: Eq.~\eqref{eq:matcov} gives each $\pm1\sigma$ pair rank $1$ and the $100$-universe PPFX band rank $\le 99$. PPFX is effectively rank-$1$ (\S\ref{sec:uq-universes}). The $43$ bands cannot span $205$ bins. \emph{This is not a missing-bands problem.} The paper's systematic-only covariance ($\texttt{TotalCov}-\texttt{StatOnlyCov}$) has rank $120$, below our $138$. We use the same \texttt{GetStandardSystematics} and propagate at least as many independent systematic directions. The statistical block ($\texttt{StatOnlyCov}$, rank $205$, near-diagonal) alone makes the paper's $V$ full-rank ($\mathrm{rank}=205$, with 204 eigenvalues above $10^{-12}\lambda_{\max}$; $\mathrm{cond}=1.5\times10^{12}$). \subsection{Anatomy of the paper-covariance tension}
\label{sec:tension}

This derivation supports \S\ref{sec:results-tension}. Its numbers are for the exact-split cross-check, which was the quoted 2D central value when it was made (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). For the quoted LightGBM central value, \S\ref{sec:results-tension} gives the same decomposition from the same code: a smaller distance ($\chiPaper$), an $8\times$ rather than $10\times$ off-diagonal inflation, \SI{9.6}{\percent} rather than \SI{3}{\percent} in the ten smallest-$\lambda$ modes, and its leading poorly determined modes in the highest-$\pPar$ bins, while its moderate-$\lambda$ modes remain at low-to-moderate $\pT$.

\paragraph{The paradox.} The central values agree well ($\sigma_{\tot}^{\ours}/\sigma_{\tot}^{\paper} = 1.011$, median bin ratio $1.006$), and the per-bin pull RMS against the paper covariance is only $0.598$ for the exact-split cross-check (\S\ref{sec:pull} gives $\pullRMS$ for the quoted value). A diagonal-only $\chi^{2}$, ignoring all off-diagonal correlations, is therefore
\begin{equation}
\chi^{2}_{\diag}/\mathrm{ndf}
= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\frac{\Delta_{i}^{2}}{C_{ii}}
= \mathrm{RMS}(\mathrm{pull})^{2} + \mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{pull})^{2}
\approx 0.366,
\end{equation}
versus full-covariance $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=3.661$. The $10\times$ inflation comes entirely from published \texttt{TotalCov}'s off-diagonal and small-eigenvalue structure.
% (\texttt{diagnose\_tension.py})

\paragraph{Eigenmode decomposition.} Diagonalizing the reported-block paper covariance, $\mathbf{C}=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\T$ ($\lambda_{1}\!\ge\!\cdots\!\ge\!\lambda_{n}$), and projecting $c_{k}=\mathbf{u}_{k}\T\boldsymbol{\Delta}$,
\begin{equation}
\chi^{2}=\boldsymbol{\Delta}\T\mathbf{C}^{+}\boldsymbol{\Delta}
=\sum_{k}\frac{c_{k}^{2}}{\lambda_{k}},
\qquad
\text{``eigen-pull''}_{k}\equiv\frac{c_{k}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}},
\end{equation}
reproduces \texttt{pinv} $\chi^{2}=750.49$ to $3\times10^{-9}$. The tension is neither a few pathological null directions nor the absolute smallest eigenvalues. The largest contributions lie at $\lambda_{k}/\lambda_{\max}\sim10^{-4}$--$10^{-5}$, in the lower-middle spectrum, $\sim7$--$8$ decades above the condition floor ($\lambda/\lambda_{\max}\sim10^{-12}$). Their standardized-component magnitudes $5$--$6$ are descriptive coordinates in the published basis, without calibrated significance for independent results. The ten \emph{smallest}-$\lambda$ modes carry only $3\%$ of the $\chi^{2}$ and about $90$ modes are needed to reach $90\%$: the $\chi^{2}$ is broadly distributed, not localized to the ill-conditioned tail. A \texttt{pinv}-rcond scan excludes a numerical artifact: the $73$ best-determined directions ($\lambda_{k}/\lambda_{\max}>10^{-4}$) give $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=1.42$, and $139$ directions ($>10^{-6}$) give $2.79$. The rank-truncation scan of \S\ref{sec:chisq} rises smoothly with no late jump, so the tension does not ride on the last few least-determined modes.

\paragraph{Shape contribution.} Splitting $\boldsymbol{\Delta}$ along $\mathbf{x}^{\paper}$ and its orthogonal remainder gives $\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{norm}}=0.10$ for scale, $\chi^{2}_{\text{shape}}=750.1$ for shape, and cross term $0.28$; the split is not $\mathbf{C}^{+}$-orthogonal. The $+1.1\%$ normalization offset is statistically irrelevant to the tension; it is a correlated shape difference, consistent with Jacobian shape-only $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=3.60$ (\S\ref{sec:shape}).

\paragraph{Where it lives.} Mapping dominant eigenvectors and per-bin contributions $\Delta_{i}(\mathbf{C}^{+}\boldsymbol{\Delta})_{i}$ to the $14\times16$ grid (numbers for the exact-split cross-check; Figs.~\ref{fig:tension} and~\ref{fig:tensionmodes} show the LightGBM central value) locates tension on the low-$\pT$ peak ridge ($\pT\lesssim0.4$ GeV/c, $\pT$-bins 2/7/10 carry $16/11/12\%$) and low-$\pPar$ near-edge cells ($\pPar$-bins 0/1/8). Carrier modes oscillate in sign across the peak. They form neither vertical $\pT$-columns characteristic of a flux $1/\Phi(\pT)$ normalization error nor the $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$ acceptance boundary. Flux and Muon\_Energy, the largest bands and only named cross-band correlation (Bashyal, \S\ref{sec:rank}), dominate there. Tight bin-to-bin correlations leave little freedom to absorb a coherent $\sim1$--$2\%$ shape difference, so a small OmniFold-vs-IBU shape difference becomes a large $\chi^{2}$.\footnote{The pipeline uses one global $\mathrm{POT}_{\mathrm{data}}/\mathrm{POT}_{\mathrm{MC}}$ ratio; per-playlist ratios span $0.1707$--$0.2371$. Sensitivity at $1$--$2\%$ motivated a direct mixture check: $R_{\mathrm{glob}}\sum_p \mathrm{MC}_p$ versus $\sum_p R_p\,\mathrm{MC}_p$ over twelve playlists changes reco shape by at most $0.07\%$ in $\pT$, $0.14\%$ in $\pPar$, and $0.03\%$ on the low-$\pT$ ridge. The residual $+0.12\%$ is normalization. Nearly shape-identical playlists make even large mixture reweighting almost pure normalization, leaving the statement above unaffected at the $14$--$30\times$ level.}

\subsection{Closure tests: detecting bias}
\label{sec:closure}

The variance methods of \S\ref{sec:variance} quantify the scatter of the unfolded result. Closure tests measure its bias, a consistent offset from a known injected truth, which the variance methods cannot show. Each of three tests injects a known MC deformation, unfolds, and compares the median per-bin $|\text{residual}|$ of the recovered answer with a threshold. The thresholds lie comfortably above the $0.166\%$ ML floor of the earlier ten-seed scan, which was in force when the tests were graded (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, superseded values: retired material). All use playlist-1A LightGBM 5-iteration unfolds. Thresholds and results are in \S\ref{sec:validation}; procedures and otherwise unreported values follow.
% PHASE2 Q6: the floor in force at grading; the quoted training-seed block is C08 (0.057 %).

\subsubsection{Truth-reweight closure}
\label{sec:closure-tr}
For known $f(\pT,\pPar)$, both reco and truth MC event weights are multiplied by $f$. Reweighted pseudo-data are unfolded with the unchanged CV response and compared with the reweighted truth marginal. Two shapes are tested: \texttt{gauss\_pt}, $f = 1 + A\exp\!\big[-((\pT - p_{T}^{0})/\sigma)^{2}\big]$ with $A=0.2$, $p_{T}^{0}=0.4$ GeV/c, $\sigma=0.1$ GeV/c; and \texttt{tilt\_pz}, $f = 1 + \alpha(\pPar - \pPar^{\text{ref}})$ with $\alpha=0.1$, $\pPar^{\text{ref}}=5$ GeV/c. Both residuals are at least $30\times$ below the threshold: OmniFold recovers the injected truth shape.

\subsubsection{Hidden-variable closure}
\label{sec:closure-hv}
OmniFold sees reco and truth $(\pT,\pPar)$ but excludes resolution variable $\dpT \equiv \pT^{\text{reco}}-\pT^{\text{truth}}$. A bias confined to this hidden axis tests leakage into the measured marginal. Pseudo-data receive
\begin{equation}
w^{\text{reco}}
\;\to\;
w^{\text{reco}}\cdot
\Big(1 + A\exp\!\big[-((\dpT - c)/\sigma_{\dpT})^{2}\big]\Big),
\end{equation}
with $c=+0.1$ GeV/c, $\sigma_{\dpT}=0.05$ GeV/c, and amplitude scanned over $A\in\{0.05,0.10,0.30\}$. Truth weights are \emph{unchanged}, so the reference is the unaltered CV truth marginal. For median per-bin signed residual $\bar r(A)$, a linear fit gives $\bar r(A) \approx \kappa_{\text{leak}} A$, with $\kappa_{\text{leak}} = \partial \bar r/\partial A$, the fraction of hidden $\dpT$ bias entering the visible answer. The $3.0\%$ acceptance bound is looser than the truth-reweight bound because the weak $\dpT$--$\pT$ correlation prevents complete removal of the leakage.

On the $205$ reported bins the per-amplitude median residuals, $0.57\% / 1.14\% / 3.43\%$ for $A = 0.05/0.10/0.30$, are linear in $A$, as expected for small perturbations, with fitted slope $\kappa_{\text{leak}} = \boxed{0.113}$: about $11\%$ of a hidden $\dpT$ bias leaks into the measured $(\pT,\pPar)$ marginal. Against the $3.0\%$ acceptance bound, two of the three arms pass and the largest does not: $A=0.30$ gives $3.43\%$, which \emph{exceeds} $3.0\%$ (\S\ref{sec:validation}). The scan establishes the linearity and the coefficient: the acceptance bound is satisfied for hidden-$\dpT$ deformations up to roughly $A\simeq0.26$ and is violated above it. No non-closure uncertainty is assigned from this scan either way (\S\ref{sec:validation}). A $\dpT$-shaped mismodeling at the $30\%$ level would leak more than the analysis declared tolerable; this is an open sensitivity, not a passing test.

\subsubsection{Alternative-model closure}
\label{sec:closure-alt}
The OmniFold response is trained on the CV weights and the pseudo-data are built from an alternative-universe reweighting: \texttt{MaCCQE:0} ($+1\sigma$), which probes an interaction-model shape change, or \texttt{Flux:50} (PPFX index $50$), which probes flux reweighting --- two distinct kinds of model dependence. The reference is the in-acceptance-extrapolated alternative truth,
\begin{equation}
\tilde X^{\text{ref}}_{i}
\;=\;
X^{\CV,\text{truth}}_{i}\cdot
\frac{X^{\text{alt},\text{in-acc}}_{i}}{X^{\CV,\text{in-acc}}_{i}},
\end{equation}
scaling the CV truth marginal by the alternative-to-CV in-acceptance count ratio to isolate model-shape response from acceptance/efficiency effects. In-acceptance biases are $-1.68\%$ for \texttt{MaCCQE:0}, removed by extrapolation, and $-7.32\%$ for \texttt{Flux:50}. Both residuals pass the $2.0\%$ bound with at least $5\times$ headroom, establishing robustness to model variations of order $\pm 1\sigma$ GENIE and full PPFX flux universes.
