\subsection{Numerical conventions: inverting a covariance (Cholesky, ridge jitter, pseudo-inverse)}
\label{sec:invert}

Equation~\eqref{eq:chi2} requires inverting $\mathbf{C}$.

\paragraph{Cholesky and ridge jitter.} $\mathbf{C}$ is positive-definite (PD) iff its Cholesky factorization $\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}\T$ exists with lower-triangular $\mathbf{L}$. Then Eq.~\eqref{eq:chi2} is well-posed. Numerical noise that leaves a PD matrix barely non-PD is repaired with an isotropic ridge $\varepsilon\,\mathbf{I}$, set to
% set in \texttt{uq/analyze\_uq.py}
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon \;=\; 10^{-12} \,\frac{\Tr\mathbf{C}}{n},
\end{equation}
twelve orders below the mean diagonal, far below any real signal in $\mathbf{C}$ and safely above floating-point round-off.

\paragraph{Rank deficiency at $N \le n$.} A sample covariance from $N\le n$ replicas in $n$ bins has rank at most $N-1$. The 300-replica bootstrap ($N=300 > 205$) has more replicas than bins, which permits full rank and does not establish it. The rank of the systematic block is limited at any universe count (\S\ref{sec:rank}).

\paragraph{Pseudo-inverse.} For the $\chi^{2}$ against the paper covariance, the comparison replaces $\mathbf{C}^{-1}$ with the Moore--Penrose pseudo-inverse computed via SVD, $\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{U}\Sigma\mathbf{V}\T$ with $\Sigma=\diag(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n})$:
% (\texttt{compare\_to\_paper\_fullcov.py})
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{+} \;=\; \mathbf{V}\,\Sigma^{+}\,\mathbf{U}\T,
\qquad
\Sigma^{+}_{ii} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
1/\sigma_{i} & \sigma_{i} > \tau,\\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The cutoff is $\tau = 10^{-15}\,\sigma_{\max}$, not the $n\,\sigma_{\max}\varepsilon_{\text{machine}}$ convention. Modes below the cutoff receive inverse weight $0$ rather than $\infty$, keeping the quadratic form stable for zero or near-zero eigenvalues.

\subsubsection{Training-seed variation}
\label{sec:seedscan}
The training-seed block is built from five settings of the GBDT seed triplet, each unfolded on the same $20$ bootstrap replicas (at the central value's seed, $42$, the first $20$ of the $300$ of \S\ref{sec:bootstrap}). With $\bar{\mathbf X}^{(s)}$ the mean of the $20$ replica $205$-vectors at setting $s$ and $\bar{\bar{\mathbf X}}$ the mean over the five settings,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{\ML}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{4}\sum_{s}\big(\bar{\mathbf X}^{(s)}-\bar{\bar{\mathbf X}}\big)
\big(\bar{\mathbf X}^{(s)}-\bar{\bar{\mathbf X}}\big)\T ,
\end{equation}
a sample covariance of rank $4$ that includes the bootstrap\,$\times$\,seed interaction at its $1/20$ share. The relative spread of its total cross section is $\boxed{0.0010\%}$, the per-bin median relative spread $\boxed{0.048\%}$, and $\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}^{\ML}} = 1.351\times10^{-41}$.

\subsubsection{Poisson bootstrap}
\label{sec:bootstrap}
A coherent bootstrap carries each data factor through step-1 classifier training and final cross-section construction. Each MC factor is shared by the reco-tree and truth-tree copies, preserving migration consistency. The three sub-RNGs (data, signal MC, background MC; Table~\ref{tab:variance-sources}) make the sources uncorrelated by construction, and pinning the GBDT seed isolates the bootstrap from the ML noise of \S\ref{sec:seedscan}. A Poisson(1) factor can be zero. Dropping those events from each replica mimics a resample with replacement. Poisson(1) is the large-$N$ limit of the multinomial counts (\S\ref{app:poisson}). Each replica's $14\times16$ histogram becomes a $205$-vector $\mathbf{X}^{(k)}$ of reported bins, row-major over $(\pT,\pPar)$, with unbiased sample covariance
\begin{equation}
\big(\mathbf{C}^{\boot}\big)_{ij}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}
\big(X_{i}^{(k)} - \bar X_{i}\big)\big(X_{j}^{(k)} - \bar X_{j}\big),
\qquad
\bar X_{i} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k} X_{i}^{(k)},
\label{eq:bootcov}
\end{equation}
checked with the Cholesky factorization and ridge jitter of \S\ref{sec:invert}. The quoted $N=300$ block varies only the bootstrap seed and carries no training-seed variation.
% (flags \texttt{-{}-seed 42} pinned, \texttt{-{}-bootstrap-seed} varied, \texttt{-{}-bootstrap-streams three};
% stageC/production/DESIGN.md §3.4, S1)

For these replicas (205 bins, ME~FHC LightGBM, seed~42) the relative standard deviation of the total cross section is $\boxed{0.061\%}$, the per-bin median relative spread $\boxed{0.676\%}$, and the per-bin $p_{84}$ $1.602\%$. With $300$ replicas the per-bin $\sigma$ has a relative standard error of about $4.1\%$ by the Gaussian formula $1/\sqrt{2(N-1)}$. $N=300$ is chosen for full rank.

\subsubsection{Universes}
\label{sec:universes}
The universe sweep used by the quoted budget has 187 universes across 44 bands, dominated by the $100$-universe PPFX flux band (hadron-production sampling). The quoted band sum drops one band (Rvp1pi) and holds $43$ bands plus the normalization. Almost all other bands are $\pm1\sigma$ pairs ($N_{u}=2$). They include the MINOS and lateral MINERvA muon energy scales, the MINOS muon-match efficiency, the GENIE parameters \texttt{MaRES}, \texttt{MvRES} and \texttt{MaCCQE}, and the lateral GEANT FSI responses for neutrons, pions and protons. Where selection migration matters, the five kinematic bands (the two beam-angle components, the muon resolution and the two muon energy scales) require re-selected event loops, run with one universe active at a time (weight-only bands: \S\ref{sec:uq-higherdim}). The quoted universe block takes these five bands from re-unfolds of re-selected universes.
% stageC/production/DESIGN.md §S5 universe row (S3 laterals; Rvp1pi dropped); J3/J4 provisional (DECISION-20261018, packet W3: D10).

MINERvA's MAT framework (\texttt{PlotUtils/\allowbreak MnvVertErrorBand::\allowbreak CalcCovMx}, \S\ref{sec:minerva}) uses the mean-centered, biased sample covariance of the $205$-vectors $\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)}$, referenced to the universe mean with normalization $1/N_{u}$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\mathbf{C}^{B}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{N_{u}}\sum_{u=1}^{N_{u}}
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)} - \langle\mathbf{X}\rangle_{B}\big)
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)} - \langle\mathbf{X}\rangle_{B}\big)\T,
\qquad
\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle_{B} = \frac{1}{N_{u}}\sum_{u}\mathbf{X}^{(B,u)}.
\;}
\label{eq:matcov}
\end{equation}
uniformly for all $N_{u}\ge 2$, with no special $\pm1\sigma$ pair formula. At $N_{u}=2$ it reduces to rank-1 $\tfrac14(\mathbf{X}_{+}-\mathbf{X}_{-})(\mathbf{X}_{+}-\mathbf{X}_{-})\T$. Mean-centering removes a common offset from the central value. The half-difference remains and need not be small. For a one-sided or asymmetric pair, CV-centering adds that offset as a second direction (\S\ref{app:pair}). The $1.4\%$ target-nucleon normalization uncertainty (Aliaga NIM, arXiv:1305.5199) lies outside \texttt{MnvVertErrorBand} and is added as a fully-correlated rank-1 outer product. Bands are assumed independent, with different physics sources and RNGs,
\begin{align}
\mathbf{C}^{\text{norm}} \;&=\; (\sigma_{N}\,\mathbf{X}^{\CV})(\sigma_{N}\,\mathbf{X}^{\CV})\T,
\qquad \sigma_{N} = 0.014,
\label{eq:normband}
\\
\mathbf{C}^{\syst}
\;&=\;
\sum_{B}\mathbf{C}^{B}\;+\;\mathbf{C}^{\text{norm}}.
\label{eq:systblocksum}
\end{align}
Independence is a modeling assumption with one known correlated exception, the Bashyal flux\,$\leftrightarrow$\,Muon\_Energy\_MINOS joint block (\S\ref{sec:rank}).

Equation~\eqref{eq:systblocksum} omits a larger correction. The band sum is linear, but OmniFold responds nonlinearly to input perturbations, so joint re-unfolding produces a cross term beyond any pairwise correlation. A unified throw measures it through $N$ re-unfolds, simultaneously perturbing every reweightable band and the flux multisim per event ($w_{\CV}\prod_B\rho_B^{g_B}$, $g_B\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$), then re-extracting the cross section. The corrected construction follows. Its use in the five-axis covariance of the configuration-(a) central value is in \S\ref{sec:eavailw-adopted} (Eq.~\eqref{eq:zinflation}).

The procedure interpolates asymmetrically between actual $-1\sigma$ and $+1\sigma$ event-weight endpoints, fixes the estimator seed for every throw, and centers the joint covariance on the throw mean. The matched block-sum comparator re-unfolds both endpoints of each band using MAT's universe-mean centering and biased $1/N$ normalization. A single-throw superposition probe is insufficient to test nonlinear response. No scalar run-to-run jitter floor is subtracted. \subsubsection{The $\pm 1\sigma$ pair covariance: mean-centered (MAT-conformant)}
\label{app:pair}

For two universes $u=0,1$ at $\pm 1\sigma$, with vectors $\mathbf{X}_{0}$, $\mathbf{X}_{1}$ and mean $\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle = \tfrac12(\mathbf{X}_{0}+\mathbf{X}_{1})$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:matcov} at $N_{u}=2$ gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{B}_{\text{mean}}
\;=\;
\tfrac12\sum_{u=0,1}(\mathbf{X}_{u}-\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle)(\mathbf{X}_{u}-\langle\mathbf{X}\rangle)\T
\;=\;
\tfrac14(\mathbf{X}_{0}-\mathbf{X}_{1})(\mathbf{X}_{0}-\mathbf{X}_{1})\T,
\end{equation}
a rank-1 outer product of the half-difference. A common offset from $X_{\CV}$ contributes nothing. Naive CV-centering instead uses $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{u} = \mathbf{X}_{u}-\mathbf{X}_{\CV}$, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{0}=\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{a}$, $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}=\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{a}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{B}_{\text{CV-centered}}
\;=\;
\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{0}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{0}\T
                + \boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}\T\big)
\;=\;
\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}\T + \mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}\T.
\end{equation}
The estimators agree only for CV-symmetric pairs ($\mathbf{m}=0$), both reducing to $\mathbf{a}\mathbf{a}\T$. For asymmetric or one-sided pairs, CV-centering adds the rank-1 $\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}\T$ common-offset direction. Every per-band block $C_{b}$ here is mean-centered to match MAT (\S\ref{sec:minerva}); this matters for BeamAngle, MuonResolution and the one-sided NC band.

\paragraph{Scope of mean-centering.} Mean-centering applies to the $\pm1\sigma$ band estimator only. The five-axis covariance of the configuration-(a) central value deliberately retains the CV-centered term: the unified-throw covariance diagonal that enters its inflation is centered on the central value (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-construction}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:zinflation}).

\subsubsection{How MINERvA builds its published covariance}
\label{sec:minerva}
MAT's C++ framework defines the covariance construction that the published paper leaves unspecified. It is the mean-centered $1/N$ per-band sample covariance and element-wise band sum used here.


\paragraph{MAT per band and in total.} \texttt{MnvVertErrorBand::CalcCovMx} implements Eq.~\eqref{eq:matcov}, referenced to the universe mean $\langle x\rangle_{u}$ rather than $X_{\CV}$, normalized by biased $1/N$ rather than Bessel's $1/(N-1)$ (a $1\%$ offset for PPFX, $N=100$), with no special case for $N=2$. The conventional CV-centered pair formula (``MINERvA-101 $\pm1\sigma$ pair''), $\tfrac12(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{+}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{+}\T + \boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-}\T)$, agrees with it only for CV-symmetric shifts. The quoted universe block, filled into a \texttt{PlotUtils::MnvH1D} with MAT's universe weights on Flux, agrees with \texttt{MnvH1D::GetTotalErrorMatrix} to $1.3\times10^{-16}$ of its largest element. The quoted Flux band carries MAT's $\nu$--$e$ and inverse-muon-decay constraint weights.

\texttt{MnvH1D::GetTotalErrorMatrix} sums bands and the statistical matrix element-wise, assuming band independence. Cross-band correlations exist only if encoded in a combined band, such as the Bashyal joint fit handled outside this loop. MAT applies no ridge, shrinkage, SVD truncation or rank-deficiency handling to the published total covariance. The statistical block makes $V$ full-rank. Its systematic-only rank is $120$, below the $138$ of our universe block (eigenvalues above $10^{-12}\lambda_{\max}$; \S\ref{sec:rank}).


\subsubsection{Block-sum combination}
\label{sec:combine}
The bootstrap varies events through distinct data, signal-MC and background-MC sub-RNGs; universes vary model weights at a pinned GBDT seed; the training-seed block varies the GBDT seed over five settings, each on the same $20$ bootstrap replicas (at seed~42, replicas of the statistical block); the run-period block compares separate unfolds of three run periods. The bootstrap and the universes use the central value's LightGBM seed ($42$), and the training-seed settings include it. The block sum is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}^{\tot}
\;=\;
\mathbf{C}^{\ML} \,+\, \mathbf{C}^{\stat} \,+\, \mathbf{C}^{\syst} \,+\, \mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{PL}}.
\label{eq:blocksum}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Covariance-aware $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$}
\label{sec:chisq}

With $\boldsymbol{\Delta}\equiv\mathbf{x}^{\ours}-\mathbf{x}^{\paper}$ on the $205$-bin set and $\mathbf{C}$ the chosen covariance,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chi2}
\chi^{2}
\;=\;
\boldsymbol{\Delta}\T\,\mathbf{C}^{+}\,\boldsymbol{\Delta},
\qquad
\mathrm{ndf} \;=\; n_{\text{reported}} = 205,
\qquad
\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf} \;=\; \chi^{2}/205.
\end{equation}
The comparison applies the pseudo-inverse of \S\ref{sec:invert} to the $205\times205$ block and computes the quadratic form. Options add OmniFold covariances via Eq.~\eqref{eq:blocksum} or subtract the paper's stat block to avoid counting it alongside the bootstrap (\S\ref{sec:rank}).

\paragraph{Dimension-conditional ndf.} Setting $\mathrm{ndf}=n_{\text{reported}}$ requires nearly that many well-determined directions. The choice rests on the $205$-bin 2D truncation scan rather than being assumed: the scan rises smoothly with the retained rank $r$, $0.39\,(r{=}50)\to1.04\,(100)\to1.76\,(180)\to2.27\,(205)$ (exact-split cross-check: $0.69\to2.35\to3.30\to3.66$), with no plateau or late jump, indicating an effective rank comparable to the dimension. \emph{(i) Missing cross-covariance prevents calibration.} For estimates $x$ and $y$ from the same data,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:diffcov}
  \operatorname{Cov}(x-y) \;=\; C_{x} + C_{y} - C_{xy} - C_{yx},
\end{equation}
and the published IBU-era and OmniFold results share MINERvA data and flux, detector and GENIE systematics. Without $C_{xy}$, the summed $C_{x}+C_{y}$ double-counts shared systematics and omits their correlation. Its net error has unknown size and sign. No value of the statistic, including $1.5$ or $1$, can be converted to a goodness-of-fit probability or a $p$-value.

\emph{(ii) Near-one realizations are not evidence of correct uncertainties.} Under calibrated assumptions, $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ has expectation $1$ and spread of order $\sqrt{2/\mathrm{ndf}}$, about $0.10$ for $\mathrm{ndf}=205$. One realization near $1$, such as the quoted $\chiCombined$, is not evidence of correct uncertainties.

The covariance and the central value share the LightGBM estimator and, in the universe and statistical blocks, its training seed (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). \subsubsection{Shape-only $\chi^{2}$ with Jacobian propagation}
\label{sec:shape}

This tests whether the \emph{shape} of the $(\pT,\pPar)$ cross section matches the paper independently of absolute normalization, which depends on the flux integral, target nucleon count, and POT (each can drift by $\mathcal{O}(\%)$); the shape is a pure unfolding diagnostic. For bin widths $w_{j} = (\Delta\pT)_{j}\,(\Delta\pPar)_{j}$ and total $T(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{j\in\text{mask}} w_{j}x_{j}$, the unit-area shape is $s_{i}(\mathbf{x}) = x_{i}/T(\mathbf{x})$, satisfying $\sum_{i} w_{i} s_{i} = 1$, with
% (\texttt{normalize\_xsec\_shape.py}: \texttt{shape\_jacobian}, \texttt{shape\_chi2})
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial s_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{T}\big(\delta_{ij} - s_{i}\,w_{j}\big),
\qquad
\mathbf{J} \;=\; \frac{1}{T}\big(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{s}\,\mathbf{w}\T\big),
\qquad
\mathbf{C}^{\text{shape}} \;=\; \mathbf{J}\,\mathbf{C}^{\paper}\,\mathbf{J}\T.
\end{equation}
The unit-area constraint removes one degree of freedom. $\mathbf{w}\T\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{0}\T$ is a left-null relation. The right kernel of $\mathbf{J}$ is the direction $\mathbf{s}$, proportional to $\mathbf{x}$, leaving $\mathbf{C}^{\text{shape}}$ one zero eigenvalue and rank $n-1$. Thus $\chi^{2}_{\text{shape}} = (\mathbf{s}^{\ours}-\mathbf{s}^{\paper})\T (\mathbf{C}^{\text{shape}})^{+} (\mathbf{s}^{\ours}-\mathbf{s}^{\paper})$ drops that direction, with $\mathrm{ndf} = n_{\text{bins}} - 1 = 204$ for $205$ bins. For the exact-split cross-check, which as above is not recomputed for the LightGBM central value, with the paper's \texttt{TotalCovariance} (Jacobian-propagated) the shape $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ on $205$ bins is $\boxed{3.596}$: essentially the absolute-normalization $3.661$ \emph{minus} the contribution of overall scale. The total-cross-section ratio $\sigma_{\tot}^{\ours}/\sigma_{\tot}^{\paper} = 1.0111$ is a $1\%$ scale difference. The remaining residual comes from $\sim 1\%$ shape disagreement (median bin ratio $1.0064$; $94\%$ of bins within $10\%$ of the paper).

\subsection{Closure tests: detecting bias}
\label{sec:closure}

The variance methods of \S\ref{sec:variance} quantify the scatter of the unfolded result. Closure tests measure its bias, a consistent offset from a known injected truth, which the variance methods cannot show. Each of three tests injects a known MC deformation, unfolds, and compares the median per-bin $|\text{residual}|$ of the recovered answer with a threshold. The thresholds lie comfortably above the $0.166\%$ ML floor of the earlier ten-seed scan, which was in force when the tests were graded (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, superseded values: retired material). All use playlist-1A LightGBM 5-iteration unfolds. Thresholds and results are in \S\ref{sec:closure-map}; procedures and otherwise unreported values follow.
% PHASE2 Q6: the floor in force at grading; the quoted training-seed block is C08 (0.057 %).

\subsubsection{Truth-reweight closure}
\label{sec:closure-tr}
For known $f(\pT,\pPar)$, both reco and truth MC event weights are multiplied by $f$. Reweighted pseudo-data are unfolded with the unchanged CV response and compared with the reweighted truth marginal. Two shapes are tested: \texttt{gauss\_pt}, $f = 1 + A\exp\!\big[-((\pT - p_{T}^{0})/\sigma)^{2}\big]$ with $A=0.2$, $p_{T}^{0}=0.4$ GeV/c, $\sigma=0.1$ GeV/c; and \texttt{tilt\_pz}, $f = 1 + \alpha(\pPar - \pPar^{\text{ref}})$ with $\alpha=0.1$, $\pPar^{\text{ref}}=5$ GeV/c.

\subsubsection{Hidden-variable closure}
\label{sec:closure-hv}
OmniFold sees reco and truth $(\pT,\pPar)$ but excludes resolution variable $\dpT \equiv \pT^{\text{reco}}-\pT^{\text{truth}}$. A bias confined to this hidden axis tests leakage into the measured marginal. Pseudo-data receive
\begin{equation}
w^{\text{reco}}
\;\to\;
w^{\text{reco}}\cdot
\Big(1 + A\exp\!\big[-((\dpT - c)/\sigma_{\dpT})^{2}\big]\Big),
\end{equation}
with $c=+0.1$ GeV/c, $\sigma_{\dpT}=0.05$ GeV/c, and amplitude scanned over $A\in\{0.05,0.10,0.30\}$. Truth weights are \emph{unchanged}, so the reference is the unaltered CV truth marginal. For median per-bin signed residual $\bar r(A)$, a linear fit gives $\bar r(A) \approx \kappa_{\text{leak}} A$, with $\kappa_{\text{leak}} = \partial \bar r/\partial A$, the fraction of hidden $\dpT$ bias entering the visible answer. The $3.0\%$ acceptance bound is looser than the truth-reweight bound because the weak $\dpT$--$\pT$ correlation prevents complete removal of the leakage.

\subsubsection{Alternative-model closure}
\label{sec:closure-alt}
The OmniFold response is trained on the CV weights and the pseudo-data are built from an alternative-universe reweighting: \texttt{MaCCQE:0} ($+1\sigma$), which probes an interaction-model shape change, or \texttt{Flux:50} (PPFX index $50$), which probes flux reweighting --- two distinct kinds of model dependence. The reference is the in-acceptance-extrapolated alternative truth,
\begin{equation}
\tilde X^{\text{ref}}_{i}
\;=\;
X^{\CV,\text{truth}}_{i}\cdot
\frac{X^{\text{alt},\text{in-acc}}_{i}}{X^{\CV,\text{in-acc}}_{i}},
\end{equation}
scaling the CV truth marginal by the alternative-to-CV in-acceptance count ratio to isolate model-shape response from acceptance/efficiency effects.

\paragraph{Same-ensemble pull diagnostic (not a coverage test).} An earlier ensemble of $N_{\text{toy}}=200$ 2D closure unfolds (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, \recentry{coverage-toys}) uses weighted MC reco pseudo-data and stores the truth marginals in truth histograms. Each toy has its own bootstrap seed $k$ and per-event Poisson(1) draws, as in production, with seeds chosen not to collide with the production replicas. Toy seeds are independent. Bins within a toy are correlated. The MC bootstrap multiplies the truth weight. It fluctuates both the unfolded result and the stored truth. The stored truth varies in all 205 reported bins, and its all-toy mean $\bar X^{\text{truth}}$ is therefore not an independent fixed truth for a split-sample coverage claim. It only centers a same-ensemble diagnostic.
