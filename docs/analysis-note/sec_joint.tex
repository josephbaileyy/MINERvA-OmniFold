\subsection{Calibrated joint tests of generator predictions}
\label{sec:joint5d}

% Campaign: s5p (terminal 2026-10-06).
This section tests five generator predictions jointly in the five observables $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$, each in total and in shape. Each test evaluates a hybrid null hypothesis about one prediction (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-design}) against a null distribution obtained by simulating the full analysis chain. The tests are hypothesis tests, not a measured cross section, and they add no reportable uncertainty. They neither use nor validate the five-axis covariance of the five-dimensional central value unfolded with purity-based background subtraction (the \estPurity{}, \S\ref{sec:eavailw-adopted}, Table~\ref{tab:config-key}). Reference~\cite{JointTestRecord2026} gives the specification and the decisions, and each later study's own reference is cited where the study is described.

Two properties must be distinguished.  The coverage of unfolded intervals is the rate at which they contain the true cross section.  The calibration of a test is the rate at which it rejects a true hypothesis.  Calibration can be obtained without validated intervals by simulating the whole analysis chain at the hypothesis, so that the estimator's bias enters the simulated null distribution.  A calibration obtained in this way is valid to the extent that the simulated chain reproduces the analysis of the data. The comparison with a test at reconstruction level is in the records document.

\subsubsection{Hypotheses, grid and statistics}
\label{sec:joint5d-design}

For each of five predictions $G$ (the MINERvA Tune~v1, GENIE~2.12.10 without and with MEC, NuWro~21.09 and GiBUU~2019; \S\ref{sec:eavailw-band}), the null $H_{0}(G)$ is that the true cross section equals $G$ on every cell of a fine five-dimensional grid. Below that grid, the null truth has the MINERvA Tune~v1 simulation's event composition, varied in each pseudo-experiment by the drawn interaction-model universes. Every fine-cell integral is held fixed in that variation. The background is the Tune~v1 simulation's, with its uncertainty bands. This \emph{hybrid null} is the hypothesis the calibration simulates. Whether it represents the physical generator prediction depends on condition~(i) (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}). Whether the calibration reproduces the analysis of the data is calibration fidelity (conditions~(ii)--(iv)). Each prediction is tested in total (rate and shape together) and in shape alone: \jtNtests{} tests. The fine grid is the analysis binning of \S\ref{sec:method-binning}, $14\times16\times7\times7\times6$ bins in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$; the test cells below are its integrals.

\subsubsection{Calibration within the implemented simulation model}
\label{sec:joint5d-calibration}

The null distribution of each statistic is obtained from pseudo-experiments that run the full analysis chain at the null truth, using the joint-test estimator (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}). That estimator is deterministic LightGBM OmniFold with five iterations and the negative-weight background treatment, whose refinement classifier has 400 trees and 31 leaves.
The data are unfolded once, with the full simulation. The Monte Carlo $p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$ of $B$ null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic. This $p$ value is never zero. The $p$ value on which each decision rests, the \emph{claim} $p$ value, is the largest over the specified \emph{claim variants}, which shift the null. The variants move it by an upper bound on the simulation-size difference between the pseudo-experiments and the data unfold (condition~(iv), \S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}), and by $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ (condition~(i)). The list of what each pseudo-experiment draws is in the records document.

\subsubsection{Multiplicity, stopping rule and design}
\label{sec:joint5d-frozen}

The ten tests are carried out with the Holm procedure~\cite{Holm:1979} at familywise level $\alpha=\jtAlpha$. A determinacy requirement also applies: a step rejects only when the 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval~\cite{Clopper:1934} of $k/B$ lies entirely below its Holm threshold. Calibration proceeded in batches of \jtBatch{} pseudo-experiments per null, to at most \jtBcap{}, under a sequential rule that stopped a null once both of its decisions were determined.

Two assumptions are made: (A1) each true null's calibrated tail probability is uniformly distributed or conservative, and (A2) the pseudo-experiments' exceedances are independent given the data. Under A1 and A2, the probability that this upper end falls below the threshold, at any number of pseudo-experiments up to \jtBcap{}, is below the threshold. This holds whatever rule determines when sampling stops, including the budget extensions. The decision rule therefore controls the familywise error rate of the ten decisions at 5\% under these assumptions. The error control applies only to the decisions. The point estimates $(k+1)/(B+1)$ carry no separate error-control guarantee. Under these assumptions, the bound $\mathrm{FWER}\le0.8902\,\alpha<0.9\,\alpha$ is derived in \S\ref{sec:joint5d-fwer}.

A2 requires the lost pseudo-experiments to have been lost independently of their outcomes, which has not been shown. When rerun, however, none of them reaches the observed statistic (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-lost}). The calibrated size is averaged over the nuisance priors: at a fixed true value of the nuisance parameters it can exceed $\alpha$. The stopping rule's precision requirement and the definition of the familywise error rate are in the records document.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the sub-fine-grid residual.}
\label{sec:joint5d-kappa}

The multiplier $\kappa$ scales the last measured refinement change $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$: $\kappa=1$ means one such change, not one standard deviation or a confidence level. A scan of $\kappa$ along the direction $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ of the claim variants~\cite{JointTestKappa2026} (for reporting only) found, in the calibration ensembles used for the decisions, all ten decisions to remain determinate rejections at every sampled multiplier up to 5.25. The scan probes one prescribed direction. It establishes neither convergence nor a bound on the actual below-grid residual (condition~(i), \S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}). Where determinate rejection is first lost, and by which tests, is given in the records document.

\paragraph{Physical-model adequacy.} Condition~(ii) concerns the detector model. It is the simulation's per event, varied only by the drawn detector bands (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-calibration}, each at $\pm1\sigma$) and by the linear surrogate for the five lateral bands. \subsubsection{Matched coarse projections}
\label{sec:joint5d-projections}

The same calibrated tests are restricted to matched $3\times3$ coarse projections of the same cells, onto $(\pT,\pPar)$ and onto $(\Eavail,W)$. The projected tests do not enter the Holm family and change no decision of Table~\ref{tab:joint5d}. The comparison and its criterion were specified after the five-dimensional outcomes were known but before the comparison was run.

Under the criterion, a test would count as evidence of additional joint discrimination only if both of its projected claim $p$ values were at least 0.05 in both the fixed-shift and the recomputed-shift evaluations. No test meets it, and the values are identical in the two evaluations. This study therefore provides no evidence of additional discrimination from the joint five-observable information. In the muon-only $(\pT,\pPar)$ projection, GENIE~2.12.10 is not rejected ($p=\woneGcvPtTot$ in total, $\woneGcvPtShape$ in shape; a post hoc observation), whereas its $(\Eavail,W)$ projection is. Hadronic information therefore participates in that rejection. This does not establish added power of the joint test over all matched projections, or accept GENIE as true. The powers of the joint and projected tests have not been compared. Every projected claim $p$ value is listed in the records document.

\subsubsection{Hadronic-response condition and recoil-energy sensitivity}
\label{sec:joint5d-response}

MINERvA's recoil-energy analyses assign a hadronic-energy response uncertainty derived from test-beam calorimetry~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam}, separate from the GEANT hadron-interaction reweights~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq,MINERvA:2022incl}. The specified nuisance model here includes the reweights but no calorimetric response band. A response mismodeling could move each observed statistic in either direction, and a band drawn in the calibration would also broaden and could shift each null distribution. The net effect on each $p$ value is unmeasured. The GENIE~2.12.10 projection result of \S\ref{sec:joint5d-projections} shows that the information rejecting that prediction involves the hadronic axes, on which a response band would act. The size of that uncertainty in the low-recoil measurement, and how a response band would act on all five nulls, are in the records document.
% Search: net effect on each; calorimetric response band; response band drawn @ 1a015eef

In an exploratory sensitivity study of the response~\cite{JointTestRecoil2026}, the data are re-unfolded with the simulation's reconstructed recoil energy scaled by $\pm\SI{4}{\percent}$, and the ten tests are re-evaluated against the null distributions used for the decisions. The $\pm4\%$ scale is not a validated uncertainty prescription. At both $-4\%$ and $+4\%$ every one of the ten rejections holds. The null distributions were not regenerated with the variation, although they are simulated through the same detector model, so the size of this omission is unmeasured for these strongly non-central nulls. The rejections are therefore conditional on the simulation's hadronic response, and the response model is not shown to be complete. The observed statistics at each scale, and the study's controls, are given in the records document.

\subsubsection{What the tests establish}
\label{sec:joint5d-reading}

The comparisons recover the generator disagreements already established by the published two-dimensional measurement on the same data~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. What they add is calibration within the implemented simulation model, which puts the estimator's bias toward its prior and the specified nuisances into each null distribution. The rejections are global assessments of the five specified predictions under the specified model. Assigning a discrepancy to a kinematic region or nuclear mechanism would require separate tests. The rejections apply to the generator versions listed in \S\ref{sec:eavailw-band}, and they retain all six conditions of \S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}. Extending the inference would require a response band drawn through the calibration and a demonstrated bound on the residual below the fine grid.
