\section{Calibrated joint tests of generator predictions}
\label{sec:joint5d}

% Campaign: s5p (terminal 2026-10-06).
This section describes the joint five-dimensional tests of generator predictions. The tests evaluate hybrid null hypotheses about five predictions (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-design}). They are not a measured cross section, and they add no reportable uncertainty. They do not use, and do not validate, the five-axis covariance of the configuration-(a) central value (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-adopted}). The section gives the design, the six conditions of the declared model in the words of the test specification (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}), the decisions (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-results}; lost pseudo-experiments: \S\ref{sec:joint5d-lost}) and the later sensitivity studies, with their numbers. Reference~\cite{JointTestRecord2026} gives the specification and the decisions, and each later study's own reference is cited where the study is described.

\subsection{Hypotheses, grid and statistics}
\label{sec:joint5d-design}

For each of five predictions $G$ (the MINERvA Tune~v1, GENIE~2.12.10 without and with MEC, NuWro~21.09 and GiBUU~2019; \S\ref{sec:eavailw-band}), the null $H_{0}(G)$ is that the true cross section equals $G$ on every cell of a fine five-dimensional grid. Below that grid, the null truth has the MINERvA Tune~v1 simulation's event composition, varied in each pseudo-experiment by the drawn interaction-model universes. Every fine-cell integral is held fixed in that variation. The background is the Tune~v1 simulation's, with its uncertainty bands. This \emph{hybrid null} is the hypothesis the calibration simulates. For the Tune~v1, which is the analysis simulation itself, no external prediction is combined with the Tune~v1 composition. Three requirements are distinct. The hybrid null is defined exactly on the finite grid, and no convergence is needed to define it. Whether it represents the physical generator prediction depends on condition~(i) (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}). Whether the calibration reproduces the analysis of the data is calibration fidelity (conditions~(ii)--(iv)). Each prediction is tested in total (rate and shape together) and in shape alone: \jtNtests{} tests. The fine grid is the analysis binning of \S\ref{sec:method-binning}, $14\times16\times7\times7\times6$ bins in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$; the test cells below are its integrals.

The tests are evaluated on a coarse $3^{5}$ grid in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$ with edges $\pT\in[0,0.55,0.85,4.5]\,\si{GeV/c}$, $\pPar\in[1.5,3.5,6,60]\,\si{GeV/c}$, $\Eavail\in[0,0.4,1.5,100]\,\si{GeV}$, $q_{3}\in[0,1.2,2,100]\,\si{GeV}$ and $W\in[0,1.4,2.2,100]\,\si{GeV}$. Each inner edge is the fine-grid edge nearest a tertile of the weighted simulated truth distribution of the signal sample in that coordinate, so the cells keep physical bin widths. That distribution is taken from the development simulation. The tests are restricted to the \jtNcells{} supported cells (J). For GiBUU, whose sample lacks high-energy neutrinos, the domain is the \jtNcellsGiBUU{} cells with $\pPar<\SI{6}{GeV/c}$. The grid specifies a finite hybrid hypothesis and a reporting statistic. It does not turn OmniFold's event-level training into binned unfolding.

Two statistics are formed from the unfolded cell values $F$ and the prediction $\mu_{G}$. The total (rate-and-shape) statistic is $T_{\mathrm{tot}}=r^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}r$ with $r=F-\mu_{G}$. The shape statistic $T_{\mathrm{sh}}$ has the same form on the normalized cell fractions, with the normalization Jacobian applied to $\Sigma$ and one cell dropped. The metric $\Sigma=V+\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ adds the prediction's own finite-sample variance to $V$. Here $V$ is a Ledoit--Wolf-shrunk covariance~\cite{LedoitWolf:2004} of \jtVn{} pilot pseudo-experiments at the Tune~v1 null. The metric defines how hypotheses are compared, and so it affects power. The null calibration below, not the metric, sets each test's size.

\subsection{Calibration within the implemented simulation model}
\label{sec:joint5d-calibration}

The null distribution of each statistic is obtained from pseudo-experiments that run the full analysis chain at the null truth, using the joint-test estimator (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}). That estimator is deterministic LightGBM OmniFold with five iterations and the negative-weight background treatment, whose refinement classifier has 400 trees and 31 leaves. Each pseudo-experiment
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item draws Poisson pseudo-data from one half of the simulation together with an independent background source, refits the background-refinement classifier, and unfolds with the estimator trained on the other half;
  \item draws one of \jtFluxUniverses{} flux universes and every one of the \jtModelBands{} interaction-model bands at one of its universes;
  \item draws the weight-based detector bands (MINOS muon-matching efficiency and the GEANT4 neutron, pion and proton interaction reweights) and the five muon and beam-angle lateral bands through a linear surrogate;
  \item draws a \SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization, the data's rounding noise (taken from 20 real-data re-unfolds with rounding-level input changes), and the prediction's finite-sample residual.
\end{itemize}
The data are unfolded once, with the full simulation. The Monte Carlo $p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$ of $B$ null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic. This $p$ value is never zero. The \emph{claim} $p$ value is the largest over declared variants that move the null. The variants move it by an upper bound on the simulation-size difference between the pseudo-experiments and the data unfold (condition~(iv) below), and by $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ (condition~(i)). The external nulls are strongly non-central, with non-centralities $\lambda$ of \jtLambdaMin{}--\jtLambdaMax{} in these units. They are non-central because the calibration reproduces the estimator's pull toward its prior (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}). Each observed statistic is compared with that displaced distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:joint5d-nulls}).

\paragraph{Generative model of one pseudo-experiment.} Each calibration pseudo-experiment splits the simulation at random into two halves (Table~\ref{tab:joint5d-genmodel}) and refits the background refinement. Its truth equals $G$ on every fine cell that the source half populates. Cells it leaves empty lower a coarse cell by at most \SI{0.2}{\percent}, or about \SI{4}{\percent} of the null standard deviation (the per-experiment spread is below \SI{1.5}{\percent} of it). On the signal, each interaction-model band is renormalized within each fine truth cell, so it moves only the composition within the cell, and with it the efficiency and the migrations. The flux universe and the band universes are drawn independently, and their weight ratios multiply. The muon-energy, muon-resolution, and beam-angle lateral bands enter as $\sum_{b}z_{b}\Delta_{b}$, with $\Delta_{b}$ from endpoint unfoldings of the data. The detector weight bands include the GEANT4 hadron-interaction reweights. Because the $\Delta_{b}$ come from the observed data, the null distributions are conditional on these data-derived inputs, which would differ in a repeated experiment. That conditioning has not been validated.

\subsection{Multiplicity, stopping rule and design}
\label{sec:joint5d-frozen}

The ten tests are carried out with the Holm procedure~\cite{Holm:1979} at familywise level $\alpha=\jtAlpha$. A determinacy requirement also applies: a step rejects only when the 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval~\cite{Clopper:1934} of $k/B$ lies entirely below its Holm threshold. Calibration proceeded in batches of \jtBatch{} pseudo-experiments per null, to at most \jtBcap{}, under a sequential rule that stopped a null once both of its decisions were determined. A null stopped when a 99.5\% Clopper--Pearson interval of $k/B$ lay entirely on one side of each of its thresholds and met a fixed precision requirement. The decision rule uses the upper end of the two-sided 95\% interval, a one-sided 97.5\% upper bound. Two assumptions are made: (A1) each true null's calibrated tail probability is uniformly distributed or conservative, and (A2) the pseudo-experiments' exceedances are independent given the data. Under A1 and A2, the probability that this upper end falls below the threshold, at any number of pseudo-experiments up to \jtBcap{}, is below the threshold. This holds whatever rule determines when sampling stops, including the budget extensions. The decision rule therefore controls the familywise error rate of the ten decisions at 5\% under these assumptions. That rate is the probability of falsely rejecting at least one true hypothesis among them. Under these assumptions, the bound $\mathrm{FWER}\le0.8902\,\alpha<0.9\,\alpha$ is derived in \S\ref{sec:joint5d-fwer}. A2 requires the lost pseudo-experiments to have been lost independently of their outcomes, which has not been shown. When rerun, however, none of them reaches the observed statistic (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-lost}). The calibrated size is averaged over the nuisance priors: at a fixed true value of the nuisance parameters it can exceed $\alpha$.

The joint-test estimator (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-calibration}) was chosen before the design was fixed. The hypotheses, statistics, variants, multiplicity rule, stopping rule and every input were fixed before any production pseudo-experiment or observed statistic, with two data-informed elements. First, the design's reviewers had seen descriptive per-cell residuals of the data before it was fixed. Second, a bounded extension of the production budget was decided during production, while the calibration's monitoring output showed observed claim $p$ values, so that the sequential rule, rather than the budget, could set the stop. An independent recomputation, with separately written code, of every $p$ value and decision from the stored products agrees with the production evaluation on all \jtRecompRows{} compared rows. \noindent In the terms of this note, they fall into three kinds.

\paragraph{Numerical convergence.} (i) Below the fine grid the null truth has the Tune~v1 composition of the hypothesis definition, varied by the drawn interaction-model universes with every fine-cell integral fixed. The fine-grid convergence check has not shown convergence. In the $L_{2}$ norm of the per-cell relative changes, the last refinement, from a twofold-merged grid to the fine grid, is \jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax{} of the whole change from the coarse test grid to the fine grid. In that norm the steps are therefore not shown to shrink, and the residual below the fine grid is unmeasured and potentially material. The design takes the step, in a proxy of the test metric, to shrink by a factor of \jtMoneWnormMin--\jtMoneWnormMax{} per halving. This factor comes from the review of the design, and its derivation is not given. If it kept shrinking geometrically at that rate, the residual would be about one to three times the last step, $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. Here $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ is the change of the noise-free unfolded result when the null truth is refined from the twofold-merged grid to the fine grid. That estimate sets the claim variants at $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$, the central extrapolated estimate, and the robustness check, for reporting only, at $\pm\jtMoneKappaRobust\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. The extrapolation assumes geometric convergence. The measured $L_{2}$ ratios also allow slower rates and larger residuals. Measured before any observed statistic, the claim variants move the external nulls by \jtMoneClaimSdMin--\jtMoneClaimSdMax{} null standard deviations and the robustness variants by \jtMoneRobSdMin--\jtMoneRobSdMax{}. For the hybrid null as simulated, the residual plays no role. The variants carry the rejections over to $G$ itself only if replacing the Tune~v1's shape below the fine grid by $G$'s moves the noise-free unfolded result by no more than $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ along $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. (vi) The predictions' own finite samples enter the metric as $\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ and the simulated residuals as a drawn term.

\subsection{Hadronic-response condition and recoil-energy sensitivity}
\label{sec:joint5d-response}

MINERvA's recoil-energy analyses assign a hadronic-energy response uncertainty derived from test-beam calorimetry~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam}, separate from the GEANT hadron-interaction reweights~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq,MINERvA:2022incl}. In the low-recoil measurement the resulting hadronic-energy uncertainty rises to \SI{10}{\percent} at its highest three-momentum transfers, $0.9<q_{3}<\SI{1.2}{GeV}$~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl}. The declared nuisance model here includes the reweights but no calorimetric response band. Such a band would act on all five nulls together, through the shared detector simulation and unfolding. A response mismodelling could move each observed statistic in either direction, and a band drawn in the calibration would also broaden and could shift each null distribution. The net effect on each $p$ value is unmeasured. The published comparison in muon kinematics alone, whose observables do not use the reconstructed recoil energy, also disfavours the same generator families~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. It provides context for a disagreement involving muon observables, but the hypotheses tested here use a different estimator and calibration. The GENIE~2.12.10 projection result of \S\ref{sec:joint5d-projections} shows that the information rejecting that prediction involves the hadronic axes, on which a response band would act.

\subsection{What the tests establish}
\label{sec:joint5d-reading}

The comparisons recover the generator disagreements already established by the published two-dimensional measurement on the same data~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. Their methodological contribution is calibration within the implemented simulation model, which puts the estimator's bias toward its prior and the declared nuisances into each null distribution. The rejections are global assessments of the five specified predictions under the declared model. Assigning a discrepancy to a kinematic region or nuclear mechanism would require separate tests. The rejections apply to the generator versions listed in \S\ref{sec:eavailw-band}, and they retain all six conditions of \S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}. Extending the inference would require a response band drawn through the calibration and a demonstrated bound on the residual below the fine grid.
