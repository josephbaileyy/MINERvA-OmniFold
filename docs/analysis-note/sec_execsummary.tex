\section*{Executive summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive summary}
\label{sec:execsummary}

We apply \emph{unbinned machine-learning unfolding}
(OmniFold~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw, Andreassen:2021zzk}) to MINERvA's
medium-energy neutrino data.

The main results are the validation findings, which hold for this implementation and for the
configurations, observables and deformations tested; the generator tests are an application that
illustrates calibrated inference with a biased estimator.

\paragraph{Results overview:}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt, parsep=0pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Reproduction.}
  Unbinned $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$ reproduces the published integrated cross section~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} within about one percent, with a correlated shape difference: total cross-section ratio $\ratioTot$, \SI{\binsTen}{\percent} of bins within \SI{10}{\percent}, and a separately constructed uncertainty comparable to the published one (per-bin median \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent} vs.\ \SI{\uqPaper}{\percent}, integral \SI{\uqIntegral}{\percent} vs.\ \SI{\uqPaperIntegral}{\percent}); its flux band carries MAT's $\nu$--$e$ and inverse-muon-decay constraint weights (\S\ref{sec:results-uq}). The central value and that uncertainty share the LightGBM estimator, chosen over the exact-split estimator, now a cross-check, after both comparisons with the publication were known (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). Against the full published covariance, $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiPaper$ (exact-split: $\chiPaperExact$) is a descriptive distance whose source has not been identified (\S\ref{sec:results-tension}).

  \item \textbf{Unbinned 3D/4D/5D MINERvA unfolds.}
  Adding $\Eavail$, $q_{3}$ and $W$ gives simultaneous unfolds. In configuration (a), the 4D and 5D marginal-normalization checks against the lower-dimensional result pass to $\approx$1--2\,\%; the configuration-(e) 3D marginal agrees with the configuration-(a) 2D result to \SI{0.141}{\percent} in total, a cross-configuration comparison (\S\ref{sec:3d}); binned MINERvA measurements already reach triple-differential~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme}. The 3D and 4D injected-bump closures, of configuration (a), leave 0.10 of the injected $\Eavail$ change and close $q_{3}$ to \SI{1.1}{\percent}; neither has an acceptance threshold recorded in advance (\S\ref{sec:3d-closure}, \S\ref{sec:4d}). Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five are simultaneous, not independent, observables (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-definitions}). $\Eavail$ keeps the inherited \SI{135}{MeV} charged-pion constant (\S\ref{sec:eavail-definition}).

  \item \textbf{Low-$\Eavail$ central values (descriptive).}
  All four truth-level generators (GENIE~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq}, MINERvA Tune~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}, NuWro~\cite{Golan:2012wx}, GiBUU~\cite{Buss:2011mx}; GENIE and NuWro flux-repaired, \S\ref{sec:3d-models}) under-predict the unfolded central value in the quasielastic--$\Delta$ dip ($0.1\le\Eavail<\SI{0.4}{GeV}$), the region of the established MINERvA low-recoil discrepancy~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil,MINERvA:2022incl}. Comparisons with the tested pion-FSI dials and with Valencia 2p2h~\cite{Nieves:2011pp,Gran:2013kda} use central values without uncertainties and do not identify a nuclear mechanism (\S\ref{sec:3d-2p2h}, Fig.~\ref{fig:3dmodels}).

  \item \textbf{High-$\Eavail$, high-$W$ excess (descriptive).}

  \item \textbf{Application: joint five-dimensional generator tests.}
  Calibrated tests of hybrid null hypotheses for five predictions (Tune~v1, GENIE with and without MEC, NuWro, GiBUU; each prediction on the fine grid, with the Tune~v1 composition below it), in total (rate and shape) and in shape, reject all ten under six stated conditions, with the family-wise error rate controlled at 5\% under the stated calibration assumptions and the size averaged over the nuisance priors (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-fwer}). The closest decisions, the NuWro and GENIE~CV shape tests, lie 1.11 and 1.30 null standard deviations beyond their boundary (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}). The rejections are not physical exclusions, matched coarse projections reject as well (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-projections}), and the nuisance model has no calorimetric recoil-response band (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-response}). Each rejection also holds in a robustness variant, not used for the decisions, that assumes a larger residual below the finest tested grid; that residual is unmeasured. The pseudo-experiments lost to time limits were rerun afterwards, for reporting only, so every submitted draw has been observed, and no recovered draw changes a decision (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-lost}). Correcting the carbon-only NuWro and GiBUU predictions to the CH target moves both toward the data; in this descriptive re-evaluation, which leaves the recorded decisions standing, NuWro's total-test rejection is not robust to the correction (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-ch}). These are hypothesis tests, not a measured uncertainty (\S\ref{sec:joint5d}).

  \item \textbf{A methodological finding.}
  The standard covariance sums single-source systematic matrices, each with all other sources nominal, and so omits nonlinear cross-source response terms; a corrected unified-throw test perturbs all reweightable sources together and stores the joint mean shift separately (\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}).

\paragraph{What is not claimed:}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt, parsep=0pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Coverage.}
  In a fixed-truth coverage test, an earlier 2D statistical band undercovers in its tails (the fraction within $2\sigma$, $C_2=\covTwoDCTwo$, vs.\ $0.954$; \S\ref{sec:coverage}). A rebuilt band was rescored only post hoc, on the same pseudo-experiments, and the quoted statistical block, a third ensemble, has not been tested.

  \item \emph{Regularization bias.}
  The estimator recovers only part of a generator-sized $\Eavail$ shape change (a fixed-size known-truth deformation, not a measured generator difference), with residuals of up to \SI{74}{\percent} in the highest-$W$ column, with or without background. This residual bias under the tested deformations is interpreted as regularization bias, because it persists over the tested iteration numbers and classifier capacities; no tested remedy removes it, and no quoted uncertainty includes it. Negative-weight background treatment removes most of the purity method's high-$W$ bias, leaving up to about \SI{1}{\percent} in some joint cells. It is the background treatment of estimator configurations (d) and (e) of Table~\ref{tab:estimators-matrix}, which give validation studies of \S\S\ref{sec:val-negweight}--\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence} and the generator tests; App.~\ref{app:negweight} also compares it with the purity subtraction on the 2D measurement. The 3D and 4D central values use configuration (e). The 2D and 5D central values and all quoted uncertainties use the purity subtraction, and the 5D central value keeps its bias of up to \SI{4}{\percent} in the highest-$W$ cells, which no quoted uncertainty includes (\S\ref{sec:val-negweight}, \S\ref{sec:val-departure}). In a 2D closure test with backgrounds (20 pseudo-experiments at nominal truth), the purity and refined estimates differ by more than one statistical standard deviation in 14 of the 205 cells; there the purity estimate lies up to $5.9\,\sigma_{\mathrm{stat}}$ from the truth (more than one total standard deviation in one cell), while the refined estimate stays within $0.44\,\sigma_{\mathrm{stat}}$ in every cell. The 2D result is being moved to the refined estimator (\S\ref{sec:negweight-footing}). No full five-dimensional comparison of the two background treatments has been made; the revisit of the choice of purity is in progress, with this 2D test as its first result (App.~\ref{app:negweight}).

  \item \emph{Estimator stability.}
  The one tested candidate estimator, with raised background-refinement capacity, fails the predeclared data-stability test: floating-point-level input changes move its results on real data by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of the baseline estimator by up to 2.5, so the instability belongs to the estimator family. The candidate's refit-per-replica data bootstrap already contains this rounding spread (about \SI{37}{\percent} of its variance), and central values are reproducible only to that size (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}).
