\subsection{Cells and result}
\label{sec:fived16-result}
The cells are formed from the H2 grid of \S\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}, which divides each axis once into \fvval{h2-nboxes} boxes, with edges $\pT=(\fvval{h2-edges-pt})$, $\pPar=(\fvval{h2-edges-pz})$, $\Eavail=(\fvval{h2-edges-eavail})$, $q_3=(\fvval{h2-edges-q3})$ and $W=(\fvval{h2-edges-W})$, in the units of App.~\ref{sec:gbdt-diagnostic-definitions}. Of these, \fvval{h2-nsupported} meet that appendix's support rule (minimum expected signal and truth weight). Each supported box with purity below $\tau=\fvval{h2-tau}$ is merged into a neighbour, in \fvval{h2-nmerge} predefined steps. The merges absorb \fvval{h2-nabsorbed} unsupported box into cell R09; the other \fvval{h2-nunreported} are not reported. Each of the 16 resulting cells meets the support rule. Four of them, \fvval{h2-lowstab-cells}, have a reconstruction stability of \fvval{h2-lowstab-min}--\fvval{h2-lowstab-max} (the other twelve, \fvval{h2-stab-rest-min}--\fvval{h2-stab-rest-max}): of the simulated signal events generated in such a cell and reconstructed, that fraction is reconstructed in the same cell. No stability criterion is applied in forming the cells. Table~\ref{tab:fived16-cells} gives each cell's box, its cell-integrated cross section $f$ and its relative uncertainty.

\subsection{Quadrant sums against the lower-dimensional results}
\label{sec:fived16-quadrants}
The sixteen cells form four $(\pT,\pPar)$ quadrants, divided at $\pT=\fvval{h2-mid-pt}$ and $\pPar=\fvval{h2-mid-pz}$~GeV/$c$, of four cells each. In each quadrant the sum $S_{16}$ of the sixteen-cell result is compared with the sum $S_{\mathrm{2D}}$ of the adopted two-dimensional result over the same region, through $d=S_{16}-S_{\mathrm{2D}}$. On the sixteen-cell side the covariance is the reported one; on the two-dimensional side it is the four-block covariance of \S\ref{sec:uq-combine} in the first comparison below, and that covariance with its generator-shape term $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ in the second. The two results share the data, the flux and most systematic variations, and come from different estimators: configuration~(e) for the sixteen cells and configuration~(f) for the two-dimensional result (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). The three-observable marginal of configuration~(e), compared with the two-dimensional result over all 205 reported cells, agrees with it to \SI{0.141}{\percent} in area-weighted total (\S\ref{sec:3d-anchor}); that is a different quantity from the quadrant differences below, of which QA's is $\fvval{cr-QA-dpct}$\,\%.

\paragraph{Without the cross covariance.} The first comparison bounds the unknown correlation $\rho$ between the two sums: over $\rho\in[0,1]$, $\sigma_d$ lies between $|\sigma_{16}-\sigma_{\mathrm{2D}}|$ and $(\sigma_{16}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{2D}}^2)^{1/2}$. A quadrant agrees if $|d|\leq2|\sigma_{16}-\sigma_{\mathrm{2D}}|$, which holds at every $\rho$; it agrees below a correlation if $|d|$ lies between that and $2(\sigma_{16}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{2D}}^2)^{1/2}$, and $\rho^{*}$ is the correlation at which $|d|=2\sigma_d$; it differs if $|d|$ exceeds $2(\sigma_{16}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{2D}}^2)^{1/2}$, beyond two standard deviations at every non-negative $\rho$. The rule was fixed before the comparison was made. Table~\ref{tab:fived16-quad-cr} gives each quadrant. All four agree, QA at every $\rho$ and the others below correlations of \fvval{cr-QC-rhostar} (QC) to \fvval{cr-QB-rhostar} (QB). Since $\sigma_{16}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{2D}}$ are close in every quadrant, the band valid at every $\rho$ is narrow, which is why three quadrants agree only below a correlation.

\paragraph{With the cross covariance.} The second comparison evaluates the covariance between the two results from what they share, and tests the four differences jointly. The cross covariance sums paired terms: \fvval{x-nbands-paired} of the \fvval{x-nbands} systematic bands, universe by universe, after their signal weights were checked to agree event by event between the two analyses; the \fvval{x-nlat} lateral shifts; the normalization; the run-period terms, paired period by period by construction; and the $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ shifts, paired generator by generator. Two parts could not be paired. The statistical correlation is not measured: the paired bootstrap replicas give cross correlations that do not fit the statistical blocks of the two results, which were built from other replica sets, and the joint matrix they form is not positive semidefinite. The weight check failed in \fvval{x-bounded}, on non-finite ratios only, between the two analyses' universe-to-central weight ratios. These five enter as $r\,S_{16}^{1/2}S_{\mathrm{2D}}^{1/2}$, with $S$ each side's own block, for $r\in[0,1]$: a one-parameter family that keeps the joint covariance positive semidefinite, not every admissible correlation. The rule, fixed in advance, is that the results agree if the joint $\chi^2$ of the four differences has $p\geq0.05$ and every $|z|\leq2.50$; it is read at every point of a 10\,001-point grid in $r$, and \fvval{x-ncontrols} structural and block-by-block controls passed. Table~\ref{tab:fived16-quad-x} gives the result. The two results agree at every $r$ and in every reading. In the primary reading $\chi^2=\fvval{x-prim-chi2-r0}$ ($p=\fvval{x-prim-p-r0}$) at $r=0$ and \fvval{x-prim-chi2-r1} ($p=\fvval{x-prim-p-r1}$) at $r=1$, and the largest $|z|$ is \fvval{x-zmax}, in QC at $r=1$. Each $|z|$ is largest at an end of the family, since its variance is linear in $r$, and $\chi^2$ is convex in $r$, so it is largest, and $p$ smallest, at an end; the value of $\chi^2$ between the ends is not recorded. The implied correlation between the two quadrant sums is \fvval{x-QC-rho0}--\fvval{x-QA-rho0} at $r=0$ and \fvval{x-QC-rho1}--\fvval{x-QA-rho1} at $r=1$.

\label{sec:fived16-cov}
The reported covariance is $C_{\mathrm{total}}+C_{\mathrm{resp}}+C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$, with $C_{\mathrm{total}}=C_{\mathrm{e}}+C_{\mathrm{model}}$; the run-period block $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ is described in \S\ref{sec:fived16-pl}. $C_{\mathrm{e}}$ sums the statistical bootstrap of configuration~(e), 50 replicas, each of which refits the whole unfolding with its own rounding-level perturbation of the inputs; the PPFX flux universes, weighted by the neutrino--electron and inverse-muon-decay constraint; the interaction-model and detector bands, which reweight simulated events; the lateral bands, which shift reconstructed kinematics (\S\ref{sec:systematics}); the normalization; and the training-seed block. With full-sample binning and deterministic training, the unfolds at the other seeds are bitwise equal to the central one, so the measured training-seed block is zero in every cell. Table~\ref{tab:fived16-groups} gives each group's per-cell relative $\sigma$.

For $C_{\mathrm{model}}$, the prior is reweighted in turn by the ratio of GENIE + MEC, NuWro or GiBUU to GENIE CV, and the data are unfolded again. Each generator's shift is the relative change of the data result, and $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ is the unscaled sum of their outer products. That sum is a fractional covariance. In cross-section units it is $\mathrm{diag}(x)\,C_{\mathrm{model}}\,\mathrm{diag}(x)$, with $x$ the cell-integrated cross sections on the reporting cells. It is generator-shape model dependence, not a calibrated bound; its coverage at truths held out of its construction has not been validated. The GiBUU reweighting is applied only below $\pPar=\fvval{cmodel-gibuu-pz-max}$~GeV/$c$; above it the prior is unchanged. Between $\pPar=\fvval{cmodel-gibuu-pz-lack}$ and \fvval{cmodel-gibuu-pz-max}~GeV/$c$, the GiBUU sample lacks the contribution from $E_\nu>\fvval{cmodel-gibuu-enu}$~GeV, which is at least \fvval{cmodel-gibuu-lack-lo}--\fvval{cmodel-gibuu-lack-hi}\% of the cross section there in the GENIE CV and NuWro samples (which stop at $E_\nu=\fvval{gagg-range-max}$~GeV). The GiBUU shift contributes \fvval{cmodel-trace-gibuu} of the trace of $C_{\mathrm{model}}$, the NuWro shift \fvval{cmodel-trace-nuwro} and the GENIE + MEC shift \fvval{cmodel-trace-genie_mec}. In every cell, \fvval{cmodel-step-lo}--\fvval{cmodel-step-hi}\% of the prior weight lies next to a discontinuity larger than \fvval{cmodel-step-r} in the reweighting ratio, created by the ratio's statistical gate or by a cut; the shifts are used unmodified.

Every $\chi^2$ of this section inverts the full covariance of the cells compared, $16\times16$ or $8\times8$ for GiBUU's cells, by Cholesky decomposition and without truncation, so the inverted dimension $p$ equals the number of cells and is fixed in advance; the shape-only $\chi^2$ inverts the same matrix with $100\,f f^{T}$ added and has one degree of freedom fewer. The blocks inverted are of unlike kinds. Two are sample covariances of an ensemble: the statistical block, from \fvval{s1-stat-replicas} bootstrap replicas with the unbiased $1/(N-1)$ normalization, and the flux block, from \fvval{fb-universes} PPFX universes with constraint weights, normalized by the sum of the weights (the weighted analogue of $1/N$; effective number of universes \fvval{s1-flux-neff}). The 2p2h band has three universes. The other bands and the lateral shifts are two-point shifts, the normalization is one outer product, $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ sums three chosen generator shifts, $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ four $\pm1\sigma$ shifts, and $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ is built from three exposure-weighted periods; the training-seed block is zero. Each comparison with a prediction adds $V_g$, its diagonal sum of squared event weights, and each fake-data comparison the variance of the mean over its pseudo-experiments. No finite-ensemble correction is applied to any block. The standard correction is derived for a single unweighted sample covariance, which none of these sums is; the flux bootstrap below measures the scatter of the $\chi^2$ from estimating the flux block, and is not a correction.

\subsection{The run-period block}
\label{sec:fived16-pl}
On the sixteen cells, the unfolded results of the three run-period groups (playlists 1A--1F, 1G--1M and 1N--1P), each over its own simulated truth (the double ratio), differ from one another by more than the scatter of their bootstrap replicas. The offsets of the twelve playlists differ between the three period groups by more than expected from their playlist-to-playlist scatter. The cause is not known, and these data cannot establish which period, if any, is right. As for the two-dimensional result (\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}, Table~\ref{tab:pl-added}), the block $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ is built from these double ratios with no reference period. With $e_X$ the exposure share of period group $X$, $r_X$ that group's unfolded result over its own simulated truth, and $m=\sum_X e_X r_X$, the fractional offset is $g_X=r_X/m-1$. The reliability-weight factor is $k=V_2/(1-V_2)$, where $V_2=\sum_X e_X^2$. The block is $k\sum_X e_X (x\circ g_X)(x\circ g_X)^{T}$, with $x$ the central value on these cells. It has rank \fvval{pl-rank} and is not debiased for the replica noise it contains. With three groups its own size is poorly determined. The 68\,\% interval on the ratio of its true to its estimated $\sigma$, \fvval{pl-sigratio-lo}--\fvval{pl-sigratio-hi}, is the interval of a chi-squared distribution with the effective degrees of freedom of this exposure-weighted spread, and it depends only on the exposure shares. In cells \fvval{pl-noise-cells} the block is dominated by the replica noise it contains.

Each of the twelve playlists was also unfolded on its own, with its own simulation and bootstrap replicas, in a study whose tests, bounds and readings were fixed before any playlist result was read. A playlist's offset is its double ratio on the sixteen cells relative to the exposure-weighted mean of the twelve. The statistic $F$ of a division of the playlists into groups is the share of the offsets' exposure-weighted squared size, measured with the inverse of the relative $C_{\mathrm{total}}+C_{\mathrm{resp}}$, that the groups' exposure-weighted mean offsets carry. For the three run-period groups $F=\fvval{rpd-fflux}$, the largest of all \fvval{rpd-npart-flux} divisions of the twelve playlists into groups of six, three and three, so the permutation $p$-value is $1/\fvval{rpd-npart-flux-math}=\fvval{rpd-pflux}$, the smallest the test can give. The permutation is approximate: the playlists differ in exposure and hence in noise, so their labels are not exchangeable, and the test's validity rests on a simulated null. In each of \fvval{rpd-datasets} simulated datasets the playlists' offsets are drawn independently from one covariance, estimated from the twelve playlists and scaled by 0, 0.5, 1 or 2, with each playlist's own bootstrap and simulation noise added; the share of datasets with $p<0.05$ is \fvval{rpd-alpha-lo}--\fvval{rpd-alpha-hi} over the four scales, within the bound of \fvval{rpd-alpha-gate}. For an alternative chronological division into groups of five, two, two and three playlists (1A--1E, 1F and 1G, 1L and 1M, and 1N--1P), the same statistic gives $p=\fvval{rpd-pera}$ over its \fvval{rpd-npart-era} divisions; this comparison is descriptive.

In the form of the two-dimensional test (\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}), the five-observable unfolds of the run-period groups give the double ratio of 1G--1M to 1A--1F a $\pPar$ step of $(\fvval{rpd-s0}\pm\fvval{rpd-s0-sigma})\,\%$ across \SI{8}{GeV/c}, $\fvval{rpd-s0-z}$ times its bootstrap scatter. Each playlist's own step, against 1A--1F (leaving the playlist out when it is one of them), was classed as consistent with none within two standard deviations (FREE), larger than half of the 1G--1M group's step, recombined from its three playlists, by two standard deviations (CARRIES), or neither (PARTIAL). The classes are CARRIES for \fvval{rpd-r1-carries}; PARTIAL for \fvval{rpd-r1-partial}; and FREE for \fvval{rpd-r1-free}. The study's two patterns are that all three of 1G, 1L and 1M carry the step, or that some carry it and the others are free, each with 1A--1F free. Neither holds, so no statement of where the step lies is drawn from these classes. None of these readings identifies a cause.

\subsection{Dependence on the unfolding algorithm}
\label{sec:fived16-ibu}
A comparison declared before it ran puts the result beside a binned D'Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU) of the same data, with the same simulation, the same background template and the same weight-only systematic universes as configuration~(e), at \lrval{rg1-K} iterations, integrated to the same sixteen cells. It forms $T=\Delta^{\mathsf{T}}C_{\Delta}^{-1}\Delta$ from the difference $\Delta$ of the two results and its covariance $C_{\Delta}$, which holds the statistical, universe and normalization blocks of the difference (not the model, lateral, $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ or run-period blocks), and compares $T$ with bars calibrated on its own simulated null of exact agreement. The two results differ: $T=\lrval{rg1-T}$, against calibrated bars of \lrval{rg1-bar1} (\lrval{rg1-lev1}) and \lrval{rg1-bar2} (\lrval{rg1-lev2}). The reported cells therefore depend on the unfolding algorithm at the level of $T$. This dependence is a stated limitation of the result: no uncertainty block is added for it, and the reported cells and covariance are unchanged. A reading of differ means that a difference is detectable at this noise; it does not show that either method is correct.

Descriptively, at a NuWro closure the IBU's median absolute residual is \lrval{rg1-r2-nuwro} times that of configuration~(e), and at the simulation's own truth, a trivial closure, the IBU's residual vanishes; neither is a coverage statement for either estimator. The IBU uncertainty is a median \lrval{rg1-r3-med} times that of configuration~(e) over the sixteen cells (\lrval{rg1-r3-lo}--\lrval{rg1-r3-hi}); configuration~(e)'s statistical block includes its estimator's own noise, refit per replica, and the IBU has none.

The fake-data bias exceeds the stated thresholds at the GiBUU hybrid truth under the reported covariance, and also at the GiBUU $\Eavail$ shape without $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ (\S\ref{sec:fived16-fd}), so the GiBUU comparison carries that limitation.

The sensitivity of the reported $\chi^2$ to the correlations is probed by shrinking one group's correlations toward the diagonal by a fraction $\alpha$ of the way, with its variances kept. These are descriptive sensitivities, not alternative results. Shrinking only the flux correlations by \fvval{sens-alpha-pct}\,\% lowers the $\chi^2$ of the 16-cell predictions by \fvval{sens-flux-fall-lo}--\fvval{sens-flux-fall-hi}\,\%. The same change to the detector or lateral correlations lowers it by at most \fvval{sens-detlat-fall-max}\,\%. With the correlations of the interaction, flux, detector and lateral groups removed, every 16-cell prediction keeps $\chi^2\geq\fvval{sens-joint-chi2-min}$ for 16 degrees of freedom. Table~\ref{tab:fived16-sens} gives each group's sensitivity, GiBUU's eight-cell values included. The flux correlations are themselves estimated from the \fvval{fb-universes} PPFX flux universes (\S\ref{sec:fived16-cov}). Rebuilding the flux block from \fvval{fb-replicates} bootstrap replicates of those universes, with its variances kept, gives \fvval{fb-plo}--\fvval{fb-phi}\,\% percentile bands of the total $\chi^2$: \fvval{label-gcv} \fvval{fb-gcv-lo}--\fvval{fb-gcv-hi} around \fvval{g-gcv-total-chi2int}, \fvval{label-gmec} \fvval{fb-gmec-lo}--\fvval{fb-gmec-hi} around \fvval{g-gmec-total-chi2int}, \fvval{label-nuwro} \fvval{fb-nuwro-lo}--\fvval{fb-nuwro-hi} around \fvval{g-nuwro-total-chi2int}, \fvval{label-tune} \fvval{fb-tune-lo}--\fvval{fb-tune-hi} around \fvval{g-tune-total-chi2int}, and \fvval{label-gibuu}, on its \fvval{g-gibuu-total-ndf} cells, \fvval{fb-gibuu-lo}--\fvval{fb-gibuu-hi} around \fvval{g-gibuu-total-chi2int}. These bands measure the estimation scatter of the reported $\chi^2$ given these universes, not how much it would change if the flux correlations were wrong, which the shrinking above probes; they are not intervals for the $\chi^2$ under the true correlations.

\subsection{The predictions' target and definitions}
\label{sec:fived16-gen-target}
% GENCMP round (DECISION-20261101-joseph-gencmp-paper-adoption.md items 1-4 and 6; director's rulings of 2026-11-01). Sources: FD-GENCMP-REPORT.md sec. 2.1-2.4 (#648) and its readouts fd/gencmp/readout/{defs,ch,native,overlap}.json and receipts/d_projection.txt, each pinned in make_values_fived16.py; the construction and the definitions, FD-GENCMP-DECLARATION.md sec. 2-3 and 5; C_model's samples, nd-unfolding/pv-ladder/config.json:58-65 (the frozen C12 products of the declaration's control A1). The sensitivities, the bootstrap and the modes above are Stage G's chain rerun on Table IV's predictions (GENCMP amendment E, E-1 rerun1: FD-GENCMP-AMENDMENT-E-REPORT.md sec. 3.1, readouts fd/gencmp-e/rerun1/chain/chdefs/); Table tab:fived16-gen-stageg keeps Stage G as first made, from its committed readouts (pin g0 in make_values_fived16.py). C_model's target (DECISION-20261102-joseph-cmodel-keep-carbon-state-effect.md items 2-4): FD-GENCMP-AMENDMENT-E-ADDENDUM-E2-REPORT.md sec. 3 (fd/gencmp-e2/rerun1/chi2/e2.json), sec. 5 (rerun1/sens/x_e2_sens.out.txt) and sec. 7 (the limits), pinned as e2 and e2sens; the reported covariance keeps C_model on carbon.
The predictions of Table~\ref{tab:fived16-gen} differ from the generators' own products in two ways. First, NuWro and GiBUU, generated on carbon, are corrected to CH per fine cell as $x_{\mathrm{CH}}=\tfrac{12}{13}\,x_{\mathrm{C}}+\tfrac{1}{13}\,x_{\mathrm{H}}$, with $x_{\mathrm{H}}$ the generator's own free-proton product, as in \S\ref{sec:joint5d-ch}. Second, all four use the analysis definitions of $W$ and of the muon angle: $W$ is computed with the struck nucleon's mass, or an average nucleon mass where the generator records no single struck nucleon, and the muon angle is measured from the beam axis, each event's neutrino direction being drawn from the analysis simulation's distribution in its $E_\nu$ bin. The prediction is the mean over five such draws. With the generators' own definitions restored, every sample reproduces its committed product bit for bit. The change of definitions moves no cell of any of the four by more than \fvval{gdef-dmax} of the cell's reported $\sigma$, and the standard deviation over the five draws is at most \fvval{gdef-seedmax}\,$\sigma$. MINERvA Tune~v1 is already in the analysis definitions and is unchanged.

Table~\ref{tab:fived16-gen-target} gives the carbon values and the sensitivities. On carbon, in the analysis definitions, NuWro gives \fvval{g-nuwro-carbon-total-chi2} (shape-only \fvval{g-nuwro-carbon-shape_only-chi2}) and GiBUU \fvval{g-gibuu-carbon-total-chi2} (\fvval{g-gibuu-carbon-shape_only-chi2}). The correction lowers NuWro's total $\chi^2$ and raises GiBUU's by \fvval{g-gibuu-chrise}, while GiBUU's shape-only $\chi^2$ falls. On the joint cells of \S\ref{sec:joint5d-ch} both corrections move the prediction toward the data; in the sixteen-cell comparison GiBUU's total $\chi^2$, on its eight cells, rises, because the CH prediction is paired with $C_{\mathrm{model}}$'s NuWro and GiBUU shape ratios from carbon (below). With the reported $C_{\mathrm{model}}$, that rise also depends on the hydrogen sample: with GENIE CV's hydrogen in place of GiBUU's own, in the generators' own definitions, GiBUU's total is \fvval{g-gibuu-genieh-total-chi2}, below its carbon value of \fvval{g-gibuu-gen-carbon-total-chi2}. GENIE's hydrogen sample is thin, so that spread is largely its own Monte Carlo noise, and the GENIE-hydrogen rows are sensitivities, never the comparison. GiBUU's CH comparison carries the label of \S\ref{sec:joint5d-ch}: the sampling-closure check failed on its hydrogen sample, a bias check of that check's premise, added post hoc, passed at $\le\fvval{g-gibuu-hlab-sigc}\,\sigma_c$, and deep-inelastic events, \fvval{g-gibuu-hlab-dis}\,\% of its hydrogen sample, do not conserve energy.

A native CH generation of NuWro, with only the target changed in the production card, was compared cell by cell with the combination under a rule fixed in advance: $\sum z^2<\fvval{gnat-rule-sumz2}$ over the sixteen cells and no $|z|>\fvval{gnat-rule-zmax}$, with $z$ from the two samples' Monte Carlo variances. It agrees in the generators' definitions ($\sum z^2=\fvval{gnat-gen-sumz2}$, largest $|z|=\fvval{gnat-gen-zmax}$) and in the analysis definitions (\fvval{gnat-ana-sumz2} and \fvval{gnat-ana-zmax}). Its $\chi^2$ is \fvval{g-nuwro-native-gen-total-chi2} against the combination's \fvval{g-nuwro-gen-ch-total-chi2}; the comparison uses the combination. A diagnostic that was not a declared test: in one chunk of the generation's probe, NuWro's total cross section per nucleon is \fvval{gnat-probe-pct}\,\% above $(12\sigma_{\mathrm{C}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{H}})/13$ computed from one chunk each of the production carbon sample and the free-proton sample, mostly in deep-inelastic events.

The $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ components of the reported covariance are built from the generators' samples in their own definitions, with NuWro and GiBUU on carbon, not from the predictions of Table~\ref{tab:fived16-gen}. Table~\ref{tab:fived16-gen-cmodel} measures the effect of that target. There, NuWro's and GiBUU's shape ratios in $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ are taken from their CH combinations in the generators' own definitions (the ``CH (generator)'' rows of Table~\ref{tab:fived16-gen-target}), with everything else fixed; the reported covariance keeps them on carbon. Under the reported covariance the total $\chi^2$ changes by $\fvval{e2-with-gcv-total-dch}$ (GENIE CV), $\fvval{e2-with-gmec-total-dch}$ (GENIE + MEC), $\fvval{e2-with-nuwro-total-dch}$ (NuWro) and $\fvval{e2-with-gibuu-total-dch}$ (GiBUU). Over both matrices and both kinds, the largest change is $-\fvval{e2-dmax}$, in GENIE + MEC's shape-only $\chi^2$ without $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$. Only the NuWro and GiBUU components change, but every prediction's $\chi^2$ moves, because the components enter one matrix. With the CH shape ratios, GiBUU's CH total is \fvval{e2-with-gibuu-total-ch}, below its carbon value of \fvval{g-gibuu-carbon-total-chi2} with the reported covariance, so the rise described above comes from pairing the CH prediction with the carbon shape ratios. For the scale of $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ itself, whose size is not calibrated, the table also gives the $\chi^2$ with $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ removed and doubled. Every $\chi^2$ falls as $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ grows. Removing $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ raises GiBUU's total most, to \fvval{e2-with-gibuu-total-x0} from \fvval{e2-with-gibuu-total-x1}. With the CH shape ratios, the reported per-cell $\sigma$ falls in \fvval{e2-sig-with-nfall} of the 16 cells and rises in \fvval{e2-sig-with-upcell}, by \fvval{e2-sig-with-up}\,\%. It falls most in \fvval{e2-sig-with-cell}, where $\sigma/f$ goes from \fvval{e2-sig-with-carbon} to \fvval{e2-sig-with-ch} ($-\fvval{e2-sig-with-fall}$\,\%; $-\fvval{e2-sig-nopl-fall}$\,\% without $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$). $C_{\mathrm{model}}$'s own $\sigma/f$ changes most in \fvval{e2-cm-cell}, from \fvval{e2-cm-carbon} to \fvval{e2-cm-ch}, and most in relative terms in \fvval{e2-cm-relcell}, where it is small: from \fvval{e2-cm-relcarbon} to \fvval{e2-cm-relch} ($+\fvval{e2-cm-rel}$\,\%). This measures $C_{\mathrm{model}}$'s dependence on the target only: its definitions stay the generators' own, its coverage is still not validated, and GiBUU's CH shape ratio carries the hydrogen label of \S\ref{sec:joint5d-ch}. The changes are those of these data; their statistical uncertainty, from the data entering the unfolds that build $C_{\mathrm{model}}$, is not measured. Table~\ref{tab:fived16-gen-stageg} gives the comparison in the generators' own definitions with NuWro and GiBUU on carbon.
