\subsection{Double-differential cross section}
\label{sec:results-xsec}
Figure~\ref{fig:xsec} shows the 1D projections of the unfolded $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$; Figs.~\ref{fig:ptslices}--\ref{fig:pzslices} show it slice by slice. The total cross section is \SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon} (published: \SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon}).

The shapes are those expected for CC-inclusive scattering at $\langle E_\nu\rangle\sim\SI{6}{GeV}$. The $\pT$ projection vanishes at $\pT=0$ and peaks broadly at $\pT\approx0.55$--$\SI{0.70}{GeV/c}$, reflecting the $Q^{2}\lesssim\SI{1}{GeV^2}$ scale of few-GeV scattering through $\pT^{2}\simeq Q^{2}\,E_\mu/E_\nu$, with $E_\mu/E_\nu\sim0.6$ for these forward muons. It falls by more than two orders of magnitude toward the \SI{4.5}{GeV/c} edge. The $\pPar$ projection rises from the \SI{1.5}{GeV/c} threshold to a broad $\pPar\approx3$--$\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$ maximum (the NuMI medium-energy flux peak at $E_\nu\approx\SI{6}{GeV}$), then falls by two orders of magnitude by $\pPar\approx\SI{20}{GeV/c}$. The $\theta_\mu<20^{\circ}$ acceptance empties the 2D map's high-$\pT$, low-$\pPar$ corner (19 unreported diagonal cells; \S\ref{sec:method-binning}).

\subsection{Comparison to the published measurement}
\label{sec:results-compare}
The published and unbinned results share the data and the flux, detector and generator systematics, and their cross-covariance is not available. Every $\chi^{2}$ and standardized difference here is therefore descriptive, not a calibrated goodness-of-fit test.

Over the 205 reported bins, the total cross section reproduces the published result~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} to \SI{1.0}{\percent} (ratio $\ratioTot$; Table~\ref{tab:compare}). The median per-bin ratio is $\medianBinRatio$. $\binsFive\%$ of bins agree within \SI{5}{\percent} and $\binsTen\%$ within \SI{10}{\percent}. Indicative values of $\chi^{2}$ per reported bin are $\chiPaper$ with the published covariance alone (analyzed in \S\ref{sec:results-tension}) and $\chiCombined$ with the sum of the published covariance and that of this result. The comparison is not an independent test of the method (\S\ref{sec:method-config} and Table~\ref{tab:test-index}).

The latter, $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiCombined$, double-counts the flux, GENIE and MINOS systematics that the two results share. With the covariance of this result alone, $\chi^{2}$ is computed only in the covariance's well-constrained subspace, with a truncated spectral pseudo-inverse. The per-bin pulls and the truncated result are therefore quoted together, and an unconstrained pseudo-inverse is not used as a goodness-of-fit test on its own. The regularization scan (App.~\ref{sec:rank}) was made for the exact-split cross-check on an earlier covariance and was not repeated for the quoted one.

\subsection{Where the tension with the published covariance lies}
\label{sec:results-tension}
With the published uncertainties as a descriptive scale, the central values have a pull standard deviation of $\pullRMS$ (a diagonal-only $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ would be $0.29$), whereas $\chi^{2}$ per bin with the full published covariance is $\chiPaper$. The eightfold increase lies entirely in the off-diagonal structure of the published covariance. It is a difference in \emph{shape}. Normalization does not explain it ($\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{norm}}=0.04$ against $\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{shape}}=465$). The eigenmode decomposition (Fig.~\ref{fig:tension}) spreads it over many modes: the largest has a paper-covariance-standardized component of magnitude $4.7$, and the next nine $3.4$--$3.8$. The 10 smallest-$\lambda$ modes carry \SI{9.6}{\percent} of the total $\chi^{2}$. Of the five largest contributors, two lie in the smallest-$\lambda$ tenth of the spectrum. Restricted to the 73 best-measured directions, $\chi^{2}=136$ ($\chi^{2}/205=0.66$; the divisor is the number of reported bins, not the number of directions kept). Of the six leading modes, the three in the highest-$\pPar$ bins are all poorly determined directions ($\lambda/\lambda_{\max}\lesssim4\times10^{-9}$). The other three are moderate-$\lambda$ modes ($\lambda/\lambda_{\max}$ between $4\times10^{-6}$ and $6\times10^{-5}$) and lie below the highest $\pPar$ bins (Fig.~\ref{fig:tensionmodes}). \subsection{Comparison to GENIE MINERvA Tune v1}
\label{sec:results-model}
GENIE 2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} with MINERvA Tune v1 under-predicts the published total cross section over the 205 reported bins by $\sim\SI{9}{\percent}$ ($\sigma_{\mathrm{tune}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{data}}=0.91$), and the deficit is concentrated in the low-$\pT$ peak. Figure~\ref{fig:xsec} compares the prediction from the published ancillary files (dashed) with both the published data and this result. Under this covariance the quadratic distance to that prediction is $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=33.0$ for the published data and $31.3$ for this result ($26.5$ for the exact-split cross-check). These are descriptive distances. This covariance gives no calibrated significance for a generator comparison (\S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}). Our result tracks the data far more closely than the model does (Fig.~\ref{fig:modelmaps}). The published analysis~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} also compares with NuWro~\cite{Golan:2012wx}, GiBUU~\cite{Buss:2011mx} and NEUT~\cite{Hayato:2009zz}. OmniFold's per-event reweighted truth ensemble can be compared with any such prediction in \emph{any} projection of the classifier features without re-unfolding. An observable outside that space requires a new unfolding, as in the 3D extension (\S\ref{sec:3d}), which adds $\Eavail$ as a classifier feature.
