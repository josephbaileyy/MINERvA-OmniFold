\subsection{Double-differential cross section}
\label{sec:results-xsec}
Figure~\ref{fig:xsec} shows the unfolded $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$ and its 1D projections. The total cross section is \SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon} (published: \SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon}).

The shapes are those expected for CC-inclusive scattering at $\langle E_\nu\rangle\sim\SI{6}{GeV}$. The $\pT$ projection vanishes at $\pT=0$ and peaks broadly at $\pT\approx0.55$--$\SI{0.70}{GeV/c}$, reflecting the $Q^{2}\lesssim\SI{1}{GeV^2}$ scale of few-GeV scattering through $\pT^{2}\simeq Q^{2}\,E_\mu/E_\nu$, with $E_\mu/E_\nu\sim0.6$ for these forward muons. It falls by more than two orders of magnitude toward the \SI{4.5}{GeV/c} edge. The $\pPar$ projection rises from the \SI{1.5}{GeV/c} threshold to a broad $\pPar\approx3$--$\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$ maximum (the NuMI medium-energy flux peak at $E_\nu\approx\SI{6}{GeV}$), then falls by two orders of magnitude by $\pPar\approx\SI{20}{GeV/c}$. The $\theta_\mu<20^{\circ}$ acceptance empties the 2D map's high-$\pT$, low-$\pPar$ corner (19 unreported diagonal cells; \S\ref{sec:method-binning}).

\subsection{Comparison to the published measurement}
\label{sec:results-compare}
The published and unbinned results share the data and the flux, detector and generator systematics, and their cross-covariance is not available. Every $\chi^{2}$ and standardized difference here is therefore descriptive, not a calibrated goodness-of-fit test.

Over the 205 reported bins, the total cross section reproduces the published result~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} to \SI{1.0}{\percent} (ratio $\ratioTot$; Table~\ref{tab:compare}). The median per-bin ratio is $\medianBinRatio$; $\binsFive\%$ of bins agree within \SI{5}{\percent} and $\binsTen\%$ within \SI{10}{\percent}. Indicative values of $\chi^{2}$ per reported bin are $\chiPaper$ with the published covariance alone (analysed in \S\ref{sec:results-tension}) and $\chiCombined$ with the sum of the published covariance and that of this measurement.

% chi^2 caveat, moved here from sec_systematics.tex (formerly \paragraph{$\chi^{2}$ caveat.}).
The latter, $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiCombined$, double-counts the flux, GENIE and MINOS systematics that the two results share. With the covariance of this measurement alone, $\chi^{2}$ is computed only in the covariance's well-constrained subspace, with a truncated spectral pseudo-inverse. We therefore quote the per-bin pulls and the truncated result together, with a regularization scan made for the exact-split cross-check on an earlier covariance and not repeated for the quoted one, rather than treating an unconstrained pseudo-inverse as a goodness-of-fit test on its own (App.~\ref{sec:rank}).

\subsection{Uncertainty budget}
\label{sec:results-uq}
Table~\ref{tab:uq} gives the budget over the 205 reported bins, constructed as described in \S\ref{sec:systematics}. Our own combined budget, without the published covariance, is comparable to the published total: its per-bin median, \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent}, is smaller than the published \SI{\uqPaper}{\percent}, and its value on the integrated cross section, \SI{\uqIntegral}{\percent}, is larger than the published \SI{\uqPaperIntegral}{\percent} (the width-weighted total $\sigma$ of App.~\ref{sec:chisq}). The comparison leaves out the generator-shape component $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ (\S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}), because the published uncertainty has no such component either; with it, the per-bin median is \SI{\qrCmodelMedian}{\percent}. The systematic-universe block accounts for a median 0.87 of the per-bin variance and 0.83 of the integral's variance, and the run-period block (\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}) raises the per-bin median from \SI{\uqSFiveMedian}{\percent} to \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent}. The largest band is the flux, as in the publication (Fig.~\ref{fig:uqbands}), and it accounts for part of the difference between the per-bin medians: its median is \SI{\fluxBand}{\percent}, against \SI{4.01}{\percent} for the published flux block. The public release does not let us determine the origin of this difference; our leading hypothesis is the FHC-only constraint file. Our flux band carries the per-universe $\nu$--$e$ and inverse-muon-decay constraint weights of the MINERvA Analysis Toolkit. The statistical and ML blocks together contribute under a tenth of the combined $\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}\,C}$. Every block is computed with the LightGBM estimator of the quoted central value (\S\ref{sec:method-config}), and relative uncertainties are taken with respect to that central value, which is the universe sweep's central value at training seed~42. Taken with respect to the exact-split cross-check instead, the same combined covariance has a per-bin median of \SI{6.462}{\percent}.

\subsection{Eigenmode analysis of the published-covariance tension, $\chi^{2}=\chiPaper$}
\label{sec:results-tension}
With the published uncertainties as a descriptive scale, the central values have a pull RMS of $\pullRMS$ (a diagonal-only $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ would be $0.29$), whereas $\chi^{2}$ per bin with the full published covariance is $\chiPaper$. The eightfold increase lies entirely in the off-diagonal structure of the published covariance, and it is a difference in \emph{shape}, not normalization ($\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{norm}}=0.04$ against $\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{shape}}=465$). The eigenmode decomposition (Fig.~\ref{fig:tension}) spreads it over many modes: the largest has a paper-covariance-standardized component of magnitude $4.8$, and the next nine $3.4$--$3.8$. The 10 smallest-$\lambda$ modes carry \SI{9.6}{\percent} of the total $\chi^{2}$. Of the five largest contributors, two lie in the smallest-$\lambda$ tenth of the spectrum. Restricted to the 73 best-measured directions, $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ is $0.66$. Of the six leading modes, the three in the highest-$\pPar$ bins are all poorly determined directions ($\lambda/\lambda_{\max}\lesssim4\times10^{-9}$); the other three are moderate-$\lambda$ modes ($\lambda/\lambda_{\max}$ between $4\times10^{-6}$ and $6\times10^{-5}$) and lie below the highest $\pPar$ bins (Fig.~\ref{fig:tensionmodes}). No single $\pT$ or $\pPar$ band carries more than \SI{15}{\percent} of the total.

\subsection{Comparison to GENIE MINERvA Tune v1}
\label{sec:results-model}
GENIE 2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} with MINERvA Tune v1 under-predicts the total cross section by $\sim\SI{9}{\percent}$ ($\sigma_{\mathrm{tune}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{data}}=0.91$), and the deficit is concentrated in the low-$\pT$ peak. Figure~\ref{fig:model} compares the prediction from the published ancillary files with both the published data and this measurement. The data covariance strongly disfavors the tune, with $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=33.0$ for the published data and $31.3$ for this measurement ($26.5$ for the exact-split cross-check), and our result tracks the data far more closely than the model does (Fig.~\ref{fig:modelmaps}). The published analysis~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} also compares with NuWro~\cite{Golan:2012wx}, GiBUU~\cite{Buss:2011mx} and NEUT~\cite{Hayato:2009zz}. OmniFold's per-event reweighted truth ensemble can be compared with any such prediction in \emph{any} projection of the classifier features without re-unfolding. An observable outside that space requires a new unfolding, as in the 3D extension (\S\ref{sec:3d}), which adds $\Eavail$ as a classifier feature.
