\subsection{Double-differential cross section}
\label{sec:results-xsec}
Figure~\ref{fig:xsec} shows the unfolded $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$ and its
1D projections. The total cross section is
\SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon} (published: \SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon}).

The shapes are those expected for CC-inclusive scattering at
$\langle E_\nu\rangle\sim\SI{6}{GeV}$. The $\pT$ projection vanishes at
$\pT=0$ and peaks broadly at $\pT\approx0.55$--$\SI{0.70}{GeV/c}$, reflecting
the $Q^{2}\lesssim\SI{1}{GeV^2}$ scale of few-GeV scattering through
$\pT^{2}\simeq Q^{2}\,E_\mu/E_\nu$, with $E_\mu/E_\nu\sim0.6$ for these forward
muons. It falls by more than two orders of magnitude toward the
\SI{4.5}{GeV/c} edge. The $\pPar$ projection rises from the
\SI{1.5}{GeV/c} threshold to a broad $\pPar\approx3$--$\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$ maximum
(the NuMI medium-energy flux peak at $E_\nu\approx\SI{6}{GeV}$), then falls by two
orders of magnitude by $\pPar\approx\SI{20}{GeV/c}$. The
$\theta_\mu<20^{\circ}$ acceptance empties the 2D map's high-$\pT$, low-$\pPar$
corner (19 unreported diagonal cells; \S\ref{sec:method-binning}).

\subsection{Comparison to the published measurement}
\label{sec:results-compare}
The published and unbinned results share the data and the flux, detector and
generator systematics, and their cross-covariance is not available. Every
$\chi^{2}$ and standardized difference here is therefore descriptive, not a
calibrated goodness-of-fit test.

Over the 205 reported bins, the total cross section reproduces the published
result~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} to \SI{1.0}{\percent} (ratio $\ratioTot$;
Table~\ref{tab:compare}). The median per-bin ratio is $\medianBinRatio$;
$\binsFive\%$ of bins agree within \SI{5}{\percent} and $\binsTen\%$ within
\SI{10}{\percent}. Indicative values of $\chi^{2}$ per reported bin are
$\chiPaper$ with the published covariance alone (analysed in
\S\ref{sec:results-tension}) and $\chiCombined$ with the combined covariance,
which includes our uncertainty.

% chi^2 caveat, moved here from sec_systematics.tex (formerly \paragraph{$\chi^{2}$ caveat.}).
The combined (published plus ours) $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiCombined$
double-counts the flux, GENIE and MINOS systematics that the two results share.
With our covariance alone, $\chi^{2}$ is computed only in the covariance's
well-constrained subspace, with a truncated spectral pseudo-inverse. We
therefore quote the per-bin pulls and the truncated result together, with a
regularization scan made for the exact-split cross-check on an earlier
covariance and not repeated for the quoted one, rather than treating an
unconstrained pseudo-inverse as a goodness-of-fit test on its own
(App.~\ref{sec:rank}).

\subsection{Uncertainty budget}
\label{sec:results-uq}
Table~\ref{tab:uq} gives the budget over the 205 reported bins, constructed as
described in \S\ref{sec:systematics}. Our own combined budget, without the
published covariance, is comparable to the published total: its per-bin
median, \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent}, is smaller than the published
\SI{\uqPaper}{\percent}, and its value on the integrated cross section,
\SI{\uqIntegral}{\percent}, is larger than the published
\SI{\uqPaperIntegral}{\percent} (the width-weighted total $\sigma$ of
App.~\ref{sec:chisq}). The systematic-universe block carries a median 0.87 of
each bin's variance and 0.83 of the integral's, and the run-period block
(\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}) raises the per-bin median from
\SI{\uqSFiveMedian}{\percent} to \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent}.
The largest band is the flux, as in the publication (Fig.~\ref{fig:uqbands}),
and it accounts for part of the difference between the per-bin medians: its median is
\SI{\fluxBand}{\percent}, against \SI{4.01}{\percent} for the published flux
block. The public release does not let us determine the origin of this
difference; our leading hypothesis is the FHC-only constraint file.
Our flux band carries MAT's per-universe $\nu$--$e$ and inverse-muon-decay
constraint weights. The
statistical and ML blocks together contribute
under a tenth of the combined $\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}\,C}$.

\subsection{Anatomy of the $\chi^{2}=\chiPaper$ tension}
\label{sec:results-tension}
With the published uncertainties as a descriptive scale, the central values
have a pull RMS of $\pullRMS$ (a diagonal-only $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ would be
$0.29$), whereas the distance with the full published covariance is
$\chiPaper$ per bin. The eightfold increase lies entirely in the off-diagonal
structure of the published covariance, and it is a difference in
\emph{shape}, not normalization ($\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{norm}}=0.04$ against
$\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{shape}}=465$). The eigenmode decomposition
(Fig.~\ref{fig:tension}) spreads it over many modes, the largest with
paper-covariance-standardized components of magnitude about $4$--$5$. The 10
smallest-$\lambda$ modes carry \SI{9.6}{\percent}, two of the five largest
contributors lie in the smallest-$\lambda$ tenth, and keeping the 73
best-measured directions gives $0.66$. The four leading modes in the
highest-$\pPar$ bins are all poorly determined directions
($\lambda/\lambda_{\max}\lesssim4\times10^{-9}$); the two leading
moderate-$\lambda$ modes lie at low-to-moderate $\pT$, below the highest $\pPar$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:tensionmodes}). No single $\pT$ or $\pPar$
band carries more than \SI{15}{\percent} of the total.

The choice of GBDT estimator spreads the distance over about one unit, a
methodological band: the
exact-split cross-check gives $\chiPaperExact$, HistGBT $2.70\pm0.04$, and
LightGBM with seeds 1--10 gives 2.64--2.75. For the exact-split cross-check the
distance is larger and distributed differently, with magnitude-$5$--$6$ components in
moderate-$\lambda$ modes on the low-$\pT$ ridge of the cross-section peak;
the 10 smallest-$\lambda$ modes carry \SI{3}{\percent} of it, and the 73
best-measured directions give $1.42$ (App.~\ref{sec:tension}). The number of
iterations has a negligible effect: going from 5 to 10 iterations moves the
LightGBM (seed~1) distance by $+0.01$.

\subsection{Comparison to GENIE MINERvA Tune v1}
\label{sec:results-model}
GENIE 2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} MINERvA Tune v1 under-predicts the total
by $\sim\SI{9}{\percent}$ ($\sigma_{\mathrm{tune}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{data}}=0.91$),
concentrated in the low-$\pT$ peak. Figure~\ref{fig:model} compares the
prediction from the published ancillary files with both the published data and
our result. The data covariance strongly disfavors the tune
($\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$: data vs tune $33.0$, ours vs tune $31.3$; $26.5$ for
the exact-split cross-check), and our result tracks the data far more closely
than the model does (Fig.~\ref{fig:modelmaps}).
The published analysis~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} also compares with
NuWro~\cite{Golan:2012wx}, GiBUU~\cite{Buss:2011mx} and NEUT~\cite{Hayato:2009zz}.
OmniFold's per-event reweighted truth ensemble can be compared with any
such prediction in \emph{any} projection of the classifier features without
re-unfolding. An observable outside that space requires a new unfolding, not a
projection: the 3D extension (\S\ref{sec:3d}) adds $\Eavail$ as a classifier
feature and is unfolded again.
