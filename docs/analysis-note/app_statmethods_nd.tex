\subsubsection{Rank, ndf and finite-ensemble declarations for N-D covariances}
\label{sec:nd-declarations}

The 2D justification in \S\ref{sec:chisq} does not transfer to N-D. An earlier 5D covariance from the standard production chain has $10\,694$ reported bins, effective positive rank $\mathbf{263}$ and infinite numerical condition number. It is distinct from $C_Z$, the five-axis covariance of the \resFiveAxis{} (Table~\ref{tab:config-key}), and was built before $C_Z$ was chosen for use. Its $4825$-bin 5D$\to$4D projection retains rank $263$ exactly. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for the construction behind this rank and its effect on $\chi^{2}$.

For an N-D covariance, $\mathrm{ndf}$ is the retained rank. Where an N-D $\chi^{2}$ is quoted, it must state five things:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the inverse actually used, including the pseudo-inverse cutoff \texttt{rcond} (singular values below this fraction of the largest are discarded) or the truncation rank;
\item the retained rank, as $\mathrm{ndf}$;
\item the rank-truncation scan of that covariance;
\item the identity of the covariance, since the earlier 5D covariance, its 4D projection and the published 2D block differ in rank;
\item for each sample block in the sum, the ensemble size $N$, the biased $1/N$ or unbiased $1/(N-1)$ normalization, the effective inverted dimension $p$ after truncation, and the finite-ensemble treatment, or that none is applied.
\end{enumerate}
The measurements, the derivation and the requirement that N-D difference statistics use a rank cut fixed before the results are seen are in Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} (\recentry{rank-inversion}).

% boundary"; docs/STATVAL_REPAIR.md, "PREDECLARED rank/pseudoinverse cut"), so it is stated as that requirement.

\paragraph{Finite-ensemble bias of the inverse.} The inverse of a noisy sample covariance is biased \emph{upward}. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for the interpretation of finite-ensemble inverse bias. For an unbiased $1/(N-1)$ estimator over $N$ independent Gaussian realizations, the standard debiasing factor is $(N-p-2)/(N-1)$. For this analysis's production convention, universe-mean centered with biased $1/N$ normalization, it is $(N-p-2)/N$.
% (\texttt{nd-unfolding/uq\_math.py}, ``universe-mean centered, biased 1/N'')

No generic correction is prescribed. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for how the finite-ensemble treatment is selected. The standard factor also assumes independent Gaussian realizations and a data-independent truncation dimension, neither established for a data-dependent cut.

\subsubsection{Scalar-5D diagnostic reductions and interval definitions}
\label{sec:gbdt-diagnostic-definitions}

Fine-grid cross-section densities are converted to integrals by multiplying by all five bin widths before aggregation. The saved trace functionals on the EW cells are differential in $(\Eavail,W)$ and are multiplied by the cell areas. Those on the J and H2 cells are already integrals. The J boundaries are $\pT=(0,0.55,0.85,4.5)$, $\pPar=(1.5,3.5,6,60)$, $\Eavail=(0,0.4,1.5,100)$, $q_3=(0,1.2,2,100)$ and $W=(0,1.4,2.2,100)$. H2 has the same outer boundaries, with one interior boundary per axis: $\pT=0.7$, $\pPar=4.5$, $\Eavail=0.8$, $q_3=2$ and $W=1.8$. Energy units are GeV and momentum units GeV/$c$. A cell is supported when both its expected reconstructed signal and selected truth weight at data exposure are at least 1000. This gives 109 J, 27 H2 and 39 EW cells. The unsupported EW11, EW36 and EW37 are omitted from the supported-cell summaries only. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for aggregation checks.

For experiment $i$ and cell $f$,
\begin{equation}
 r_{if}=\frac{\hat f_i-f_{{\rm true},i}}{f_{{\rm true},i}},\qquad
 \bar r_f=\frac1n\sum_i r_{if},\qquad
 s_f^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(r_{if}-\bar r_f)^2.
\end{equation}
The truth of the stored pseudo-data source varies with the MC split. $s_f$ measures relative-residual spread. It does not measure unconditional fixed-truth cross-section variance. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for interval scope and deterministic traces. The saved table also reports bias-retaining $\sqrt{n^{-1}\sum_i r_{if}^2}$. Pointwise 95\% mean intervals use $t_{0.975,n-1}s_f/\sqrt n$. SD intervals use $s_f\sqrt{(n-1)/\chi^2_{0.975,n-1}}$ and $s_f\sqrt{(n-1)/\chi^2_{0.025,n-1}}$.

The development assessment includes an interval diagnostic. It uses $\sigma_f$, the sample standard deviation of 100 refitted bootstrap replicas of the nominal development pseudo-experiment (experiment 700000). Assessment results are scored against $[\hat f_i-\sigma_f,\hat f_i+\sigma_f]$ and $[\hat f_i-1.96\sigma_f,\hat f_i+1.96\sigma_f]$, without bias correction or model envelope. The table gives integer hits, both empirical fractions, exact pointwise 95\% binomial intervals and fixed half-width relative to mean truth. Coverage fractions are not pooled across cells to certify them. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for development-assessment caveats.

The rounding study keeps the fine-grid membership fixed under uniform input perturbations within half a float32 unit in the last place. With 20 base jitters, 50 unperturbed bootstrap replicas $f_b$ and their jittered partners $f'_b$, the reported quantities are
\begin{equation}
 s_{\rm base}=\operatorname{SD}(f_j),\quad
 s_{\rm boot}=\operatorname{SD}(f_b),\quad
 s_{\rm pair}=\operatorname{SD}\!\left((f'_b-f_b)/\sqrt2\right).
\end{equation}
The consistency ratio is $s_{\rm pair}/s_{\rm base}$. The estimated numerical share is $s_{\rm pair}^2/s_{\rm boot}^2$. Containment, as defined before the study ran, requires a median consistency ratio in $[0.7,1.4]$ and $s_{\rm boot}^2\geq s_{\rm pair}^2$ in at least 95\% of supported cells. All supported cells meet the inequality. This finite-sample diagnostic supports the construction without proving coverage or independence from every physical uncertainty. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for the paired scaling.

For prior vertex $v$, data sensitivity is $d_{vf}=\hat f_{{\rm data},v}/\hat f_{{\rm data},0}-1$, with envelope $h_f=\max_v|d_{vf}|$. Neither $h_f$ nor an outer product of closure residuals is given a probability model. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for reduction checks.

\paragraph{Intervals of the five-dimensional coverage test.} The intervals divide each estimate by $1+\beta$, where $\beta$ is the relative closure bias at nominal truth, and add the standard error of $\beta$ times the corrected estimate in quadrature to the statistical standard deviation. A quantity fails when the upper confidence bound on its coverage lies below 0.66 for 68\% intervals or below 0.94 for 95\% intervals. Each bound uses the Bonferroni level of Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage-5d-paper}, one correction over the 153 integrals and the three generated truths. The 68\% assessment and the 95\% assessment are separate; each uses that level. The second truth (the $\Eavail$ tilt) multiplies the true signal by $1+0.2\,(\Eavail/\SI{100}{GeV}-\tfrac12)$ without renormalization, with the background unchanged; below $\Eavail=\SI{3}{GeV}$ the weight is 0.900--0.906.

\subsubsection{Inference from unfolded events and selected projections}
\label{sec:unbinned-inference}
Unfolding correlates weighted events. Treating them as independent in a downstream likelihood can underestimate parameter uncertainties~\cite{Desai:2025correlated}. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for downstream inference requirements.

The uncertainty target matters: separate bin or projection coverage does not imply simultaneous coverage. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for qualification of the linear covariance propagation. A fixed linear projection $y=P x$ propagates covariance as $C_y=P C_x P^{\top}$, with integrated bin widths in $P$ for densities.

See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for the limitations of selected-functional benchmarks.

\subsubsection{Generative model of the joint-test pseudo-experiments}
\label{app:joint5d-genmodel}
\paragraph{Generative model of one pseudo-experiment.} Each calibration pseudo-experiment splits the simulation at random into two halves (Table~\ref{tab:joint5d-genmodel}) and refits the background refinement. Its truth equals $G$ on every fine cell that the source half populates. On the signal, each interaction-model band is renormalized within each fine truth cell, so it moves only the composition within the cell, and with it the efficiency and the migrations. The flux universe is drawn uniformly from the \jtFluxUniverses{} universes. In each calibration pseudo-experiment, the flux universe and the band universes are drawn independently, and their weight ratios multiply. Each interaction-model band is drawn independently and uniformly from its own universes: either endpoint of a $\pm1\sigma$ pair, or one of three where a band has three. Their weight ratios multiply. Negative weights in two of those bands are clipped per universe (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}). Each weight-based detector band is at its $-1\sigma$ or $+1\sigma$ endpoint with equal probability. The muon-energy, muon-resolution, and beam-angle lateral bands enter as $\sum_{b}z_{b}\Delta_{b}$. Each $z_{b}$ is an independent standard normal. For each band, $\Delta_{b}$ is half the difference of the unfolded cell values from that band's two real-data endpoint unfolds. The coefficient of the \SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization is an independent standard normal. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for empty-cell effects.

The data's rounding noise, and the prediction's finite-sample error, are added as a normal draw in each cell. The prediction's variance in that draw is its own per-cell sum of squared weights. That diagonal model is an assumption of the metric. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for nuisance-model details.

Because the $\Delta_{b}$ come from the observed data, the null distributions are conditional on these data-derived inputs, which would differ in a repeated experiment. That conditioning has not been validated.

\subsubsection{Familywise error of the sequential joint-test decisions}
\label{sec:joint5d-fwer}

This derivation supports the familywise statement of \S\ref{sec:joint5d-frozen}.

\emph{Probability model.} Fix one test, and condition on the data and on the simulated sample. The calibration pseudo-experiments, taken in their fixed seed order, give exceedance indicators $X_{b}=\mathbf{1}[T_{b}\ge t_{\mathrm{obs}}]$. Assumption A2 is that, given the data, these are independent Bernoulli draws with a common probability $p$, the calibrated tail probability of the observed statistic. Assumption A1 is that, for a true null, $p$ is super-uniform over repetitions of the experiment, $P(p\le u)\le u$, with the nuisance parameters drawn from their priors. Let $k_{B}$ be the number of exceedances among the first $B$ draws and $U(k,B)$ the upper end of the two-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval, a one-sided 97.5\% upper bound. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for calibration assumptions.

Each test's probability of rejection at threshold $c$ is at most $R(c)\le\int_{0}^{1}g_{c}(u)\,du$, with $g_{c}(p)$ the probability, at tail probability $p$, that the upper bound $U$ crosses below $c$ at some $B\le\jtBcap$, whatever rule chose the stopping point; the derivation is in the records document (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}).
\emph{The decision rule, operationally.}  The tests are ordered by increasing $(k+1)/(B+1)$, with ties broken in the fixed listing order (total before shape for each null).  At step $i$ the threshold is $\alpha/(m-i+1)$: the test is rejected if $U<\alpha/(m-i+1)$, it is not rejected if the lower end of its interval exceeds the threshold, and it is undetermined otherwise. A test that is not rejected or undetermined ends the step-down, and the tests after it take the same status.  Equality $U=c$ does not reject.

\emph{Holm reduction without independence.} Let $m_{0}$ of the $m=10$ hypotheses be true. The first true null to be rejected is reached after at most $m-m_{0}$ rejections of false nulls, so its threshold is at most $\alpha/m_{0}$. A familywise error therefore requires some true null to cross below $\alpha/m_{0}$, and by the union bound $\mathrm{FWER}\le m_{0}\,R(\alpha/m_{0})\le\max_{j\le m}j\,R(\alpha/j)$. The decision $p$ value is the largest over shifted nulls that include the unshifted one, evaluated at the same $B$, so its exceedance count is at least that of the unshifted test; since $U$ increases with $k$, the bound for the unshifted test covers the decision rule. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for the dependence argument.

The largest ratio $R(c)/c$, at $j=1$, lies in $[0.8892,0.8902]$. Hence, for $\alpha=0.05$, the value used here, $\mathrm{FWER}\le0.8902\,\alpha<0.9\,\alpha$ under any stopping at or before \jtBcap{} pseudo-experiments. The bound rests on A1, the calibration's fidelity, which \S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions} discusses, and on A2. See Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026} for threshold-specific bounds.
