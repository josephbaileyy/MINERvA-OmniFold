\section{Statistical methods for the multidimensional results and inference}
\label{app:statmethods-nd}

This appendix gives the conventions for covariances and comparisons beyond the 2D result: rank, ndf
and finite-ensemble declarations for N-D covariances, the scalar five-dimensional diagnostic reductions and
interval definitions, the extended-fiducial band and pull fractions, and inference from unfolded
events and selected projections. The 2D methods and their worked examples are in
App.~\ref{app:statmethods}.

\subsection{Rank, ndf and finite-ensemble declarations for N-D covariances}
\label{sec:nd-declarations}

The 2D justification in \S\ref{sec:chisq} does not transfer to N-D.
An earlier 5D covariance from the standard production chain, distinct from $C_Z$ and built before $C_Z$ was chosen for use, has
$10\,694$ reported bins, effective positive rank $\mathbf{263}$ and infinite
numerical condition number. Its $4825$-bin 5D$\to$4D projection retains rank
$263$ exactly. About ${\sim}45$ two-endpoint MAT bands plus statistical and ML
blocks cannot span $10\,694$ directions, so the rank limit is a consequence of the
construction, not a defect. Quoting $\chi^{2}/n_{\text{reported}}$ would
understate tension by roughly $40\times$ in 5D
or $18\times$ in 4D.

N-D covariances must not use $\mathrm{ndf}=n_{\text{reported}}$.
Every N-D $\chi^{2}$ must state (i) the actual inverse, including
pseudo-inverse \texttt{rcond} or truncation rank, at the point of quotation;
(ii) retained rank as $\mathrm{ndf}$; (iii) that covariance's rank-truncation
scan, expected to lack the smoothness supporting the 2D choice in
\S\ref{sec:chisq}; (iv) covariance identity, since that earlier 5D covariance, its 4D projection and the published 2D block differ in rank; and (v) for every sample
block in the sum, ensemble size $N$, biased $1/N$ or unbiased $1/(N-1)$
normalization, effective inverted dimension $p$ after truncation, and the
finite-ensemble treatment or explicit absence of one. Measurements,
derivation and predeclared rank cut are in Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}
(\texttt{rank-inversion}).

\paragraph{Finite-ensemble bias of the inverse, and why no correction is prescribed.}
Without declaration (v), the ensemble sizes of the blocks would go unrecorded,
and an uncorrected finite-ensemble bias could enter unnoticed. The inverse of a noisy sample covariance is biased
\emph{upward}: for an unbiased $1/(N-1)$ estimator over $N$ independent Gaussian realizations the
standard debiasing factor is $(N-p-2)/(N-1)$, and for this analysis's biased $1/N$ production
convention (universe-mean centered, biased $1/N$) it is
% (\texttt{nd-unfolding/uq\_math.py}, ``universe-mean centered, biased 1/N'')
$(N-p-2)/N$. The superseded unified-throw 5D covariance has $N=160$,
truncated $p=100$, giving $58/160\simeq0.363$: uncorrected precision is too
large by $\simeq2.76$. For fixed residuals this inflates $\chi^{2}$ and
overstates tension; confidence regions from the same over-tight covariance
become over-optimistic.

No generic correction is prescribed. A sum of about 45 deterministic rank-one
MAT outer products plus statistical and ML blocks has no single debiasing
factor. The standard factor also assumes independent Gaussian realizations
and a data-independent truncation dimension, neither established for a
data-dependent cut. The treatment must be chosen for the covariance and
inverse actually used, from the quantities that declaration (v) records.

\subsection{Scalar-5D diagnostic reductions and interval definitions}
\label{sec:gbdt-diagnostic-definitions}

The synthesis in \S\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence} reuses saved outputs; it
involves no new unfolding, training or pseudo-experiments. Fine-grid
cross-section densities are converted to integrals by multiplying by all five
bin widths before aggregation. The saved EW trace functionals are differential
in $(\Eavail,W)$ and are multiplied by their EW areas; saved J and H2
functionals are already integrals. Direct tensor-slice integration checks the
index-map sums and full-domain rate. Reporting boundaries coincide with fine
edges. The J boundaries are $\pT=(0,0.55,0.85,4.5)$,
$\pPar=(1.5,3.5,6,60)$, $\Eavail=(0,0.4,1.5,100)$,
$q_3=(0,1.2,2,100)$ and $W=(0,1.4,2.2,100)$; H2 uses interior
boundaries $(0.7,4.5,0.8,2,1.8)$, respectively, with the same outer
boundaries. Energy units are GeV and momentum units GeV/$c$. A cell is supported
when both its expected reconstructed signal and selected truth weight at data
exposure are at least 1000. This gives 109 J, 27 H2 and 39 EW cells; the unsupported
EW11, EW36 and EW37 are omitted from the supported-cell summaries only.

For experiment $i$ and cell $f$,
\begin{equation}
 r_{if}=\frac{\hat f_i-f_{{\rm true},i}}{f_{{\rm true},i}},\qquad
 \bar r_f=\frac1n\sum_i r_{if},\qquad
 s_f^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(r_{if}-\bar r_f)^2.
\end{equation}
The truth of the stored pseudo-data source varies with the MC split, so $s_f$ measures
relative-residual spread, not unconditional fixed-truth cross-section variance.
The saved table also reports bias-retaining $\sqrt{n^{-1}\sum_i r_{if}^2}$.
Pointwise 95\% mean intervals use $t_{0.975,n-1}s_f/\sqrt n$;
SD intervals use $s_f\sqrt{(n-1)/\chi^2_{0.975,n-1}}$ and
$s_f\sqrt{(n-1)/\chi^2_{0.025,n-1}}$. These normal-model approximations
condition on finite simulation and make no simultaneous claim across cells.
A deterministic noise-free trace from one simulation receives no error band;
its cell-to-cell variation is not sampling uncertainty.

The interval diagnostic of the development assessment uses $\sigma_f$, the sample SD of 100
refitted bootstrap replicas of nominal development experiment 700000.
Assessment results are scored against $[\hat f_i-\sigma_f,\hat f_i+\sigma_f]$
and $[\hat f_i-1.96\sigma_f,\hat f_i+1.96\sigma_f]$, without bias correction
or model envelope. The table gives integer hits, both empirical fractions,
exact pointwise 95\% binomial intervals and fixed half-width relative to mean
truth. It conditions on estimated bootstrap widths without propagating
finite-replica uncertainty. Coverage is not pooled to certify cells.
The examples shown are a populated
low-energy cell (EW7) and the corner cell EW41, already known to fail; they are
not freshly selected validation targets.

The rounding study keeps the fine-grid membership fixed under uniform input
perturbations within half a float32 unit in the last place. With 20 base jitters,
50 unperturbed bootstrap replicas $f_b$ and their jittered partners $f'_b$, the
reported quantities are
\begin{equation}
 s_{\rm base}=\operatorname{SD}(f_j),\quad
 s_{\rm boot}=\operatorname{SD}(f_b),\quad
 s_{\rm pair}=\operatorname{SD}\!\left((f'_b-f_b)/\sqrt2\right).
\end{equation}
The consistency ratio is $s_{\rm pair}/s_{\rm base}$; the estimated numerical
share is $s_{\rm pair}^2/s_{\rm boot}^2$. The paired-model $\sqrt2$ factor
introduces no physical noise source. The predeclared design defines containment
by median ratio in $[0.7,1.4]$ and
$s_{\rm boot}^2\geq s_{\rm pair}^2$ in at least 95\% of supported cells.
All supported cells meet the inequality. This finite-sample diagnostic
supports the construction without proving coverage or independence from every
physical uncertainty.

For prior vertex $v$, data sensitivity is
$d_{vf}=\hat f_{{\rm data},v}/\hat f_{{\rm data},0}-1$, with envelope
$h_f=\max_v|d_{vf}|$. Its denominator differs from the simulated residual's.
Neither $h_f$ nor an outer product of closure residuals is given a probability model.
The reductions keep the per-cell values, the identity of each input and the
exclusions, and distinguish complete outputs from checkpoints that carry no
metadata. From a checkpoint, only the iterations that reproduce the originally
recorded values are used; later entries are excluded. The analyst cross-checked
the reductions numerically; no new independent scientific review of the synthesis is claimed.

\subsection{Inference from unfolded events and selected projections}
\label{sec:unbinned-inference}
Unfolding correlates weighted events. Treating them as independent in a
downstream likelihood can underestimate parameter
uncertainties~\cite{Desai:2025correlated}; nominal weights do not specify
uncertainty. Coherent unfolding replicas could propagate it through the fit.
Resampling unfolded rows independently is not equivalent to resampling inputs
and repeating the learned estimator. Classifier and permutation
goodness-of-fit tests also require null calibration for their fitting and data
reuse. These are requirements on such inference, with no new validation result here.

The uncertainty target matters: separate bin or projection coverage does not
imply simultaneous coverage. A fixed linear projection $y=P x$ propagates
covariance as $C_y=P C_x P^{\top}$, with integrated bin widths in $P$ for
densities. The identity neither calibrates that covariance nor accounts for
regions chosen after inspecting data.

Recent work constructs calibrated intervals for selected functionals in
constrained inverse problems~\cite{Stanley:2025functionals} and joint confidence
regions for multiple functionals~\cite{Batlle:2025simultaneous}, which
could provide independent benchmarks for specified integrals or contrasts.
Their guarantees depend on the forward model, noise law, and parameter
constraints and do not transfer automatically to a learned response with
finite simulation and detector nuisances. A rank-deficient forward operator
is also distinct from a rank-deficient sample covariance; neither truncating
the latter nor validating a few projections certifies the full distribution.
Such a benchmark would supplement, not replace, the scalar five-dimensional uncertainty construction that a measurement requires.

\subsection{Familywise error of the sequential joint-test decisions}
\label{sec:joint5d-fwer}

This derivation supports the familywise statement of \S\ref{sec:joint5d-frozen}.

\emph{Probability model.}  Fix one test, and condition on the data and on the
simulated sample.  The calibration pseudo-experiments, taken in their fixed
seed order, give exceedance indicators $X_{b}=\mathbf{1}[T_{b}\ge
t_{\mathrm{obs}}]$.  Assumption A2 is that, given the data, these are
independent Bernoulli draws with a common probability $p$, the calibrated tail
probability of the observed statistic; this requires the lost
pseudo-experiments to be lost independently of their outcomes.  Assumption A1
is that, for a true null, $p$ is super-uniform over repetitions of the
experiment, $P(p\le u)\le u$, with the nuisance parameters drawn from their
priors; the size statement is therefore averaged over the nuisance priors.
Let $k_{B}$ be the number of exceedances among the first $B$ draws and
$U(k,B)$ the upper end of the two-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval, a
one-sided 97.5\% upper bound.

\emph{Crossing event.}  The rule rejects at threshold $c$ only if
$U(k_{B},B)<c$ at the number $B\le\jtBcap$ at which sampling stopped.  Whatever
rule chose that stopping point, including stops decided with results visible,
irregular looks, lost draws, and the budget extensions, the rejection lies in
the event $E_{c}=\bigcup_{B\le\jtBcap}\{U(k_{B},B)<c\}$ that the bound crosses
below $c$ at some $B$.  Hence $P(\text{reject at }c)\le R(c)\equiv
E_{p}[g_{c}(p)]$, with $g_{c}(p)=P(E_{c}\mid p)$.

\emph{Uniform $p$ is the worst case.}  Coupling the Bernoulli draws in $p$ shows
that $g_{c}(p)$ does not increase with $p$.  For any super-uniform $p$ its
expectation is therefore at most its value for uniform $p$,
$R(c)\le\int_{0}^{1}g_{c}(u)\,du$.

\emph{The decision rule, operationally.}  The tests are ordered by increasing
$(k+1)/(B+1)$, with ties broken in the fixed listing order (total before shape
for each null).  At step $i$ the threshold is $\alpha/(m-i+1)$: the test is
rejected if $U<\alpha/(m-i+1)$, it is not rejected if the lower end of its
interval exceeds the threshold, and it is undetermined otherwise; a test that
is not rejected or undetermined ends the step-down, and the tests after it
take the same status.  Equality $U=c$ does not reject.

\emph{Holm reduction without independence.}  Let $m_{0}$ of the $m=10$
hypotheses be true.  The first true null to be rejected is reached after at
most $m-m_{0}$ rejections of false nulls, so its threshold is at most
$\alpha/m_{0}$.  A familywise error therefore requires some true null to cross
below $\alpha/m_{0}$, and by the union bound
$\mathrm{FWER}\le m_{0}\,R(\alpha/m_{0})\le\max_{j\le m}j\,R(\alpha/j)$.  The
argument uses neither independence between tests nor the order of the Holm
steps, so the shared ensemble of a null's total and shape tests, their
unequal stopping counts, and the ranking by $(k+1)/(B+1)$ with its ties do not
affect it.  The decision $p$ value is the largest over shifted nulls that
include the unshifted one, evaluated at the same $B$, so its exceedance count
is at least that of the unshifted test; since $U$ increases with $k$, the bound for the unshifted test
covers the decision rule.

\emph{Certified constant.}  For each $c=\alpha/j$, $j=1,\dots,10$, let
$k^{*}(B)$ be the largest $k$ with $U(k,B)<c$.  For fixed $p$, a dynamic
program propagates the distribution of $k_{B}$ from $B=1$ to \jtBcap{}, moving
the probability of every state with $k_{B}\le k^{*}(B)$ into an absorbing
``crossed'' state at each $B$, and pooling the counts above
$\max_{B}k^{*}(B)$, which can never cross because counts never decrease; the
absorbed probability is $g_{c}(p)$ exactly.  This is evaluated on a grid of 6000
points in $[0,6c]$.  Because $g_{c}$ is nonincreasing, a left Riemann sum
over-estimates its integral on each grid interval, and with the tail term
$(1-6c)\,g_{c}(6c)$ it is a rigorous upper bound on $\int_{0}^{1}g_{c}$, so the
integration error is included in the bound.
% X7: m2-calc/certify_crossing.py:1-4, :8-35 (kstar by bisection; absorbing state; overflow pooled; strict U < c).
The largest ratio $R(c)/c$, at $j=1$, lies in $[0.8892,0.8902]$; it decreases to
$[0.5138,0.5148]$ at $j=10$.  Hence, for $\alpha=0.05$, the value used here,
$\mathrm{FWER}\le0.8902\,\alpha<0.9\,\alpha$ under any stopping at or before \jtBcap{} pseudo-experiments.  The bound rests
on A1, the calibration's fidelity, which \S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions} discusses,
and on A2.
