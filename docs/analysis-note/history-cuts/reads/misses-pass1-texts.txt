\paragraph{Approximations and omitted terms.} In the systematic-universe unfolds, an event that fails reconstruction keeps the central-value weight and is then divided by that universe's completeness, rather than receiving the universe's own truth weight. The central value has completeness 1, so that division does not change it. The approximation is not corrected. It was tested in two of the 187 universes, \texttt{MaRES\_0} and the flux universe with the largest median completeness change (\texttt{Flux:23}), each at seeds 42 and 43. In neither was an effect resolved above the training-seed noise. The tests compared the difference with the $\sigma$ of the earlier ten-seed block, which is about 3.7 times that of the training-seed block used here. They exclude only a median effect above twice that $\sigma$, so an effect below the training noise is not excluded. Separately, retraining moves these two universes' shifts by a median 1.13--1.23 times that $\sigma$.
