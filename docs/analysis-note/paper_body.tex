\section{Introduction and scope}
\label{sec:intro}

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon
and hadronic observables.  Their interpretation depends on correcting detector
response and assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure.
Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration
matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but the response tensor grows
rapidly when additional observables are reported.  OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned
feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation
\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}.  This makes simultaneous
higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned
response tensor for every projection.  Unbinned unfolding has been applied to
collider data, for example eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at
H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at
ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}; see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey.  In
neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector
sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.

We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the
MINERvA open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  We first
reproduce the published double-differential charged-current inclusive cross
section in transverse and longitudinal muon momentum,
$d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, then add available
hadronic energy $\Eavail$, three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and hadronic
invariant mass $W$, and obtain simultaneous three-, four-, and five-observable
central values.  Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed
$(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five observables are simultaneous but not
independent (Sec.~\ref{sec:data}).  Multi-differential neutrino measurements
on data reach three
dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}.

% Framing: RECORD-20260927-s5p-stage2-exit.md sec. 5 (the estimator's bias under G is part of the simulated null).
We evaluate this implementation as a case study of multidimensional unfolding
and of inference with a biased estimator.  The question is whether it gives
reliable multidimensional cross sections when the simulation mismodels the
truth by generator-sized amounts, and what validation is needed before its
outputs can be interpreted.  Two requirements are kept apart.  The coverage of
unfolded intervals is the rate at which they contain the true cross section.
The calibration of a test is the rate at which it rejects a true hypothesis;
it can be obtained without validated intervals, by simulating the whole
analysis chain at the hypothesis, so that the estimator's bias enters the
simulated null distribution, to the extent that the simulated chain
reproduces the analysis of the data.  Sections~\ref{sec:repro2d}--\ref{sec:limits}
ask whether the unfolded results and their intervals can be validated; these
validation studies are the paper's main results, and Table~\ref{tab:findings}
lists them with the configuration, test, and principal limitation of each.
As a secondary application, Sec.~\ref{sec:joint} tests specified generator
predictions, which the published muon-kinematic measurement already
disfavours~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, with tests calibrated within the implemented
simulation model.

\emph{Scope.}  The findings concern one OmniFold implementation on one data
set and the observables, configurations, and deformations tested, not
multidimensional OmniFold in general.  The paper does not deliver a validated
five-observable cross-section measurement: the three- to five-observable
central values are outputs of the estimators of Sec.~\ref{sec:method} under the
implemented observable definitions, simulated detector response, background
treatment, and regularization, and their intervals have no validated
coverage.  The generator tests are conditional tests of simulated hybrid
hypotheses, under which the cross section equals a prediction on every cell
of a fine five-observable grid, with the MINERvA Tune~v1 composition below it
(Sec.~\ref{sec:hypotheses}).  Under the assumptions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:calibration}, their decision rule controls the familywise error
rate of the ten decisions at 5\%, on average over the nuisance priors.  The
detector model has no calorimetric recoil-response uncertainty, and the
rejections are not physical exclusions of the generators and provide no
measurement uncertainty.  The companion analysis note~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}
gives the full validation record, the construction of the uncertainties, and
the joint tests, and is cited below by section.

\section{Data, observables, and domains}
\label{sec:data}

The analysis uses the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current
run periods, corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target and an
average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, from the
MINERvA open-data release of pre-selected analysis ntuples
(AnaTuples)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  The detector, including the downstream
MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}.  The files analysed are an earlier production of
that release (see Data availability).
% Fiducial box and muon limits: MINERvA101/MINERvA-101-Cross-Section/runEventLoopOmniFold.cpp:1708, :1717-1721,
% :1737-1740; background and fakes :1447-1456; selection :1710-1715; charge from MINOS curvature, CVUniverse.h:380-382.
% Counts: stageF/methods/M4-W-RECORD.md:68, :108 (two trees, so no subset is asserted).
The signal is a $\nu_{\mu}$ charged-current interaction, with any final state,
whose true vertex lies in the tracker fiducial volume ($5980<z<\SI{8422}{mm}$,
inside a hexagon of apothem \SI{850}{mm}) and whose muon has
$\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<\pPar<\SI{60}{GeV/c}$, and
$\pT\le\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$.  Selected simulated events outside this definition,
including charged-current $\nu_{\mu}$ events whose muon lies outside these
limits, are background.  The selection requires a reconstructed vertex in the
same volume and a reconstructed muon with $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$, matched to
a MINOS track of negative charge, with a dead-time requirement, as in
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}; the flux treatment follows
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn}.  After selection, the sample contains $4.12$
million selected data events.  The simulation contains $32.8$ million
unweighted true signal events and $20.5$ million selected simulated events; it
is scaled to the data exposure.
The simulated events are generated with GENIE~2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} and the MINERvA
Tune~v1, which reweights GENIE with a non-resonant pion reduction, a
random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil
two-particle--two-hole (2p2h) enhancement~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}.
Event selection is held fixed as the classifier inputs and unfolded observables
grow from $(\pT,\pPar)$ to $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_3,W)$; adding features does
not add selected events or relax the signal definition.

% :385-410 (truth W), :208-240 (reco q3, W). Truth q3: state/s5p/gen5d/README-gen5d-build.md:50-58.
% The 135 MeV constant: docs/EAVAIL_DEFINITION.md:128-141 and sec. 3 (439 of 65,911 events change truth bin, worst bin

The unfolded observables are defined at truth level.  Besides the muon
momentum components $\pT=p_{\mu}\sin\theta_{\mu}$ and
$\pPar=p_{\mu}\cos\theta_{\mu}$, they are the three-momentum transfer
$q_{3}=|\vec{q}\,|$ from the generator's four-vectors, the hadronic invariant
mass $W=\sqrt{M^{2}+2M(E_{\nu}-E_{\mu})-Q^{2}}$ with the struck-nucleon mass
$M$, and the available energy
\begin{equation}
  \Eavail=\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV})
  +\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma},
  \label{eq:eavail}
\end{equation}
with a defined constant of \SI{135}{MeV}, rather than the charged-pion mass,
in the charged-pion term; all other final-state particles, including neutrons,
contribute nothing.  The sums run over the generator's
final-state particles after final-state interactions, in which neutral pions
appear undecayed.  The \SI{135}{MeV} constant is inherited from the analysis
framework's definition; it defines the reported observable and is used
consistently by the analysis and every prediction.  Relative to the
charged-pion mass, in a simulated sample it moves \SI{0.67}{\percent} of
events into another true-$\Eavail$ bin and changes no true-$\Eavail$ bin by
more than \SI{1.05}{\percent}; its materiality has not been established,
because it has not been assessed against the covariance
(\nsec{sec:eavail-definition}).
Reconstructed $\Eavail$ is $1.17$ times the calorimetric recoil energy in the
tracker and electromagnetic calorimeter, with muon deposits removed;
reconstructed $q_{3}$ and $W$ instead combine the muon kinematics with a
separate calorimetric estimate $q_{0}$ of the energy transfer.

Reconstructed $W$ is an exact function of the reconstructed
$(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five reconstructed inputs share information and
do not, by themselves, provide five independent constraints: the result is
five simultaneous observables, not five independent measurements.  The
relation holds because
$W_{\mathrm{reco}}^{2}=(\bar M+q_{0})^{2}-q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}^{2}$, with
$\bar M$ an average nucleon mass, and $q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}$ increases with
$q_{0}$ at fixed muon kinematics; it is exact apart from the clamp at
$W^{2}=0$, which applies to \SI{1.5}{\percent} of simulated events.  Only
reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the other inputs; reconstructed $\Eavail$ comes
from its own calorimetric estimator and is not a function of them.  At truth
level, $\theta_{\mu}$, and with it $\pT$ and $\pPar$, is measured from a fixed
beam axis rather than from the true neutrino direction, so truth $W$ is fixed
by $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$ only up to that event-by-event angle, of about
\SI{1}{mrad}, and the choice of $M$; the residual is within \SI{\pm2}{MeV}
for 68\% of events (\nsec{sec:eavailw-definitions}).

The external predictions are the untuned GENIE~2.12.10 without (``GENIE CV'')
and with (``GENIE + MEC'') the Valencia 2p2h component~\cite{Nieves:2011pp},
which models meson-exchange currents (MEC), for CH;
NuWro~21.09~\cite{Golan:2012wx} and GiBUU~2019~\cite{Buss:2011mx}, for carbon.
All are per nucleon of their target.  The GENIE and NuWro predictions are
reweighted exactly, event by event, to the analysis flux, and the GiBUU sample
stops at $E_{\nu}\approx\SI{20}{GeV}$.  The predictions compute $W$ with the
neutron mass for every event, which changes their coarse-cell predictions by
at most about one Monte Carlo standard error in each cell where this was evaluated (it cannot be evaluated for GiBUU), and measure $\pT$ about their own neutrino axis, which differs from
the analysis truth $\pT$ by \SI{\pm4}{MeV} for 68\% of events
(Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}, Secs.~\ref*{N-sec:eavailw-axis}

Hydrogen has no quasielastic, 2p2h, or coherent $\nu_{\mu}$ charged-current
channel, so normalizing per nucleon does not remove the difference between
the targets: in GENIE's own CH sample, the carbon-only cross section per
nucleon exceeds that of CH by \SI{8.3}{\percent} for $W<\SI{1.1}{GeV}$
(\nsec{sec:eavailw-generators}).  The NuWro and GiBUU comparisons contain a
target difference of this kind, which the tests of Sec.~\ref{sec:joint} do not

\section{Estimators and validation design}
\label{sec:method}

Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification steps.  A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and
defines a likelihood-ratio weight.  The event pairing transfers that weight to
truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a
smooth reweighting to the next iteration.  Cross sections are formed by
histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and
dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width.  Events that fail
reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate efficiency
correction is applied.

Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the
classifier learns to match.  In the purity-based subtraction every data event in
reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight
$w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that
bin, so the target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$.  The unbinned
negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative
weight to the data, so the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise; a classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at
zero, converts the signed sample into non-negative weights before the first
iteration.  The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever
the background fraction varies within it (\napp{app:negweight}).

% Training details: stageF/methods/M-TRAINING-DETAILS.md (unbinned_unfolding/python/omnifold.py:203-272;
% nd-unfolding/omnifold_nn_core.py:145-155, :225, :249-266).
Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets,
with no held-out or cross-fitted split, no early stopping, and no class
balancing, and no effective sample size of the unfolded ensemble is computed
(\nsec{sec:method-algo}).

All estimator configurations use five OmniFold iterations with
gradient-boosted decision-tree classifiers~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn,Ke:2017lightgbm}, and each result
below states which configuration it uses (Table~\ref{tab:estimators}).  The
iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding.  No
hyperparameter search was performed on data: the nominal settings are
scikit-learn defaults, with LightGBM matched to their capacity, and the
refinement capacity of the joint-test estimator was chosen in simulation
closure studies.  The two- to five-observable central values use
configuration~(a), the central-value estimator, with the purity-based
subtraction, whose bias in simulation is given in Sec.~\ref{sec:closure}, and
were not recomputed with the refinement (\nsec{sec:method-config}); the
two-dimensional one is the central value of its systematic-universe ensemble,
so its covariance is built with the same estimator.  The joint tests use
configuration~(e), the joint-test estimator, whose negative-weight treatment
and higher-capacity refinement remove most of the bias of the purity method at
nominal truth in the highest-$W$ cells (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).  It keeps five
iterations under a rule fixed before the comparison, because 10, 15, or 20 did
not reduce the rule's measure of model dependence, the largest residual proxy
over the development truth changes (\nsec{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}).

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known
simulated truth is recovered, with the prior, the initial truth distribution,
held fixed.  Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated
experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together; the
coverage tested here is pointwise, for each reported quantity separately, not
the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them.  The closure, deformation, and coverage
studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data,
which changes their reconstructed and truth distributions together and keeps
the detector response at fixed truth; the prior used to unfold them stays at
Tune~v1.  The envelope of Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status} instead reweights
the prior used to unfold the data.  Every study uses the analysis
simulation's detector response, except the calibration of the tests, which
also draws the detector bands.

\section{Two-dimensional benchmark}
\label{sec:repro2d}

\subsection{Comparison with the published measurement}
\label{sec:repro2d-pub}

% chi2: receipts/2d-lgbm42-20261011/tension_report.txt:13 (rank); publication/release/figs/fig_numbers.py:42-43 (pinv).
% Shape not normalization: note sec_results.tex sec:results-tension. Learner difference: receipts/2d-lgbm42-20261011/
% lgbm_central_metrics.out, row CV42_universe_file (median 0.983%, maximum 12.18%).
The two-dimensional result is an end-to-end test of the unfolding against a
measurement from the same data.  Its integrated cross section is
\SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon}, compared with \SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon} in
the published analysis, a ratio of $\ratioTot$.  Of the 205 reported bins,
\SI{\binsTen}{\percent} agree within \SI{10}{\percent}; the standardized bin
differences formed with the published uncertainties have an RMS of
$\pullRMS$.  The covariance-weighted distance against the full published
covariance is $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiPaper$, computed with the
pseudo-inverse of the published covariance over the 205 bins, which keeps all
205 eigenmodes (rank 205, condition number $1.5\times10^{12}$), so that ndf is
that rank; we use it only as a descriptive distance.  It indicates a
correlated shape difference, not a normalization difference, spread over many eigenmodes of the
published covariance, with no single $\pT$ or $\pPar$ band carrying more than
\SI{15}{\percent} of it; its source has not been identified.  The distance
depends on the learner: ten LightGBM training seeds give 2.64--2.75, and the
exact-split estimator gives $\chiPaperExact$ unseeded and
$\chiPaperExactSeeded$ in one run with a fixed seed (both learners:

\subsection{Coverage}
\label{sec:coverage}

Statistical intervals from configuration~(c), corrected for the estimator's
closure bias at nominal truth, miscover at the tested truths, most strongly
when the true signal rate is lowered by about \SI{10}{\percent}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}).  The end-to-end test unfolds
400 pseudo-experiments per truth.  The tested change reweights the true signal,
without renormalization and with the background unchanged, and is in effect a

Under that change, 51 of the 153 reported cell and projection integrals have a

\nsec{sec:coverage-5d}).

A diagnosis of the stored pseudo-experiments attributes the miscoverage to a
residual bias, growing with $W$, that the nominal-truth bias correction does
not remove: the rate change itself is recovered, and the intervals are not,
apart from a minor effect, too narrow.  The per-quantity bias and spread are
inferred from a Gaussian fit to each quantity's 68\% and 95\% coverage, not
measured directly, and the source of the bias is not established
(\nsec{sec:coverage-5d}).  No frequentist coverage is therefore claimed for
the five-observable intervals.  Agreement in the marginal normalizations thus
coexists with substantial residual bias under the tested deformations.

\subsection{Covariance and estimator stability}
\label{sec:cov5d}

% Status: note sec_5d.tex sec:eavailw-adopted (DECISION-20260920-joseph-adopts-z-cv-under-the-6.4-exception.md sec. 2).
% Seed movement: \sprojMeasured = 6.145 (values.tex), one seed pair; \pinnedBandsSqrtTr (values.tex); note sec_5d.tex,
% the list under sec:eavailw-adopted.
The covariance of the five-observable central value, which has a
\zprojCells-cell $(\Eavail,W)$ projection, does not meet the validation
criteria of its specification (\nsec{sec:eavailw-construction}).  We use it,
and no other five-observable covariance, for the comparisons of
Sec.~\ref{sec:departure}; it is separate from the calibration of the joint
tests (Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}).  We quote no covariance-based generator
significance, because covariances from this estimator depend measurably on
its random seed: in two further covariances built at two seed settings,
changing the seed moves the projected uncertainty by about
\SI{6.1}{\percent}, against a stability limit of \SI{\sprojBound}{\percent}.
This rests on one seed pair, and the spread over seed pairs has not been
measured; five bands built at a fixed seed, which carry
\SI{\pinnedBandsSqrtTr}{\percent} of $\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}\,C}$, cannot vary with
it, so their seed sensitivity, which could move the total in either direction,
is also unmeasured.

% OUTCOME-20260926-s5e-oi192-diagnosis-and-candidate.md:81, :90-93 (bounds 0.3/1.0; baseline 0.80 median, 2.48 max;
% the failure belongs to the estimator family); note sec_5d.tex sec:val-departure.
The estimators are also unstable at the level of rounding.  Rounding-level
input changes move the results of the higher-capacity refinement by up to 2.6
statistical standard deviations, as they move those of the baseline estimator
(2.5), so the refinement fails a data-stability requirement of at most 0.3
(median) and 1.0 (maximum) statistical standard deviations.  For the joint-test estimator this spread
lies inside the data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty, but it limits how
exactly the central values can be reproduced.

\subsection{Reporting definitions and the precision target}
\label{sec:uncertainty-status}

% Source: note sec_5d.tex sec:gbdt-model-dependence ("Reporting definitions for a five-dimensional measurement":
% medians 12.0, 9.6, 13.4; the 11.0% and the linear rule); RECORD-20260927-s5p-stage2-exit.md:96-106, :124-136 (VL155).
None of the tested reporting definitions of a five-observable measurement
meets our \SI{10}{\percent} precision target under the specified envelope
prescription.  A dedicated study searched for a definition whose total
uncertainties meet such targets.  The envelope of a cell is the largest shift
of its data result when the simulation's prior is reweighted to each of five
generator-sized changes of the truth; it is a sensitivity diagnostic that
tracks the regularization bias, not an interval, and the study adds it
linearly to the probabilistic half-width.  With the joint-test estimator, the
envelope alone exceeds the target, a \SI{10}{\percent} median half-width for
the total 68\% interval, for the \jtNcells{} coarse joint cells and for the
fallback definition, the 39 supported $(\Eavail,W)$ cells; for a coarser,
non-nested partition of all five observables into 27 cells it does not, but
the \SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization that every probabilistic half-width
contains lifts the median above it (\nsec{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}).

Over these definitions the envelope has a median of about 10--13\% per cell,
comparable to the generator differences that such a measurement would need
to resolve.  It motivates the different route to inference of
Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}: after a descriptive comparison of the central values,
the generator predictions are tested as hypotheses.

\section{What limits the estimator}
\label{sec:limits}

% The common thread of Secs. V B, V C, V E and VII E in the body's own words; the coverage failure's source is not

Three results point to the unfolded result retaining part of its prior: the
residual under a generator-sized change of the truth
(Sec.~\ref{sec:departure}), which we interpret as regularization bias; the
envelope, which tracks the same bias and alone exceeds the precision target in
two of the three tested reporting definitions (Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status});
and the upward displacement of the external nulls by the estimator's pull
toward its prior, which the calibration reproduces
(Sec.~\ref{sec:joint-results}).  The miscoverage of Sec.~\ref{sec:coverage}
is also attributed to a residual bias, inferred from the stored
pseudo-experiments and not removed by the nominal-truth correction; whether it
has the same origin is not established.

% with a reconstruction-level test was computed. Moved here from Sec. VIII (outside-model ask, xmodel r10/r11).
Unfolded results can be compared with any prediction at truth level without
simulating each generator through the detector, which is what makes the
two-dimensional result reusable and the higher-dimensional central values
usable for qualitative comparison.  For hypothesis tests the advantage is
smaller.  A test at reconstruction level would avoid the regularization bias,
but it would still need the same response model: each external prediction
would have to be folded through the analysis simulation's response, which is
the hybrid construction of Sec.~\ref{sec:hypotheses}, with the same
composition below the fine grid.  The tests of this section instead calibrate
the unfolded statistic in simulation, so that the bias enters the null
distribution rather than the decision.  We have not compared their power with
that of a reconstruction-level test.

\subsection{Hypotheses and calibration}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

% H0(G): stageF/methods/M2-AUDIT.md Q1 text fix (:46, as corrected by M2-ARCHIVE-CHECKS.md:6-8). Code:
% nd-unfolding/s5p_nullexp.py:299, :158-174, :215, :213; s5n_pseudo.py:135-142.
For each of five predictions $G$ (the MINERvA Tune~v1, GENIE CV, GENIE + MEC,
NuWro~21.09, and GiBUU~2019), the null hypothesis $H_{0}(G)$ is that the true
cross section equals $G$ on every cell of the fine grid of
Sec.~\ref{sec:data}.  Below that grid, the null truth has the MINERvA
Tune~v1 simulation's event composition, varied in each pseudo-experiment by
the drawn interaction-model universes with every fine-cell integral held fixed,
and the background is the Tune~v1 simulation's, with its uncertainty bands.
We call this hypothesis the hybrid null: it is the hypothesis the calibration
simulates.  Each prediction is tested in total and in shape on the supported
coarse cells of Sec.~\ref{sec:data}.  Three requirements are distinct: the
hybrid null is defined exactly on the finite grid, with no convergence needed;
whether it represents the physical generator prediction depends on the
composition below the fine grid and on convergence of that grid
(condition~(i) below); and calibration fidelity is whether the calibration
reproduces the analysis of the data (conditions~(ii)--(iv)).

% Generative model: M2-AUDIT.md:10-33 (Q1); code at 19b46af9, byte-identical to 4f5a613f (M2-ARCHIVE-CHECKS.md:26).
% Data-derived lateral shifts: M2-AUDIT.md:182-184 (Q4d); s5p_joint.py:108, :118. Element-by-element list and
% flowchart: note sec_joint.tex sec:joint5d-calibration (tab:joint5d-genmodel).
Each calibration pseudo-experiment splits the simulation at random into two
halves.  The source half is reweighted per fine cell to $G$ and carries the
flux, interaction-model, and detector nuisance draws; the interaction-model
bands are renormalized within each fine truth cell on the signal, so that
they move only the composition within the cell, and with it the efficiency
and the migrations, and the lateral detector bands enter as
$\sum_{b}z_{b}\Delta_{b}$, with $\Delta_{b}$ from endpoint unfoldings of the
data.  Poisson pseudo-data and background are drawn from it, and the
joint-test estimator unfolds the pseudo-data with the other half; a
\SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization, the data's rounding noise, and the
prediction's finite-sample residual are then added.  The data are unfolded
once, with the full simulation.  Because the $\Delta_{b}$ come from the
observed data, the null distributions are conditional on these data-derived
inputs (\nsec{sec:joint5d-calibration}).

% Statistics: nd-unfolding/s5p_inference.py:35-49; s5p_joint.py:147-162 (W = V + diag Var(mu)), :61-80 (diagonal).
% 82 of 109 eigenvalues below lambda (M2-AUDIT.md Q4a, :159-161, as corrected by M2-ARCHIVE-CHECKS.md:7, :92-97).
A total statistic $T_{\mathrm{tot}}=r^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}r$ is formed from
the residual $r=F-\mu_{G}$ of the unfolded cell values $F$ and the prediction
$\mu_{G}$; it tests the rate and the shape together, and we call it the total
(rate-and-shape) test.  A shape statistic $T_{\mathrm{sh}}$ of the same form
uses the normalized cell fractions, with one cell dropped.  The metric
$\Sigma=V+\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ adds the prediction's own
finite-sample variance to $V$, a Ledoit--Wolf-shrunk
covariance~\cite{LedoitWolf:2004} of \jtVn{} pilot pseudo-experiments at the
Tune~v1 null, whose shrinkage floors about 80 of the 109 nearly degenerate
correlation directions.  The metric influences each test's power; the null
calibration determines its size.

\subsection{Decision rule and familywise error}
\label{sec:calibration}

% Variants c in {0, 1/2, 1} D and +-2 delta_fine: nd-unfolding/s5p_joint.py:228-258 (M2-AUDIT.md:61).
The Monte Carlo $p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$
of $B$ null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic; it is never zero.
Each decision uses the largest $p$ value over a set of shifted nulls, which
add a fixed vector to every null draw: zero, half, or all of an upper bound $D$
on the simulation-size difference along the null's bias direction
(condition~(iv) below), or $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$, where
$\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ is the change of the noise-free unfolded result in the
last step of a refinement of the null truth's grid (condition~(i);
\nsec{sec:joint5d-calibration}).

% Rule and bound: stageF/methods/M2-FWER-RECORD.md:49-52, :56-66, :69-74 (provisional, J4); stopping rule
% nd-unfolding/s5p_inference.py:89-155. Derivation: note app_statmethods_nd.tex sec:joint5d-fwer.
The ten tests are controlled at a familywise level $\alpha=\jtAlpha$ by the
Holm procedure~\cite{Holm:1979}, with pseudo-experiments drawn sequentially,
to at most \jtBcap{} per null.  A hypothesis is rejected only when the upper
end of the two-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval~\cite{Clopper:1934} of its
exceedance fraction lies below its Holm threshold.  This rule rests on two
assumptions: each true null's calibrated tail probability is uniformly
distributed or conservative (A1), and the pseudo-experiments' exceedances are
independent given the data (A2).  Under them, the probability of meeting the
criterion at any number of pseudo-experiments up to \jtBcap{} is below the
threshold, whatever rule determines when sampling stops, so the decision rule
controls the familywise error rate of the ten decisions at 5\%
(\napp{sec:joint5d-fwer}).  The calibrated size is averaged over the nuisance
priors: at a fixed true value of the nuisance parameters it can exceed
$\alpha$.  The point estimates $(k+1)/(B+1)$ carry no separate validity claim.

% Design and its data-informed elements: amendment 7 data_informed_disclosure; DECISION-20261004-s5p-production-budget-
% extension.md; note sec_joint.tex sec:joint5d-frozen. Lost pseudo-experiments: RECORD-20261005-s5p-joint-5d-inference-
% result.md sec. 5; RECORD-20261006-s5p-lost-seed-recovery-resolution.md (b354cdf7); note sec_joint.tex sec:joint5d-lost.
% Recomputation: REPORT-20261005-s5p-recompute-final-verification.md sec. 8.
The test design was fixed before any observed statistic was computed; its
reviewers had seen
descriptive per-cell residuals of the data before it was fixed, and a bounded extension of the
calibration's computing budget was decided with observed $p$ values visible,
which the result above covers, since it holds for any stopping rule
(\nsec{sec:joint5d-frozen}).  Computing time limits interrupted or prevented \jtLostCal{} calibration and
\jtLostPow{} power pseudo-experiments; when rerun, none of them reaches the observed
statistic in any test or shifted null, so no assignment of the lost outcomes
changes any decision (\nsec{sec:joint5d-lost}).  Independently written code reproduces every $p$ value and decision.

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:conditions}

The decisions test the hybrid null and hold under six conditions on the
nuisance model, numbered as in \nsec{sec:joint5d-conditions}.

% (i): \jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax (values_inference.tex); amendment 7 claims.rejection; note sec_joint.tex
% sec:joint5d-conditions (the design-review rate, whose calculation is not recorded, and the geometric extrapolation).
\emph{(i) Convergence below the fine grid.}  Within each fine-grid cell the
null truth has the Tune~v1 composition of the hypothesis definition, and
convergence of the fine grid has not been shown: in the $L_{2}$ norm of the
per-cell relative changes, the last refinement step is
\jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax{} of the whole change from the coarse test grid, so the
residual below the fine grid is unmeasured and potentially material.  The
shifts $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ were set by assuming that the
refinement steps shrink geometrically; the measured $L_{2}$ ratios also allow
slower convergence.  The rejections carry over from the simulated hybrid null
to $G$ itself only if replacing the Tune~v1's shape below the fine grid by
$G$'s moves the noise-free unfolded result by no more than
$\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ along $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$.

% (ii): the 10% response uncertainty, MINERvA:2022incl (note sec_joint.tex sec:joint5d-response).
\emph{(ii) Detector and hadronic response.}\label{sec:response}  The
detector model is the simulation's, varied only by the drawn detector bands and
a linear surrogate for the lateral bands.  It has no band for the calorimetric
hadronic-energy response, whose uncertainty in MINERvA's recoil-energy analyses
is derived from test-beam calorimetry~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam} and rises to
\SI{10}{\percent} at the highest three-momentum transfers of the low-recoil
measurement~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl}.  A response mismodelling could move each
observed statistic in either direction, and a band drawn in the calibration
would also broaden and could shift each null distribution; the net effect on
each $p$ value has not been measured.  This matters for the rejections: the
calibrated tests restricted to the matched $(\pT,\pPar)$ projection
(Sec.~\ref{sec:joint-results}) do not reject GENIE~2.12.10 CV
($p=\woneGcvPtTot$ in total, $\woneGcvPtShape$ in shape; a post hoc
observation), so the information
that rejects it involves the hadronic axes, on which a response band would act.
% Recoil sensitivity: publication/w2/W2B-REPORT-20261006.md (940d84aa) and its 2026-10-08 addendum (the "about 11%":
% max |T_shifted/T_frozen - 1| = 0.1095); independent check REPORT-20261006-s5p-recompute-w2-recoil-check.md (9ffc4ddd).
In an exploratory sensitivity study, scaling the simulation's reconstructed
recoil energy by $\pm\SI{4}{\percent}$, the level of data--simulation agreement
reported for test-beam protons, pions, and electrons of
0.35--\SI{2.0}{GeV/c}~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam}, and re-unfolding the data
leaves every rejection in place against the original null distributions, with
the observed statistics moving by up to about \SI{11}{\percent}.  Neutrons
and higher-momentum hadrons, which matter at high $W$, lie outside the
test-beam sample; the study regenerates no null distribution and tests no
other form or size of response change (untested effects:
\nsec{sec:joint5d-response}).  The rejections therefore remain
conditional on the simulation's hadronic response.

\emph{(iii) Interaction-model prior.}  The prior is the
analysis's \jtModelBands-band set, with negative weights of two bands clipped.

% (iv): M2-AUDIT.md Q4b, Q4c, Q4e, :165-193; SF1-REPORT.md:5, :23-35 (all ten decisions rejected; NOT CITABLE FOR the
% data process, :9). Margins: state/measurement-paper/phase1/a7margins/RECEIPT.md:65-78 (descriptive, not a re-grade).
\emph{(iv) Simulation size.}  The pseudo-experiments use half of the
simulation and the data all of it; the resulting shift, at most about 0.07
null standard deviations, enters as the upper-bound shift $D$; it was measured for the mean only, without
noise or nuisance draws.  Repeating part
of the calibration with a quarter of the simulation resolved no change of the
null distributions, and under an extrapolation to the full simulation that is
conservative by design all ten decisions remain rejected.  The two decisions
closest to their boundary exceed the value at which they would change by 1.11
and 1.30 null standard deviations (all margins: \nsec{sec:joint5d-conditions}).

\emph{(v), (vi) Domain and prediction samples.}  GiBUU lacks
$E_{\nu}>\SI{20}{GeV}$, and its out-of-domain truth enters the migrations into
its domain.  The predictions' own finite samples enter the metric and the
simulated residuals.

% Calibration validity: M2-AUDIT.md Q3, Q4e, :187-193; RECORD-20261005 sec. 4 (the recomputation "does not establish
% the calibration's adequacy").
The calibration is valid for the simulated null to the extent that the
pseudo-experiment chain reproduces the analysis applied to the data, which is
assumption A1.  The simulation-size check of condition~(iv) is not a test of
the data process: every pseudo-experiment resamples the same simulated events,
so the null distributions are conditional on that simulation, and their
conditioning on the data-derived lateral shifts has not been validated.  The
independent recomputation verifies the calculation, not whether the chain
reproduces the data analysis, and no rejection rate has been measured for null
pseudo-data processed like the data, which would require an independent
simulation sample.

The methodological contribution of these comparisons is a calibration,
within the implemented simulation model, that puts the estimator's bias
toward its prior and the nuisance terms into each null distribution; the
disagreements they recover were already established by the published
two-dimensional measurement on the same data~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.
Restricting the same calibrated tests to matched $3\times3$ coarse projections
of the same cells, onto $(\pT,\pPar)$ and onto $(\Eavail,W)$, gives $p<0.05$,
without a multiplicity correction, for every test in at least one of the two
projections, so this study provides no evidence of additional discrimination
from the joint five-observable information (\nsec{sec:joint5d-projections}).

discrepancy to a kinematic region or nuclear mechanism would require separate
tests.  Power studies characterize the response to chosen alternatives: at the

power 1.0 at the 0.005 level, and the third, a MEC-like $(\Eavail,W)$ shape,
with 0.23; at the GENIE~2.12.10 null the shape powers are 0.74, 0.02, and 0.00

\section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

% The scope clause is the one stated in Sec. I; each limitation prints with its numbers at its claim (Secs. IV-VII).
Within the scope stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}, the study's results are its
validation findings, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:findings}.  The
two-dimensional result depends on the learner at the level of one unit of
$\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$, and its quoted statistical block has had no coverage
test.  The higher-dimensional central values keep the
purity-based subtraction and its bias of up to \SI{4}{\percent} in the
highest-$W$ cells, and their intervals are not validated.
Section~\ref{sec:limits} relates three of these results to the unfolded
result retaining part of its prior.

% The familywise bound conditioned on the calibration's exactness: M2-FWER-RECORD.md:11-14, :58-60.
The calibrated tests illustrate inference with a biased estimator: the
decision rule controls the familywise error rate at 5\% provided the
calibration is exact, and the physical interpretation of the rejections,
which depends on the simulation's calorimetric recoil response and on the
assumed convergence below the fine grid, would require a response band drawn
through the calibration and a demonstrated bound on the residual below the
fine grid.
