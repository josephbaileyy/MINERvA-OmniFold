\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% r5:13, unchanged.
Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon and hadronic observables. Their interpretation requires corrections for detector response and an assessment of the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but its response tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}. This allows simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding without a separate binned response tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been applied to collider data, for example to eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and to twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets} (see ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.

% r5:16, with its last sentence replaced by the three findings (ledger L-01 to L-03; W-2).
This paper is a benchmark and diagnostic study of a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}, in the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta $\pT$ and $\pPar$, the available hadronic energy $\Eavail$, the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and the hadronic invariant mass $W$. Because reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, the five observables are unfolded simultaneously but are not independent. The event weights allow the same unfolded sample to be histogrammed in projections or cells chosen afterwards. An uncertainty for a new choice must be constructed and checked for that choice, and a practical gain over binned unfolding remains to be shown.

The findings apply to one OmniFold implementation on one data set, for the observables, configurations and deformations tested. The sixteen-cell cross section that the estimator gives on the data illustrates the method and is not a measurement. The supplementary material gives it with its covariance and descriptive comparisons with generator predictions (\ssec{S9}{supp:output}).

We report three findings and the tests supporting them, including tests that failed (table~\ref{tab:outcomes}). First, a benchmark against the published double-differential cross section $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$ from the same data~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} shows agreement in normalization and a correlated shape difference (section~\ref{sec:bench}). On fixed inputs, a binned unfolding lies closer to the published shape, and the mechanism of the difference has not been identified. Second, controlled changes of the simulated truth show that the five-observable result retains part of its prior on the sixteen reporting cells, while individual $(\Eavail,W)$ cells can move opposite to a generator-sized change or overshoot it (section~\ref{sec:prior}). Third, the estimator is reproducible but numerically unstable, and the calibration of its uncertainties has not been validated: these are distinct properties (section~\ref{sec:stability}).

\label{sec:data}

The signal is charged-current $\nu_\mu$ interactions in the central-tracker fiducial volume with $\theta_\mu<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<\pPar<\SI{60}{GeV/c}$ and $\pT<\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$, the phase space of ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. The selection is given in \ssec{S1}{supp:selection}. The data come from the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current run periods, or playlists (1A--1G and 1L--1P), corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target, with an average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. We use pre-selected analysis ntuples from an earlier production of the MINERvA open-data release (see Data availability)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}. Only playlist 1A predates the current release, and it was checked against that release. The check compares the selected samples of the two productions. It does not compare these inputs with those of the published analysis (\ssec{S1}{supp:selection}). For the run-period studies the playlists form three groups: 1A--1F, 1G--1M (the playlists 1G, 1L and 1M) and 1N--1P. Momenta are given in GeV/$c$, energies in GeV, and cross sections per nucleon.

The unfolded observables are defined at truth level, with muon momentum components $\pT=p_{\mu}\sin\theta_{\mu}$ and $\pPar=p_{\mu}\cos\theta_{\mu}$. The magnitude of the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}=|\vec{q}|$ comes from the generator's four-vectors, and the hadronic invariant mass $W=\sqrt{M^{2}+2M(E_{\nu}-E_{\mu})-Q^{2}}$ uses the struck-nucleon mass $M$, with $Q^{2}$ computed for a massless muon at its angle from the fixed beam axis, whereas $q_{3}$ uses the exact four-vectors (\ssecc{S2}{supp:observables}). We define the available energy as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Eavail={}&\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV})\\
&+\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma},
\end{split}
\label{eq:eavail}
\end{equation}
% r5:75, its first five sentences and last two; the comparison with published definitions and the first-order effect of the constant moved to S2, which carries both (supplemental_body.tex sec. S2).
which subtracts a defined constant of \SI{135}{MeV}, rather than the charged-pion mass, in the charged-pion term. The constant defines the reported observable and is used consistently by the analysis and every prediction. The sums run over the generator's final-state particles after final-state interactions, where neutral pions appear undecayed. All other final-state particles, including neutrons, contribute nothing. No single published definition of $\Eavail$ exists. The supplementary material compares eq.~(\ref{eq:eavail}) with the definitions in use and gives the effect of the constant on the result (\ssecc{S2}{supp:observables}). Reconstructed $\Eavail$ is $1.17$ times the calorimetric recoil energy in the tracker and electromagnetic calorimeter, with muon deposits removed. Reconstructed $q_{3}$ and $W$ instead combine the muon kinematics with a separate calorimetric estimate $q_{0}$ of the energy transfer.

Reconstructed $W$ is an exact function of the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, apart from a clamp at $W^{2}=0$, because $W_{\mathrm{reco}}^{2}=(\bar M+q_{0})^{2}-q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}^{2}$, with $\bar M$ an average nucleon mass, and $q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}$ increases with $q_{0}$ at fixed muon kinematics. The five reconstructed inputs therefore share information and do not provide five independent constraints. Only reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the others. Reconstructed $\Eavail$ comes from its own calorimetric estimator and is not a function of them. The definitions, and the truth-level relation among the observables, are detailed in \ssecc{S2}{supp:observables}.

The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn,Ke:2017lightgbm} of nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps, and negative-weight background subtraction with a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves. Results from the two estimators are not combined: the two-dimensional benchmark uses one, and the five-observable results use the other (table~\ref{tab:estimators}).

The two-observable estimator, LightGBM~\cite{Ke:2017lightgbm} at a fixed seed, gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance (section~\ref{sec:repro2d}). We quote its result as the central value because its covariance uses the same estimator: its systematic-universe ensemble is built around that central value. The five-observable results use the five-observable estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with the same background treatment and refinement capacity. The note's calibrated joint tests of generator predictions also use it (\ssec{S8.7}{supp:joint}). Its seeds are fixed, and its training is reproducible. The five-observable estimator is the baseline this study characterizes (configuration~(e) of \ssecc{S4}{supp:estimators}): the stability probe and the ensemble average of section~\ref{sec:cov5d} were tested against it, and neither replaced it.

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The pairing of reconstructed and true events transfers that weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Because events that fail reconstruction enter as truth events without a reconstructed partner, no separate efficiency correction is applied. Their step-1 weight comes from a regressor of that weight on the truth coordinates, fitted with equal event weights to the events that have a partner: it estimates the mean weight at fixed truth over simulated events, not over their physical weights. Events without a reconstructed partner carry \SIrange{\fvval{ess-nrp-min}}{\fvval{ess-nrp-max}}{\percent} of a cell's final weight (\ssecc{S4}{supp:estimators}). The cross section in a bin of the observables $x_1,\dots,x_n$ is
\begin{equation}
\frac{d^{n}\sigma}{dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}}=\frac{U}{c\,\Phi\,N_{\mathrm{T}}\,P\,\Delta x_{1}\cdots\Delta x_{n}},
\label{eq:xsec}
\end{equation}
with $U$ the unfolded yield, the sum of the final weights of the simulated truth events in the bin. The completeness $c$ is the fraction of the bin's simulated truth-level signal that the unfolding input contains. It is 1 in every bin, because every truth event without a reconstructed partner is appended to the input, and it is computed as a check (\nsec{sec:method-xsec}). $\Phi$ is the integrated flux per unit area and per proton on target, $N_{\mathrm{T}}$ the number of nucleons in the fiducial volume, $P$ the data exposure in protons on target, and $\Delta x_i$ the bin widths.

Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the classifier learns to match. In the purity-based subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight $w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that bin. The classifier's target density is then $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$. The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative weight to the data. The target is then $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise. A classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at zero, converts the signed sample to non-negative weights before the first iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction varies within it (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}). With the refinement classifier, the negative-weight treatment removes most of the bias of purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-$W$ cells (\nsec{sec:val-negweight}).

% r5:95, its first two sentences and its last three, with the iteration count's rule in one sentence (the editor's B1); the nominal-setting, refinement-capacity and two-observable iteration checks moved to S4 with r5:98's detail.
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding. No learner setting was tuned on data, including capacity, learning rate, refinement capacity and iteration count. The five-observable estimator uses five iterations because 10, 15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model dependence for the truth changes used in its development (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}). Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, without a held-out or cross-fitted split or early stopping. The two classes retain their physical weights (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}). Two choices of method for the two-dimensional result are described with its benchmark (section~\ref{sec:repro2d}).

\subsection{The reported covariances}
\label{sec:cov16}

% r5:118, with the generator-comparison clauses moved to S10 and the block's run-period numbers kept (ledger L-07).
We report covariances for the sixteen-cell and two-dimensional results. The sixteen-cell covariance is
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{rep}}=C_{\mathrm{total}}+C_{\mathrm{resp}}+C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}},\qquad C_{\mathrm{total}}=C_{\mathrm{e}}+C_{\mathrm{model}}.
\label{eq:crep}
\end{equation} In eq.~(\ref{eq:crep}), $C_{\mathrm{e}}$ is the sum of the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization uncertainties of the five-observable estimator and its training-seed block, and $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ is the model term. The lateral uncertainties shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than reweighting events (\ssec{S9}{supp:output}). $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ is a recoil-response term and $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ a run-period term, each described below.

% Split from the paragraph above (the fresh referee read on 283f3044, RESPONSE.md row Gs4): the statistical block and its rounding perturbation.
Each bootstrap replica of the statistical block refits the whole unfolding with its own rounding-level perturbation of the inputs. The block therefore includes the estimator's sensitivity to such perturbations (section~\ref{sec:cov5d}). The perturbation moves each input by at most half a float32 unit in the last place and holds values on an edge of the analysis grid (section~\ref{sec:strategy}) fixed. The block estimates the spread of a randomized procedure, data resampling with a fresh rounding perturbation in each replica. For the deterministic reported output, it adds a numerical-variability term to the sampling variance. If the two variances add, the perturbation makes up a median \fvval{rdec-share-med}\,\% (\fvval{rdec-share-lo}--\fvval{rdec-share-hi}\,\%) of each sixteen-cell bootstrap variance. Without it, the reported uncertainty of any cell would be at most \fvval{rdec-rep-drop-max}\,\% smaller. Both figures are for the diagonal elements only.

In the weight universes, every simulated event's truth and reconstructed weights, and the truth denominator, take the universe's values. The events without a reconstructed partner therefore carry their universe weights too (\ssec{S5}{supp:cov16}).

The model term $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ is constructed from the change in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn, with GENIE CV as the common reference. It measures the result's unscaled dependence on the generator shape and is not calibrated as a bound. It is an implementation-specific shape sensitivity: its GiBUU shift, which carries \fvval{cmodel-trace-gibuu} of its trace, also contains the target difference of a carbon sample, that sample's energy truncation, a cut in $\pPar$ and the steps of the ratio's statistical gate (\ssec{S5}{supp:cov16}). The response term $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ is the response of the result to a one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil energy in the simulation. The scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and other deposits. $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ does not cover neutron or muon deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale.

% Split (#1025, group A item 3): the empirical run-period term.
The unfolded results of the three run-period groups, each relative to its own simulation, differ by more than their bootstrap scatter. The cause is unknown, and these data cannot establish which period is correct. The run-period block $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ includes this inconsistency in the reported covariance. The block was added after the differences were observed and is constructed from the double ratios among the groups: each group's unfolded result divided by its own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the reference. It is built to contain these differences. It has rank \fvval{pl-rank} and is not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains. It is an empirical sensitivity allowance, not an independently established uncertainty. With three groups its size is poorly determined. An illustrative interval on its size under a stated model is given in \ssec{S5}{supp:cov16}, and that interval describes no uncertainty in the block's orientation.

A closure test measures how well the unfolding recovers a specified deformation of the known simulated truth while holding the prior (the initial truth distribution) fixed. Coverage is the frequency with which intervals contain their target over repeated experiments. It tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The unfolding prior remains Tune~v1 throughout. Every study uses the analysis simulation's detector response.

Two findings indicate that the unfolded result retains part of its prior. The first is that, under the generator-sized changes of figure~\ref{fig:deformation}, the median unfolded $(\Eavail,W)$ cell moves by only \SIrange{\fvval{rec-w-fmed-lo}}{\fvval{rec-w-fmed-hi}}{\percent} of the injected change (section~\ref{sec:departure}). A study tests which settings of the five-observable estimator change the fraction of a truth change that remains unrecovered. It uses noise-free pseudo-data with backgrounds at one split of the simulation and measures the retained fraction $\rho=-\sum_c b_c d_c/\sum_c d_c^2$ at each graded truth. Here $d_c=(X_c-P_c)/P_c$ is the truth's departure from the prior in cell $c$ and $b_c=(\hat X_c-X_c)/P_c$ is the bias, with $\hat X$, $X$ and $P$ the unfolded, true and prior cross sections. The sums run over cells where $|d_c|$ is at least \SI{1}{\percent}. Then $\rho=1$ if the result is the prior and $\rho=0$ if the departure is recovered. The recovered fraction of section~\ref{sec:departure} is a different quantity: it is computed per $(\Eavail,W)$ cell and is 1 when the cell follows its truth change in full, whereas $\rho$ is one projection over the sixteen cells.

The study changes one setting at a time: the iteration count, classifier capacity, training statistics or observables used by the classifiers. It also runs an exact binned IBU on the same pseudo-data. A level removes a truth's bias flag if the truth is no longer flagged under the criterion of section~\ref{sec:strategy} applied to the noise-free pseudo-data.

The studies of sections~\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:stability} and of the companion note give five checks to make before the intervals of an unbinned unfolding are interpreted. The checks are general. The findings stated with them apply to this implementation.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Closure should include the backgrounds (section~\ref{sec:closure}).
\item Generator-sized truth changes should be recovered, and the retained part of the prior measured (sections~\ref{sec:departure} and~\ref{sec:prr}).

\end{enumerate}
