\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon and hadronic observables.  Their interpretation depends on correcting detector response and assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but the response tensor grows rapidly when additional observables are reported.  OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation \cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}.  This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned response tensor for every projection.  Unbinned unfolding has been applied to collider data, for example eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}; see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey.  In neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}.
% Register round r17 (S1): the state of the art on data, moved here from the next paragraph.

We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the MINERvA open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}. The result is a simultaneous charged-current inclusive cross section in five observables, the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta $\pT$ and $\pPar$, available hadronic energy $\Eavail$, three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and hadronic invariant mass $W$, reported in sixteen cells with its covariance (Sec.~\ref{sec:result16}) and compared with five generator predictions (Sec.~\ref{sec:gen16}). We first reproduce the published double-differential cross section in transverse and longitudinal muon momentum, $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, as an end-to-end test of the unfolding, and we also give three- and four-observable central values.  Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five observables are simultaneous but not independent.

The validation studies assess the result through the two-dimensional reproduction, background-subtraction tests, recovery of generator-sized truth changes, a coverage test and a fake-data study of estimator bias. They examine whether the unfolding gives reliable multidimensional cross sections when the simulation mismodels the truth by generator-sized amounts.  Two properties must be distinguished.  The coverage of unfolded intervals is the rate at which they contain the true cross section.  The calibration of a test is the rate at which it rejects a true hypothesis.  Calibration can be obtained without validated intervals by simulating the whole analysis chain at the hypothesis, so that the estimator's bias enters the simulated null distribution.  A calibration obtained in this way is valid to the extent that the simulated chain reproduces the analysis of the data.

\section{Data, simulation, and observables}
\label{sec:data}

The analysis uses the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current run periods, corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target and an average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, from an earlier production of the MINERvA open-data release of pre-selected analysis ntuples (AnaTuples)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData} (see Data availability). The detector, including the downstream MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}. The signal is a $\nu_{\mu}$ charged-current interaction, with any final state, whose true vertex lies in the tracker fiducial volume ($5980<z<\SI{8422}{mm}$, inside a hexagon of apothem \SI{850}{mm}) and whose muon has $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<\pPar<\SI{60}{GeV/c}$, and $\pT\le\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$.  Selected simulated events outside this definition, including charged-current $\nu_{\mu}$ events whose muon lies outside these limits, are background.  The selection requires a reconstructed vertex in the same volume and a reconstructed muon with $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$, matched to a MINOS track of negative charge, with a dead-time requirement, as in Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.  The flux treatment follows Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn}.  After selection, the sample contains $4.12$ million selected data events.  The simulation contains $32.8$ million unweighted true signal events and $20.5$ million selected simulated events. The simulation is scaled to the data exposure. The simulated events are generated with GENIE~2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} and the MINERvA Tune~v1, which reweights GENIE with a non-resonant pion reduction, a random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil two-particle--two-hole (2p2h) enhancement~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}. Event selection is held fixed as the classifier inputs and unfolded observables grow from $(\pT,\pPar)$ to $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_3,W)$.  Adding features does not add selected events or relax the signal definition.

\section{Cross-section extraction}
\label{sec:method}

Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification steps.  A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight.  The event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to the next iteration.  Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width.  Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate efficiency correction is applied.

Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the classifier learns to match.  In the purity-based subtraction every data event in reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight $w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that bin, so the target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$.  The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative weight to the data, so the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise; a classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at zero, converts the signed sample into non-negative weights before the first iteration.  The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction varies within it (\napp{app:negweight}).

% Training details: stageF/methods/M-TRAINING-DETAILS.md (unbinned_unfolding/python/omnifold.py:203-272; nd-unfolding/omnifold_nn_core.py:145-155, :225, :249-266).
Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, without a held-out or cross-fitted split, early stopping, or class balancing; the training details are in \nsec{sec:method-algo}.

The refinement capacity of the joint-test estimator was chosen in simulation closure studies.  No hyperparameter search was performed on data.

\subsection{Reporting definitions and the precision target}
\label{sec:uncertainty-status}

% Source: note sec_5d.tex sec:gbdt-model-dependence ("Reporting definitions for a five-dimensional measurement": medians 12.0, 9.6, 13.4; the 11.0% and the linear rule); RECORD-20260927-s5p-stage2-exit.md:96-106, :124-136 (VL155). Register round r15 (R9): the envelope is defined before the result that depends on it. Register round r17 (R9, R10): the precision target is also defined before the verdict.
To include model dependence, a dedicated study defines the envelope of a cell as the largest shift of its data result when the simulation's prior is reweighted to each of five generator-sized changes of the truth, and adds it linearly to the probabilistic half-width.  The envelope is a sensitivity diagnostic that tracks the regularization bias, not an interval.  Our precision target is a \SI{10}{\percent} median half-width for the total 68\% interval.  With the envelope added, none of the reporting definitions of a five-observable measurement tested in the study meets this target.  With the joint-test estimator, the envelope alone exceeds it for the \jtNcells{} coarse joint cells and for the fallback definition, the 39 supported $(\Eavail,W)$ cells.  For a coarser, non-nested partition of all five observables into 27 cells, the same estimator's envelope alone does not exceed it, but the \SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization contained in every probabilistic half-width lifts the median above it.  The study is described in \nsec{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}.

Over these definitions, the envelope has a median of about 10--13\% per cell, comparable to the generator differences that such a measurement would need to resolve.

\section{Uncertainties and validation}
\label{sec:validation}

% Sources: nd-unfolding/s5c_pseudo.py:125-128; s5n_pseudo.py:133-134, :146-151 (prior at CV); RECORD-20260927-s5p-stage2-exit.md:96-106 (envelope: the MC prior reweighted on data).
A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed.  Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together; the coverage tested here is pointwise, for each reported quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them.  The closure, deformation, and coverage studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed and truth distributions together and keeps the detector response at fixed truth; the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune~v1.  The envelope of Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status} instead reweights the prior used to unfold the data.  Every study uses the analysis simulation's detector response, except the calibration of the tests, which also draws the detector bands.

The source of this residual bias has not been established.  The rate change itself is recovered, and, apart from a minor effect, the intervals are not too narrow. \subsection{Hypotheses and calibration}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

% H0(G): stageF/methods/M2-AUDIT.md Q1 text fix (:46, as corrected by M2-ARCHIVE-CHECKS.md:6-8). Code: nd-unfolding/s5p_nullexp.py:299, :158-174, :215, :213; s5n_pseudo.py:135-142.
For each of five predictions $G$ (the MINERvA Tune~v1, GENIE CV, GENIE + MEC, NuWro~21.09, and GiBUU~2019), the null hypothesis $H_{0}(G)$ is that the true cross section equals $G$ on every cell of the fine grid of Sec.~\ref{sec:data}.  Below that grid, the null truth has the MINERvA Tune~v1 simulation's event composition, varied in each pseudo-experiment by the drawn interaction-model universes with every fine-cell integral held fixed. The background is the Tune~v1 simulation's, with its uncertainty bands. We call this hypothesis the hybrid null: it is the hypothesis the calibration simulates.  Each prediction is tested in total and in shape on the supported coarse cells of Sec.~\ref{sec:data}.  Three properties are distinct.  The hybrid null is defined exactly on the finite grid and needs no convergence. Whether it represents the physical generator prediction depends on the composition below the fine grid and on convergence of that grid (condition~(i) below).  Calibration fidelity is whether the calibration reproduces the analysis of the data (conditions~(ii)--(iv)).

Each calibration pseudo-experiment splits the simulation at random into two halves.  The source half is reweighted per fine cell to $G$ and carries the flux, interaction-model, and detector nuisance draws.  The interaction-model bands are renormalized within each fine truth cell on the signal, so that they move only the composition within the cell, and with it the efficiency and the migrations. \subsection{Decision rule and familywise error}
\label{sec:calibration}

% Variants c in {0, 1/2, 1} D and +-2 delta_fine: nd-unfolding/s5p_joint.py:228-258 (M2-AUDIT.md:61).
The Monte Carlo $p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$ of $B$ null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic; it is never zero. Each decision uses the largest $p$ value over a set of shifted nulls, which add a fixed vector to every null draw: zero, half, or all of an upper bound $D$ on the simulation-size difference along the null's bias direction (condition~(iv) below), or $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$, where $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ is the change of the noise-free unfolded result in the last step of a refinement of the null truth's grid (condition~(i)).

% Rule and bound: stageF/methods/M2-FWER-RECORD.md:49-52, :56-66, :69-74 (provisional, J4); stopping rule nd-unfolding/s5p_inference.py:89-155. Derivation: note app_statmethods_nd.tex sec:joint5d-fwer.
The ten tests are controlled at a familywise level $\alpha=\jtAlpha$ by the Holm procedure~\cite{Holm:1979}, with pseudo-experiments drawn sequentially, to at most \jtBcap{} per null.  A hypothesis is rejected only when the upper end of the two-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval~\cite{Clopper:1934} of its exceedance fraction lies below its Holm threshold.  This rule rests on two assumptions: each true null's calibrated tail probability is uniformly distributed or conservative (A1), and the pseudo-experiments' exceedances are independent given the data (A2).  Under them, the probability of meeting the criterion at any number of pseudo-experiments up to \jtBcap{} is below the threshold, whatever rule determines when sampling stops, so the decision rule controls the familywise error rate of the ten decisions at 5\% (\napp{sec:joint5d-fwer}).  The calibrated size is averaged over the nuisance priors: at a fixed true value of the nuisance parameters it can exceed $\alpha$.  The error control applies to the decisions, not separately to the point estimates $(k+1)/(B+1)$.  The calibration is valid for the simulated null to the extent that the pseudo-experiment chain reproduces the analysis of the data (A1).  Every pseudo-experiment resamples the same simulated events, so measuring a rejection rate for null pseudo-data processed like the data would require an independent simulation sample.  No such rate has been measured.

The design and the rerun are described in Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}, Secs.~\ref*{N-sec:joint5d-frozen} and~\ref*{N-sec:joint5d-lost}.

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:conditions}

The decisions rest on six conditions on the nuisance model, numbered as in \nsec{sec:joint5d-conditions}.

% (i): \jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax (values_inference.tex); amendment 7 claims.rejection; note sec_joint.tex sec:joint5d-conditions (the design-review rate, whose calculation is not recorded, and the geometric extrapolation).
\emph{(i) Convergence below the fine grid.}  Within each fine-grid cell the null truth has the Tune~v1 composition of the hypothesis definition.  In the $L_{2}$ norm of the per-cell relative changes, the last refinement step is \jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax{} of the whole change from the coarse test grid. Convergence of the fine grid has therefore not been shown, and the residual below it is unmeasured and potentially material.  The shifts $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ were set by assuming that the refinement steps shrink geometrically.  The measured $L_{2}$ ratios also allow slower convergence.  The rejections carry over from the simulated hybrid nulls to the generator predictions themselves only if replacing the Tune~v1's shape below the fine grid by $G$'s moves the noise-free unfolded result by no more than $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ along $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. Showing this would require a demonstrated bound on the residual below the fine grid.

\emph{(ii) Detector and hadronic response.}\label{sec:response}  The detector model is the simulation's, varied only by the drawn detector bands and a linear surrogate for the lateral bands.  It has no band for the calorimetric hadronic-energy response.  In MINERvA's recoil-energy analyses, the uncertainty of that response is derived from test-beam calorimetry~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam} and rises to \SI{10}{\percent} at the highest three-momentum transfers of the low-recoil measurement~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl}.  The omission matters for the rejections.  A check made after the decisions shows that the calibrated tests restricted to the matched $(\pT,\pPar)$ projection of Sec.~\ref{sec:joint-results} do not reject GENIE~2.12.10 CV ($p=\woneGcvPtTot$ in total and $\woneGcvPtShape$ in shape), so the information that rejects it involves the hadronic axes, on which a response band would act.  The net effect of the missing band on each $p$ value has not been measured.  The rejections remain conditional on the simulation's hadronic response, and their physical interpretation would require a response band drawn through the calibration.

Three results point to the unfolded result retaining part of its prior. The studies of Secs.~\ref{sec:repro2d} and~\ref{sec:validation} show what must be checked before unfolded intervals are interpreted: closure with the backgrounds included; truth changes of generator size, not only nominal closure; coverage under changes of the truth, not only at nominal truth; stability under training seeds and rounding-level input changes; and run-period consistency.  When intervals cannot be validated, tests can still be calibrated by simulating the analysis chain at the hypothesis, as in Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}.
