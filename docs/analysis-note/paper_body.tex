\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% r5:13, unchanged.
Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon and hadronic observables. Their interpretation depends on correcting detector response and on assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but its response tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}. This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned response tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been applied to collider data, for example to eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and to twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets} (see ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.

% r5:16, with its last sentence replaced by the three findings (ledger L-01 to L-03; W-2).
This paper is a benchmark and diagnostic study of a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}, in the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta $\pT$ and $\pPar$, the available hadronic energy $\Eavail$, the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and the hadronic invariant mass $W$. Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five observables are simultaneous but not independent. Event weights let the same unfolded sample be histogrammed in projections or cells chosen afterwards. An uncertainty for a new choice must be constructed and checked for that choice; this study does not establish a practical gain over binned unfolding. The study reports three findings, each with the tests that establish it, including those that failed (table~\ref{tab:outcomes}). First, a benchmark against the published double-differential cross section $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$ from the same data~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} shows agreement in normalization and a correlated shape difference whose source has not been identified (section~\ref{sec:bench}). Second, controlled changes of the simulated truth show that the five-observable result retains part of its prior (section~\ref{sec:prior}). Third, reproducibility, numerical stability and the calibration of the uncertainties are distinct properties: the estimator has the first, fails the second, and the third is not established (section~\ref{sec:stability}).

The findings are those of one OmniFold implementation on one data set, for the observables, configurations, and deformations tested, rather than of multidimensional OmniFold in general. The sixteen-cell cross section the estimator gives on the data is therefore not a measurement; the supplementary material gives it as illustrative output, with its covariance and descriptive comparisons with generator predictions (\ssec{S10}{supp:output}).

\section{Data and observables}
\label{sec:data}

% r5:63, its first two sentences, with the 1A comparison moved to S1 (the editor's B6: the clause that it was checked stays). A playlist is a MINERvA data-taking period (the editor's D).
The data come from the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current run periods, or playlists (1A--1G and 1L--1P), corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target, with an average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. The input is an earlier production of the MINERvA open-data release (see Data availability), in the form of pre-selected analysis ntuples~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}; its one playlist that predates the current release was checked against that release (\ssec{S1}{supp:selection}). For the run-period studies the playlists form three groups: 1A--1F; 1G, 1L and 1M (written 1G--1M); and 1N--1P. Momenta are given in GeV/$c$, energies in GeV, and cross sections per nucleon.

% r5:67-74, unchanged.
The unfolded observables are defined at truth level, with muon momentum components $\pT=p_{\mu}\sin\theta_{\mu}$ and $\pPar=p_{\mu}\cos\theta_{\mu}$. The magnitude of the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}=|\vec{q}|$ comes from the generator's four-vectors, and the hadronic invariant mass $W=\sqrt{M^{2}+2M(E_{\nu}-E_{\mu})-Q^{2}}$ uses the struck-nucleon mass $M$. We define the available energy as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Eavail={}&\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV})\\
&+\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma},
\end{split}
\label{eq:eavail}
\end{equation}
% r5:75, its first five sentences and last two; the comparison with published definitions and the first-order effect of the constant moved to S2, which carries both (supplemental_body.tex sec. S2).
which subtracts a defined constant of \SI{135}{MeV}, rather than the charged-pion mass, in the charged-pion term. The constant defines the reported observable and is used consistently by the analysis and every prediction. The sums run over the generator's final-state particles after final-state interactions, where neutral pions appear undecayed. All other final-state particles, including neutrons, contribute nothing. No single published definition of $\Eavail$ exists; the supplementary material compares eq.~(\ref{eq:eavail}) with the definitions in use and gives the effect of the constant on the result (\ssecc{S2}{supp:observables}). Reconstructed $\Eavail$ is $1.17$ times the calorimetric recoil energy in the tracker and electromagnetic calorimeter, with muon deposits removed. Reconstructed $q_{3}$ and $W$ instead combine the muon kinematics with a separate calorimetric estimate $q_{0}$ of the energy transfer.

% r5:79, its first four sentences; the truth-level sentence moved to S2.
Reconstructed $W$ is an exact function of the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, apart from a clamp at $W^{2}=0$, because $W_{\mathrm{reco}}^{2}=(\bar M+q_{0})^{2}-q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}^{2}$, with $\bar M$ an average nucleon mass, and $q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}$ increases with $q_{0}$ at fixed muon kinematics. The five reconstructed inputs therefore share information and do not provide five independent constraints. Only reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the others; reconstructed $\Eavail$ comes from its own calorimetric estimator and is not a function of them. The definitions, and the truth-level relation among the observables, are detailed in \ssecc{S2}{supp:observables}.

% r5:81, its first sentence and the NuWro/GiBUU target clause, kept so that the truths of section 3.3 are defined in the paper (the editor's B4); the analysis-definition and CH-correction detail of the data comparison moved to S10.
The generator predictions used as prior reweightings and as fake-data truths are the untuned GENIE~2.12.10 without (``GENIE CV'') and with (``GENIE + MEC'') the Valencia 2p2h component~\cite{Nieves:2011pp}, which models meson-exchange currents (MEC), NuWro~21.09~\cite{Golan:2012wx} and GiBUU~2019~\cite{Buss:2011mx}; NuWro and GiBUU are generated on carbon. The analysis simulation is the MINERvA Tune~v1 of GENIE, which is also the prior of every unfolding.

% r5:84, with the support rule kept in the paper (the editor's B3).
The fine grid is the analysis binning: the published $14\times16$ muon-momentum bins, of which the publication reports 205, crossed with 7 $\Eavail$, 7 $q_{3}$, and 6 $W$ bins. A coarse $3^{5}$ grid in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$ has its edges at the fine edges nearest the simulated tertiles. A coarse cell is kept if its expected reconstructed signal and its truth weight at the data exposure are both at least 1000 events. We call this requirement the support rule. The \jtNcells{} coarse cells that meet it are the joint cells. The others, which hold \SI{0.47}{\percent} of the expected signal, stay in the total only (\ssec{S7}{supp:joint}).

% r5:105, with the output pointer moved to S10 (the editor's B3: the cell definition stays in the paper).
The five-observable results are reported on 16 cells, R00--R15. They come from the \fvval{h2-nboxes} boxes formed by one interior edge per axis, at $\pT$ \fvval{h2-mid-pt}, $\pPar$ \fvval{h2-mid-pz} and $q_3$ \fvval{h2-mid-q3}~GeV/$c$, and at $\Eavail$ \fvval{h2-mid-eavail} and $W$ \fvval{h2-mid-W}~GeV. In forming the cells, each supported box (one that meets the support rule) with purity, the fraction of the simulated signal reconstructed in it that was generated in it, below \fvval{h2-tau} is merged into a neighbor, \fvval{h2-nunreported} boxes remain unreported, and every reported cell passes the support rule. The diagonal fraction of a cell is the fraction of the simulated signal generated in it and reconstructed that is reconstructed in the same cell. Each cell's box is listed in \stab{S2}{tab:fived16-cells}, and the construction of the cells is given in \ssecc{S3}{supp:cells}; other reporting definitions, with their precision, are studied in \nsec{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}.

\section{Unfolding method and test design}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Unfolding}
\label{sec:unfolding}

% r5:89, unchanged.
Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate efficiency correction is applied.

% r5:91, unchanged.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the classifier learns to match. In the purity-based subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight $w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that bin, so the classifier's target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$. The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative weight to the data, so that the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise. A classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at zero, converts the signed sample to non-negative weights before the first iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction varies within it (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}).

% r5:93, with "(Sec. sec:result16)" replaced by the S10 pointer; registry ALIASES rows 38 and 47 cite this paragraph's line. The baseline sentence after it is new (DECISION-20261109-joseph-methods-baseline-and-plan.md item 1; ledger L-06).
The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn,Ke:2017lightgbm} of nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps, and negative-weight background subtraction with a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves. The two-observable estimator, LightGBM~\cite{Ke:2017lightgbm} at a fixed seed, gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance (section~\ref{sec:repro2d}); its central value is the one its systematic-universe ensemble is built around, so the covariance uses the same estimator. The five-observable results use the five-observable estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with the same background treatment and refinement capacity; the note's calibrated joint tests of generator predictions use it too (\ssec{S7}{supp:joint}). Its seeds are fixed, so its training is reproducible. The two estimators differ in the observables their classifiers see, two or five, and in LightGBM's training mode, seeded or deterministic (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}). This estimator removes most of the bias of purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-$W$ cells (\nsec{sec:val-negweight}). Results from the two estimators are not combined. The five-observable estimator is the baseline this study characterizes (configuration~(e) of \ssecc{S4}{supp:estimators}): the stability probe and the ensemble average of section~\ref{sec:cov5d} were tested against it, and neither replaced it.

% r5:95, its first two sentences and its last three, with the iteration count's rule in one sentence (the editor's B1); the nominal-setting, refinement-capacity and two-observable iteration checks moved to S4 with r5:98's detail.
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding. No learner setting, whether capacity, learning rate, refinement capacity or iteration count, was tuned on data. The five-observable estimator keeps five iterations because 10, 15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model dependence over the truth changes used in its development (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}). Two choices for the two-dimensional result were made with the comparison to the published result in view: the learner implementation and the background-subtraction treatment (section~\ref{sec:repro2d}). That comparison is therefore not an independent test of the method. Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, so no held-out or cross-fitted split is used; it is trained without early stopping, and the two classes keep their physical weights (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}).

\subsection{The reported covariances}
\label{sec:cov16}

% r5:118, with the generator-comparison clauses moved to S10 and the block's run-period numbers kept (ledger L-07).
The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is $C_{\mathrm{total}}+C_{\mathrm{resp}}+C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$. The term $C_{\mathrm{total}}$ sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization uncertainties of the five-observable estimator, whose sum is $C_{\mathrm{e}}$, and $C_{\mathrm{model}}$; the lateral uncertainties shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than reweighting events (\ssec{S10}{supp:output}). The model term $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ is built from the change in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn, with GENIE CV as the common reference. It is a measure of unscaled generator-shape dependence, not a calibrated bound (\ssec{S5}{supp:cov16}). The response term $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ is the response of the result to a one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil energy in the simulation. The scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and other deposits. $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ does not cover neutron or muon deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale. The unfolded results of the three run-period groups, each relative to its own simulation, differ by more than their bootstrap scatter. The cause is not known, and these data cannot establish which period is correct. The run-period block $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ includes this inconsistency in the reported covariance. It is built from the double ratios among the groups, each group's unfolded result over its own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the reference, so it contains the differences by construction. It was added after these differences were seen. It has rank \fvval{pl-rank} and is not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains, and with three groups its size is poorly determined: the 68\,\% interval on the ratio of its true to its estimated size is \fvval{pl-sigratio-lo}--\fvval{pl-sigratio-hi}. The median per-cell relative uncertainty is \fvval{sigmed-reported}\%, \fvval{sigmed-reported-nopl}\% without $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$, and \fvval{sigmed-C_total}\% without $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ or $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$. The fake-data tests are given with and without it (section~\ref{sec:fd16}; \nsec{sec:fived16-pl}).

% r5:125, its first sentence; the block-by-block ensembles and normalizations moved to S5.
% r5:187 and r5:200, moved here from section 6.2 (the editor's B5): the 2D covariance's construction, beside the five-observable one; its calibration tests stay in section 6.2.
The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central value's estimator: systematic universes (187 universes, including the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap replicas), run period (three period groups), and training seed (five seed settings, so that its own size is poorly determined). The resulting relative uncertainty has a median of \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent} per bin and is \SI{\uqIntegral}{\percent} on the integrated cross section, compared with \SI{\uqPaper}{\percent} and \SI{\uqPaperIntegral}{\percent} for the publication. The covariance omits the approximation in the systematic-universe unfoldings for events that fail reconstruction, the learner dependence of section~\ref{sec:repro2d}, and the dependence on the regularization (\ssec{S6}{supp:cov2d}). The dependence on the prior's generator shape is reported beside it as a separate covariance $C_{\mathrm{model}}$, built from the change of the result when the prior is reweighted to each generator's $(\pT,\pPar)$ shape; its median is \SI{\cmodelMedian}{\percent} per bin, and it is a measure of model dependence, not a calibrated bound on the unfolding bias.

Every $\chi^2$ of the five-observable result inverts the full covariance of the cells compared, $16\times16$, or $8\times8$ for GiBUU's eight cells with $\pPar<\fvval{h2-mid-pz}$~GeV/$c$, by Cholesky decomposition and without truncation, so its number of degrees of freedom is the number of cells. The blocks, their ensembles and their normalizations are given in the \ssec{S5}{supp:cov16}.

\subsection{Test design}
\label{sec:strategy}

% r5:112, unchanged, then the truth sets, criteria and estimator of each finding stated once (the editor's A1 and A2; ledger L-08 to L-11). The truth counts: F0b's 12 graded and 6 descriptive truths (sec_fived16.tex; FD-STAB-DECLARATION.md:105-122, the 18 F0b truths), 17 of them distinct (ENS1-DECLARATION.md:122; the Tune v1 hybrid is the prior, ens1_stage2.json's determinism control).
A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The closure, deformation, and coverage studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed and true distributions together and keeps the detector response at fixed truth. Throughout, the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune~v1. Every study uses the analysis simulation's detector response.

The five-observable tests use one set of truths. \fvval{f0b-all-truths} are graded (table~\ref{tab:fd16}): the simulation's prior; five hybrid truths, each equal to a generator prediction in every cell of the fine grid and to the Tune~v1 simulation's event composition within each cell; four generator shape changes, among them the GiBUU-to-GENIE $\Eavail$ ratio (the GiBUU $\Eavail$ shape); a noise-free closure at the NuWro shape; and a synthetic tilt of the $q_3$ distribution at fixed $(\Eavail,W)$. \fvval{f0b-nungraded} further truths, given in the note, are descriptive. The truths are not held out of the analysis. Two GENIE~v3 tunes, \fvval{f1-tune-g18} and \fvval{f1-tune-ar23}, were generated for these tests and held out of it; each reweights the simulated truth by its shape ratio to GENIE CV. The bias is graded on Poisson pseudo-experiments, whose mean is compared with the truth, or on noise-free pseudo-data, in which every Poisson draw is replaced by its mean; the comparison of the ensemble average with the baseline (section~\ref{sec:cov5d}) uses noise-free pseudo-data at all \fvval{ens1-ntruths} distinct truths, graded and descriptive, since the Tune~v1 hybrid equals the prior.

The grading rules and their timing are stated with the individual tests; comparisons without a grading rule are descriptive. A truth is flagged when the bias gives $p<\fvval{trig-p}$ in the $\chi^2$ over the 16 cells, or when a cell has $|z|>\fvval{trig-z}$, where $z$ is the cell's bias divided by its uncertainty under the same covariance. An estimator is stable when rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by at most 0.3 statistical standard deviations in the median and 1.0 at most. A coverage test grades the fraction of pseudo-experiments whose result lies within its interval, per cell and jointly, against a threshold set from the test's own null distribution. The two-dimensional benchmark (section~\ref{sec:bench}) concerns the two-observable estimator; the truth-recovery and stability findings (sections~\ref{sec:prior} and~\ref{sec:cov5d}) concern the five-observable estimator, whose rounding sensitivity is carried in its statistical uncertainty (section~\ref{sec:cov5d}).

\subsection{Normalization, per-bin agreement and the covariance-weighted distance}
\label{sec:repro2d}
\label{sec:repro2d-pub}

The two-dimensional result benchmarks the unfolding against a measurement from the same data. Its integrated cross section is \SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon}, compared with \SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon} in the published analysis, a ratio of $\ratioTot$. The standardized bin differences formed with the published uncertainties have an RMS of $\pullRMS$ (figure~\ref{fig:validation}). The two results share the data and the flux, detector and generator systematics, and their cross-covariance is not available, so no difference between them can be given a calibrated significance. The published covariance has a condition number of $1.5\times10^{12}$, and 73 of its 205 eigenvalues exceed $10^{-4}$ of the largest. Against it alone, the covariance-weighted distance is $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiPaper$, computed with the pseudo-inverse over the 205 reported bins, which keeps all 205 eigenmodes, with ndf $=205$, the number of reported bins, since no parameter is fitted. The value is descriptive, and no $p$-value is quoted for it. It indicates a correlated shape difference, not a normalization difference: its normalization part is $\chi^{2}=0.04$, against 465 for the shape. Keeping the 73 directions with $\lambda/\lambda_{\max}>10^{-4}$ gives $\chi^{2}=136$, and the 139 with $\lambda/\lambda_{\max}>10^{-6}$ give $\chi^{2}=285$. Most of the $\chi^{2}$ therefore lies outside the best-determined directions, and those directions also contribute to it (figure~\ref{fig:tension2d}; \ssecc{S6}{supp:cov2d}). The source of the shape difference has not been identified.

The learner implementation was chosen after three had been compared with the published result: with purity-based subtraction, their $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ values were $\chiPaperExact$ (scikit-learn exact-split), $\chiPaperPurity$ (LightGBM) and 2.70 (scikit-learn histogram, a mean over ten seeds), so the distance depends on the estimator. Two comparisons, each specified before it ran, put the two-dimensional result beside other unfoldings of its own inputs. Each compares a statistic $T$, formed from the difference of the two results and its covariance over paired replicas, with bars from a null of exact agreement (\ssec{S8}{supp:ibu}), so a reading of ``differ'' means that a difference is detectable at this noise, not that it is large. \subsection{Run periods and the five-observable quadrant sums}

In the two-dimensional result, data from different run periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties: one period group's unfolded $\pPar$ distribution, relative to its own simulation, differs from that of the earliest periods by a step of about \SI{7}{\percent} near \SI{8}{GeV/c}, of unknown cause. Summed over each of the four $(\pT,\pPar)$ quadrants, the sixteen-cell result agrees with the two-dimensional result: the sums differ by $\fvval{x-dpct-min}$ to $\fvval{x-dpct-max}$ per cent, and with the covariance between the two results evaluated from the systematic variations they share, the four differences give $\chi^2\leq\fvval{x-chi2-hi}$ for four degrees of freedom ($p\geq\fvval{x-p-min}$), with no quadrant beyond one standard deviation. The statistical correlation, and that of four systematic terms that could not be evaluated directly, are bounded within a one-parameter family of valid covariances, each such cross term taken as a common fraction, from zero to one, of its fully correlated value, over which the agreement holds. On noise-free pseudo-data with backgrounds, at one split of the simulation, it measures at each graded truth the retained fraction $\rho=-\sum_c b_c d_c/\sum_c d_c^2$. Here $d_c=(X_c-P_c)/P_c$ is the truth's departure from the prior in cell $c$ and $b_c=(\hat X_c-X_c)/P_c$ the bias, with $\hat X$, $X$ and $P$ the unfolded, true and prior cross sections, and the sums run over the cells where $|d_c|$ is at least \SI{1}{\percent}; $\rho=1$ if the result is the prior and $\rho=0$ if the departure is recovered. It changes one setting at a time, the iteration count, the classifier capacity, the training statistics and the observables the classifiers see, and runs an exact binned IBU on the same pseudo-data. At the two flagged truths, each setting is classed by whether some level reduces or increases $\rho$ by more than a bar fixed in advance, the larger of 0.05 and 9.03 times the standard deviation of the paired change of $\rho$ over five splits of the simulation (9.03 is the two-sided Student-$t$ quantile on four degrees of freedom at a familywise rate of 0.01 over the study's twelve primary readings), and the IBU at five iterations by whether it retains less or more of the prior than the five-observable estimator, by the same rule with its own bar, and by whether its retention falls or rises by at least 0.05 when it runs to 200 iterations. A level clears a flagged truth if the truth is no longer flagged, by the criterion of section~\ref{sec:strategy} applied to the noise-free pseudo-data. The readings are combined into labels fixed in advance: consistent with finite-iteration regularization (more iterations reduce $\rho$, and so do longer IBU runs); consistent with an information limit at these settings (neither more iterations nor more capacity reduces $\rho$, nor do longer IBU runs); and consistent with a share specific to the implementation (the IBU retains less of the prior, or more capacity reduces $\rho$).
