\section{Introduction}

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon
and hadronic observables.  Their interpretation depends on correcting detector
response and assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure.
Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration
matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but the response tensor grows
rapidly when additional observables are reported.  OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned
feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation
\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}.  This makes simultaneous
higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned
response tensor for every projection.  Unbinned unfolding has been applied to
collider data, for example eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at
H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at
ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}; see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey.  In
neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector
sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.  We are not aware of an application to
neutrino-scattering data.

We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the
MINERvA open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  We first
reproduce the published double-differential charged-current inclusive cross
section in transverse and longitudinal muon momentum,
$d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, then add available
hadronic energy $\Eavail$, three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and hadronic
invariant mass $W$ to obtain simultaneous three-, four-, and five-observable
central values.  Multi-differential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}.
The observables share reconstructed inputs: $\Eavail$, $q_{3}$, and $W$ are all
built from reconstructed hadronic energy.

We assess how reliable the method is in practice on data.  The
two-dimensional reproduction provides an external reference.  As observables
are added, comparisons with the lower-dimensional projections test consistency,
closure tests with injected shape changes test shape recovery, and
pseudo-experiments that include backgrounds, together with the ensembles of
repeated unfoldings used for the uncertainties, test the sensitivity to the
background treatment, the regularization, and the estimator's random seed
(Secs.~\ref{sec:validation} and~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status}).  Together
they identify which parts of the method work and which limit a
five-dimensional measurement with total uncertainties.

As an application, we test specified generator predictions.  The published
muon-kinematic measurement already disfavours these generator
families~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.  We calibrate each test through the full
analysis chain, using an estimator with unbinned background refinement, so that the
estimator's bias toward its prior enters each simulated null distribution
(Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}).

The two-dimensional reproduction agrees with the published total cross section
to about one percent (ratio $\ratioTot$), with \SI{\binsTen}{\percent} of the
205 bins within \SI{10}{\percent}.  The higher-dimensional central values agree
with their lower-dimensional marginals and recover the known low-recoil
discrepancy; the three- and four-observable central values also recover
injected shape changes in closure tests.  Pseudo-experiments that include
backgrounds, however, reveal a background-subtraction bias of up to
\SI{4}{\percent} in simulation in the highest-$W$ cells.  An unbinned
treatment, used only for the generator tests, largely removes it; the quoted
uncertainties do not include it.  The unfolding also recovers only part of a
change of the $\Eavail$ shape as large as the difference between generators.
This regularization bias, the dependence on the estimator's random seed, and
the untested coverage limit the method, and the measured model

\section{Data, signal, and simulation}
\label{sec:data}

The analysis uses the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current
run periods, corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target and an
average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, from the
MINERvA open-data release of pre-selected analysis ntuples
(AnaTuples)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  The detector, including the downstream
MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}.  The files analysed are an earlier production of
that release; their names and sizes are recorded for reproduction
(see Data availability).  The signal definition, fiducial volume, flux
treatment~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn}, and reconstruction-level selection follow
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.  After selection, the sample contains $4.12$
million data events, and the simulation contains $32.8$ million true signal
events, generated with GENIE~2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} and the MINERvA
Tune~v1, which reweights GENIE with a non-resonant pion reduction, a
random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil
two-particle--two-hole (2p2h) enhancement~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}.

The unfolded observables are defined at truth level.  Besides the muon
momentum components $\pT$ and $\pPar$, they are the three-momentum transfer
$q_{3}=|\vec{q}\,|$ from the generator, the hadronic invariant mass
$W=\sqrt{M^{2}+2M(E_{\nu}-E_{\mu})-Q^{2}}$ with the struck-nucleon mass $M$, and
the available energy
\begin{equation}
  \Eavail=\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV})
  +\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma},
\end{equation}
in which protons contribute kinetic energy, charged pions their energy minus an
inherited constant of \SI{135}{MeV} rather than the charged-pion mass, and
neutral pions and photons their total energy; all other final-state particles,
including neutrons, contribute nothing.  We have not corrected this
constant.  Relative to the charged-pion mass, in a simulated sample it moves \SI{0.67}{\percent} of
events into another true-$\Eavail$ bin and changes no true-$\Eavail$ bin by more than
\SI{1.05}{\percent}; this effect has not been compared with the full
covariance.  Reconstructed
$\Eavail$ is the calorimetric recoil energy in the tracker and electromagnetic
calorimeter after removal of the muon's energy deposits.  Reconstructed $q_{3}$
and $W$ combine the muon kinematics with a separate calorimetric estimate of
the energy transfer, so the five reconstructed inputs share information and do
not, by themselves, provide five independent constraints.  The implemented
reconstructed and truth definitions are given in the analysis note's section on
the higher-dimensional extensions~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}.

Event selection is held fixed as the classifier inputs and unfolded observables grow from
$(\pT,\pPar)$ to $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_3,W)$. It requires a tracker vertex
and a MINOS-matched muon; adding features does not add selected events or
relax the signal definition. The external
generator predictions compared below are the untuned GENIE~2.12.10 on CH without (``GENIE
CV'') and with (``GENIE + MEC'') the Valencia 2p2h
component~\cite{Nieves:2011pp}, which models meson-exchange currents (MEC),
and NuWro~21.09~\cite{Golan:2012wx} and GiBUU~2019~\cite{Buss:2011mx}, both on
carbon.
The GENIE and NuWro predictions are reweighted exactly, event by event, to the
analysis flux, with added 50--\SI{100}{GeV} samples;
the GiBUU sample stops at $E_{\nu}\approx\SI{20}{GeV}$.

\section{Unbinned unfolding}
\label{sec:method}

Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification steps.  A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and
defines a likelihood-ratio weight.  The event pairing transfers that weight to
truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a
smooth reweighting to the next iteration.  Cross sections are formed by
histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and
dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width.

Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the
classifier learns to match.  In the purity-based subtraction every data event in
reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight
$w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that
bin, so the target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$.  The unbinned
negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative
weight to the data, so the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise; a classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at
zero, converts the signed sample into non-negative weights before the first
iteration.  The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever
the background fraction varies within it.  Both are defined in the analysis
note's appendix on negative-weight background subtraction~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}.

All estimator configurations use five OmniFold iterations with
gradient-boosted decision-tree classifiers~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn,Ke:2017lightgbm}, and each result
below states which configuration it uses.  The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize
the unfolding, and no hyperparameter search was performed on data: the nominal
settings are scikit-learn defaults, with LightGBM matched to their capacity,
and the joint-test estimator's refinement capacity was chosen in simulation
closure studies.  A
neural-network classifier in the same five-iteration loop changes the
three-dimensional total cross section by \SI{0.8}{\percent} and the
one-dimensional projections by median absolute differences of
\SIrange{0.7}{1.4}{\percent}.  The two- to five-dimensional central values use
LightGBM at matched nominal capacity with the purity-based subtraction; the
two-dimensional one is the central value of its systematic-universe ensemble,
so its covariance is built with the same estimator.

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

In closure tests, truth denotes the known simulated target, the prior is
the initial truth distribution, and the response maps truth to selected
observations.  A closure test measures how well a specified deformation is
recovered.  Coverage measures how often intervals contain their target over
repeated experiments; it tests the estimator and the interval construction
together.  The bookkeeping of missed events (those that fail reconstruction)
is checked for invariance, and the dependence on the number of iterations is
a stability diagnostic; neither establishes recovery or coverage.

\subsection{Two-dimensional reproduction}
\label{sec:repro2d}

The two-dimensional result is an end-to-end test of the unfolding against a
measurement from the same data.  Its integrated cross section is \SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon}, compared with
\SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon} in the published analysis, a ratio of
$\ratioTot$.  Of the 205 reported bins, \SI{\binsTen}{\percent} agree within
\SI{10}{\percent}; the standardized bin differences formed with the published
uncertainties have an RMS of $\pullRMS$; against the full published covariance,
$\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiPaper$, a correlated shape difference (indicative
only; see below).  This value depends on the learner: ten LightGBM training
seeds give 2.64--2.75, and the exact-split estimator gives $\chiPaperExact$
unseeded and $\chiPaperExactSeeded$ in one run with a fixed seed (its spread
over seeds has not been measured).
We quote the LightGBM result as the central value, so that it uses the same
estimator as its covariance.  This choice, which replaced the exact-split
result, was made after both comparisons were known.  The two differ by a
per-bin median of \SI{0.98}{\percent} (maximum \SI{12.2}{\percent}), and this
learner dependence is not included in any term of the covariance.

\subsection{Higher-dimensional central values}

Each higher-dimensional central value is checked against the
lower-dimensional result it extends.  The three-, four-, and five-observable marginal
normalizations agree at the approximately $1$--$2\%$ level, and a closure test
recovers an injected $\Eavail$ distortion.  These closure tests reweight the
simulated signal only.  End-to-end pseudo-experiments that include the
purity-based background subtraction reproduce the input at nominal truth
except in the highest-$W$ cells, which are biased by up to \SI{4}{\percent} in
simulation, about one quoted standard deviation in one $(\Eavail,W)$ cell; the
quoted uncertainties omit this bias.  The unbinned negative-weight background
treatment, as first measured with a deterministic variant of the
five-dimensional LightGBM central-value estimator, reduces it to below \SI{0.3}{\percent}, leaving up to
\SI{1}{\percent} in some joint cells; on data the two treatments
differ by at most \SI{1.4}{\percent}.
% Source: OUTCOME-20260925-s5c-purity-background-bias-at-high-W.md; VL149,
% VL151, VL152; KNOWN_ISSUES 75.

The unfolding recovers only part of a change of the $\Eavail$ shape as large as
the difference between generators.  When the true shape is reweighted by the
GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio, the $(\Eavail,W)$ cells deviate from it by
several percent in the largest cells and by up to \SI{74}{\percent} at high
$W$, with or without background, so background subtraction alone does not
explain the residual.  This regularization bias is not included in the quoted
uncertainties, and it persists over the tested ranges of iteration number and
classifier capacity; for
other fixed generator shapes, measured with the joint-test estimator, it is
\SIrange{16}{31}{\percent} in the most affected $(\Eavail,W)$ cell and within
the quoted total uncertainty in about half of those cells.  Input changes at
the level of floating-point rounding move the unfolded values on data by up to
a few statistical standard deviations; for the joint-test estimator this
spread lies inside the data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty, but it limits
how exactly the central values can be reproduced.  An end-to-end coverage test
of bias-corrected statistical intervals from a deterministic variant of the
estimator failed its criterion, fixed in advance, at
nominal truth and under an $\Eavail$ tilt, so frequentist coverage of the
five-dimensional intervals has not been established.  In this implementation,
then, agreement with the lower-dimensional marginals coexists with substantial
regularization bias, and the model dependence and the calibration of the
uncertainties remain limiting.  This motivates the full-chain calibrated
comparisons of Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}.

low-recoil discrepancy~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil,MINERvA:2022incl}.  GENIE,

GENIE's Valencia 2p2h contribution fills \SI{52}{\percent} of the

\section{Calibrated joint tests of generator predictions}
\label{sec:joint}

\subsection{Hypotheses, statistics, and calibration}

For each of five predictions $G$ (the MINERvA Tune~v1, GENIE CV, GENIE + MEC,
NuWro~21.09, and GiBUU~2019), the null hypothesis $H_{0}(G)$ is that the true cross section equals
$G$ on a fine five-dimensional grid, with the MINERvA Tune~v1's shape and $G$'s
finite-sample fluctuations below that grid.  We call this the hybrid null: it is
the hypothesis the calibration simulates, and for the Tune~v1, the analysis
simulation itself, it is $H_{0}$ exactly.  The tests are evaluated on a coarse
$3^{5}$ grid in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$ with edges
$\pT\in[0,0.55,0.85,4.5]\,\si{GeV/c}$, $\pPar\in[1.5,3.5,6,60]\,\si{GeV/c}$,
$\Eavail\in[0,0.4,1.5,100]\,\si{GeV}$, $q_{3}\in[0,1.2,2,100]\,\si{GeV}$ and
$W\in[0,1.4,2.2,100]\,\si{GeV}$, restricted to the \jtNcells{} supported cells;
for GiBUU, whose sample lacks high-energy neutrinos, the domain is the
\jtNcellsGiBUU{} cells with $\pPar<\SI{6}{GeV/c}$.
% Source: amendment 7 claims.family; nd-unfolding/s5p_stage1_inspect.py:45 (J_EDGES).

A total statistic $T_{\mathrm{tot}}=r^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}r$ is formed from
the residual $r=F-\mu_{G}$ of the unfolded cell values $F$ and the prediction
$\mu_{G}$, and a shape statistic $T_{\mathrm{sh}}$ of the same form from the
normalized cell fractions, with the normalization Jacobian applied to
$\Sigma$ and one cell dropped.  The metric
$\Sigma=V+\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ adds the prediction's own
finite-sample variance to $V$, a Ledoit--Wolf-shrunk
covariance~\cite{LedoitWolf:2004} of \jtVn{} pilot pseudo-experiments at the
Tune~v1 null.  The metric influences each test's power; the null calibration
determines its size.
% Source: nd-unfolding/s5p_inference.py:30-49; nd-unfolding/s5p_joint.py:147-162;
% amendment 7 calibration.metric and metric_frozen.

The null distribution of each statistic is obtained from pseudo-experiments that
run the full analysis chain at the null truth.  Each draws Poisson pseudo-data
from one half of the simulation together with an independent background source,
refits the background-refinement classifier, and unfolds with the joint-test
estimator trained on the other half.  It also draws the nuisance terms of the
model:
one of \jtFluxUniverses{} flux universes, each of the \jtModelBands{}
interaction-model uncertainty bands at one of its universes, the weight-based detector bands
(including the GEANT4~\cite{GEANT4:2002zbu} hadron-interaction reweights), the
muon and beam-angle lateral bands through a linear surrogate, a
\SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization, the data's rounding-level noise, and the
predictions' finite-sample residual.  The data are unfolded once with the full
simulation.  The Monte Carlo $p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$
of $B$ null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic; it is never zero.
Each decision uses the largest $p$ value over a set of shifted nulls fixed in
advance: shifts by an upper bound
on the simulation-size difference (half of the simulation in the
pseudo-experiments, all of it for the data), and by $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$, where
$\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ is the change of the noise-free unfolded result in the
last step of a refinement of the null truth's grid (Sec.~\ref{sec:conditions}).  The
null distributions for the external predictions are strongly non-central, with
non-centralities $\lambda$ of \jtLambdaMin{}--\jtLambdaMax{} in these units,
because the calibration reproduces the estimator's pull toward its prior.
% Source: amendment 7 calibration.process, calibration.process_shift, m1_shift,
% prefreeze_measurements.summary.

The ten tests are controlled at a familywise level $\alpha=\jtAlpha$ by the
Holm procedure~\cite{Holm:1979}, and a step rejects only when the 95\%
Clopper--Pearson interval~\cite{Clopper:1934} of $k/B$ lies entirely below its
Holm threshold.  Calibration proceeded in batches of \jtBatch{}
pseudo-experiments per null, to at most \jtBcap{}, until both of a null's
decisions were determined, that is, until each interval lay entirely on one
side of its threshold.  The hypotheses, statistics, shifted nulls,
multiplicity and stopping rules, and every input were fixed before any
production pseudo-experiment was run or any observed statistic computed.  Two
choices were informed by the data.  The reviewers of the design had seen
descriptive per-cell residuals before it was fixed.  A bounded extension of
the production computing budget was decided while the outputs of the
computing jobs showed the observed $p$ values used for the decisions; it
changed no statistic, shifted null, threshold, or stopping rule, and the Monte
Carlo $p$ values remain valid at the number of pseudo-experiments reached.  An
independent recomputation with separately written code agrees with the
production evaluation in all \jtRecompRows{} compared quantities.  The
specification fixed in advance and the result are given in
Ref.~\cite{JointTestRecord2026}, and full details in the analysis note's
joint-test section~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}.

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:conditions}

The decisions test the hybrid null.  Each holds under six conditions on the
nuisance model, numbered as in the specification fixed in
advance~\cite{JointTestRecord2026}.  (i) Within each fine-grid cell the null
truth has the Tune~v1's shapes, and convergence of the fine grid has not been
shown.  In the $L_{2}$ norm of the per-cell relative changes, the last
refinement step, from a twofold-merged grid to the fine grid, is
\jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax{} of the whole change from the coarse test grid.  The
residual below the fine grid is therefore unmeasured and potentially material.
Before any result was known, the size of the shifts was set from an assumed
extrapolation.  If the step kept shrinking geometrically, at a rate taken
from the review of the test design, for which no calculation is recorded, the
residual would be about one to three times the last step.  The shifted nulls
used for the decisions take its central estimate,
$\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$; robustness checks, which do not
enter the decisions, use its upper end,
$\pm\jtMoneKappaRobust\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$.  The measured $L_{2}$ ratios
also allow slower convergence.  The rejections carry over from the
simulated hybrid null to $G$ itself only if replacing the Tune~v1's shape below
the fine grid by $G$'s moves the noise-free unfolded result by no more than
$\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ along $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$.
Separately from the decision rule, the scan of Sec.~\ref{sec:joint-results}
finds the rejections still determined for shifts along that direction up to
about $5.2\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$; this bounds neither the residual nor the
convergence.  (ii) The
detector model is the simulation's, varied only by the drawn detector bands and
the lateral surrogate; there is no band for the calorimetric recoil-energy
response (Sec.~\ref{sec:response}).  (iii) The interaction-model prior is the
analysis's \jtModelBands-band set, with negative weights of two bands clipped.
(iv) The pseudo-experiments use half of the simulation and the data all of it;
the resulting shift, at most about 0.07 null standard deviations but not
always consistent with zero, enters as the upper-bound shift described above.  (v) GiBUU lacks $E_{\nu}>\SI{20}{GeV}$, and
its out-of-domain truth enters the migrations into its domain.  (vi) The
predictions' own finite samples enter the metric and the simulated residuals.
The Monte Carlo $p$ values are exact for the simulated null to the extent that
the pseudo-experiment chain reproduces the analysis applied to the data; the
independent recomputation verifies the calculation, not whether the chain does
so.

\subsection{Hadronic-response condition}
\label{sec:response}

MINERvA's recoil-energy analyses assign a hadronic-energy response uncertainty
derived from test-beam calorimetry~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam}, separate from
the GEANT hadron-interaction reweights~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq,MINERvA:2022incl};
in the low-recoil measurement it rises to \SI{10}{\percent} at the highest
three-momentum transfers~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl}.  The nuisance model of the
tests has no such band.  A response mismodelling could move each observed statistic
in either direction, and a band drawn in the calibration would also broaden
and could shift each null distribution; the net effect on each $p$ value has
not been measured.  This matters for the rejections: the published muon-kinematic
comparison, which does not use reconstructed recoil energy, also disfavours
these generator families~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, but within this analysis the
calibrated tests restricted to the matched $(\pT,\pPar)$ projection do not
reject GENIE~2.12.10 CV ($p=\woneGcvPtTot$ in total, $\woneGcvPtShape$ in
shape; a post hoc observation)~\cite{JointTestProjections2026}, so the
information that rejects it involves the hadronic axes, on which a response
band would act.

In an exploratory sensitivity study~\cite{JointTestRecoil2026}, the data are
re-unfolded with the simulation's reconstructed recoil energy scaled by
$\pm\SI{4}{\percent}$, the level of data--simulation agreement reported for
test-beam protons, pions, and electrons of
0.35--\SI{2.0}{GeV/c}~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam} (neutrons and higher-momentum
hadrons, which matter at high $W$, lie outside that sample), and the tests
are re-evaluated against the original null distributions.  At both signs every
rejection holds: the observed statistics move by up to about
\SI{11}{\percent}, nine tests keep $k=0$, and NuWro's shape test changes from
$k=1$ to $k=3$ of $1751$ at $+4\%$ ($p=0.0023$, against a Holm threshold of
$0.05$).  The null distributions were not regenerated with the variation.
Particle-species, energy-dependent, and resolution effects, a response
difference applied before calibration, and larger variations were not tested.
The rejections therefore remain conditional on the simulation's hadronic
response.

\section{Status of a five-dimensional uncertainty}
\label{sec:uncertainty-status}

We have built a covariance for the five-dimensional central value and formed
its \zprojCells-cell $(\Eavail,W)$ projection; it is separate from the
joint-test calibration.  It has not met the criteria that the analysis set in
advance for an uncertainty.  One of these criteria requires a bound fixed
before production and can no longer be applied to it, and the covariance is
used only under a documented exception to the criteria, limited to this one
covariance.  We quote no covariance-based generator
significance, because covariances from this estimator depend measurably on
its random seed.  In two further covariances built at two seed settings (separate
from the one in use), changing the seed moves the projected uncertainty by
\SI{\sprojMeasured}{\percent}, against a \SI{\sprojBound}{\percent} limit set
before they were produced.  This movement did not fall with ensemble size over
the range tested (\SI{\seedEffectNforty}{\percent},
\SI{\seedEffectNeighty}{\percent}, and \SI{\sprojMeasured}{\percent} with 40,
80, and 160 random throws), whereas the movement from resampling the throws at a
fixed seed, a noise floor, fell from \SI{\floorNforty}{\percent} to
\SI{\floorNeighty}{\percent} between 40 and 80.  The comparison rests on one
seed pair, with nested subsets of the throws, so the spread over seed pairs and
the behaviour at much larger ensembles have not been measured.  Five of the
covariance's 45 uncertainty bands are built at a fixed seed that the variation
cannot change; they make up \SI{\pinnedBandsSqrtTr}{\percent} of
$\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}\,C}$, the square root of the covariance's trace.  Their
seed sensitivity, and its effect on the total in either direction, has not
been measured.

A separate study looked for a definition of a five-dimensional measurement
whose total uncertainties could meet these criteria.  None of the definitions
it considered could meet the precision targets set in advance, within the
study's rules and computing budget.  For its fallback definition, the bounded
model-dependence component alone exceeded the target total width.  The joint
tests above came from the same study, as a separate route to inference, and
they produce no new uncertainty that could be reported for a measurement.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This study applies OmniFold to MINERvA inclusive charged-current data,
reproduces the published two-dimensional result, and extends the unfolding
to five simultaneous observables.  The results are of three kinds.  The
two-dimensional comparison is an end-to-end test of the unfolding against a
measurement from the same data.  The three- to five-dimensional central values
support only descriptive comparisons, without validated coverage of their
total intervals and without a covariance-based generator significance.  The
joint tests are calibrated generator tests; they are conditional on six stated
conditions and yield no new uncertainty that could be reported for a
measurement.  The higher-dimensional central values agree with their
lower-dimensional marginals and recover the established low-recoil
discrepancy, and the three- and four-observable central values recover
injected shape changes in closure tests.  Pseudo-experiments that include
backgrounds reveal a subtraction bias, which the unbinned background
refinement reduces, while a regularization bias persists under the tested
shape changes of generator size.  Studies of coverage and of the dependence on
the estimator's seed show further limits: the quoted two-dimensional
statistical block awaits an independent coverage test,

 the two-dimensional comparison depends on the

that meet our predefined criteria at useful precision.  These outcomes provide
practical checks for future multidimensional neutrino unfolding.

The generator comparisons illustrate calibration through the full analysis
chain with an estimator that retains substantial model dependence.  Under the stated
conditions, all ten total and shape hypotheses for the five predictions
are rejected at familywise level 0.05, and recovering every lost
pseudo-experiment changes no decision.  Each test also has $p<0.05$ in at
least one matched coarse projection, so we have not shown that the joint
information adds discrimination.  The physical interpretation depends
on the simulation's calorimetric recoil response and on the assumed
convergence below the fine grid; extending it would require a response band
drawn through the calibration and a demonstrated bound on the residual below
the fine grid.
