\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon and hadronic observables. Their interpretation depends on correcting detector response and on assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but its response tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}. This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned response tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been applied to collider data, for example to eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and to twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets} (see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}.

This paper is a methods and validation study of a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on real neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}, in the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta $\pT$ and $\pPar$, the available hadronic energy $\Eavail$, the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and the hadronic invariant mass $W$. Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five observables are simultaneous but not independent. The unfolding is benchmarked against the published double-differential cross section $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$ from the same data~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, and the five-observable estimator is tested for bias, numerical stability, coverage, and run-period consistency.

Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} gives each test and its result; the failures and the stopped test are findings of the study. The two-dimensional result reproduces the published integral within about 1\% but differs from it in shape, for a reason not identified; the step between run periods does not account for the distance in the best-determined directions of the published covariance, at the resolved size. A binned iterative Bayesian unfolding of the same inputs differs from the method both in two dimensions and on the sixteen cells, so the reported cells depend on the unfolding algorithm, and in two dimensions the comparison does not localise the shape difference. Closures at the simulation's own truth meet the bias criteria, but the five-observable result retains part of its prior: its fake-data bias exceeds criteria fixed before grading at a GiBUU-like hybrid truth and at a synthetic $q_3$ tilt. The estimator is bitwise independent of its training seed, yet fails a stability requirement under rounding-level changes of its inputs; on one simulated pseudo-experiment, the perturbation reaches it by breaking ties among equal float32 values. The procedure's two-dimensional covariance, rebuilt at each of two held-out generator tunes, under-covers at both. The declared five-observable coverage test did not run to a result: it was stopped before any coverage pseudo-experiment ran, at a check that compared unlike objects for the interaction and, mostly, the flux uncertainties, and its redesign stopped at its first stage under a declared fail-closed rule. The run periods differ for a cause not known. The sixteen-cell cross section the estimator gives on the data is therefore not a measurement; Appendix~\ref{app:output} gives it as illustrative output (Sec.~\ref{sec:result16}).

\section{Data, simulation, and observables}
\label{sec:data}

The data come from the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current run periods (playlists 1A--1G and 1L--1P), corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target, with an average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. The input is an earlier production of the MINERvA open-data release (see Data availability), in the form of pre-selected analysis ntuples (AnaTuples)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}. Of its playlists only 1A predates the current release; within the selection, its events and their kinematics are the same in both copies, and its selected yield per proton on target differs by $-0.9\times10^{-5}$ in the data and $-1.8\times10^{-5}$ in the simulation, of order $10^{-6}$ on the combined sample. This compares selected samples, not unfolded results, and not these inputs with those of the published analysis.

The analysis uses $4.12$ million selected data events. The simulation contains $32.8$ million unweighted true signal events and $20.5$ million selected simulated events, and it is scaled to the data exposure. The simulated events are generated with GENIE~2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} and reweighted with the MINERvA Tune~v1, which applies a non-resonant pion reduction, a random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil two-particle--two-hole (2p2h) enhancement~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}. Event selection is the same for every number of observables.

The unfolded observables are defined at truth level, with muon momentum components $\pT=p_{\mu}\sin\theta_{\mu}$ and $\pPar=p_{\mu}\cos\theta_{\mu}$. The magnitude of the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}=|\vec{q}|$ comes from the generator's four-vectors, and the hadronic invariant mass $W=\sqrt{M^{2}+2M(E_{\nu}-E_{\mu})-Q^{2}}$ uses the struck-nucleon mass $M$. We define the available energy as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Eavail={}&\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV})\\
&+\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma},
\end{split}
\label{eq:eavail}
\end{equation}
which subtracts a defined constant of \SI{135}{MeV}, rather than the charged-pion mass, in the charged-pion term. The constant defines the reported observable and is used consistently by the analysis and every prediction. The sums run over the generator's final-state particles after final-state interactions, where neutral pions appear undecayed. All other final-state particles, including neutrons, contribute nothing. No single published definition of $\Eavail$ exists. Eq.~(\ref{eq:eavail}) keeps the species of the closed list of Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil} except electrons and positrons, omits species such as kaons that the open list of Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} includes, and has a charged-pion term \SI{4.57}{MeV} per pion above the kinetic energy used in Refs.~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil,MINERvA:2023avz} (\nsec{sec:eavail-definition}). To first order in the unfolding, without retraining, replacing the constant alone by the charged-pion mass moves the sixteen-cell result by at most \fvval{eav-cellmax}$\sigma$ in any cell and by \fvval{eav-coherent}$\sigma$ as one coherent shift, the norm of the shift under the reported covariance (\fvval{eav-cellmax-nopl}$\sigma$ and \fvval{eav-coherent-nopl}$\sigma$ without the run-period block). Reconstructed $\Eavail$ is $1.17$ times the calorimetric recoil energy in the tracker and electromagnetic calorimeter, with muon deposits removed. Reconstructed $q_{3}$ and $W$ instead combine the muon kinematics with a separate calorimetric estimate $q_{0}$ of the energy transfer.

\section{Cross-section extraction}
\label{sec:method}

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate efficiency correction is applied.

Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the classifier learns to match. In the purity-based subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight $w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that bin, so the classifier's target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$. The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative weight to the data, so that the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise. A classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at zero, converts the signed sample to non-negative weights before the first iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction varies within it (\napp{app:negweight}).

The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn,Ke:2017lightgbm} of nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps, and negative-weight background subtraction with a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves. The two-observable estimator, LightGBM~\cite{Ke:2017lightgbm} at a fixed seed, gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro2d}). This reproducible central value is that of its own systematic-universe ensemble, so its covariance is built with the same estimator. The three- and four-observable central values and the sixteen-cell five-observable result (Sec.~\ref{sec:result16}) use the joint-test estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with the same background treatment and refinement capacity, named for the note's calibrated joint tests of generator predictions, which use it (\nsec{sec:joint5d}). Its seeds are fixed, so its training is reproducible, and it also gives the held-out shape tests of Fig.~\ref{fig:deformation}b. This estimator removes most of the bias of purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-$W$ cells (\nsec{sec:val-negweight}). Results from the two estimators are not combined. The other configurations used below, and their results, are described in \nsec{sec:method-config}.

The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding. No learner setting, whether capacity, learning rate, refinement capacity or iteration count, was tuned on data. The nominal settings are the scikit-learn defaults, with LightGBM matched to their capacity. The refinement capacity, which the two estimators share, was chosen in simulation closure studies, and so were the joint-test estimator's five iterations (below). The two-observable estimator uses five iterations following common practice; this choice was checked on data, against ten iterations with purity-based subtraction and against the published result, without a search over the iteration count (\nsec{sec:method-config}). Two other choices for the two-dimensional result were made with the comparison to the published result in view: the learner implementation and the background-subtraction treatment (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro2d-pub}). That comparison is therefore not an independent test of the method. Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, so no held-out or cross-fitted split is used; it is trained without early stopping, and the two classes keep their physical weights (\nsec{sec:method-algo}).

\section{Uncertainties and the validation programme}
\label{sec:validation}

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The closure, deformation, and coverage studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed and true distributions together and keeps the detector response at fixed truth. Throughout, the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune~v1. Every study uses the analysis simulation's detector response.

\subsection{Sensitivity to input rounding}
\label{sec:cov5d}

The joint-test estimator is unstable under floating-point rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, the reported quantities scatter by a median of \SI{0.23}{\percent} per joint cell, against a median data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty of \SI{0.37}{\percent}, and each bootstrap replica carries this scatter at the same size. Rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of the lower-capacity development variant by up to 2.5, so the estimator fails a data-stability requirement of at most 0.3 (median) and 1.0 (maximum) statistical standard deviations. The training-seed block of Sec.~\ref{sec:cov16} is zero because, in the unfolds we ran, the result is bitwise independent of the training seed and of input row order; this is distinct from the sensitivity to rounding-level input changes quoted above. That sensitivity enters the reported statistical block by construction: each of its 50 bootstrap replicas refits the whole unfolding with its own rounding-level perturbation of the inputs. On the finer joint cells, the rounding spread is about \SI{37}{\percent} of the statistical variance and below a tenth of any total uncertainty (Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}); on the 16 reported cells, over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, it is a median \SI{0.133}{\percent} of the cross section (\SIrange{0.055}{0.252}{\percent}); the quoted 2.6 statistical standard deviations is the largest shift, over 153 quantities (the joint cells, the $(\Eavail,W)$ cells and two totals) under two such perturbations of the data, not the typical one. \section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The method provides a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables, without a separate binned response tensor for each projection, whose normalization agrees with the two-dimensional result. Its reported cells depend on the unfolding algorithm, by the binned comparison of Sec.~\ref{sec:ibu}. It does not yet provide validated interval coverage, numerical stability, recovery of every generator-sized truth change tested within the reported uncertainty, run-period consistency, or an identified source of the shape difference from the published result (Table~\ref{tab:outcomes}).

\label{sec:limits}
Two validation findings point to the unfolded result retaining part of its prior. The first is the residual under a generator-sized change of the truth in Sec.~\ref{sec:departure}, which for the GiBUU-like change is consistent with finite-iteration regularization. The second is the shift of the data result when the simulation's prior is reweighted to generator-sized changes of the truth, which tracks the same bias (\nsec{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}). The note's calibrated tests of the predictions show the same bias toward the prior (\nsec{sec:joint5d-results}).
% - PRD read 1, R7: the null distribution and the hybrid null as sec_joint.tex sec:joint5d and sec:joint5d-design define them (the null truth equals G on every fine cell; below the grid, the Tune v1 composition; the null distribution from pseudo-experiments running the full chain).

The studies of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} and of the companion note show what must be checked before unfolded intervals are interpreted: closure with the backgrounds included; truth changes of generator size, not only nominal closure; coverage under changes of the truth, not only at nominal truth; robustness to training seeds and rounding-level input changes; and run-period consistency.

Until the coverage, the prior dependence, the numerical stability, and the run-period effect are resolved, the sixteen-cell output is a demonstration of the method.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Apart from the validation of the uncertainties and the low stability of four of the sixteen cells (below), the limitations of these results are stated with the results they qualify, in Secs.~\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:result16}, and those of the calibrated tests in the companion note. The principal ones are these.
\begin{enumerate}
\item No reported interval has validated coverage. The fake-data tests of Sec.~\ref{sec:fd16} assess bias, not coverage, so interval widths cannot be tested, and the coverage test declared for the sixteen-cell covariance was stopped by one of its preliminary checks before any coverage pseudo-experiment ran, and its staged redesign stopped at its first stage, so it did not run to a result; that check compared unlike objects for the interaction group at the nominal truth and mostly for the flux band, two detector bands depart for a cause not established, and the blocks' non-additivity and the truth dependence stay open (Sec.~\ref{sec:fd16}). Neither the coverage of $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ at truths held out of its construction nor the size of either run-period block is independently validated (Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}, Secs.~\ref*{N-sec:fived16-cov}, \ref*{N-sec:fived16-pl} and~\ref*{N-sec:uq-combine}). The two-dimensional covariance gives no calibrated significance for generator comparisons: an earlier statistical band of that result undercovered in its tails in a coverage test at fixed truth (\nsec{sec:coverage}), and the procedure's covariance, rebuilt at each of two held-out tunes without the run-period block, under-covers at both (Sec.~\ref{sec:uq2d}), a result about the procedure's covariance at those tunes, not the reported intervals at the data. The coverage tests cited are pointwise, for each reported quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them. We make no coverage claim on independent simulated samples or simultaneously over all reported bins and projections.
