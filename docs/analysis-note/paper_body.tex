\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% r5:13, unchanged.
Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon and hadronic observables. Their interpretation requires corrections for detector response and an assessment of the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but its response tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}. This allows simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding without a separate binned response tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been applied to collider data, for example to eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and to twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets} (see ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.

% r5:16, with its last sentence replaced by the three findings (ledger L-01 to L-03; W-2).
This paper is a benchmark and diagnostic study of a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}, in the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta $\pT$ and $\pPar$, the available hadronic energy $\Eavail$, the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and the hadronic invariant mass $W$. Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five observables are unfolded simultaneously but are not independent. The event weights allow the same unfolded sample to be histogrammed in projections or cells chosen afterwards. An uncertainty for a new choice must be constructed and checked for that choice; this study does not establish a practical gain over binned unfolding. We report three findings and the tests supporting them, including tests that failed (table~\ref{tab:outcomes}). First, a benchmark against the published double-differential cross section $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$ from the same data~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} shows agreement in normalization and a correlated shape difference whose source has not been identified (section~\ref{sec:bench}). Second, controlled changes of the simulated truth show that the five-observable result retains part of its prior (section~\ref{sec:prior}). Third, the estimator is reproducible but numerically unstable, and its uncertainty calibration is not established: these are distinct properties (section~\ref{sec:stability}).

The findings are those of one OmniFold implementation on one data set, for the observables, configurations and deformations tested, not of multidimensional OmniFold in general. The sixteen-cell cross section that the estimator gives on the data illustrates the method and is not a measurement; the supplementary material gives it with its covariance and descriptive comparisons with generator predictions (\ssec{S10}{supp:output}).

\label{sec:data}

The signal is charged-current $\nu_\mu$ interactions in the central-tracker fiducial volume with $\theta_\mu<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<\pPar<\SI{60}{GeV/c}$ and $\pT<\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$, the phase space of ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}; the selection is given in \ssec{S1}{supp:selection}. The data come from the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current run periods, or playlists (1A--1G and 1L--1P), corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target, with an average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. We use pre-selected analysis ntuples from an earlier production of the MINERvA open-data release (see Data availability)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}. The one playlist that predates the current release was checked against that release (\ssec{S1}{supp:selection}). For the run-period studies the playlists form three groups: 1A--1F, 1G--1M (the playlists 1G, 1L and 1M) and 1N--1P. Momenta are given in GeV/$c$, energies in GeV, and cross sections per nucleon.

The unfolded observables are defined at truth level, with muon momentum components $\pT=p_{\mu}\sin\theta_{\mu}$ and $\pPar=p_{\mu}\cos\theta_{\mu}$. The magnitude of the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}=|\vec{q}|$ comes from the generator's four-vectors, and the hadronic invariant mass $W=\sqrt{M^{2}+2M(E_{\nu}-E_{\mu})-Q^{2}}$ uses the struck-nucleon mass $M$. We define the available energy as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Eavail={}&\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV})\\
&+\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma},
\end{split}
\label{eq:eavail}
\end{equation}

which subtracts a defined constant of \SI{135}{MeV}, rather than the charged-pion mass, in the charged-pion term. The constant defines the reported observable and is used consistently by the analysis and every prediction. The sums run over the generator's final-state particles after final-state interactions, where neutral pions appear undecayed. All other final-state particles, including neutrons, contribute nothing. No single published definition of $\Eavail$ exists; the supplementary material compares eq.~(\ref{eq:eavail}) with the definitions in use and gives the effect of the constant on the result (\ssecc{S2}{supp:observables}). Reconstructed $\Eavail$ is $1.17$ times the calorimetric recoil energy in the tracker and electromagnetic calorimeter, with muon deposits removed. Reconstructed $q_{3}$ and $W$ instead combine the muon kinematics with a separate calorimetric estimate $q_{0}$ of the energy transfer.

Reconstructed $W$ is an exact function of the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, apart from a clamp at $W^{2}=0$, because $W_{\mathrm{reco}}^{2}=(\bar M+q_{0})^{2}-q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}^{2}$, with $\bar M$ an average nucleon mass, and $q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}$ increases with $q_{0}$ at fixed muon kinematics. The five reconstructed inputs therefore share information and do not provide five independent constraints. Only reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the others; reconstructed $\Eavail$ comes from its own calorimetric estimator and is not a function of them. The definitions, and the truth-level relation among the observables, are detailed in \ssecc{S2}{supp:observables}.

\section{Unfolding method and test design}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Unfolding}
\label{sec:unfolding}

% r5:89, unchanged.
Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The pairing of reconstructed and true events transfers that weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. We form cross sections by histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated flux, target count and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate efficiency correction is applied.

% r5:91, unchanged.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the classifier learns to match. In the purity-based subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight $w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that bin, so the classifier's target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$. The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative weight to the data, so that the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise. A classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at zero, converts the signed sample to non-negative weights before the first iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction varies within it (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}). With the refinement classifier, the negative-weight treatment removes most of the bias of purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-$W$ cells (\nsec{sec:val-negweight}).

% r5:93, with "(Sec. sec:result16)" replaced by the S10 pointer; registry ALIASES rows 38 and 47 cite this paragraph's line. The baseline sentence after it is new (DECISION-20261109-joseph-methods-baseline-and-plan.md item 1; ledger L-06).
The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn,Ke:2017lightgbm} of nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps, and negative-weight background subtraction with a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves. The two estimators differ in the observables their classifiers see, two or five, and in LightGBM's training mode, seeded or deterministic (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}). Results from the two estimators are not combined.

% Split from the paragraph above (the interim referee round, #1025, group A item 3): the two estimators and their uses.
The two-observable estimator, LightGBM~\cite{Ke:2017lightgbm} at a fixed seed, gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance (section~\ref{sec:repro2d}); its central value is the one its systematic-universe ensemble is built around, so the covariance uses the same estimator. The five-observable results use the five-observable estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with the same background treatment and refinement capacity; the note's calibrated joint tests of generator predictions use it too (\ssec{S7}{supp:joint}). Its seeds are fixed, so its training is reproducible. The five-observable estimator is the baseline this study characterizes (configuration~(e) of \ssecc{S4}{supp:estimators}): the stability probe and the ensemble average of section~\ref{sec:cov5d} were tested against it, and neither replaced it.

% r5:95, its first two sentences and its last three, with the iteration count's rule in one sentence (the editor's B1); the nominal-setting, refinement-capacity and two-observable iteration checks moved to S4 with r5:98's detail.
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding. No learner setting was tuned on data, including capacity, learning rate, refinement capacity and iteration count. The five-observable estimator uses five iterations because 10, 15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model dependence for the truth changes used in its development (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}). Two choices for the two-dimensional result were made with the comparison to the published result in view: the learner implementation and the background-subtraction treatment (section~\ref{sec:repro2d}). That comparison is therefore not an independent test of the method. Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, without a held-out or cross-fitted split or early stopping. The two classes retain their physical weights (\ssec{S4}{supp:estimators}).

\subsection{The reported covariances}
\label{sec:cov16}

% r5:118, with the generator-comparison clauses moved to S10 and the block's run-period numbers kept (ledger L-07).
The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is $C_{\mathrm{total}}+C_{\mathrm{resp}}+C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$. The term $C_{\mathrm{total}}$ combines the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization uncertainties of the five-observable estimator, whose sum is $C_{\mathrm{e}}$, and $C_{\mathrm{model}}$; the lateral uncertainties shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than reweighting events (\ssec{S10}{supp:output}).

% Split (#1025, group A item 3): the model and response terms.
The model term $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ is constructed from the change in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn, with GENIE CV as the common reference. It measures the result's unscaled dependence on the generator shape and is not calibrated as a bound (\ssec{S5}{supp:cov16}). The response term $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ is the response of the result to a one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil energy in the simulation. The scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and other deposits. $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ does not cover neutron or muon deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale.

% Split (#1025, group A item 3): the empirical run-period term.
The unfolded results of the three run-period groups, each relative to its own simulation, differ by more than their bootstrap scatter. The cause is unknown, and these data cannot establish which period is correct. The run-period block $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ includes this inconsistency in the reported covariance. It was added after the differences were observed and is constructed from the double ratios among the groups: each group's unfolded result divided by its own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the reference. It therefore contains the differences by construction. It has rank \fvval{pl-rank} and is not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains. With three groups its size is poorly determined: the 68\,\% interval on the ratio of its true to its estimated size is \fvval{pl-sigratio-lo}--\fvval{pl-sigratio-hi}. The median per-cell relative uncertainty is \fvval{sigmed-reported}\%, \fvval{sigmed-reported-nopl}\% without $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$, and \fvval{sigmed-C_total}\% without $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ or $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$. The fake-data tests are reported with and without this block (section~\ref{sec:fd16}; \nsec{sec:fived16-pl}).

% r5:125, its first sentence; the block-by-block ensembles and normalizations moved to S5.
% r5:187 and r5:200, moved here from section 6.2 (the editor's B5): the 2D covariance's construction, beside the five-observable one; its calibration tests stay in section 6.2.
The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central value's estimator: systematic universes (187 universes, including the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap replicas), run period (three period groups), and training seed (five seed settings, so its size is poorly determined). The resulting relative uncertainty has a median of \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent} per bin and is \SI{\uqIntegral}{\percent} on the integrated cross section, compared with \SI{\uqPaper}{\percent} and \SI{\uqPaperIntegral}{\percent} for the publication. The covariance omits the approximation in the systematic-universe unfoldings for events that fail reconstruction, the learner dependence of section~\ref{sec:repro2d}, and the dependence on the regularization (\ssec{S6}{supp:cov2d}). The dependence on the prior's generator shape is reported separately as a covariance $C_{\mathrm{model}}$, constructed from the change in the result when the prior is reweighted to each generator's $(\pT,\pPar)$ shape. Its median is \SI{\cmodelMedian}{\percent} per bin. It measures model dependence and is not calibrated as a bound on the unfolding bias.

For every $\chi^2$ of the five-observable result, we invert the full covariance of the cells compared, $16\times16$, or $8\times8$ for GiBUU's eight cells with $\pPar<\fvval{h2-mid-pz}$~GeV/$c$, by Cholesky decomposition and without truncation, so its number of degrees of freedom is the number of cells. Each block is defined in \ssec{S5}{supp:cov16}.

\subsection{Test design}
\label{sec:strategy}

% r5:112, unchanged, then the truth sets, criteria and estimator of each finding stated once (the editor's A1 and A2; ledger L-08 to L-11). The truth counts: F0b's 12 graded and 6 descriptive truths (sec_fived16.tex; FD-STAB-DECLARATION.md:105-122, the 18 F0b truths), 17 of them distinct (ENS1-DECLARATION.md:122; the Tune v1 hybrid is the prior, ens1_stage2.json's determinism control).
A closure test measures how well the unfolding recovers a specified deformation of the known simulated truth while holding the prior (the initial truth distribution) fixed. Coverage is the frequency with which intervals contain their target over repeated experiments; it tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The closure, deformation, and coverage studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed and true distributions together and keeps the detector response at fixed truth. The unfolding prior remains Tune~v1 throughout. Every study uses the analysis simulation's detector response.

The five-observable tests use one set of truths. \fvval{f0b-all-truths} are graded (table~\ref{tab:fd16}): the simulation's prior, five hybrid truths, four generator shape changes, a noise-free closure at the NuWro shape, and a synthetic tilt of the $q_3$ distribution at fixed $(\Eavail,W)$. The abstract calls the GiBUU hybrid truth GiBUU-like and the $q_3$ tilt the synthetic momentum-transfer tilt. Each hybrid truth equals a generator prediction in every cell of the fine grid and the Tune~v1 simulation's event composition within each cell. The shape changes include the GiBUU-to-GENIE $\Eavail$ ratio (the GiBUU $\Eavail$ shape). \fvval{f0b-nungraded} further truths, given in the note, are descriptive. The truths are not held out of the analysis. Two GENIE~v3 tunes, \fvval{f1-tune-g18} and \fvval{f1-tune-ar23}, were generated for these tests and held out of it; each reweights the simulated truth by its shape ratio to GENIE CV. The bias is graded on Poisson pseudo-experiments, whose mean is compared with the truth, or on noise-free pseudo-data, in which every Poisson draw is replaced by its mean; the comparison of the ensemble average with the baseline (section~\ref{sec:cov5d}) uses noise-free pseudo-data at all \fvval{ens1-ntruths} distinct truths, graded and descriptive, since the Tune~v1 hybrid equals the prior.

The grading rules and their timing are stated with the individual tests; comparisons without a grading rule are descriptive. A truth is flagged when the bias gives $p<\fvval{trig-p}$ in the $\chi^2$ over the 16 cells, or when a cell has $|z|>\fvval{trig-z}$, where $z$ is the cell's bias divided by its uncertainty under the same covariance. An estimator meets the stability requirement when rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by at most 0.3 statistical standard deviations in the median and 1.0 at maximum. A coverage test grades the fraction of pseudo-experiments whose result lies within its interval, per cell and jointly, against a threshold set from the test's own null distribution. The two-dimensional benchmark (section~\ref{sec:bench}) concerns the two-observable estimator; the truth-recovery and stability findings (sections~\ref{sec:prior} and~\ref{sec:cov5d}) concern the five-observable estimator, whose rounding sensitivity is carried in its statistical uncertainty (section~\ref{sec:cov5d}).

We benchmark the two-dimensional unfolding against a measurement from the same data. Its integrated cross section is \SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon}, compared with \SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon} in the published analysis, a ratio of $\ratioTot$. The two results share the data and the flux, detector and generator systematics, and their cross-covariance is unavailable, so the significance of their difference cannot be calibrated.

We quote the LightGBM result as the central value because its covariance uses the same estimator. Here ndf $=205$ is the number of reported bins; no parameter is fitted. The value is descriptive, and no $p$-value is quoted for it. The difference is a correlated shape difference, not a normalization difference: its normalization part is $\chi^{2}=0.04$, against 465 for the shape. Keeping the 73 directions with $\lambda/\lambda_{\max}>10^{-4}$ gives $\chi^{2}=136$, and the 139 with $\lambda/\lambda_{\max}>10^{-6}$ give $\chi^{2}=285$. Most of the $\chi^{2}$ therefore lies outside these 73 largest-eigenvalue directions, although they also contribute (figure~\ref{fig:tension2d}; \ssecc{S6}{supp:cov2d}). \subsection{Run periods and the five-observable quadrant sums}
\label{sec:closure2d}

% r5:192 and r5:194, cut: the 132-direction, six-mode and per-group detail moved to S6.
In the two-dimensional result, data from different run periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties: one period group's unfolded $\pPar$ distribution, relative to its own simulation, differs from that of the earliest periods by a step of about \SI{7}{\percent} near \SI{8}{GeV/c}, of unknown cause. The step is already in the reconstructed data: the 1G--1M group has a step of $+6.10\,\%$ relative to 1A--1E, the reconstruction-level reference without 1F. In a per-period unfolding, the double ratio of each period's result to its own simulated truth gives $+(6.94\pm0.41)\,\%$. The step therefore survives unfolding at about its reconstruction-level size. Compared with the published result in the 73 largest-eigenvalue directions of its covariance, removing the 1G--1M group does not change the distance to the published result beyond noise; the smallest change resolved is $3\sigma=0.29$ per direction, about \SI{15}{\percent} of the three-group value. At this resolution, the run-period step does not account for the distance in those directions (\ssecc{S6}{supp:cov2d}).

% r5:208, its first two sentences; the three-observable marginal comparison stays in the note (sec_fived16.tex sec:fived16-quadrants).
When summed over each of the four $(\pT,\pPar)$ quadrants, the sixteen-cell result agrees with the two-dimensional result: the sums differ by $\fvval{x-dpct-min}$ to $\fvval{x-dpct-max}$ per cent, and the four differences, using the covariance between the two results evaluated from their shared systematic variations, give $\chi^2\leq\fvval{x-chi2-hi}$ for four degrees of freedom ($p\geq\fvval{x-p-min}$), with no quadrant beyond one standard deviation. The statistical correlation, and that of four systematic terms that could not be evaluated directly, are bounded within a one-parameter family of valid covariances, with each such cross term taken as a common fraction, from zero to one, of its fully correlated value. The agreement holds throughout this family. The five-observable result therefore agrees with the two-dimensional normalization in each muon-kinematic quadrant; the benchmark does not test its distribution in the three hadronic observables.

\section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

% r5:316, rewritten around the three findings (ledger L-21; W-2).
This implementation performs simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables, without a separate binned response tensor for each projection. The two-dimensional result agrees with the published measurement in normalization but differs in shape for an unidentified reason. The five-observable result agrees with the two-dimensional result in each muon-kinematic quadrant. The reported cells depend on the unfolding algorithm. Until the coverage, the prior dependence, the numerical stability, and the run-period effect are resolved, the sixteen-cell output is an illustration of the method; its limitations are listed in section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

% r5:333-341, item 1 cut to the statements not already made with their results. Items 2-5 complete the list (#1025, A7): items 2 and 4 carry the qualifications of the conclusions' first paragraph, item 3 section 6.1's result, item 5 section 3.3's statement of the tests' detector response.
\begin{enumerate}
\item No reported interval has validated coverage. The fake-data tests assess only bias. The first coverage test of the five-observable covariance did not run to a result. The second test of section~\ref{sec:ll5d} grades the procedure's covariance at two truths. The procedure's two-dimensional covariance under-covers at both held-out tunes (section~\ref{sec:calib}). The per-cell coverage readings cited are pointwise, for each reported quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them. The joint readings grade the fraction of pseudo-experiments whose $\chi^2$ lies in a region fixed in advance.
\item Recovery within the reported uncertainty is not achieved for every generator-sized truth change tested, and the unfolded result retains part of its prior (section~\ref{sec:prior}; table~\ref{tab:outcomes}).
\item The estimator fails its data-stability requirement under rounding-level changes of its inputs; an ensemble-averaged variant that meets it on the pseudo-data used to test stability is worse at one truth and is not adopted (section~\ref{sec:cov5d}).
\item The data show a run-period inconsistency, present before unfolding, of unknown cause (section~\ref{sec:closure2d}).
\item The simulated detector response is not varied in the closure, deformation and fake-data tests (sections~\ref{sec:strategy} and~\ref{sec:fd16}), beyond the descriptive closures with a shifted recoil response of section~\ref{sec:fd16}; the coverage tests vary it only through the detector-systematic uncertainties they draw (sections~\ref{sec:calib} and~\ref{sec:ll5d}). All use simulated pseudo-data, so none tests the detector model against data.
\item Four of the sixteen cells, \fvval{h2-lowstab-cells}, have a diagonal fraction (section~\ref{sec:data}) of only \fvval{h2-lowstab-min}--\fvval{h2-lowstab-max}.
