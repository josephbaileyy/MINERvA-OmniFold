\section{Introduction}

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon
and hadronic observables.  Their interpretation depends on correcting detector
response and assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure.
Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration
matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but the response tensor grows
rapidly when additional observables are reported.  OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned
feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation
\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}.  This makes simultaneous
higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned
response tensor for every projection.  Unbinned unfolding has been applied to
collider data, for example eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at
H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at
ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}; see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey.  In
neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector
sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.  We are not aware of an application to
neutrino-scattering data.

We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the
MINERvA open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  We first
reproduce the published double-differential charged-current inclusive cross
section in transverse and longitudinal muon momentum,
$d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, then add available
hadronic energy $\Eavail$, three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and hadronic
invariant mass $W$ to obtain simultaneous three-, four-, and five-observable
central values.  Multi-differential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}.
The observables share reconstructed inputs: $\Eavail$, $q_{3}$, and $W$ are all
built from reconstructed hadronic energy.

We assess the practical reliability of this construction on data.  The
two-dimensional reproduction provides an external reference.  Projection
anchors, injected-shape tests, background-inclusive pseudo-experiments, and
uncertainty ensembles test consistency, shape recovery, and sensitivity to
background treatment, regularization, and estimator seeds as observables are
added (Secs.~\ref{sec:validation} and~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status}).  Together
they identify which parts of the construction work and which limit a
five-dimensional measurement with total uncertainties.

As an application, we test specified generator predictions.  The published
muon-kinematic measurement already disfavours these generator
families~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.  We calibrate each test through the full
analysis chain, using a variant with unbinned background refinement, so that the
estimator's bias toward its prior enters each simulated null distribution
(Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}).  The tests hold under six stated conditions
(Sec.~\ref{sec:conditions}); an
omitted calorimetric recoil-response uncertainty and unresolved fine-grid
convergence limit their physical interpretation.  Matched coarse-projection
tests assess whether the joint information adds discrimination.
Sections~\ref{sec:data} and~\ref{sec:method} describe the data and estimators,
Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} the validation, Sec.~\ref{sec:central} the central
values, Sec.~\ref{sec:joint} the joint tests, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status} the status of a five-dimensional
uncertainty.  The companion analysis note~\cite{AnalysisNote2026} gives the
complete validation, uncertainty, and joint-test records.

\section{Data, signal, and simulation}
\label{sec:data}

The analysis uses the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current
run periods, corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target and an
average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, from the
MINERvA open-data release of pre-selected analysis ntuples
(AnaTuples)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  The detector, including the downstream
MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}.  The files analysed are an earlier production of
that release; their names and sizes are recorded for reproduction
(see Data availability).  The signal definition, fiducial volume, flux
treatment~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn}, and reconstruction-level selection follow
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.  After selection, the sample contains $4.12$
million data events, and the simulation contains $32.8$ million true signal
events, generated with GENIE~2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} and the MINERvA
Tune~v1, which reweights GENIE with a non-resonant pion reduction, a
random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil
two-particle--two-hole (2p2h) enhancement~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}.

The unfolded observables are defined at truth level.  Besides the muon
momentum components $\pT$ and $\pPar$, they are the three-momentum transfer
$q_{3}=|\vec{q}\,|$ from the generator, the hadronic invariant mass
$W=\sqrt{M^{2}+2M(E_{\nu}-E_{\mu})-Q^{2}}$ with the struck-nucleon mass $M$, and
the available energy
\begin{equation}
  \Eavail=\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV})
  +\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma},
\end{equation}
in which protons contribute kinetic energy, charged pions their energy minus an
inherited constant of \SI{135}{MeV} rather than the charged-pion mass (an
uncorrected deviation whose effect has not been assessed), and neutral pions and
photons their total energy; all other final-state particles, including neutrons,
contribute nothing.  Reconstructed
$\Eavail$ is the calorimetric recoil energy in the tracker and electromagnetic
calorimeter after removal of the muon's energy deposits.  Reconstructed $q_{3}$
and $W$ combine the muon kinematics with a separate calorimetric estimate of
the energy transfer, so the five reconstructed inputs share information and do
not by themselves establish five independently constrained physical
directions.  The implemented
reconstructed and truth definitions are given in Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}.

% note sec_3d.tex eq:eavail-implemented (the 135 MeV charged-pion term) and scalar_kinematics.tex
% eq:shared-reco-inputs, which the article input until 2026-10-11; sec_eavailw.tex sec:eavailw-axis (truth W).

Event selection is held fixed as the scalar classifier grows from
$(\pT,\pPar)$ to $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_3,W)$. It requires a tracker vertex
and a MINOS-matched muon; adding features does not add selected events or
relax the signal definition. The external
generator predictions compared below are the untuned GENIE~2.12.10 on CH without (``GENIE
CV'') and with (``GENIE + MEC'') the Valencia 2p2h
component~\cite{Nieves:2011pp}, which models meson-exchange currents (MEC),
and NuWro~21.09~\cite{Golan:2012wx} and GiBUU~2019~\cite{Buss:2011mx}, both on
carbon.
The GENIE and NuWro predictions use a corrected flux sampling (an exact event-by-event
reweighting to the analysis flux, with added 50--\SI{100}{GeV} samples);
the GiBUU sample stops at $E_{\nu}\approx\SI{20}{GeV}$.

\section{Unbinned unfolding}
\label{sec:method}

Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification steps.  A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and
defines a likelihood-ratio weight.  The event pairing transfers that weight to
truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a
smooth reweighting to the next iteration.  Cross sections are formed by
histogramming the reweighted truth events in a declared reporting binning and
dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width.

All four estimator configurations use five OmniFold iterations with
gradient-boosted decision-tree classifiers~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn,Ke:2017lightgbm}, and each result
names the one it uses.  The two-dimensional
central value uses exact-split scikit-learn boosting with the purity-based
background subtraction.  The three- to five-dimensional central values use
LightGBM at matched nominal capacity with the same subtraction, as do the
two-dimensional uncertainty ensembles; that covariance therefore belongs to a
different estimator from the two-dimensional central value, and whether it also
describes that central value has not been measured.  The joint tests
(Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}) use a deterministic LightGBM variant, selected before the
joint-test design was frozen, whose unbinned negative-weight background
treatment and higher-capacity refinement classifier together remove most of the
purity method's nominal-truth bias in the highest-$W$ cells
(Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).  Classifier settings are given in
Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}.

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

In closure tests, truth denotes the known simulated target and the prior is
the initial truth distribution; the response maps truth to selected
observations. Closure measures recovery under a specified deformation.
Coverage measures how often intervals contain their target over repeated
experiments, testing the estimator and interval construction jointly.
Missed-event bookkeeping is an invariant check, and iteration dependence is
a stability diagnostic. Neither establishes recovery or coverage.

\subsection{Two-dimensional reproduction}

The two-dimensional result provides a stringent end-to-end validation.  Its
integrated cross section is \SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon}, compared with
\SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon} in the published analysis, a ratio of
$\ratioTot$.  Of the 205 reported bins, \SI{\binsTen}{\percent} agree within
\SI{10}{\percent}; the standardized bin differences formed with the published
uncertainties have an RMS of $\pullRMS$; against the full published covariance
the indicative distance is $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiPaper$, a correlated
shape difference along the low-$\pT$ peak ridge.  Figure~\ref{fig:validation}
shows the principal one-dimensional projections.  Both results derive from the
same data, making this a direct test of the unfolding procedure.  The published
covariance sets the scale of the displayed differences; a significance for
their difference would additionally require the cross-covariance of the two
estimates.

\subsection{Two-dimensional uncertainty and its coverage}
\label{sec:uq2d}

The standalone covariance combines systematic-universe, statistical, and
training-seed blocks.  Its median relative uncertainty is
\SI{\uqMedian}{\percent}, compared with \SI{\uqPaper}{\percent} for the
publication.  These ensembles use LightGBM, not the exact-split estimator of
the quoted central value (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).  At the statistical
ensemble's training seed the two central values differ by a per-bin median of
\SI{0.97}{\percent}, 1.3 times the per-bin standard deviation of the
statistical block; no block of the covariance carries this difference, and
whether the covariance describes the exact-split central value has not been
measured.  Coverage of the total remains untested.

The statistical block, as first built, undercovered in its tails in
\covTwoDNtoy{} pre-registered closure pseudo-experiments against a fixed
truth: pooled over experiments and bins, \SI{\covTwoDCTwoPctOne}{\percent} of
standardized residuals fell within two standard deviations, against
\SI{95.4}{\percent} nominal.  The main cause, a completeness division that the
bootstrap replicas carried and the central value does not, was removed.  The
rebuilt band, now the quoted one, has a median statistical uncertainty of
\SI{0.674}{\percent} and leaves the total median at \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent}.
Rescoring the same pseudo-experiments against it, which is post hoc and partly
circular, removes the tail undercoverage: \SI{97.7}{\percent} fall within two
standard deviations and \SI{78.1}{\percent} within one, so the rebuilt band is
wider than the toy scatter, for reasons not established (a post hoc
diagnostic points to under-scatter of the closure pseudo-experiments, whose
simulation-side fluctuation shares the band's resampling mechanism).  It has
not been independently re-tested.  An assessment of the saved products also
finds construction defects, among them a truth-input mismatch between the
adopted systematic sweep and its reference and unpropagated
background-template statistics~\cite{TwoDFollowup2026}; the printed budget has
not been revised for them, and their effect is unmeasured.  Validated total
intervals over the full reporting domain require new inputs and construction
repairs~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}.

\subsection{Higher-dimensional central values}

Every higher-dimensional central-value construction is tested against its
lower-dimensional predecessor.  The three-, four-, and five-observable marginal
normalizations agree at the approximately $1$--$2\%$ level, and a closure test
recovers an injected $\Eavail$ distortion.  These closure tests reweight the
simulated signal only.  End-to-end pseudo-experiments that include the
purity-based background subtraction reproduce the input at nominal truth
except in the highest-$W$ cells, which are biased by up to \SI{4}{\percent} in
simulation, about one quoted standard deviation in one $(\Eavail,W)$ cell; the
quoted uncertainties omit this bias.  The unbinned negative-weight background
treatment, as first measured with a deterministic variant of the
five-dimensional LightGBM production estimator, reduces it to below \SI{0.3}{\percent}, leaving up to
\SI{1}{\percent} in some joint cells; on data the two treatments
differ by at most \SI{1.4}{\percent}.
% Source: OUTCOME-20260925-s5c-purity-background-bias-at-high-W.md; VL149,
% VL151, VL152; KNOWN_ISSUES 75.

The unfolding follows a generator-sized change of the $\Eavail$ shape only
partly.  When the true shape is reweighted by the GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio (a test
deformation of generator size), the $(\Eavail,W)$ cells deviate from it by
several percent in the largest cells and by up to \SI{74}{\percent} at high
$W$, with or without background, so background subtraction alone does not
explain the residual.  This regularization bias lies outside the quoted uncertainty
construction and persists across the tested iteration and capacity scans; for
other fixed generator shapes, measured with the joint-test estimator, it is
\SIrange{16}{31}{\percent} in the most affected $(\Eavail,W)$ cell and within
the quoted total uncertainty in about half of those cells.  Input changes at
the level of floating-point rounding move the unfolded values on data by up to
a few statistical standard deviations; for the joint-test estimator this
spread lies inside the data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty, but it limits
how exactly the central values can be reproduced.  An end-to-end coverage test
of bias-corrected statistical intervals from a deterministic variant of the
estimator failed its predeclared criterion at
nominal truth and under an $\Eavail$ tilt, so frequentist coverage of the
five-dimensional intervals remains unestablished.  In this implementation,
then, agreement with the marginal anchors coexists with substantial
regularization bias, and model dependence and uncertainty calibration remain
limiting; this motivates the full-chain calibrated comparisons of
Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}.

\section{Central-value results}
\label{sec:central}

The $\Eavail$ dimension provides an independent view of the established
low-recoil discrepancy~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil,MINERvA:2022incl}.  GENIE,
the MINERvA Tune~v1, NuWro, and GiBUU all underpredict the unfolded central
value at low $\Eavail$, between the quasielastic and $\Delta$ peaks.  Pion
final-state-interaction (FSI) variations (same-sample ratios computed with GENIE
before its flux correction) change $d\sigma/d\Eavail$ by less than one percent, whereas the empirical
Valencia 2p2h contribution fills \SI{52}{\percent} of the
data--GENIE gap below $\Eavail=\SI{0.4}{GeV}$.  The conclusion about FSI applies
to the chosen pion variations.  Adding $q_{3}$ resolves
the momentum-transfer dependence of this feature: on the maximal common
$(\Eavail,q_{3})$ grid, the central value lies within \SI{10}{\percent} of the
dedicated published low-recoil measurement~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} (ratios $1.09$
and $1.06$ in its two cells).  That measurement defines true $\Eavail$ more
broadly, counting every final-state particle except neutrons, several species of
which our definition omits; in our simulation this moves about \SI{11}{\percent} of our lowest
true-$\Eavail$ bin out of it, with the sign of the residual, but its effect on
the two common cells is uncomputed, so the comparison is not quantitative.

\section{Calibrated joint tests of generator predictions}
\label{sec:joint}

\subsection{Hypotheses, statistics, and calibration}

For each of five predictions $G$ (the MINERvA Tune~v1, GENIE CV, GENIE + MEC,
NuWro~21.09, and GiBUU~2019), the null hypothesis $H_{0}(G)$ is that the true cross section equals
$G$ on a fine five-dimensional grid, with the MINERvA Tune~v1's shape and $G$'s
finite-sample fluctuations below that grid.  We call this the hybrid null: it is
the hypothesis the calibration simulates, and for the Tune~v1, the analysis
simulation itself, it is $H_{0}$ exactly.  The tests are evaluated on a coarse
$3^{5}$ grid in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$ with edges
$\pT\in[0,0.55,0.85,4.5]\,\si{GeV/c}$, $\pPar\in[1.5,3.5,6,60]\,\si{GeV/c}$,
$\Eavail\in[0,0.4,1.5,100]\,\si{GeV}$, $q_{3}\in[0,1.2,2,100]\,\si{GeV}$ and
$W\in[0,1.4,2.2,100]\,\si{GeV}$, restricted to the \jtNcells{} supported cells;
for GiBUU, whose sample lacks high-energy neutrinos, the domain is the
\jtNcellsGiBUU{} cells with $\pPar<\SI{6}{GeV/c}$.
% Source: amendment 7 claims.family; nd-unfolding/s5p_stage1_inspect.py:45 (J_EDGES).

A total statistic $T_{\mathrm{tot}}=r^{\top}\mathcal{W}^{-1}r$ is formed from
the residual $r=F-\mu_{G}$ of the unfolded cell values $F$ and the prediction
$\mu_{G}$, and a shape statistic $T_{\mathrm{sh}}$ of the same form from the
normalized cell fractions, with the normalization Jacobian applied to
$\mathcal{W}$ and one cell dropped.  The metric
$\mathcal{W}=V+\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ adds the prediction's own
finite-sample variance to $V$, a Ledoit--Wolf-shrunk
covariance~\cite{LedoitWolf:2004} of \jtVn{} pilot pseudo-experiments at the
Tune~v1 null.  The metric influences each test's power; the null calibration
determines its size.
% Source: nd-unfolding/s5p_inference.py:30-49; nd-unfolding/s5p_joint.py:147-162;
% amendment 7 calibration.metric and metric_frozen.

The null distribution of each statistic is obtained from pseudo-experiments that
run the full analysis chain at the null truth.  Each draws Poisson pseudo-data
from one half of the simulation together with an independent background source,
refits the background-refinement classifier, and unfolds with the joint-test
estimator trained on the other half.  It also draws the declared nuisances:
one of \jtFluxUniverses{} flux universes, each of the \jtModelBands{}
interaction-model uncertainty bands at one of its universes, the weight-based detector bands
(including the GEANT4~\cite{GEANT4:2002zbu} hadron-interaction reweights), the
muon and beam-angle lateral bands through a linear surrogate, a
\SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization, the data's rounding-level noise, and the
predictions' finite-sample residual.  The data are unfolded once with the full
simulation.  The Monte Carlo $p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$
of $B$ null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic; it is never zero.
The claim $p$ value is the largest over declared variants that move the null
by an upper bound on the simulation-size difference between the
pseudo-experiments and the data unfold and by
$\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$, where $\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$ is the
last measured step of a fine-grid refinement (Sec.~\ref{sec:conditions}).  The
external nulls are strongly non-central, with non-centralities $\lambda$ of
\jtLambdaMin{}--\jtLambdaMax{} in these units, because the calibration
reproduces the estimator's pull toward its prior.

The ten tests are controlled at a familywise level $\alpha=\jtAlpha$ by the
Holm procedure~\cite{Holm:1979}, and a step rejects only when the 95\%
Clopper--Pearson interval~\cite{Clopper:1934} of $k/B$ lies entirely below its
Holm threshold.  Calibration proceeded in batches of \jtBatch{}
pseudo-experiments per null, to at most \jtBcap{}, until both of a null's
decisions were determined.  The hypotheses, statistics, variants, multiplicity
and stopping rules, and every input were frozen before any production
pseudo-experiment or observed statistic.  Two data-informed elements are
disclosed: reviewers of the design had seen descriptive per-cell residuals
before it was frozen, and a bounded extension of the production budget, which changed no statistic,
variant, threshold, or stopping rule, was decided while job-status outputs
exposed observed claim $p$ values; the Monte Carlo $p$ values remain valid at
the number of pseudo-experiments reached.  An independent recomputation with
separately written code agrees with the production evaluation in all
\jtRecompRows{} compared quantities.  The frozen contract and recorded result
are in Ref.~\cite{JointTestRecord2026}; the complete record is in the
analysis note~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}.

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:conditions}

Each decision holds under six conditions of the declared model, numbered as in
the frozen contract~\cite{JointTestRecord2026}.  (i) Below the fine grid the
null truth carries the Tune~v1's shapes, and the fine-grid convergence check has not
shown convergence: in the $L_{2}$ norm of the per-cell relative changes, the last
refinement step, from a twofold-merged grid to the fine grid, is
\jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax{} of the whole change from the coarse test grid, so the
residual below the fine grid is unmeasured and potentially material.  If the
step kept shrinking geometrically at a rate taken from the design review, whose
calculation is not recorded, the residual would be about one to three times the
last step; the claim variants $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$ take its
central estimate, and the report-only robustness variants, which do not enter
the decisions, use the upper end, $\pm\jtMoneKappaRobust\,\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$.  The measured $L_{2}$ ratios
also allow slower convergence.  The variants carry the rejections from the
simulated hybrid null to $G$ itself only if replacing the Tune~v1's shape below
the fine grid by $G$'s moves the noise-free unfolded result by no more than
$\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$ along $\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$.  (ii) The
detector model is the simulation's, varied only by the drawn detector bands and
the lateral surrogate; there is no band for the calorimetric recoil-energy
response (Sec.~\ref{sec:response}).  (iii) The interaction-model prior is the
analysis's \jtModelBands-band set, with negative weights of two bands clipped.
(iv) The pseudo-experiments use half of the simulation and the data all of it;
the resulting shift, at most about 0.07 null standard deviations but not
always consistent with zero, enters as the declared upper-bound variant.  (v) GiBUU lacks $E_{\nu}>\SI{20}{GeV}$, and
its out-of-domain truth enters the migrations into its domain.  (vi) The
predictions' own finite samples enter the metric and the simulated residuals.
The Monte Carlo $p$ values are exact for the simulated null to the extent that
the pseudo-experiment chain reproduces the analysis applied to the data; the
independent recomputation verifies the calculation, not that adequacy.

\subsection{Hadronic-response condition}
\label{sec:response}

MINERvA's recoil-energy analyses assign a hadronic-energy response uncertainty
derived from test-beam calorimetry~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam}, separate from
the GEANT hadron-interaction reweights~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq,MINERvA:2022incl};
in the low-recoil measurement it rises to \SI{10}{\percent} at the highest
three-momentum transfers~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl}.  The declared nuisance model
has no such band.  A response mismodelling could move each observed statistic
in either direction, and a band drawn in the calibration would also broaden
and could shift each null distribution; the net effect on each $p$ value is
unmeasured.  This matters for the rejections: the published muon-kinematic
comparison, which does not use reconstructed recoil energy, also disfavours
these generator families~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, but within this analysis the
calibrated tests restricted to the matched $(\pT,\pPar)$ projection do not
reject GENIE~2.12.10 CV ($p=\woneGcvPtTot$ in total, $\woneGcvPtShape$ in
shape; a post hoc observation)~\cite{JointTestProjections2026}, so the
information that rejects it involves the hadronic axes, on which a response
band would act.

In an exploratory sensitivity study~\cite{JointTestRecoil2026}, the data are
re-unfolded with the simulation's reconstructed recoil energy scaled by
$\pm\SI{4}{\percent}$, the level of data--simulation agreement reported for
test-beam protons, pions, and electrons of
0.35--\SI{2.0}{GeV/c}~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam} (neutrons and higher-momentum
hadrons, which matter at high $W$, lie outside that sample), and the frozen
tests are re-evaluated against the frozen null distributions.  At both signs every
rejection holds: the observed statistics move by up to about
\SI{11}{\percent}, nine tests keep $k=0$, and NuWro's shape test changes from
$k=1$ to $k=3$ of $1751$ at $+4\%$ ($p=0.0023$, against a Holm threshold of
$0.05$).  The null distributions were not regenerated with the variation, and
particle-species, energy-dependent, and resolution effects, a response
difference applied before calibration, and larger variations were not tested,
so the rejections remain conditional on the simulation's hadronic response.

\section{Status of a five-dimensional uncertainty}
\label{sec:uncertainty-status}

One scalar five-dimensional covariance has been adopted under a documented
exception that covers that product alone, and its \zprojCells-cell
$(\Eavail,W)$ projection has been built; it is separate from the joint-test
calibration.  No covariance-based generator significance is quoted, because of
measured estimator-seed sensitivity.  In two companion covariances built at two
estimator-seed settings (products distinct from the adopted one), varying the seed moves the projected uncertainty by
\SI{\sprojMeasured}{\percent}, against a \SI{\sprojBound}{\percent} limit fixed
before production.  The movement did not fall with ensemble size over the range
tested (\SI{\seedEffectNforty}{\percent}, \SI{\seedEffectNeighty}{\percent},
and \SI{\sprojMeasured}{\percent} at 40, 80, and 160 throws), whereas a
same-seed resampling floor fell from \SI{\floorNforty}{\percent} to
\SI{\floorNeighty}{\percent} between 40 and 80.  The comparison is a single
seed pair whose 40- and 80-throw points are nested subsets of the same 160
throws, so the seed-pair width and the behaviour at much larger ensembles are
unmeasured.  Five of the adopted covariance's 45 bands are built at a fixed
seed the variation cannot reach (\SI{\pinnedBandsSqrtTr}{\percent} of
$\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}\,C}$); their seed sensitivity, and its effect on the total
in either direction, is unmeasured.

A separate study assessed reporting definitions for a five-dimensional
measurement with qualified total uncertainties.  It admitted no definition that
could meet the predeclared useful-precision targets within its rules and
budget; for its fallback definition, the bounded model-dependence component
alone exceeded the total-width target.  The joint tests above were that study's
separately admitted inference branch and produced no new reportable
uncertainty.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This study applies OmniFold to MINERvA inclusive charged-current data,
reproduces the published two-dimensional result, and extends the construction
to five simultaneous observables.  These results differ in standing: the
two-dimensional comparison is an end-to-end test of the unfolding against a
measurement from the same data; the three- to five-dimensional central values
support only descriptive comparisons, without validated total-interval coverage
or a covariance-based generator significance; and the joint tests are
calibrated generator tests that are conditional on six stated conditions and
yield no new reportable uncertainty.  The higher-dimensional central values pass
marginal anchors and injected-shape closure tests and recover the established
low-recoil discrepancy.  Background-inclusive tests reveal a subtraction bias
that unbinned background refinement reduces, while regularization bias persists
under the tested generator-sized shape changes.  Coverage and estimator-seed studies
expose further limits: the rebuilt two-dimensional statistical band awaits an
independent coverage test, the transfer of the two-dimensional covariance to
the exact-split central value is unmeasured, and the measured model
dependence prevents a qualified five-dimensional measurement with total
uncertainties at useful precision.  These outcomes provide practical checks for future
multidimensional neutrino unfolding.

The generator comparisons illustrate calibration through the full analysis
chain with an estimator that retains substantial model dependence.  Under the stated
conditions, all ten total and shape hypotheses for the five predictions
are rejected at familywise level 0.05, and the report-only recovery of every
lost pseudo-experiment leaves the frozen decisions unchanged.  Each test also has $p<0.05$ in at
least one matched coarse projection, so additional discrimination from the
joint information remains undemonstrated.  The physical interpretation depends
on the simulation's calorimetric recoil response and on the assumed
convergence below the fine grid; extending it would require a response band
drawn through the calibration and a demonstrated bound on the residual below
the fine grid.
