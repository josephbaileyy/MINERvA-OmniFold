\section{Introduction and scope}
\label{sec:intro}

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon
and hadronic observables.  Their interpretation depends on correcting detector
response and assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure.
Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration
matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but the response tensor grows
rapidly when additional observables are reported.  OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned
feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation
\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}.  This makes simultaneous
higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned
response tensor for every projection.  Unbinned unfolding has been applied to
collider data, for example eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at
H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at
ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}; see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey.  In
neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector
sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.
% Methods rewrite fix round 1 (REVIEW-1 note 21; OUTLINE-DECISION sec. 1, no priority framing): the priority sentence
% cut (M8).

We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the
MINERvA open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  We first
reproduce the published double-differential charged-current inclusive cross
section in transverse and longitudinal muon momentum,
$d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, then add available
hadronic energy $\Eavail$, three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and hadronic
invariant mass $W$.  Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed
$(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five observables are simultaneous but not
independent (Sec.~\ref{sec:data}); we obtain simultaneous three-, four-, and
five-observable central values.

Multi-differential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}.

\emph{Scope.}  This paper studies one OmniFold implementation on one data
set.  The three- to five-observable central values are outputs of the
estimators of Sec.~\ref{sec:method} under the implemented observable
definitions, simulated detector response, background treatment, and
regularization; their total intervals have no validated coverage, and we
quote no covariance-based generator significance for them.  The generator
tests are conditional tests of explicitly simulated hybrid hypotheses.  Each
null hypothesis sets the cross section equal to a prediction on every cell of
a fine five-observable grid.  Below that grid the event composition is the
MINERvA Tune~v1 simulation's, varied by the drawn interaction-model universes
with every fine-cell integral held fixed.  The calibrated size is averaged
over the stated nuisance priors; it is not guaranteed at each fixed value of
the nuisance parameters.  The detector model has the implemented variations
but no calorimetric recoil-response uncertainty, and two of the predictions
are for a carbon target, the data for CH.

MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}.  The files analysed are an earlier production of
that release (see Data availability).

\section{Estimators and validation design}
\label{sec:method}

Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification steps.  A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and
defines a likelihood-ratio weight.  The event pairing transfers that weight to
truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a
smooth reweighting to the next iteration.  Cross sections are formed by
histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and
dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width.  Events that fail
reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate efficiency
correction is applied.
% Truth-only misses are appended, so the completeness is 1 (note sec_experiment.tex:88-89; sec_method.tex sec:method-xsec).
% Shortening: the flux integral (6.243e-8 cm^-2 per POT, 0-100 GeV after the nu-e constraint;
% README-gen5d-fluxfix.md:43-49, :74) and the nucleon count (3.235e30) moved to the note, sec_method.tex
% sec:method-xsec, which carries both with these sources.

Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the
classifier learns to match.  In the purity-based subtraction every data event in
reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight
$w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that
bin, so the target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$.  The unbinned
negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative
weight to the data, so the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise; a classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at
zero, converts the signed sample into non-negative weights before the first
iteration.  The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever
the background fraction varies within it (analysis note, appendix on
negative-weight background subtraction).

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known
simulated truth is recovered, with the prior, the initial truth distribution,
held fixed.  Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated
experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together; the
coverage tested here is pointwise, for each reported quantity separately, not
the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them.  The closure, deformation, and coverage
studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data,
which changes their reconstructed and truth distributions together and keeps
the detector response at fixed truth; the prior used to unfold them stays at
Tune~v1.  The envelope of Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status} instead reweights
the prior used to unfold the data.  Every study uses the analysis
simulation's detector response, except the calibration of the tests, which
also draws the detector bands.

A total statistic $T_{\mathrm{tot}}=r^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}r$ is formed from
the residual $r=F-\mu_{G}$ of the unfolded cell values $F$ and the prediction
$\mu_{G}$; it tests the rate and the shape together, and we call it the total
(rate-and-shape) test.  A shape statistic $T_{\mathrm{sh}}$ of the same form
uses the normalized cell fractions, with the whole metric transformed by the
normalization Jacobian at the unfolded values and one cell dropped.  The metric
$\Sigma=V+\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ adds the prediction's own
finite-sample variance to $V$, a Ledoit--Wolf-shrunk
covariance~\cite{LedoitWolf:2004} of \jtVn{} pilot pseudo-experiments at the
Tune~v1 null, whose shrinkage floors about 80 of the 109 nearly degenerate
correlation directions.  The metric influences each test's power; the null
calibration determines its size.

\subsection{Decision rule and familywise error}
\label{sec:calibration}

The Monte Carlo $p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$
of $B$ null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic; it is never zero.
Each decision uses the largest $p$ value over a set of shifted nulls fixed in
advance.  Each shifted null adds a fixed vector to every null draw: $cD$ with
$c\in\{0,\tfrac12,1\}$, where $D$ is an upper bound on the simulation-size
difference along the null's bias direction (half of the simulation in the
pseudo-experiments, all of it for the data), and $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$, where
$\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ is the change of the noise-free unfolded result in the
last step of a refinement of the null truth's grid (Sec.~\ref{sec:conditions}).

including the budget extensions.  The decision rule therefore controls the
familywise error rate of the ten decisions at 5\% under the stated
calibration assumptions; from these two assumptions alone, with a union bound

the analysis note's appendix section on the familywise error of the sequential

\subsection{Hadronic-response condition}
\label{sec:response}

MINERvA's recoil-energy analyses assign a hadronic-energy response uncertainty
derived from test-beam calorimetry~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam}, separate from
the GEANT hadron-interaction reweights~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq,MINERvA:2022incl}
(which the calibration's detector bands include); that response uncertainty rises to \SI{10}{\percent} at the highest three-momentum transfers of the
low-recoil measurement~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl}; the nuisance model of the
tests has no such band.  A response mismodelling could move each observed statistic
in either direction, and a band drawn in the calibration would also broaden
and could shift each null distribution; the net effect on each $p$ value has
not been measured.  This matters for the rejections: the published muon-kinematic
comparison, which does not use reconstructed recoil energy, also disfavours
these generator families~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, but within this analysis the
calibrated tests restricted to the matched $(\pT,\pPar)$ projection do not
reject GENIE~2.12.10 CV ($p=\woneGcvPtTot$ in total, $\woneGcvPtShape$ in
shape; a post hoc observation), so the
information that rejects it involves the hadronic axes, on which a response
band would act.

and NuWro's total-test rejection does not survive the hydrocarbon correction
robustly (Sec.~\ref{sec:joint-results}).  The decision rule controls the
familywise error rate at 5\% provided the calibration is exact; of that
exactness, the dependence on the unfolding-simulation size has been checked,
with no resolved change of the null distributions, but the rejection rate of
null pseudo-data processed like the data, which would need an independent
simulation sample, has not been measured.  The physical interpretation of the
tests depends on the simulation's calorimetric recoil response and on the
assumed convergence below the fine grid; extending it would require a response
band drawn through the calibration and a demonstrated bound on the residual
below the fine grid.

\emph{Why unfold before testing.}  Unfolded results can be compared with any
prediction at truth level without simulating each generator through the
detector, which is what makes the two-dimensional result reusable and the
higher-dimensional central values usable for qualitative comparison.
For the hypothesis tests the advantage is smaller.  A test at
reconstruction level would avoid the regularization bias, but it would still
need the same response model: each external prediction would have to be folded
through the analysis simulation's response, which is the hybrid construction of
Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}, with the same composition below the fine grid.  Our tests
instead calibrate the unfolded statistic within the implemented simulation
model, so the bias
enters the null distribution rather than the decision.  We have not compared
their power with that of a reconstruction-level test.
