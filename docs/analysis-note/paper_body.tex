\section{Introduction}

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon
and hadronic observables.  Their interpretation depends on correcting detector
response and assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure.
Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration
matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but the response tensor grows
rapidly when additional observables are reported.  OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned
feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation
\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}.  This makes simultaneous
higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned
response tensor for every projection.  Unbinned unfolding has been applied to
collider data, for example eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at
H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at
ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}; see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey.  In
neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector
sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.  We are not aware of an application to
neutrino-scattering data.

We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the
MINERvA open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  We first
reproduce the published double-differential charged-current inclusive cross
section in transverse and longitudinal muon momentum,
$d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, then add available
hadronic energy $\Eavail$, three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and hadronic
invariant mass $W$ to obtain simultaneous three-, four-, and five-observable
central values.  Multi-differential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}.
The observables share reconstructed inputs: $\Eavail$, $q_{3}$, and $W$ are all
built from reconstructed hadronic energy.

The study assesses the practical reliability of this construction on data.
Reproduction of the established two-dimensional result provides an external
reference.  Projection anchors and injected-shape tests assess consistency and
shape recovery as observables are added, while background-inclusive
pseudo-experiments and uncertainty ensembles expose sensitivity to background
treatment, regularization, and estimator seeds
(Secs.~\ref{sec:validation} and~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status}).
Together these checks identify which parts of the construction work and which
limit a five-dimensional measurement with total uncertainties
(Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status}).

Generator comparisons provide an application of the method.  The published
muon-kinematic measurement already disfavours these generator
families~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.  We use a variant with unbinned background
refinement to calibrate a comparison through the full analysis chain, so that
the estimator's bias toward its prior
enters each simulated null distribution (Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}).  The tests
evaluate specified predictions under six conditions, including the detector
and interaction-model assumptions.  An omitted calorimetric recoil-response
uncertainty and unresolved fine-grid convergence limit their physical
interpretation (Secs.~\ref{sec:conditions} and~\ref{sec:response}).  Matched
coarse-projection tests assess whether the joint information supplies additional
discrimination.  These studies address the practical checks needed when
extending an unbinned neutrino analysis to additional observables.
Sections~\ref{sec:data} and~\ref{sec:method} describe the data and estimators,
Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} the validation, Sec.~\ref{sec:central} the central
values, Sec.~\ref{sec:joint} the joint tests and their conditions, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status} the status of a five-dimensional
uncertainty.

\section{Data, signal, and simulation}
\label{sec:data}

The analysis uses the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current
run periods, corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target and an
average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, from the
MINERvA open-data release of pre-selected analysis ntuples
(AnaTuples)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  The detector, including the downstream
MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}.  The files analysed are an earlier production of
that release; their names and sizes are recorded for reproduction
(see Data availability).  The signal definition, fiducial volume, flux
treatment~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn}, and reconstruction-level selection follow
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.  After selection, the sample contains $4.12$
million data events, and the simulation contains $32.8$ million true signal
events, generated with GENIE~2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} and the MINERvA
Tune~v1, which reweights GENIE with a non-resonant pion reduction, a
random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil
two-particle--two-hole (2p2h) enhancement~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}.

Reconstructed $\Eavail$ is the calorimetric recoil energy in the tracker and
electromagnetic calorimeter after removal of the muon's energy deposits; the
reconstructed energy transfer $q_{0}$ is the calorimetric recoil energy, from
which $Q^{2}$, $q_{3}$, and $W$ follow with the muon kinematics.  The external
generator predictions compared below are GENIE~2.12.10 on CH without (``GENIE
CV'') and with (``GENIE + MEC'') the Valencia 2p2h
component~\cite{Nieves:2011pp}, which models meson-exchange currents (MEC),
NuWro~21.09~\cite{Golan:2012wx}, and GiBUU~2019~\cite{Buss:2011mx} on carbon.
The GENIE and NuWro predictions use a corrected flux sampling (an exact event-by-event
reweighting to the analysis flux, with added 50--\SI{100}{GeV} samples);
the GiBUU sample stops at $E_{\nu}\approx\SI{20}{GeV}$.

\section{Unbinned unfolding}
\label{sec:method}

Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification steps.  A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and
defines a likelihood-ratio weight.  The event pairing transfers that weight to
truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a
smooth reweighting to the next iteration.  Cross sections are formed by
histogramming the reweighted truth events in a declared reporting binning and
dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width.

Four estimator configurations appear in this paper, and each result names the
one it uses.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Two-dimensional central value} (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}): five
iterations of depth-three gradient-boosted decision trees with 100 trees, fitted
with exact-split scikit-learn~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn} gradient boosting, and a
purity-based background subtraction.
\item \emph{Three- to five-dimensional central values}
(Secs.~\ref{sec:validation}--\ref{sec:central}, Figs.~\ref{fig:joint}
and~\ref{fig:generator-context}): the same five-iteration configuration fitted
with LightGBM~\cite{Ke:2017lightgbm} at matched nominal capacity, with the
purity-based background subtraction.
\item \emph{Two-dimensional uncertainty ensembles}: the finalized systematic-universe
and Poisson-bootstrap ensembles use LightGBM at matched nominal capacity, so
the uncertainty ensembles use a different implementation from the
two-dimensional central-value estimator.
\item \emph{Joint-test estimator} (Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}): a deterministic
LightGBM OmniFold, five iterations, with an unbinned negative-weight background
treatment whose refinement classifier uses 400 trees and 31 leaves.  The
negative-weight treatment reduces the purity method's nominal-truth bias in the
highest-$W$ cells about fifteen-fold, and the larger refinement classifier
removes most of the background-related bias that remains; the configuration was
selected before the joint-test design was frozen.
\end{itemize}
The use of AI tools in this work is described in the Acknowledgments.

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

\subsection{Two-dimensional reproduction}

The two-dimensional result provides a stringent end-to-end validation.  Its
integrated cross section is \SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon}, compared with
\SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon} in the published analysis, a ratio of
$\ratioTot$.  Of the 205 reported bins, \SI{\binsTen}{\percent} agree within
\SI{10}{\percent}; the standardized bin differences formed with the published
uncertainties have an RMS of $\pullRMS$; against the full published covariance
the indicative distance is $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiPaper$, a correlated
shape difference along the low-$\pT$ peak ridge.  Figure~\ref{fig:validation}
shows the principal one-dimensional projections.  Both results derive from the
same data, making this a direct test of the unfolding procedure.  The published
covariance sets the scale of the displayed differences; a significance for
their difference would additionally require the cross-covariance of the two
estimates.

\subsection{Two-dimensional uncertainty and its coverage}

The standalone covariance combines matched-central-value systematic universes
with statistical and training-seed blocks.  Its median relative uncertainty is
\SI{\uqMedian}{\percent}, compared with \SI{\uqPaper}{\percent} for the
publication.  Coverage of this total remains untested.  Its statistical block,
as first built, was tested against a fixed truth in \covTwoDNtoy{} pre-registered
closure pseudo-experiments and undercovered in its tails:
\SI{\covTwoDCTwoPctOne}{\percent} of standardized residuals fell within two standard
deviations, against \SI{95.4}{\percent} nominal, while the one-standard-deviation
fraction, $\covTwoDCOne$, was nominal; the pull mean was consistent with zero.  The
main cause, a completeness division that the bootstrap replicas carried and the
central value does not, was found and removed.  The rebuilt statistical band, now
the quoted one, has a median statistical uncertainty of \SI{0.674}{\percent} and
leaves the total median at \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent}.
Rescoring the same closure pseudo-experiments against the rebuilt band, which is
post hoc and partly circular, removes the tail undercoverage: \SI{97.7}{\percent}
fall within two standard deviations and \SI{78.1}{\percent} within one, both above
their nominal windows, so the rebuilt band is wider than the toy scatter.  The
residual excess width lies in the data-statistics component and is not explained
by background purity.  Its cause is not established: a post hoc diagnostic is
consistent with the closure pseudo-experiments under-scattering rather than the
data bootstrap over-scattering, but the decisive held-out test has not been run.
The rebuilt band has not been independently re-tested.

\subsection{Higher-dimensional central values}

Every higher-dimensional central-value construction is tested against its
lower-dimensional predecessor.  The three-, four-, and five-observable marginal
normalizations agree at the approximately $1$--$2\%$ level, and a closure test
recovers an injected $\Eavail$ distortion.  These closure tests reweight the
simulated signal and leave the background treatment outside their scope.
End-to-end pseudo-experiments that include the purity-based background subtraction
reproduce the input at nominal truth except in the highest-$W$ cells, which are
biased by up to \SI{4}{\percent} in simulation, about one quoted standard
deviation in one $(\Eavail,W)$ cell; the quoted uncertainties omit this bias.
The unbinned negative-weight background treatment, as first
measured with a deterministic variant of the five-dimensional LightGBM production estimator, reduces it to
below \SI{0.3}{\percent}, leaving up to \SI{1}{\percent} in some joint cells; on
data the two treatments differ by at most \SI{1.4}{\percent}.
% Source: OUTCOME-20260925-s5c-purity-background-bias-at-high-W.md; VL149,
% VL151, VL152; KNOWN_ISSUES 75.

The same pseudo-experiments show that the unfolding follows a generator-sized
change of the $\Eavail$ shape only partly.  In a test, the true shape is reweighted
by the GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio.  A flux-sampling error in the GENIE input limits
this ratio's use to a test deformation of generator size.  The $(\Eavail,W)$
cells then deviate from it by several percent in the largest cells and by up to \SI{74}{\percent}
at high $W$, with or without background.  This regularization bias lies outside
the quoted uncertainty construction and persists across the tested iteration
and capacity scans; for other fixed generator shapes, measured with the joint-test
estimator, it is \SIrange{16}{31}{\percent} in the most affected $(\Eavail,W)$ cell
and within the quoted total uncertainty in about half of those cells.  The unfolded values on data also move by up to a few
statistical standard deviations under input changes at the level of
floating-point rounding.  For the joint-test estimator this spread lies inside
the data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty, which refits every replica, and
well below the total uncertainty, but it limits how exactly the central values
can be reproduced.  An end-to-end coverage test of bias-corrected
statistical intervals from a deterministic variant of the estimator failed its
predeclared criterion at nominal truth and under an $\Eavail$ tilt.
Frequentist coverage of the five-dimensional intervals therefore remains
unestablished.

These checks probe distinct properties of the estimator.  Marginal anchors
assess consistency across dimensions; injected-shape tests measure the recovery
of imposed structure; background-inclusive pseudo-experiments expose the bias
introduced by the subtraction procedure.  The uncertainty and rounding studies
test statistical calibration and numerical stability.  In this implementation,
agreement with the anchors coexists with substantial regularization bias.
Background refinement reduces one source of bias, while model dependence and
uncertainty calibration remain limiting.  These findings motivate the
full-chain calibrated comparisons in Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}.

\section{Central-value results}
\label{sec:central}

The $\Eavail$ dimension provides an independent view of the established
low-recoil discrepancy~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil,MINERvA:2022incl}.  GENIE,
the MINERvA Tune~v1, NuWro, and GiBUU all underpredict the unfolded central
value at low $\Eavail$, between the quasielastic and $\Delta$ peaks.  Pion
final-state-interaction (FSI) variations (same-sample ratios computed with GENIE
before its flux correction) change $d\sigma/d\Eavail$ by less than one percent, whereas the empirical
Valencia 2p2h contribution fills \SI{52}{\percent} of the
data--GENIE gap below $\Eavail=\SI{0.4}{GeV}$.  The conclusion about FSI applies
to the chosen pion variations.  Adding $q_{3}$ resolves
the momentum-transfer dependence of this feature: on the maximal common
$(\Eavail,q_{3})$ grid, the central value lies within \SI{10}{\percent} of the
dedicated published low-recoil measurement~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} (ratios $1.09$
and $1.06$ in its two cells).  The two analyses define true $\Eavail$
differently: this analysis sums the proton and charged-pion kinetic energies
(the latter computed with the neutral-pion mass) and the total energies of
neutral pions and photons, whereas Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} also counts every
other final-state particle except neutrons, less a nucleon mass for strange
baryons.  In this analysis's simulation, the broader definition moves about
\SI{11}{\percent} of the population of our lowest true-$\Eavail$ bin
($\Eavail<\SI{0.1}{GeV}$) out of it.  The two common cells span our three lowest
bins ($\Eavail<\SI{0.4}{GeV}$), for which the shift remains uncomputed.  The
single-bin shift has the sign of the residual; its propagation to the common
cells would be needed to compare the two observable definitions quantitatively.

\section{Calibrated joint tests of generator predictions}
\label{sec:joint}

\subsection{Hypotheses, statistics, and calibration}

For each of five predictions $G$ (the MINERvA Tune~v1, GENIE CV, GENIE + MEC,
NuWro~21.09, and GiBUU~2019), the null hypothesis $H_{0}(G)$ is that the true cross section equals
$G$ on a fine five-dimensional grid, with the MINERvA Tune~v1's shape and $G$'s
finite-sample fluctuations below that grid.  We call this the hybrid null: it is
the hypothesis the calibration simulates.  For the Tune~v1, the analysis
simulation itself, this is $H_{0}$ exactly.  The tests are evaluated on a coarse
$3^{5}$ grid in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$ with edges
$\pT\in[0,0.55,0.85,4.5]\,\si{GeV/c}$, $\pPar\in[1.5,3.5,6,60]\,\si{GeV/c}$,
$\Eavail\in[0,0.4,1.5,100]\,\si{GeV}$, $q_{3}\in[0,1.2,2,100]\,\si{GeV}$ and
$W\in[0,1.4,2.2,100]\,\si{GeV}$, restricted to the \jtNcells{} supported cells;
for GiBUU, whose sample lacks high-energy neutrinos, the domain is the
\jtNcellsGiBUU{} cells with $\pPar<\SI{6}{GeV/c}$.
% Source: amendment 7 claims.family; nd-unfolding/s5p_stage1_inspect.py:45 (J_EDGES).

Two statistics are formed from the unfolded cell values $F$ and the prediction
$\mu_{G}$: a total statistic $T_{\mathrm{tot}}=r^{\top}\mathcal{W}^{-1}r$ with
$r=F-\mu_{G}$, and a shape statistic $T_{\mathrm{sh}}$ of the same form on the
normalized cell fractions, with the normalization Jacobian applied to $\mathcal{W}$
and one cell dropped.  The metric
$\mathcal{W}=V+\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ adds the
prediction's own finite-sample variance to $V$, a Ledoit--Wolf-shrunk
covariance~\cite{LedoitWolf:2004} of \jtVn{} pilot pseudo-experiments at the
Tune~v1 null.  This pilot covariance defines the metric used to compare
hypotheses.  The null calibration determines the test's size, while the metric
influences its power.
% Source: nd-unfolding/s5p_inference.py:30-49; nd-unfolding/s5p_joint.py:147-162;
% amendment 7 calibration.metric and metric_frozen.

The null distribution of each statistic is obtained from pseudo-experiments that
run the full analysis chain at the null truth.  Each draws Poisson pseudo-data
from one half of the simulation together with an independent background source,
refits the background-refinement classifier, and unfolds with the joint-test
estimator trained on the other half.  Every experiment also draws one of
\jtFluxUniverses{} flux universes and every one of the \jtModelBands{}
interaction-model uncertainty bands at one of its universes.  It further draws the weight-based
detector bands (MINOS muon-matching efficiency and GEANT4~\cite{GEANT4:2002zbu}
neutron, pion, and proton interaction reweights), and the five muon and beam-angle lateral bands through a linear
surrogate, together with a \SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization draw, the data's rounding
noise taken from 20 real-data re-unfolds with rounding-level input changes, and the
predictions' finite-sample residual.  The data are unfolded once with the full
simulation.  The Monte Carlo
$p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$ of $B$ null pseudo-experiments
reach the observed statistic; it is never zero.  The claim $p$ value is the
largest over declared variants that move the null by an upper bound on the
simulation-size difference between the pseudo-experiments and the data unfold
and by $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$.  Here $\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$, the
last refinement step, is the change of the noise-free unfolded result when the
null truth is refined from a twofold-merged grid to the fine grid, and
$\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$ is the central extrapolated estimate of the
residual below the fine grid (Sec.~\ref{sec:conditions}).  The
external nulls are strongly non-central, with non-centralities $\lambda$ of
\jtLambdaMin{}--\jtLambdaMax{} in these units, because the calibration
reproduces the estimator's pull toward its prior.

% prefreeze_measurements.summary.

The ten tests (two per prediction) are controlled at a familywise level
$\alpha=\jtAlpha$ by the Holm procedure~\cite{Holm:1979}.  Under the testing
assumptions, this limits the probability of falsely rejecting at least one true
hypothesis among the ten tests to 5\%.  A determinacy requirement also applies:
a step rejects only when the 95\% Clopper--Pearson
interval~\cite{Clopper:1934} of $k/B$ lies entirely below its Holm threshold.
Calibration proceeded in batches of \jtBatch{} pseudo-experiments per null, to
at most \jtBcap{}, under a sequential rule that stopped a null once both of its
decisions were determined.  The hypotheses, statistics, variants, multiplicity
rule, stopping rule, and every input were frozen before any production
pseudo-experiment or observed statistic.  Two data-informed elements are
disclosed.  Reviewers of the design had seen descriptive per-cell residuals
before it was frozen, and the status outputs of the job controller exposed
observed claim $p$ values during production.  A bounded extension of the production
budget was decided
while they were visible, to let the sequential rule reach its frozen stop rather
than a budget stop.  No statistic, variant, threshold, or stopping rule was changed,
and the Monte Carlo $p$ values remain valid at the number of pseudo-experiments
reached.

\subsection{Conditions}
\label{sec:conditions}

The joint tests evaluate the simple hypotheses $H_{0}(G)$ defined above.
Each decision holds under six conditions of the declared model.  These
conditions specify the scope of the calibration and the assumptions needed to
interpret the rejections.  They are numbered as in the frozen
contract~\cite{JointTestRecord2026} and grouped here by kind.  The sensitivity
variants below probe prescribed shifts within these tests.

\emph{Numerical convergence.}  (i) Below the fine grid the null truth carries
the Tune~v1's shapes, and the fine-grid convergence check is not converged.  In
the $L_{2}$ norm of the per-cell relative changes, the last refinement, from a
twofold-merged grid to the fine grid, is \jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax{} of the whole
change from the coarse test grid to the fine grid.  In that norm the steps are
therefore not shown to shrink, and the residual below the fine grid is
unmeasured and potentially material.  The frozen design takes the step in a
proxy of the test metric to shrink by \jtMoneWnormMin--\jtMoneWnormMax{} per
halving, a design-review value whose calculation is not recorded.  If it kept
shrinking geometrically at that rate, the residual would be about one to three
times the last step, which sets the claim variants at
$\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$ and the robustness check, which is
report-only and does not enter the decisions, at
$\pm\jtMoneKappaRobust\,\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$.  This extrapolation assumes
geometric convergence.  The measured $L_{2}$ ratios also allow slower rates
and larger residuals.  Measured before any
observed statistic, the claim variants move the external nulls by
\jtMoneClaimSdMin--\jtMoneClaimSdMax{} null standard deviations and the
robustness variants by \jtMoneRobSdMin--\jtMoneRobSdMax{}.  For the hybrid null
as simulated the residual plays no role; the variants carry the rejections over
to $G$ itself only if replacing the Tune~v1's shape below the fine grid by
$G$'s moves the noise-free unfolded result by no more than
$\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$ along $\delta_{\mathrm{M1}}$.  (vi) The
predictions' own finite samples enter the metric and the simulated residuals.

\emph{Calibration validity.}  The Monte Carlo $p$ values are exact for the
simulated null to the extent that the pseudo-experiment chain reproduces the
analysis applied to the data.  Two known departures are bounded.  (iv) The
pseudo-experiments use half of the simulation and the data unfold all of it;
the resulting shift, at most about 0.07 null standard deviations but not always
consistent with zero, enters as the declared upper-bound variant.  The data
unfold's rounding-level nondeterminism, measured over the 20 re-unfolds, leaves
every observed $p$ value unchanged except NuWro's shape test (2/1752 to 3/1752).
The independent recomputation verifies the calculation from the stored
products; calibration adequacy remains a separate assumption.

\emph{Physical-model adequacy.}  (ii) The detector model is the simulation's,
varied only by the drawn detector bands and the linear surrogate for the lateral
bands; the analysis has no band for the calorimetric recoil-energy response, so
completeness of the hadronic-response model is not established
(Sec.~\ref{sec:response}).  (iii) The interaction-model prior is the analysis's
34-band set, every band drawn in every pseudo-experiment, with negative weights
of two bands clipped per universe.  (v) GiBUU lacks $E_{\nu}>\SI{20}{GeV}$, and
its out-of-domain truth enters the migrations into its domain; for all
predictions $E_{\nu}>\SI{100}{GeV}$ is absent, which is negligible.  The use of
the Tune~v1's shapes below the fine grid in (i) is also a modelling choice of
this kind.

\subsection{Hadronic-response condition and recoil-energy sensitivity}
\label{sec:response}

MINERvA's recoil-energy analyses assign a hadronic-energy response uncertainty
derived from test-beam calorimetry~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam}, separate from the
GEANT hadron-interaction reweights~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq,MINERvA:2022incl}; in the
low-recoil measurement the resulting hadronic-energy uncertainty rises to
\SI{10}{\percent} at its highest three-momentum transfers, $0.9<q_{3}<\SI{1.2}{GeV}$~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl}.  The declared nuisance model
here includes the reweights but no calorimetric response band.  Such a band
would act on all five nulls together, through the shared detector simulation and
unfolding: a response mismodelling could move each observed statistic in either
direction, and a band drawn in the calibration would also broaden and could shift
each null distribution; the net effect on each $p$ value is unmeasured.  The published
comparison in muon kinematics alone, whose observables do not use the
reconstructed recoil energy, also disfavours the same generator
families~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.  That comparison provides context for a
disagreement involving muon observables; the hypotheses tested here use a
different estimator and calibration.
Within this analysis, the same calibrated tests restricted to the matched
$(\pT,\pPar)$ projection do not reject GENIE~2.12.10 CV ($p=\woneGcvPtTot$ in total,
$\woneGcvPtShape$ in shape; a post hoc observation)~\cite{JointTestProjections2026},
whereas its $(\Eavail,W)$ projection does, so the information that rejects it
involves the hadronic axes, on which a response band would act.

For an exploratory response sensitivity study, the data are
re-unfolded with the simulation's reconstructed recoil energy scaled by
$\pm\SI{4}{\percent}$, and the frozen tests are re-evaluated against the frozen
null distributions~\cite{JointTestRecoil2026}.  The size is the level of
data--simulation agreement reported for test-beam protons, pions, and electrons
of 0.35--\SI{2.0}{GeV/c}~\cite{MINERvA:2015testbeam}; neutrons and
higher-momentum hadrons, which matter at high $W$, lie outside that sample.  A
re-unfold with unscaled recoil reproduces the frozen decisions, with every claim
$p$ value inside the range spanned by the data's rounding variations.  At both
$-4\%$ and $+4\%$ every one of the ten rejections holds.  The observed statistics
move by up to about \SI{11}{\percent} from their frozen values, nine tests keep $k=0$, and NuWro's shape
test, the closest to its null, changes from $k=1$ to $k=3$ of $1751$ at $+4\%$
($p=0.0023$, against a Holm threshold of $0.05$).  The evaluation was
reproduced independently.

The calibration distributions remain fixed during this study, which measures
the sensitivity of the observed statistics and decisions to one coherent scale
of reconstructed available energy and recoil energy, applied after the energy
calibration at a single magnitude.  A response-uncertainty construction would
also need to vary the null distributions.  The study leaves untested
(a) the response of the null distributions, which were not regenerated with
the variation although they are simulated through the same detector model, so
the size of this omission is unmeasured for these strongly non-central nulls;
(b) particle-species, energy-dependent, or resolution effects, and a response
difference applied before calibration; and (c) larger variations.  The
rejections are therefore conditional on the simulation's hadronic response, and
the response model is not shown to be complete.

\section{Status of a five-dimensional uncertainty}
\label{sec:uncertainty-status}

One scalar five-dimensional covariance has been adopted under a documented
exception that covers that product alone, and its \zprojCells-cell
$(\Eavail,W)$ projection has been built.  This covariance is separate from the
joint-test calibration.  Measured estimator-seed sensitivity in companion
constructions motivates withholding covariance-based generator significances.
In a dedicated study of two companion covariances built at two estimator-seed settings
(products distinct from the adopted one), varying the
seed moves the projected uncertainty by \SI{\sprojMeasured}{\percent}, against a
\SI{\sprojBound}{\percent} movement limit fixed before production.  That movement
did not fall with ensemble size over the range tested:
\SI{\seedEffectNforty}{\percent} averaged over four disjoint 40-throw subsets,
\SI{\seedEffectNeighty}{\percent} over the two 80-throw halves, and
\SI{\sprojMeasured}{\percent} on the full 160-throw ensemble.  A same-seed resampling floor, measured at $N=40$ and
$N=80$, falls steeply over that step --- \SI{\floorNforty}{\percent} to
\SI{\floorNeighty}{\percent}, two-point exponent $\floorExponent$.  The comparison
is a single seed pair and the 40- and 80-throw points are nested subsets of the
same 160 throws, so the width of the seed-pair distribution and the behaviour at
much larger $N$ are both unmeasured; the statistic is a maximum over the declared
offset set, so further pairs can only raise it.  Five of the covariance's 45
bands are built at a fixed seed the variation cannot reach, carrying
\SI{\pinnedBandsSqrtTr}{\percent} of $\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}\,C}$ and contributing none of
the movement, so their seed sensitivity is unprobed and the effect on the total
were they also to vary is not measured in either direction.  The same variation
moves the projected central values by at most
\SI{\cvMoveOverSigmaMax}{\percent} of their own quoted uncertainty (median
\SI{\cvMoveOverSigmaMed}{\percent}).

A separate study assessed reporting definitions for a five-dimensional
measurement with qualified total uncertainties.  It admitted no reporting
definition that could meet the predeclared useful-precision targets with any
configuration supported within its rules and budget.  For its
fallback definition, the bounded model-dependence component alone exceeded the
total-width target.  The limitation arose from measured model dependence.  The
joint tests above were the study's separately admitted inference branch and
produced no new reportable uncertainty.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This real-data study applies OmniFold to MINERvA inclusive charged-current
interactions, reproduces the published two-dimensional result, and extends the
construction to five simultaneous observables.  The validation distinguishes
consistency across dimensions from recovery of injected structure, treatment
of backgrounds, and calibration of uncertainty ensembles.  The central values
pass marginal anchors and injected-shape closure tests and recover the
established low-recoil discrepancy.  Background-inclusive tests reveal a
subtraction bias that the unbinned negative-weight treatment and a larger
refinement classifier reduce, while regularization bias persists under the
tested generator-sized shape changes.  Statistical-coverage
and estimator-seed studies expose further limits on uncertainty construction;
the rebuilt two-dimensional statistical band still awaits an independent
coverage test.  These measured outcomes provide practical checks for future
multidimensional neutrino unfolding analyses.

The generator comparisons illustrate calibration through the full analysis
chain with an estimator that retains substantial model dependence.  Under the
six conditions of Sec.~\ref{sec:conditions}, all ten total and shape hypotheses
for the five specified predictions are rejected at familywise level 0.05.
Every submitted pseudo-experiment has been observed after the report-only
recovery, which preserves the frozen decisions.  Each test also has $p<0.05$
in at least one matched coarse projection, so additional discrimination from
the tested joint information remains undemonstrated.  The physical
interpretation depends on the simulation's calorimetric recoil response and
the assumed convergence rate used to extrapolate the sub-fine-grid residual.
An exploratory $\pm4\%$ coherent recoil-energy scaling leaves all rejections
intact with the calibration distributions held fixed.  Extending this inference would
require a response band drawn through the calibration and a demonstrated bound
on the residual below the fine grid.  A five-dimensional measurement with total
uncertainties would additionally require a matched uncertainty construction
with validated coverage.
