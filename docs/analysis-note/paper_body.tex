\section{Introduction and scope}
\label{sec:intro}

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon
and hadronic observables.  Their interpretation depends on correcting detector
response and assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure.
Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration
matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but the response tensor grows
rapidly when additional observables are reported.  OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned
feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation
\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}.  This makes simultaneous
higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned
response tensor for every projection.  Unbinned unfolding has been applied to
collider data, for example eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at
H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at
ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}; see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey.  In
neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector
sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}.
% Methods rewrite fix round 1 (REVIEW-1 note 21; OUTLINE-DECISION sec. 1, no priority framing): "We are not aware of an
% application to neutrino-scattering data." cut (M8).

We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the
MINERvA open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  We first
reproduce the published double-differential charged-current inclusive cross
section in transverse and longitudinal muon momentum,
$d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, then add available
hadronic energy $\Eavail$, three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and hadronic
invariant mass $W$ to obtain simultaneous three-, four-, and five-observable
central values.  Multi-differential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}.
The observables share reconstructed inputs: $\Eavail$, $q_{3}$, and $W$ are all
built from reconstructed hadronic energy (Sec.~\ref{sec:data}).

This paper evaluates that implementation as a case study of multidimensional

validated five-observable cross-section measurement.
% X5 G1: the question stated (sol-referee major 1; the evidence is Secs. V-VI).
Its question is whether the implementation gives reliable multidimensional
cross sections when the simulation mismodels the truth by generator-sized
amounts, and what validation is needed before its outputs can be interpreted;
the generator tests are a secondary application.  Two requirements are kept
apart.  The coverage of unfolded intervals is the rate at which they contain
the true cross section.  The calibration of a test is the rate at which it
rejects a true hypothesis; it can be obtained without validated intervals, by
simulating the whole analysis chain at the hypothesis, so that the estimator's
bias enters the simulated null distribution, to the extent that the simulated
chain reproduces the analysis of the data.
% REVIEW-1 finding 7: the calibration sentence conditioned as in Sec. VIII E (M8).
The two-dimensional reproduction provides an external benchmark.  As
observables are added, projections, closure tests with injected shape
changes, pseudo-experiments that include backgrounds, and the ensembles of
repeated unfoldings used for the uncertainties test consistency, shape
recovery, and the sensitivity to the background treatment, the regularization,
and the estimator's random seed.

These validation studies are the paper's main results.  The implementation
reproduces the published integrated cross section, and its higher-dimensional
central values agree in normalization with the results they extend.
Pseudo-experiments that include backgrounds reveal a background-subtraction
bias at high $W$, which an unbinned treatment largely removes; the unfolding
recovers only part of a generator-sized change of the truth; statistical
intervals from a deterministic variant of the estimator miscover at the
tested truths; and none of the reporting definitions studied achieved a
validated \SI{10}{\percent} median precision.  These findings concern this
implementation and the observables, configurations, and deformations tested,
not multidimensional OmniFold in general.  As an application, Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}
illustrates calibrated inference with a biased estimator: it tests specified
generator predictions, which the published muon-kinematic measurement already
disfavours~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, with tests calibrated within the implemented
simulation model by rerunning the analysis chain on pseudo-data.

\emph{Scope.}  This paper studies one OmniFold implementation on one data
set.  The three- to five-observable central values are outputs of the
estimators of Sec.~\ref{sec:method} under the implemented observable
definitions, simulated detector response, background treatment, and
regularization; their total intervals have no validated coverage, and we
quote no covariance-based generator significance for them.  The generator
tests are conditional tests of explicitly simulated hybrid hypotheses.  Each
null hypothesis sets the cross section equal to a prediction on every cell of
a fine five-observable grid.  Below that grid the event composition is the
MINERvA Tune~v1 simulation's, varied by the drawn interaction-model universes
with every fine-cell integral held fixed.  The calibrated size is averaged
over the stated nuisance priors; it is not guaranteed at each fixed value of
the nuisance parameters.  The detector model has the implemented variations
but no calorimetric recoil-response uncertainty, and two of the predictions
are for a carbon target, the data for CH.

\section{Data, observables, and domains}
\label{sec:data}

The analysis uses the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current
run periods, corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target and an
average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, from the
MINERvA open-data release of pre-selected analysis ntuples
(AnaTuples)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}.  The detector, including the downstream
MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}.  The files analysed are an earlier production of
that release; their names and sizes are recorded for reproduction
(see Data availability).

The signal is a $\nu_{\mu}$ charged-current interaction, with any final state,
whose true vertex lies in the tracker fiducial volume ($5980<z<\SI{8422}{mm}$,
inside a hexagon of apothem \SI{850}{mm}) and whose muon has
$\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<\pPar<\SI{60}{GeV/c}$, and
$\pT\le\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$.  Selected simulated events outside this definition,
including charged-current $\nu_{\mu}$ events whose muon lies outside these
limits, are background.  The selection requires a reconstructed vertex in the
same volume and a reconstructed muon with $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$, matched to
a MINOS track of negative charge, with a dead-time requirement, as in
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}; the flux treatment follows
Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn}.  After selection, the sample contains $4.12$
million selected data events.  The simulation contains $32.8$ million
unweighted true signal events and $20.5$ million selected simulated events; it
is scaled to the data exposure.
% REVIEW-2 finding 19: the two counts come from two trees (M4-W-RECORD.md:68, :108), so no subset is asserted.
% X5 Gm3: 32,849,103 truth-denominator rows and 20,498,013 reco-passing simulated events,
% docs/orchestration/state/2d-closeout/stageF/methods/M4-W-RECORD.md:68, :108.
The simulated events are generated with GENIE~2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} and the MINERvA
Tune~v1, which reweights GENIE with a non-resonant pion reduction, a
random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil
two-particle--two-hole (2p2h) enhancement~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}.
Event selection is held fixed as the classifier inputs and unfolded observables
grow from $(\pT,\pPar)$ to $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_3,W)$; adding features does
not add selected events or relax the signal definition.

The unfolded observables are defined at truth level (Table~\ref{tab:observables}).  Besides the muon
momentum components $\pT$ and $\pPar$, they are the three-momentum transfer
$q_{3}=|\vec{q}\,|$ from the generator, the hadronic invariant mass $W$ with
the struck-nucleon mass $M$, and the available energy
\begin{equation}
  \Eavail=\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV})
  +\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma},
  \label{eq:eavail}
\end{equation}
in which protons contribute kinetic energy, charged pions their energy minus an
inherited constant of \SI{135}{MeV} rather than the charged-pion mass, and
neutral pions and photons their total energy; all other final-state particles,
including neutrons, contribute nothing.  The sums run over the generator's
final-state particles after final-state interactions, in which neutral pions
appear undecayed.  We have not corrected this constant, which every
prediction shares.

At truth level, $q_{3}$ is computed from the true neutrino and muon
four-vectors, but $\theta_{\mu}$, and with it $\pT$ and $\pPar$, is measured
from a fixed beam axis tilted by \SI{58.87}{mrad} from the detector axis
rather than from the true neutrino direction.  Truth $W$ is therefore fixed
by $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$ only up to that event-by-event angle, of about
\SI{1}{mrad}, and the choice of $M$: the residual is within \SI{\pm2}{MeV} for
68\% of events and within $-49$ to $+67$~MeV for 99.9\%;
\SI{0.25}{\percent} of events (weighted) change their coarse $W$ bin, and
\SI{0.15}{\percent} have no real solution.

The five reconstructed inputs therefore share information and do not, by
themselves, provide five independent constraints: the result is five
simultaneous observables, not five independent measurements.

We therefore distinguish the five classifier inputs, the lower-dimensional
support they populate, and the five reported observables.  Only reconstructed
$W$ is fixed by the other inputs; reconstructed $\Eavail$ comes from a separate
calorimetric estimator and is not a function of them.

directions of the test metric (Sec.~\ref{sec:joint}) are consistent with this
redundancy, but that origin has not been shown, and the information added by
$W$ has not been measured.  The implemented
reconstructed and truth definitions are given in the analysis note's section on
the higher-dimensional extensions~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}.

\section{Estimators and validation design}
\label{sec:method}

Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification steps.  A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and
defines a likelihood-ratio weight.  The event pairing transfers that weight to
truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a
smooth reweighting to the next iteration.  Cross sections are formed by
histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and
dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width.

The integrated flux is $\Phi=\SI{6.243e-8}{cm^{-2}}$ per proton on target,
from 0 to \SI{100}{GeV} after the neutrino--electron-scattering constraint, and
the target contains $3.235\times10^{30}$ nucleons.  Events that fail
reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate efficiency
correction is applied.

Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the
classifier learns to match.  In the purity-based subtraction every data event in
reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight
$w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that
bin, so the target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$.  The unbinned
negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative
weight to the data, so the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise; a classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at
zero, converts the signed sample into non-negative weights before the first
iteration.  The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever
the background fraction varies within it.  Both are defined in the analysis
note's appendix on negative-weight background subtraction~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}.

\section{Two-dimensional benchmark}
\label{sec:repro2d}

The two-dimensional result is an end-to-end test of the unfolding against a
measurement from the same data.  Its integrated cross section is \SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon}, compared with
\SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon} in the published analysis, a ratio of
$\ratioTot$.  Of the 205 reported bins, \SI{\binsTen}{\percent} agree within
\SI{10}{\percent}; the standardized bin differences formed with the published
uncertainties have an RMS of $\pullRMS$.
% Methods rewrite (OUTLINE-DECISION sec. 2, row IV): the covariance-weighted value called a descriptive distance (M8).
The covariance-weighted distance against the full published covariance is
$\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiPaper$, computed with the pseudo-inverse of the

For each of five predictions $G$ (the MINERvA Tune~v1, GENIE CV, GENIE + MEC,

the drawn interaction-model universes with every fine-cell integral held fixed,

simulates.  For the Tune~v1, the analysis simulation itself, no external
prediction is combined with the Tune~v1 composition.  The tests are evaluated

\subsection{Statistics and metric}

% X6 G-total: the total test defined at first use (it tests rate and shape together, not an integrated rate).
% X6 G-min: the shape metric and the diagonal prediction variance, nd-unfolding/s5p_inference.py:35-49 (Jacobian at the
% unfolded values applied to the whole metric; last cell dropped); s5p_joint.py:147-162 (W = V + diag Var(mu)); the
% prediction variance is U^2 sumw2 over disjoint cells (s5p_joint.py:61-80), hence diagonal.
A total statistic $T_{\mathrm{tot}}=r^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}r$ is formed from
the residual $r=F-\mu_{G}$ of the unfolded cell values $F$ and the prediction
$\mu_{G}$; it tests the rate and the shape together, and we call it the total
(rate-and-shape) test.  A shape statistic $T_{\mathrm{sh}}$ of the same form
uses the normalized cell fractions, with the whole metric transformed by the
normalization Jacobian at the unfolded values and one cell dropped.  The metric
$\Sigma=V+\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ adds the prediction's own
finite-sample variance, diagonal because each simulated event of a prediction
falls in one cell, to $V$, a Ledoit--Wolf-shrunk
covariance~\cite{LedoitWolf:2004} of \jtVn{} pilot pseudo-experiments at the
Tune~v1 null.  The metric influences each test's power; the null calibration
determines its size.

The Monte Carlo $p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$
of $B$ null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic; it is never zero.
Each decision uses the largest $p$ value over a set of shifted nulls fixed in
advance.  Each shifted null adds a fixed vector to every null draw: $cD$ with
$c\in\{0,\tfrac12,1\}$, where $D$ is an upper bound on the simulation-size
difference along the null's bias direction (half of the simulation in the
pseudo-experiments, all of it for the data), and $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$, where
$\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ is the change of the noise-free unfolded result in the
last step of a refinement of the null truth's grid (Sec.~\ref{sec:conditions}).

interval~\cite{Clopper:1934} of $k/B$ lay entirely on one side of each of its
thresholds and met a fixed precision requirement.

\section{Familywise error of the sequential decisions}
\label{app:fwer}

\emph{Probability model.}  Fix one test, and condition on the data and on the
simulated sample.  The calibration pseudo-experiments, taken in their fixed
seed order, give exceedance indicators $X_{b}=\mathbf{1}[T_{b}\ge
t_{\mathrm{obs}}]$.  Assumption A2 is that, given the data, these are
independent Bernoulli draws with a common probability $p$, the calibrated tail
probability of the observed statistic; this requires the lost
pseudo-experiments to be lost independently of their outcomes.  Assumption A1
is that, for a true null, $p$ is super-uniform over repetitions of the
experiment, $P(p\le u)\le u$, with the nuisance parameters drawn from their
priors; the size statement is therefore averaged over the nuisance priors.
Let $k_{B}$ be the number of exceedances among the first $B$ draws and
$U(k,B)$ the upper end of the two-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval, a
one-sided 97.5\% upper bound.

\emph{Crossing event.}  The rule rejects at threshold $c$ only if
$U(k_{B},B)<c$ at the number $B\le\jtBcap$ at which sampling stopped.  Whatever
rule chose that stopping point, including stops decided with results visible,
irregular looks, lost draws, and the budget extensions, the rejection lies in
the event $E_{c}=\bigcup_{B\le\jtBcap}\{U(k_{B},B)<c\}$ that the bound crosses
below $c$ at some $B$.  Hence $P(\text{reject at }c)\le R(c)\equiv
E_{p}[g_{c}(p)]$, with $g_{c}(p)=P(E_{c}\mid p)$.

\emph{Uniform $p$ is the worst case.}  Coupling the Bernoulli draws in $p$ shows
that $g_{c}(p)$ does not increase with $p$.  For any super-uniform $p$ its
expectation is therefore at most its value for uniform $p$,
$R(c)\le\int_{0}^{1}g_{c}(u)\,du$.

\emph{Holm reduction without independence.}  Let $m_{0}$ of the $m=10$
hypotheses be true.  The first true null to be rejected is reached after at
most $m-m_{0}$ rejections of false nulls, so its threshold is at most
$\alpha/m_{0}$.  A familywise error therefore requires some true null to cross
below $\alpha/m_{0}$, and by the union bound
$\mathrm{FWER}\le m_{0}\,R(\alpha/m_{0})\le\max_{j\le m}j\,R(\alpha/j)$.  The
argument uses neither independence between tests nor the order of the Holm
steps, so the shared ensemble of a null's total and shape tests, their
unequal stopping counts, and the ranking by $(k+1)/(B+1)$ with its ties do not
affect it.  The decision $p$ value is the largest over shifted nulls that
include the unshifted one, evaluated at the same $B$, so its exceedance count
is at least that of the unshifted test; since $U$ increases with $k$, the bound for the unshifted test
covers the decision rule.

\emph{Certified constant.}  For each $c=\alpha/j$, $j=1,\dots,10$, an exact
dynamic program over $B\le\jtBcap$ gives $g_{c}$ on a grid of 6000 points in
$[0,6c]$; because $g_{c}$ is nonincreasing, a left Riemann sum plus the tail
term $(1-6c)\,g_{c}(6c)$ is a rigorous upper bound on $\int_{0}^{1}g_{c}$.  The
