\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon and hadronic observables. Their interpretation depends on correcting detector response and on assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but its response tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}. This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned response tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been applied to collider data, for example to eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and to twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets} (see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}.

We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}. The result is a simultaneous charged-current inclusive cross section in five observables: the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta $\pT$ and $\pPar$, the available hadronic energy $\Eavail$, the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and the hadronic invariant mass $W$. It is reported in sixteen cells with its covariance (Sec.~\ref{sec:result16}) and compared with five generator predictions (Sec.~\ref{sec:gen16}). We first reproduce the published double-differential cross section $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} as an end-to-end test of the unfolding. The three- and four-observable central values are given in the companion note~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}. Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five observables are simultaneous but not independent.

The validation studies assess the result through the two-dimensional reproduction, recovery of generator-sized truth changes, a coverage test, and a fake-data study of estimator bias. They examine whether the unfolding gives reliable multidimensional cross sections when the simulation mismodels the truth by generator-sized amounts.

Interval coverage has not been validated. The bias tests, the fake-data study, and the dependence of the generator $\chi^{2}$ on the flux correlations are reported for the sixteen-cell result in Secs.~\ref{sec:fd16} and~\ref{sec:gen16}. The findings are those of one OmniFold implementation on one data set, for the observables, configurations, and deformations tested, rather than of multidimensional OmniFold in general.

\section{Data, simulation, and observables}
\label{sec:data}

The data come from the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current run periods (playlists 1A--1G and 1L--1P), corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target, with an average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. The input is an earlier production of the MINERvA open-data release (see Data availability), in the form of pre-selected analysis ntuples (AnaTuples)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}. The detector, including the downstream MINOS near detector that serves as a muon spectrometer, is described in Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}. The signal is a $\nu_{\mu}$ charged-current interaction, with any final state, whose true vertex lies in the tracker fiducial volume ($5980<z<\SI{8422}{mm}$, inside a hexagon of apothem \SI{850}{mm}). The signal muon has $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<\pPar<\SI{60}{GeV/c}$, and $\pT\le\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$. Selected simulated events outside this definition, including $\nu_{\mu}$ charged-current events whose muon lies outside these limits, are background. The selection requires a reconstructed vertex in the same volume and a reconstructed muon with $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$ matched to a MINOS track of negative charge, together with a dead-time requirement, as in Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. The flux treatment follows Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn}. For the results in more than two observables, the integrated flux is $\Phi=\SI{6.243e-8}{cm^{-2}}$ per proton on target, from 0 to \SI{100}{GeV}, after the neutrino--electron-scattering constraint and averaged over the twelve playlists by exposure (\nsec{sec:method-xsec}). The analysis uses $4.12$ million selected data events. The simulation contains $32.8$ million unweighted true signal events and $20.5$ million selected simulated events, and it is scaled to the data exposure. The simulated events are generated with GENIE~2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} and reweighted with the MINERvA Tune~v1, which applies a non-resonant pion reduction, a random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil two-particle--two-hole (2p2h) enhancement~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}. Event selection is held fixed as the classifier inputs and unfolded observables grow from $(\pT,\pPar)$ to $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_3,W)$. Adding features does not add selected events or relax the signal definition.

The unfolded observables are defined at truth level, with muon momentum components $\pT=p_{\mu}\sin\theta_{\mu}$ and $\pPar=p_{\mu}\cos\theta_{\mu}$. The magnitude of the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}=|\vec{q}|$ comes from the generator's four-vectors, and the hadronic invariant mass $W=\sqrt{M^{2}+2M(E_{\nu}-E_{\mu})-Q^{2}}$ uses the struck-nucleon mass $M$. We define the available energy as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Eavail={}&\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV})\\
&+\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma},
\end{split}
\label{eq:eavail}
\end{equation}
which subtracts a defined constant of \SI{135}{MeV}, rather than the charged-pion mass, in the charged-pion term. The constant defines the reported observable and is used consistently by the analysis and every prediction. The sums run over the generator's final-state particles after final-state interactions, where neutral pions appear undecayed. All other final-state particles, including neutrons, contribute nothing. No single published definition of $\Eavail$ exists. Eq.~(\ref{eq:eavail}) keeps the species of the closed list of Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil} except electrons and positrons, omits species such as kaons that the open list of Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} includes, and has a charged-pion term \SI{4.57}{MeV} per pion above the kinetic energy used in Refs.~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil,MINERvA:2023avz} (\nsec{sec:eavail-definition}). To first order in the unfolding, without retraining, replacing the constant alone by the charged-pion mass moves the sixteen-cell result by at most \fvval{eav-cellmax}$\sigma$ in any cell and by \fvval{eav-coherent}$\sigma$ as one coherent shift, the norm of the shift under the reported covariance (\fvval{eav-cellmax-nopl}$\sigma$ and \fvval{eav-coherent-nopl}$\sigma$ without the run-period block). Reconstructed $\Eavail$ is $1.17$ times the calorimetric recoil energy in the tracker and electromagnetic calorimeter, with muon deposits removed. Reconstructed $q_{3}$ and $W$ instead combine the muon kinematics with a separate calorimetric estimate $q_{0}$ of the energy transfer.

\section{Cross-section extraction}
\label{sec:method}

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate efficiency correction is applied.

Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the classifier learns to match. In the purity-based subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight $w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that bin, so the classifier's target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$. The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative weight to the data, so that the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise. A classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at zero, converts the signed sample to non-negative weights before the first iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction varies within it (\napp{app:negweight}).

The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn,Ke:2017lightgbm} of nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps, and negative-weight background subtraction with a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves. The two-observable estimator, LightGBM~\cite{Ke:2017lightgbm} at a fixed seed, gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro2d}). This reproducible central value is that of its own systematic-universe ensemble, so its covariance is built with the same estimator. The three- and four-observable central values and the sixteen-cell five-observable result (Sec.~\ref{sec:result16}) use the joint-test estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with the same background treatment and refinement capacity, named for the note's calibrated joint tests of generator predictions, which use it (\nsec{sec:joint5d}). Its seeds are fixed, so its training is reproducible, and it also gives the held-out shape tests of Fig.~\ref{fig:deformation}b. This estimator removes most of the bias of purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-$W$ cells (\nsec{sec:val-negweight}). Results from the two estimators are not combined. The other configurations used below, and their results, are described in \nsec{sec:method-config}.

The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding. No learner setting, whether capacity, learning rate, refinement capacity or iteration count, was tuned on data. The nominal settings are the scikit-learn defaults, with LightGBM matched to their capacity. The refinement capacity, which the two estimators share, was chosen in simulation closure studies, and so were the joint-test estimator's five iterations (below). The two-observable estimator uses five iterations following common practice; this choice was checked on data, against ten iterations with purity-based subtraction and against the published result, without a search over the iteration count (\nsec{sec:method-config}). Two other choices for the two-dimensional result were made with the comparison to the published result in view: the learner implementation and the background-subtraction treatment (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro2d-pub}). That comparison is therefore not an independent test of the method. Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, so no held-out or cross-fitted split is used; it is trained without early stopping, and the two classes keep their physical weights (\nsec{sec:method-algo}).

\section{Uncertainties and validation}
\label{sec:validation}

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The closure, deformation, and coverage studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed and true distributions together and keeps the detector response at fixed truth. Throughout, the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune~v1. Every study uses the analysis simulation's detector response.

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is $C_{\mathrm{total}}+C_{\mathrm{resp}}+C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$. The term $C_{\mathrm{total}}$ sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization uncertainties of the joint-test estimator and $C_{\mathrm{model}}$; the lateral uncertainties shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than reweighting events (Appendix~\ref{app:unc16}). The model term $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ is built from the change in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn. Its contributions come from those generators' samples in their own definitions, with NuWro and GiBUU on carbon and GENIE CV as the common reference, not from the predictions compared in Sec.~\ref{sec:gen16}. The comparisons there with GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU therefore use covariance contributions derived from the same generators. It is a measure of unscaled generator-shape dependence, not a calibrated bound (\nsec{sec:fived16-cov}). The response term $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ is the response of the result to a one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil energy in the simulation. The scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and other deposits. $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ does not cover neutron or muon deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale. It enters only this result, its fake-data tests and its comparison in Sec.~\ref{sec:gen16}. The unfolded results of the three run-period groups, each relative to its own simulation, differ by more than their bootstrap scatter. The offsets of the twelve playlists differ between the three period groups by more than expected from their playlist-to-playlist scatter (\nsec{sec:fived16-pl}). The cause is not known, and these data cannot establish which period is correct. The run-period block $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ includes this inconsistency in the reported covariance. It is built from the double ratios among the groups, each group's unfolded result over its own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the reference, so it contains the differences by construction. It was added after these differences were seen, following an audit of its overlap with the other blocks and of its noise treatment, whose stopping condition was fixed before any of its numbers were read. \subsection{Sensitivity to input rounding}
\label{sec:cov5d}

The joint-test estimator is unstable under floating-point rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, the reported quantities scatter by a median of \SI{0.23}{\percent} per joint cell, against a median data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty of \SI{0.37}{\percent}, and each bootstrap replica carries this scatter at the same size. It limits how exactly the central values can be reproduced, and a single perturbation moves the most affected quantity further. Rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of the lower-capacity development variant by up to 2.5, so the estimator fails a data-stability requirement of at most 0.3 (median) and 1.0 (maximum) statistical standard deviations. The training-seed block of Sec.~\ref{sec:cov16} is zero because, in the unfolds we ran, the result is bitwise independent of the training seed and of input row order; this is distinct from the sensitivity to rounding-level input changes quoted above. \section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The principal result is the sixteen-cell cross section of Sec.~\ref{sec:result16}, with its covariance and generator comparison.

\label{sec:limits}
Two validation findings point to the unfolded result retaining part of its prior. The first is the residual under a generator-sized change of the truth in Sec.~\ref{sec:departure}, which for the GiBUU-like change is consistent with finite-iteration regularization. The second is the shift of the data result when the simulation's prior is reweighted to generator-sized changes of the truth, which tracks the same bias (\nsec{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}). The note's calibrated tests compare each prediction with the result against a null distribution, the distribution of the test statistic simulated through the whole analysis chain when the prediction's hybrid null holds, that is, when the true cross section is the prediction on every cell of the fine grid with the simulation's event composition within each cell. These tests show the same bias toward the prior: it displaces their null distributions for the external predictions upward, and the calibration, which simulates the whole analysis chain, reproduces that displacement (\nsec{sec:joint5d-results}).
% - PRD read 1, R7: the null distribution and the hybrid null as sec_joint.tex sec:joint5d and sec:joint5d-design define them (the null truth equals G on every fine cell; below the grid, the Tune v1 composition; the null distribution from pseudo-experiments running the full chain).

The studies of Sec.~\ref{sec:validation} and of the companion note show what must be checked before unfolded intervals are interpreted: closure with the backgrounds included; truth changes of generator size, not only nominal closure; coverage under changes of the truth, not only at nominal truth; robustness to training seeds and rounding-level input changes; and run-period consistency.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Apart from the validation of the uncertainties and the low stability of four of the sixteen cells (below), the limitations of these results are stated with the results they qualify, in Secs.~\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:result16}, and those of the calibrated tests in the companion note. The principal ones are these.
\begin{enumerate}
\item No reported interval has validated coverage. The fake-data tests of Sec.~\ref{sec:fd16} assess bias, not coverage: only event counts fluctuate, with systematic parameters fixed, so interval widths cannot be tested. A coverage test at two of their truths, declared in advance for the covariance without the run-period block, with pseudo-experiments that also draw the flux, interaction, and detector uncertainties, was stopped by one of its preliminary checks before any coverage pseudo-experiment ran: the uncertainties reproduced through its pseudo-experiments disagreed with the covariance blocks they draw, for reasons not established (\nsec{sec:fived16-fd}). Neither the coverage of $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ at truths held out of its construction nor the size of either run-period block is independently validated (Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}, Secs.~\ref*{N-sec:fived16-cov}, \ref*{N-sec:fived16-pl} and~\ref*{N-sec:uq-combine}). The two-dimensional covariance has not been independently validated and gives no calibrated significance for generator comparisons; an earlier statistical band of that result undercovered in its tails in a coverage test at fixed truth (\nsec{sec:coverage}), and the coverage of the current statistical block and of the total covariance has not been tested. The coverage tests cited are pointwise, for each reported quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them. We make no coverage claim on independent simulated samples or simultaneously over all reported bins and projections.
