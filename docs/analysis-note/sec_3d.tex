\subsubsection{Operational definition and provenance}
\label{sec:3d-method}
\label{sec:eavail-definition}
Truth available energy is defined by its implementation, for which ``low-recoil convention'' is shorthand. In MeV, the truth function (\texttt{CVUniverse::GetEAvailableTrue()}) computes
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm avail}^{\rm true}=
 \sum_{p}(E-938.27)
 +\sum_{\pi^\pm}(E-135)
 +\sum_{\pi^0}E
 +\sum_{\gamma}E .
 \label{eq:eavail-implemented}
\end{equation}
Protons and charged pions contribute kinetic-energy-like terms. Neutral pions and photons contribute total energy. All other final-state species contribute zero, including neutrons, the primary muon, secondary $e^\pm$, neutrinos, kaons, antiprotons, strange baryons, other mesons and nuclear-remnant records. The MINERvA Analysis Toolkit (MAT) reconstructs calorimetrically:
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm avail}^{\rm reco}=1.17\left[(E_{\rm blob}^{\rm tracker}
 -E_{\mu\text{-fuzz}}^{\rm tracker})+(E_{\rm blob}^{\rm ECAL}
 -E_{\mu\text{-fuzz}}^{\rm ECAL})\right],
 \label{eq:eavail-reco-implemented}
\end{equation}
ECAL is the electromagnetic calorimeter. The quantity is implemented in the reconstruction function (\texttt{CVUniverse::NewEavail()}), with no final clamp to non-negative values.

Both definitions are inherited from MAT-MINERvA. They were not chosen for this analysis. The comparisons below record convention differences. They do not establish the inherited definition's origin. There is no unique published $E_{\rm avail}$ definition. Rodrigues \emph{et al.}~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil} give a closed list of five species, using proton and charged-pion kinetic energy plus $\pi^0$, electron and photon total energy. The implemented truth definition (Eq.~\eqref{eq:eavail-implemented}) includes four of those five species and excludes secondary $e^\pm$. The later definition of Ascencio \emph{et al.}~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} is open. It includes all other final-state particles except neutrons and subtracts a nucleon mass for strange baryons. This includes species omitted here. Rodrigues and Henry/Su \emph{et al.} Eq.~4~\cite{MINERvA:2023avz} use charged-pion kinetic energy. The \texttt{minerva-ml} machine-learning implementation uses charged-pion total energy (implementation details, App.~\ref{sec:pet}). We therefore do not claim that our definition matches ``the published definition.''

The charged-pion term subtracts the inherited constant \SI{135}{MeV} instead of the charged-pion mass of about \SI{139.57}{MeV}. The production keeps that constant (development history, App.~\ref{app:devhistory}). Relative to the charged-pion mass, it moves \fvval{eavbin-share}\,\% of simulated signal weight to other bins on the fine grid of seven true-$\Eavail$ bins and changes each by at most \fvval{eavbin-max}\,\%. This effect has not been assessed against the projected five-dimensional covariance of the \resFiveAxis{} from the \estPurity{} (estimator definitions, Table~\ref{tab:estimators-matrix}). We tested the mass replacement on the \resSixteen{} (Sec.~\ref{sec:fived16}) from the \estMultiD{}. The test is first order in the unfolding. It uses no retraining. \subsubsection{The low-available-energy excess and the known low-recoil deficit}
\label{sec:3d-2p2h}
We compare the low-$\Eavail$ excess with candidate model changes by reweighting and by generating the relevant physics. In a low-three-momentum-transfer subsample, MINERvA first isolated the rate enhancement between quasielastic and $\Delta$ resonance peaks, with multi-nucleon final states~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}. The empirical tune was built to absorb it. Our muon-kinematics and $\Eavail$ central values show predictions falling short in the same region.

\paragraph{The tested pion-FSI variations do not account for it.} Reweighting 2 million GENIE CV events with \texttt{grwght1p} changes $d\sigma/d\Eavail$ by under a percent for both pion-FSI dials. Inelastic scattering (\texttt{FrInel\_pi}) shifts the spectrum by $\le\SI{0.74}{\percent}$ (peak in $0.10$--$\SI{0.20}{GeV}$). Absorption (\texttt{FrAbs\_pi}) shifts it by $\le\SI{0.82}{\percent}$ (peak in the lowest bin). Both are ratios within the unrepaired GENIE sample (flux repair, \S\ref{sec:3d-models}). The flux defect cancels to first order. Neither was recomputed. The integrated rate moves by $\pm\SI{0.02}{}$--$\SI{0.03}{\percent}$ at $\pm1\sigma$. A dial shifting the spectrum by less than \SI{1}{\percent} cannot account for the \SIrange{8}{36}{\percent} per-bin excess over the GENIE central value below $\Eavail=\SI{0.4}{GeV}$. This conclusion is limited to these dials and their range; other transport models or proton-FSI effects are not excluded.

\subsubsection{Fourth axis: three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$}
\label{sec:4d}
The fourth axis adds three-momentum transfer $q_{3}=|\vec{q}\,|$ to the 3D unfolding (\S\ref{sec:3d}), chosen because its $(\Eavail,q_{3})$ plane separates quasielastic, 2p2h and resonance processes and permits bin-compatible comparison to MINERvA's dedicated low-recoil $d^{2}\sigma/dq_{3}\,d\Eavail$ measurement (Ascencio \emph{et al.}~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl}). Classifier inputs are $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3})$. The deliberately coarse $q_{3}$ edges $[0,0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8,1.2,2.0,100]\,\si{GeV}$ give seven bins including a final catch bin and $14\times16\times7\times7=10\,976$ four-dimensional bins (4830 populated).

\paragraph{Marginalization check (4D$\to$3D).} With the \estPurity{}, the $q_{3}$ marginalization reproduces the 3D $\Eavail$ spectrum and low-recoil excess (Fig.~\ref{fig:q3excess}, lower right). The $\Eavail$-marginal data/Tune~v1 ratio is $1.21$ in the lowest bin and dips to $1.01$ across the quasielastic--$\Delta$ region. It rises to $1.16$--$1.23$ in the high-$\Eavail$ deep-inelastic scattering (DIS) tail (integrated data/Tune~v1 $=1.13$). These ratios use Tune~v1. \paragraph{Bin-identical comparison to the published low-recoil measurement.} Supplemental data from Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} (44-cell $d^{2}\sigma/(d\Eavail\,dq_{3})$ with its full covariance, distributed inside the public arXiv source package) permit a direct quantitative cross-check. We merge both measurements onto the maximal common grid using edges present in \emph{both} binnings. We restrict our $(\pT,\pPar)$ marginalization to $\pPar<\SI{20}{GeV/c}$ to mirror Ascencio's muon angle and momentum cuts ($\theta_\mu<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<p_\mu<\SI{20}{GeV}$). Together these give two super-cells in the low-recoil 2p2h region, with $\Eavail<\SI{0.4}{GeV}$ in $q_{3}\in[0.4,0.6)$ and $[0.6,1.2)\,\si{GeV}$. Our central result lies \SI{9.2}{\percent} and \SI{6.0}{\percent} above Ascencio. No pulls or full-covariance $\chi^{2}$ are reported (comparison scope, \S\ref{sec:3d}; common-grid comparison, Fig.~\ref{fig:ascencio}). Both share MINERvA flux/detector systematics, treated here as independent. The muon momentum cuts ($p_\mu$ and $\pPar$) differ by $\lesssim\SI{6}{\percent}$ kinematic smearing at the \SI{20}{GeV} boundary. The analyses use different true $\Eavail$ definitions (truth convention, \S\ref{sec:eavail-definition}). In our simulation, Ascencio's broader definition moves \SI{11.0}{\percent} of the population out of our lowest true-$\Eavail$ bin ($\Eavail<\SI{0.1}{GeV}$). That shift has the residual's sign and location. The two common cells span our three lowest bins ($\Eavail<\SI{0.4}{GeV}$). The aggregate shift over those bins has not been computed. The comparison's scope and limits are stated in \S\ref{sec:limitations-3d}. A comparison on all 44 cells has not been made. Binning the existing result onto Ascencio's edges requires no new unfolding (binning method, \S\ref{sec:method-binning}). A systematic sweep on that binning has not been done.
