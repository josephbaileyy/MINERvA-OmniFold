\section{Higher-dimensional extensions: available energy and momentum transfer}
\label{sec:3d}

Unbinned OmniFold adds an observable by adding an input to the step-1 and step-2 classifiers, without an explicit migration matrix or per-bin response tensor. It avoids the combinatorial binning penalty of matrix unfolding but still has a dimensional cost: classifier training, acceptance and support coverage, sample sparsity and uncertainty quantification become more demanding as observables are added. The primary methodological motivation is the absence of a practical analog of D'Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU). We extend the measurement to $d^{3}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar\,d\Eavail)$ with available energy as a third axis.

MINERvA has published binned triple-differential results (Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}, Table~\ref{tab:landscape}), including recent comparisons between low- and medium-energy beams~\cite{MINERvA:2026qeleme}. The latter measure $\pT$, $\pPar$, and available recoil energy for a quasielastic-like signal, for which the available energy is the sum of proton kinetic energies. This differs from the inclusive signal and species-dependent energy definition used here. What distinguishes this analysis is the unbinned, simultaneous CC-inclusive scalar formulation extended to three, four and five recorded observables, not the number of dimensions by itself.

\subsection{Operational definition and provenance}
\label{sec:3d-method}
\label{sec:eavail-definition}
Truth available energy is defined by its implementation, for which ``low-recoil convention'' is shorthand. In MeV, the truth function (\texttt{CVUniverse::GetEAvailableTrue()}) computes
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm avail}^{\rm true}=
 \sum_{p}(E-938.27)
 +\sum_{\pi^\pm}(E-135)
 +\sum_{\pi^0}E
 +\sum_{\gamma}E .
 \label{eq:eavail-implemented}
\end{equation}
Protons and charged pions contribute the indicated kinetic-energy-like terms; neutral pions and photons contribute total energy. All other final-state species contribute zero, including neutrons, the primary muon, secondary $e^\pm$, neutrinos, kaons, antiprotons, strange baryons, other mesons and nuclear-remnant records. Reconstructed available energy is the MAT calorimetric quantity
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm avail}^{\rm reco}=1.17\left[(E_{\rm blob}^{\rm tracker}
 -E_{\mu\text{-fuzz}}^{\rm tracker})+(E_{\rm blob}^{\rm ECAL}
 -E_{\mu\text{-fuzz}}^{\rm ECAL})\right],
 \label{eq:eavail-reco-implemented}
\end{equation}
implemented in the reconstruction function (\texttt{CVUniverse::NewEavail()}), with no final clamp to non-negative values.

Both definitions were inherited from MAT-MINERvA rather than chosen for this analysis, so agreement with a published convention would support the inherited definition without explaining its origin; nor is there a unique published $E_{\rm avail}$ definition.  Rodrigues \emph{et al.}~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil} give a closed five-species list of proton and charged-pion kinetic energy plus $\pi^0$, electron and photon total energy.  Equation~\eqref{eq:eavail-implemented} matches four of those five species and excludes secondary $e^\pm$. The later Ascencio \emph{et al.}~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} definition is open and includes other final-state particles except neutrons, subtracting a nucleon mass for strange baryons. It therefore includes species our closed list omits. The charged-pion term in Rodrigues and in Henry/Su \emph{et al.} Eq.~4~\cite{MINERvA:2023avz} is kinetic energy; the \texttt{minerva-ml} implementation (App.~\ref{sec:pet}) instead uses charged-pion total energy.  We therefore do not claim that our definition matches ``the published definition.''

The charged-pion term subtracts the inherited constant \SI{135}{MeV} rather than the charged-pion mass of about \SI{139.57}{MeV}, and the production keeps that constant. Relative to the charged-pion mass, it moves \SI{0.67}{\percent} of simulated events into another true-$\Eavail$ bin and changes no true-$\Eavail$ bin by more than \SI{1.05}{\percent}. This effect has not been assessed against the projection of the five-dimensional covariance of the configuration-(a) central value, so its materiality is not established.

\subsection{Closure: the one injected $\Eavail$ deformation is recovered}
\label{sec:3d-closure}
A reweighting closure tests the 3D unfolding of configuration~(a) only, on one injected deformation. The unfolding recovers a known $+\SI{30}{\percent}$ Gaussian bump injected into pseudo-data in \emph{truth} $\Eavail$ (center \SI{0.3}{GeV}, width \SI{0.15}{GeV}). The recovered step-2 mean weight is $1.048$ against the injected $1.0485$. Residuals against reweighted truth on the $\Eavail$-marginal have median $0.9999$ and standard deviation \textbf{\num{0.0006}} (\SI{0.06}{\percent}); the per-3D-bin residual standard deviation is \SI{3.2}{\percent} from MC statistics in sparse high-recoil corners. The closure's marginal scatter is far below the $\SI{3.7}{\percent}$ data-versus-2D scatter. Because the closure and the data comparison belong to different configurations, (a) and (e) against (f), their ratio is not quoted. Total normalization matches to better than \SI{1}{\percent}. In the metric of \S\ref{sec:validation} ($\Eavail$ projection) the residual is \SI{1.84}{\percent} vs \SI{18.1}{\percent} injected (ratio $0.102$) and is not graded, because no acceptance threshold fixed before the test is on record.

\subsection{Classifier-family cross-check (GBDT vs.\ NN)}
\label{sec:3d-classifier}
Our gradient-boosted decision trees differ from the neural-network classifiers used in published OmniFold analyses~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Canelli:2025guide}. This cross-check uses the configuration-(a) GBDT unfold, with purity-based subtraction. The weight $w=p/(1-p)$ holds only if the GBDT output is a calibrated posterior. The step-1 reco classifier (data vs.\ MC reco) has a near-diagonal reliability curve, Brier \(\approx0.25\) at AUC \(\approx0.537\). Data and reco MC are nearly indistinguishable, so the reweight is a modest correction. A full scalar cross-check with a Keras multilayer perceptron (MLP), the same weighted 3D inputs and the same five-iteration OmniFold loop gives total ratio $1.0078$ versus GBDT and median projection differences of \SIrange{0.7}{1.4}{\percent} (Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}). Agreement in the populated bulk establishes that the configuration-(a) scalar result is not specific to its classifier family.

\subsection{Generator comparison in 3D}
\label{sec:3d-models}
The published ancillary files provide only binned 2D model histograms. Quantitative 3D comparisons, or comparisons in arbitrary projections, require generating truth-level events and histogramming them as $d^{3}\sigma$ in the same fiducial phase space with the same truth $\Eavail$ definition. Because the unfolded result is at truth level, the comparisons need no detector simulation. We generated and processed GENIE, NuWro and GiBUU samples, and extracted MINERvA Tune~v1 from analysis MC truth with its full tune weight. No spectra were digitized or externally supplied.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{GENIE 2.12.10 central value}, generated with \texttt{gevgen} on a CH target with the MINERvA ME~FHC flux, using the DefaultPlusValenciaMEC cross-section splines~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} but gevgen's default event-generator list, which excludes MEC, so the sample has no 2p2h events (\S\ref{sec:3d-2p2h}). It carries no MINERvA reweights, and its events are reweighted to the analysis flux as described below.
  \item \textbf{MINERvA Tune v1}, the analysis tune (base GENIE $+$ RPA, low-recoil 2p2h, and non-resonant-pion reweights), taken directly from the truth of the analysis simulation, which reproduces the normalization of the published ancillary curve to \SI{0.01}{\percent}.
        % (the \texttt{w\_truth} weights)
  \item \textbf{NuWro 21.09}~\cite{Golan:2012wx}, an independent generator on C with the same flux (after reweighting below) and phase space.
  \item \textbf{GiBUU 2019}~\cite{Buss:2011mx}, a transport-theory generator on C, built and run natively, without a container, with the same flux and phase space.
\end{itemize}
NEUT~\cite{Hayato:2009zz} is not included. Each prediction is normalized by its own flux-averaged total CC cross section per nucleon.

\paragraph{Flux repair of the GENIE and NuWro predictions.} Except in three studies that keep the unrepaired samples, the GENIE and NuWro predictions in \S\S\ref{sec:3d}--\ref{sec:eavailw} are repaired by exact per-event reweighting to the analysis PPFX central-value flux on 0--\SI{100}{GeV}, with correctly sampled 50--\SI{100}{GeV} supplements. The three studies, each of which says so where it appears, are the FSI-dial studies, the departure ratio of \S\ref{sec:val-negweight} and the NuWro-shaped prior of \S\ref{sec:fps}. The repair corrects the flux from which the GENIE (CV and $+$Valencia~2p2h) and NuWro samples were generated. That flux had no PPFX weights, was capped at \SI{50}{GeV}, and stored flux \emph{densities} in variable-width bins that were sampled and normalized as \emph{contents}, which under-weighted every wide high-energy bin and suppressed the high-$\pPar$ predictions and the integrated rates (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, \recentry{flux-repair}). GiBUU reads its own flux and receives an energy-dependent per-event reweight to the same convention, which changes its integrated cross section by a factor $0.9966$. Its sample stops at $E_\nu\approx\SI{20}{GeV}$, so it is empty above $\pPar\approx\SI{20}{GeV/c}$. The analysis simulation, MINERvA Tune~v1, is not affected.

\subsection{The low-available-energy excess and the known low-recoil deficit}
\label{sec:3d-2p2h}
We compare the low-$\Eavail$ excess with candidate model changes, both by reweighting and by generating the relevant physics. MINERvA first isolated the rate enhancement between quasielastic and $\Delta$ peaks, with multi-nucleon final states, in a low-three-momentum-transfer subsample~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}. The empirical tune was built to absorb it; our muon-kinematics $\times\,\Eavail$ central values show the predictions falling short in the same region.

\paragraph{The tested pion-FSI variations do not account for it.} Reweighting 2M central-value GENIE events with GENIE's \texttt{grwght1p} changes $d\sigma/d\Eavail$ by less than a percent for both tested pion-FSI dials. \texttt{FrInel\_pi} shifts the spectrum by $\le\SI{0.74}{\percent}$ (peak in $0.10$--$\SI{0.20}{GeV}$) and \texttt{FrAbs\_pi} by $\le\SI{0.82}{\percent}$ (peak in the lowest bin). Both are ratios within the unrepaired GENIE sample (\S\ref{sec:3d-models}), in which the flux defect cancels to first order. Neither was recomputed. The integrated rate moves only $\pm\SI{0.02}{}$--$\SI{0.03}{\percent}$ at $\pm1\sigma$. \footnote{\texttt{FrInel\_pi} is disabled in the standard MAT-MINERvA set of systematics. This is MINERvA's current practice (private communication from the collaboration, 2026), and the reason is a degeneracy (of overlapping pion-FSI dials, one must be dropped), not a defect of that dial.} A dial that moves the spectrum by less than \SI{1}{\percent} cannot account for an excess of \SIrange{8}{36}{\percent} per bin over the GENIE central value below $\Eavail=\SI{0.4}{GeV}$, so the tested variations do not account for the excess. This conclusion is limited to these dials and their range; other transport models or proton-FSI effects are not excluded. Recent MINERvA quasielastic-like measurements point toward overestimated proton and charged-pion FSI in their model comparisons~\cite{MINERvA:2026qeleme}. Their different signal definition prevents directly identifying that effect with our inclusive discrepancy.

\paragraph{Comparison with a missing 2p2h component.} The base GENIE central value uses the default event-generator list and contains \emph{no} meson-exchange-current (2p2h/MEC) events. None of the $1.48\times10^{6}$ CC events of the generated sample is a MEC event, and the 50--\SI{100}{GeV} supplement is MEC-free as well. Before the flux reweight of \S\ref{sec:3d-models}, the sample's unweighted modes are QE \SI{11}{\percent}, RES \SI{22}{\percent}, DIS \SI{66}{\percent} and COH \SI{0.9}{\percent}. 2p2h must therefore be \emph{added}; reweighting cannot supply it. The mode decomposition of central-value $d\sigma/d\Eavail$ (per-bin mode count-fractions $\times$ bin value), overlaid with data, locates the largest positive gaps in the quasielastic--$\Delta$ dip, $[0.10,0.20)$ and $[0.20,0.40)$, while central values are much closer in the QE-dominated $[0,0.10)$ and $[0.40,1.50)$ regions. At central value, \SI{69}{\percent} of the positive deficit lies at $\Eavail\le\SI{0.4}{GeV}$, where 2p2h events populate. Closing the integrated deficit requires 2p2h at $\approx\SI{45}{\percent}$ of the QE rate (\SIrange{68}{71}{\percent} in the two dip bins), the standard magnitude of the empirical and Valencia MEC models, compared with sub-percent FSI shifts (Fig.~\ref{fig:modedecomp}).

\paragraph{GENIE with Valencia 2p2h enabled.} We regenerated the GENIE central value with Valencia 2p2h enabled (\texttt{--event-generator-list Default+CCMEC}, 2M events). The Nieves--Simo--Vacas MEC channel is present in the splines but absent from the total-CC cross-section graph written by GENIE's \texttt{gspl2root}, so we normalize by the non-MEC CC count: QE$+$RES$+$DIS$+$COH is fixed to the known total, and MEC is added through $1/(1-f_{\mathrm{MEC}})$ with $f_{\mathrm{MEC}}=\SI{2.87}{\percent}$. 2p2h falls in the dip and brings all three low-$\Eavail$ bins toward data (Fig.~\ref{fig:mec}). In central values, it closes \SI{52}{\percent} of the data$-$CV gap at $\Eavail\le\SI{0.4}{GeV}$ and \SI{63}{\percent} of the integrated deficit (CV $-\SI{7.2}{\percent}\to-\SI{2.7}{\percent}$). The prediction still falls short in the dip, especially $[0.10,0.20)$, but its integral over the reported $\Eavail$ range is closer to data than Tune~v1 ($-\SI{2.7}{\percent}$ against $-\SI{9.5}{\percent}$). Tune v1 layers MINERvA's empirical low-recoil 2p2h \emph{enhancement} and RPA on top of the stock Valencia MEC. The central values are consistent with the known need for that enhancement, and with NuWro and GiBUU (native, un-enhanced 2p2h) sitting low in the dip as well, but they do not establish it.
% ledger row \texttt{VL160}

2p2h leaves the high-$\Eavail$ $[1.50,3.00)$ bin unchanged. There, data exceed the GENIE CV by about \SI{10}{\percent} (data/CV $1.10$) and exceed Tune~v1 as well; the high-$\Eavail$ excess over Tune~v1 is localized on the $(\Eavail,W)$ plane in \S\ref{sec:eavailw}.

The generator's 2p2h event category is also not the same as a two-body current: recent NEUT studies, for example, include two-body currents that contribute to one-particle--one-hole final states and examine their interplay with axial form factors~\cite{FrancoMunoz:2026currents}. Visible-energy comparisons can, moreover, depend on both the primary interaction and the subsequent hadronic transport.

Ascencio \emph{et al.}~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} report the same deficit in MINERvA's dedicated low-recoil inclusive $d^{2}\sigma/(dq_{3}\,d\Eavail)$ measurement for $q_{3}<\SI{1.2}{GeV}$ at the same $\langle E_\nu\rangle\sim\SI{6}{GeV}$, finding GENIE and NuWro underpredicting their low-$\Eavail$ data without enhanced 2p2h. Our muon-kinematics extension integrates over all $q_3$ instead of restricting $q_3$, which limits the comparison to the \emph{shape} and direction in $\Eavail$, without absolute normalization. In those terms our central values are consistent with that established low-recoil deficit. A bin-identical comparison of central values is in \S\ref{sec:4d}.

\subsection{Fourth axis: three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$}
\label{sec:4d}
The fourth axis adds one feature (\S\ref{sec:3d}), three-momentum transfer $q_{3}=|\vec{q}\,|$, chosen because its $(\Eavail,q_{3})$ plane separates quasielastic, 2p2h and resonance processes and permits bin-compatible comparison to MINERvA's dedicated low-recoil $d^{2}\sigma/dq_{3}\,d\Eavail$ measurement (Ascencio \emph{et al.}~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl}). Truth $q_{3}$ comes from the generator (\texttt{CVUniverse::Getq3True()}); reconstructed $q_{3}$ uses the muon $Q^{2}$ and the calorimetric recoil (\texttt{CVUniverse::RecoQ3()}). Both are evaluated for all twelve playlists, as for $\Eavail$. Lateral systematic shifts change $q_{3}$ but leave $\Eavail$ unchanged, so a shifted $q_{3}$ is stored for each lateral universe instead of reusing the central value. The classifier inputs are $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3})$, with deliberately coarse $q_{3}$ binning $[0,0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8,1.2,2.0,100]\,\si{GeV}$ (seven bins including a final catch bin), giving $14\times16\times7\times7=10\,976$ four-dimensional bins (4830 populated).

\paragraph{Marginalization check (4D$\to$3D).} In configuration~(a), the $q_{3}$ marginalization reproduces the 3D $\Eavail$ spectrum and low-recoil excess (Fig.~\ref{fig:q3excess}, right). The $\Eavail$-marginal data/Tune~v1 ratio is $1.21$ in the lowest bin, dips to $1.01$ across the quasielastic--$\Delta$ region, and rises to $1.16$--$1.23$ in the high-$\Eavail$ DIS tail (integrated data/Tune~v1 $=1.13$). These ratios are to Tune~v1, whereas \S\ref{sec:3d-2p2h} uses untuned GENIE. Recovering the 3D result as a by-product of the higher-dimensional unfolding is the 4D$\to$3D analog of the 3D$\to$2D marginalization check (\S\ref{sec:3d-anchor}).

\paragraph{What $q_{3}$ adds.} The resolved $(\Eavail,q_{3})$ plane (Fig.~\ref{fig:q3excess}, left) reveals structure beneath the mild $q_{3}$-marginal discrepancy integrated over $\Eavail$ ($1.03$--$1.19$, center). At fixed $\Eavail$ in the quasielastic--$\Delta$ dip, data/Tune~v1 \emph{rises} with $q_{3}$. The central value over-predicts low-$q_{3}$, moderate-$\Eavail$ cells (ratio down to $0.66$); data excess concentrates at low $\Eavail$ (all $q_{3}$) and toward high $q_{3}$. The muon-only or fixed-$q_{3}$ projections considered here do not resolve this correlation, which is a natural input to the bin-compatible comparison with Ascencio \emph{et al.} The map's comparator is the MINERvA Tune~v1 prior, distinct from the untuned GENIE sample of \S\ref{sec:3d-2p2h}.

\paragraph{No 4D uncertainty is reported.} Nor is any ratio of the unified-throw to the block covariance. A corrected 4D covariance would use the actual minus and plus variation endpoints; MAT's mean-centered covariance blocks, normalized by $1/N$; one fixed estimator seed; and mean-centered joint throws, with the mean shift reported separately. It would not subtract a scalar jitter floor. Because the single-pass shifted observables exist only for events selected at the central value, re-selected event loops (\S\ref{sec:uq-higherdim}) would be needed to bound migration effects.

\paragraph{Bin-identical comparison to the published low-recoil measurement.} Supplemental data from Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} (44-cell $d^{2}\sigma/(d\Eavail\,dq_{3})$ with its full covariance, distributed inside the public arXiv source package) permit a direct quantitative cross-check. Both measurements are merged onto their maximal common grid, the edges present in \emph{both} binnings. Our $(\pT,\pPar)$ marginalization is restricted to $\pPar<\SI{20}{GeV/c}$ to mirror the Ascencio muon selection ($\theta_\mu<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<p_\mu<\SI{20}{GeV}$). Together, these give two super-cells, $\Eavail<\SI{0.4}{GeV}$ in $q_{3}\in[0.4,0.6)$ and $[0.6,1.2)\,\si{GeV}$: the low-recoil 2p2h region. Our central result lies \SI{9.2}{\percent} and \SI{6.0}{\percent} above Ascencio. No pulls or full-covariance $\chi^{2}$ are reported (\S\ref{sec:3d}; Fig.~\ref{fig:ascencio}). Both share MINERvA flux/detector systematics, treated here as independent. The $p_\mu$ and $\pPar$ cuts differ by $\lesssim\SI{6}{\percent}$ kinematic smearing at the \SI{20}{GeV} boundary. The two analyses also define true $\Eavail$ differently (\S\ref{sec:eavail-definition}): in this analysis's simulation, Ascencio's broader definition moves \SI{11.0}{\percent} of the population of our lowest true-$\Eavail$ bin ($\Eavail<\SI{0.1}{GeV}$) out of it. That shift has the sign and location of the residual. The two common cells, however, span our three lowest bins ($\Eavail<\SI{0.4}{GeV}$), and the aggregate shift over those bins has not been computed. The definition difference therefore remains an alternative explanation that is not excluded. At the \SI{10}{\percent} scale of the residual, the comparison neither refutes nor validates the result. A finer comparison on all 44 cells remains future work; it requires re-unfolding on the Ascencio edges, which is straightforward for the unbinned method, \emph{and} a systematic sweep on that binning.
