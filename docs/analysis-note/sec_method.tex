\subsection{The unbinned OmniFold procedure}
\label{sec:method}

\subsubsection{Algorithm}
\label{sec:method-algo}

OmniFold~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk} unfolds by iterated reweighting with machine-learned likelihood ratios, operating on per-event observables rather than on a fixed histogram binning. Each iteration has two steps:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Step 1 (detector space).} A classifier separates measured data from reconstructed simulation weighted by the current push-weights. Its calibrated probability $p$ gives the likelihood-ratio weight $w = p/(1-p)$ for each reconstructed simulated event. Each simulated event pairs truth with reconstruction (reco). The weight is \emph{pulled back} directly to its truth record. Truth events without a reconstructed partner (``misses'') receive step-1 weights from a regressor trained on the reconstructed subsample, $w(\mathrm{truth\ features})$. This native OmniFold acceptance treatment requires no separate efficiency correction.
  \item \textbf{Step 2 (truth space).} A second classifier separates the original truth-level simulation from the same sample carrying the pulled-back step-1 weights. Its smooth truth-space reweight becomes the next iteration's push-weight.
\end{enumerate}
After $n$ iterations, the push-weights define an efficiency-corrected truth-level reweighted simulation ensemble. Histogramming this ensemble in any binning chosen afterward gives the unfolded spectrum. The iteration count and learner capacity regularize the unfolding.

Unbinned OmniFold adds an observable by adding an input to the step-1 and step-2 classifiers, without an explicit migration matrix or per-bin response tensor. It avoids the combinatorial binning penalty of matrix unfolding but still has a dimensional cost: classifier training, acceptance and support coverage, sample sparsity and uncertainty quantification become more demanding as observables are added. The primary methodological motivation is the absence of a practical analog of D'Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU).

\paragraph{Training details.} Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, with no held-out or cross-fitted split and no early stopping. The two classes keep their physical weights, with no class balancing and no renormalization between iterations. The learned ratio $p/(1-p)$ therefore carries the normalization, with $p$ restricted to $[10^{-6},1-10^{-6}]$. Truth events without a reconstructed partner receive the prediction of an unweighted regressor of the step-1 weight on the truth observables. A regressor weighted with each event's current weight instead moves the sixteen-cell data result (\S\ref{sec:fived16}) by at most \fvval{rsn-wr-sig-max} of a cell's reported standard deviation. In noise-free, signal-only closures on the full simulation, it leaves the bias flags at the GiBUU hybrid truth and the $q_3$ tilt unchanged. Its effect on the flags from half-sample pseudo-data with background and noise was not tested. A held-out transfer probe among matched events at fixed truth places no cell beyond half its reported standard deviation, at the data or at those two truths. It cannot show that the missed events resemble matched events in what the five features omit. In the refined configurations, a classifier separating positive from negative weights, trained with $|w|$, gives each event the weight $|w|\max(0,2g-1)$. For the \estMultiD{}, the per-event Kish effective sample size and weight tails have been computed on the sixteen reported cells~\cite{UnfoldedWeightsESS2026}. They describe the unfolded simulated-event weights and are not an uncertainty, coverage or validity statement.

\paragraph{Background subtraction.} In binned purity subtraction, each selected data event enters step 1 with a reco-bin \emph{purity} weight. No negative-weight events are injected. The background prediction, scaled by protons on target (POT), uses the reco analysis grid. It includes genuine backgrounds and out-of-phase-space signal (selection, \S\ref{sec:selection}). A data event in reco bin $b$ receives

  w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b) \;=\;
  \max\!\bigl(0,\; N_{\mathrm{data}}(b) - N_{\mathrm{bkg}}(b)\bigr)

the predicted signal purity from data and background counts, floored at zero in rare bins where predicted background exceeds data. The step-1 classifier retains full unbinned kinematics. By design, binning enters only through this multiplicative correction before final histogramming. Its effect is ${\sim}\SI{3}{\percent}$ overall. It peaks where backgrounds concentrate (selection, \S\ref{sec:selection}).

For other experiments' subtraction methods, see \S\ref{sec:intro}. A fully unbinned alternative adds negative-weight background simulation on the measured side. Its bin-integrated target is $(D_{b}-B_{b})/S_{b}$ (data, background and signal). The treatments do not share the continuous density $(D(x)-B(x))/S(x)$. The alternative reproduces the binned correction to \SI{\nwPctTot}{\percent} on the total two-dimensional (2D) cross section and its systematic and statistical covariances to within \SI{2}{\percent} (subtraction comparison, App.~\ref{app:negweight}). The quoted 2D result and covariance, 3D and 4D central values, sixteen-cell result and covariance, and studies and tests of the \estLowCap{} and \estMultiD{} use the refined variant. All other quoted cross sections and uncertainties, including the \resFiveAxis{} of the \estPurity{} and the extended-fiducial value, use binned subtraction. \subsubsection{Estimators and configuration}
\label{sec:method-config}

The quoted 2D cross section and covariance use the \estTwoD{}. The 3D and 4D central values, the \resSixteen{} and the generator tests use the \estMultiD{}. Both subtract background with negative weights and a 400-tree refinement classifier. They differ in training histogram construction in the LightGBM learner and in deterministic settings. An earlier \estPurity{} is retained for the \resFiveAxis{} and for specified figures and closures. Three other configurations serve only as diagnostic controls in the studies listed in Table~\ref{tab:estimators-matrix}, the matrix of estimator uses and implementations.

Gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs) replace OmniFold's original neural networks when unfolding scalar observables. With at most five high-level features per event, tree ensembles typically match or outperform neural networks~\cite{Grinsztajn:2022tabular} and train deterministically and far faster on a central processing unit (CPU). All unfolding results here use GBDTs (point-cloud representations, App.~\ref{sec:pet}). Calibration tests and multilayer perceptron (MLP) cross-checks test the implementation (classifier checks, \S\ref{sec:3d-classifier}).

The unfolding code supports interchangeable learners (component terminology and code paths in App.~\ref{app:codebase}). The quoted 2D central value and all its uncertainty ensembles use LightGBM with refined negative-weight subtraction (background method, App.~\ref{app:negweight}). The estimator matrix (Table~\ref{tab:estimators-matrix}) lists this as the \estTwoD{}. The ensembles cover the systematic-universe sweep, Poisson bootstrap, training-seed variations and run-period unfoldings. The step-1 classifier, the step-2 classifier and the regressor for unreconstructed truth events share one setting: $100$ trees, $8$ leaves and learning rate $0.1$. The universe sweep supplies the central value at seed~42. Thus the systematic covariance shares its estimator and seed (systematic universes, \S\ref{sec:uq-universes}). The quoted statistical covariance also uses seed~42, with three independent bootstrap streams (data, signal and background simulation, \S\ref{sec:uq-boot}). The training-seed covariance uses five seed settings that include seed~42 (\S\ref{sec:uq-ml}).

A central unfolding at seed~45 differs from the seed-42 one by at most \SI{1.4}{\percent} in a cell. The quoted central value has a recorded configuration and repeats numerically. It agrees with the refined 2D data unfolding (background study, App.~\ref{sec:negweight-footing}). The purity-subtracted seed-42 central value has no recorded run configuration, although an independent rerun and a regeneration whose run configuration is recorded both reproduce it (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, \recentry{2d-central-value}). The \estMultiD{} keeps five iterations under the precision study's rule (\S\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}). The LightGBM learner of the central value and its ensembles uses matched capacity ($\texttt{num\_leaves}=8=2^{3}$), so both have the same deliberately modest capacity, an OmniFold regularizer (algorithm, \S\ref{sec:method-algo}).

The \estPurity{} and the \estTwoD{} leave histogram construction at its default. Bin edges come from the library's default row sample. The training seed affects the result through that sample. The configurations this section calls deterministic set those edges from every training row and enable the library's deterministic training.

The Keras cross-check (\S\ref{sec:3d-classifier}) uses the same multilayer perceptron for both classifiers and missed-event regression. It has three hidden layers of 64, 128 and 64 units and Gaussian error linear unit (GELU) activation. \subsubsection{Cross-section extraction}
\label{sec:method-xsec}
In n dimensions, the differential cross section follows from unfolded truth-level counts:
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{d^{n}\sigma}{d x_{1}\cdots d x_{n}}
  = \frac{U}{c\,\Phi\,N\,\mathrm{POT}\,\Delta x_{1}\cdots\Delta x_{n}}
    \times 10^{4},
\end{equation*}
where $U$ is the unfolded yield and $c$ is per-bin \emph{completeness}, the fraction of truth events visible to unfolding. The event loop appends truth events without reconstructed partners. Thus the defined truth sample enters and $c\equiv1$ per bin. Negative truth $W^{2}$ is excluded by the five-observable sample definition (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-definitions}). This differs from losing a reconstructed event from the input. The factor remains a monitored self-check. Dropping unmatched truth events would give $c<1$. $\Phi$ is the flux integral by transverse muon momentum $\pT$. It is tabulated per square meter and broadcast over other axes. The formula uses that value. $N$ is the fiducial nucleon count ($3.2353\times10^{30}$). POT is the data exposure ($1.057\times10^{21}$). The factor $10^{4}$ converts flux tabulated per square meter to the reported $\si{cm^2}$ ($\SI{1}{m^2}=10^{4}\,\si{cm^2}$), giving units of $\si{cm^2}$ per axis unit per nucleon. The implementation (code, App.~\ref{app:codebase}) is self-tested to reproduce stored lower-dimensional reference extractions to $<10^{-12}$. Since $\sigma\propto1/\Phi$, $\Phi$ varies per flux universe when building the systematic covariance. This variation dominates the Flux band (systematics, \S\ref{sec:systematics}).

The $\si{cm^{-2}}$ value is the converted integral. It is not substituted for $\Phi$.

\subsubsection{Binning}
\label{sec:method-binning}
% The reported binning follows \texttt{minerva\_paper\_anc/bin\_mapping.txt} (cited below).
The 2D grid has 14 $\pT$ $\times$ 16 $\pPar$ (longitudinal muon momentum) bins. Of 224, 205 are reported. The 19 unreported diagonal cells have $\pT^{\mathrm{hi}}/\pPar^{\mathrm{lo}}>\tan20^{\circ}$. The extension axes use $\Eavail$: $[0,0.1,0.2,0.4,0.8,1.5,3.0,100]\,\si{GeV}$; $q_{3}$ (three-momentum transfer): $[0,0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8,1.2,2.0,100]\,\si{GeV}$; $W$: $[0,1.1,1.4,1.8,2.2,3.0,100]\,\si{GeV}$. Wide final bins capture full charged-current (CC) inclusive tails in marginals. Truncating the top edge would break the marginalization checks in \S\ref{sec:3d-anchor}. Post-unfolding binning lets us revisit these choices without rerunning unfolding. The release appendix, App.~\ref{app:release} (\S\ref{app:release-edges}) gives all five axes' edges. \S\ref{sec:205bins} defines the 205 reported cells, and the authoritative mapping is the bin-mapping file of the published data release~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.


Truth$\to$reco migration on the analysis binning (Fig.~\ref{fig:migration}) shows the smearing OmniFold must undo. OmniFold uses no response matrix. Median reconstruction stability (same-bin fraction, Fig.~\ref{fig:migration}) is $\sim0.6$ on each axis. Such migrations would badly bias a bin-by-bin correction. Unfolding is required.

\subsubsection{Related developments and estimator scope}
Profile OmniFold~\cite{Zhu:2025profile} extends classifier-based expectation maximization (EM) to forward-model nuisance parameters. Profiling nuisances with observed data would change our inference model, which propagates an external nuisance ensemble through a fixed procedure. Neither a profiled estimator nor its uncertainty enters here.

SPINUP learns a forward mapping~\cite{Butter:2025spinup}. AUSSIE replaces OmniFold's second step with a different loss and avoids the outer iteration~\cite{Ore:2026aussie}. Both offer possible future cross-checks with different algorithmic assumptions. Reduced simulation-prior dependence does not establish detector-model independence, adequate phase-space support, or coverage for this result. Comparisons would require matched signal, selection, normalization, and uncertainty definitions.
