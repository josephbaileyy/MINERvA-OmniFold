\section{The unbinned OmniFold procedure}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Algorithm}
\label{sec:method-algo}

OmniFold~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk} unfolds by iterated reweighting with machine-learned likelihood ratios, operating on per-event observables rather than on a fixed histogram binning. Each iteration has two steps:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Step 1 (detector space).} A classifier separates measured data from reconstructed simulation weighted by the current push-weights. Its calibrated output $p$ gives the likelihood-ratio reweight $w = p/(1-p)$ for each reconstructed simulated event. Each simulated event is a (truth, reco) pair, allowing the weight to be \emph{pulled back} directly to its truth record. Truth events without a reconstructed partner (``misses'') receive step-1 weights from a regressor trained on the reconstructed subsample, $w(\mathrm{truth\ features})$. This native OmniFold acceptance treatment requires no separate efficiency correction.
  \item \textbf{Step 2 (truth space).} A second classifier separates the original truth-level simulation from the same sample carrying the pulled-back step-1 weights. Its smooth truth-space reweight becomes the next iteration's push-weight.
\end{enumerate}
After $n$ iterations, the push-weights define an efficiency-corrected truth-level reweighted simulation ensemble. Histogramming this ensemble in any binning chosen afterwards gives the unfolded spectrum. The iteration count and learner capacity regularize the unfold.

\paragraph{Training details.} Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, with no held-out or cross-fitted split and no early stopping. The two classes keep their physical weights, with no class balancing and no renormalization between iterations, so the learned ratio $p/(1-p)$, with $p$ restricted to $[10^{-6},1-10^{-6}]$, carries the normalization. Truth events without a reconstructed partner receive the prediction of an unweighted regressor of the step-1 weight on the truth observables. In the refined configurations, a classifier separating positive from negative weights, trained with $|w|$, gives each event the weight $|w|\max(0,2g-1)$. No effective sample size of the unfolded ensemble is computed.

\paragraph{Background subtraction.} In the binned purity subtraction, all selected data events enter step 1; none is discarded and no negative-weight events are injected. Each receives a reco-bin \emph{purity} weight. The POT-scaled background prediction includes genuine backgrounds and out-of-phase-space signal (\S\ref{sec:selection}) and is binned on the reconstructed analysis grid. A data event in reco bin $b$ receives

This correction is, by design, the one place the analysis binning enters before the final histogramming; other experiments' treatments are summarized in \S\ref{sec:intro}. At the sub-percent genuine-background scale, a per-reco-bin weight is a conservative middle ground. A fully unbinned alternative injects negatively weighted background simulation on the measured side, targeting the identical background-subtracted density $(D-B)/S$. It reproduces the binned correction to \SI{\nwPctTot}{\percent} on the total 2D cross section and its systematic and statistical covariances to within \SI{2}{\percent} (App.~\ref{app:negweight}). It removes residual binning dependence and suits the extended-fiducial analysis, where backgrounds are larger. \subsection{Estimators and configuration}
\label{sec:method-config}

Gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs) replace the original OmniFold neural networks for the scalar unfolds. With at most five high-level features per event, tree ensembles typically match or outperform neural networks~\cite{Grinsztajn:2022tabular} and train deterministically and far faster on CPU. Ref.~\cite{Milton:2025tools} demonstrates their effectiveness for unbinned unfolding with few high-level features, complementing deep models for low-level event representations. We use GBDTs for all unfolds in this note (point-cloud representations: App.~\ref{sec:pet}). Calibration tests and MLP/transformer cross-checks test the implementation in \S\ref{sec:3d-classifier}.

The unfolding code supports interchangeable learners (component terminology and code paths in App.~\ref{app:codebase}). The quoted 2D central value and all of its uncertainty ensembles (the systematic-universe sweep, the Poisson bootstrap, the training-seed block and the run-period unfolds) use LightGBM with $100$ trees, $8$ leaves and learning rate $0.1$, with the same settings for the step-1 classifier, the step-2 classifier and the miss regressor. They use refined negative-weight background subtraction (App.~\ref{app:negweight}), whose refinement classifier has 400 trees and 31 leaves and takes the estimator seed plus three as its seed. This is configuration~(f) below. The central value is the universe sweep's own central value at seed~42, so the systematic block shares its estimator and seed (\S\ref{sec:uq-universes}). The quoted statistical block, a bootstrap drawn in three independent streams (data, signal and background simulation), also uses seed~42, and the training-seed block uses five seed settings that include seed~42 (\S\ref{sec:uq-boot}, \S\ref{sec:uq-ml}). A central unfold at seed~45 differs from the seed-42 one by at most \SI{1.4}{\percent} in a cell. With the purity-based subtraction (configuration~(a)), the seed-42 central value differs from the seed-1 one by a per-bin median of \SI{0.18}{\percent} (maximum \SI{1.79}{\percent}). That is 0.30 (maximum 1.28) of the per-bin standard deviation of the earlier statistical band, which was built at seed~1. Both of those central values are regenerated to $\lesssim2\times10^{-11}$ by runs with one driver and one input that differ only in the seed, so the difference is attributable to the seed, as inferred from the two regenerations. The quoted central value records its run configuration; it repeats to $9\times10^{-12}$ and equals the refined 2D data unfold of App.~\ref{sec:negweight-footing} to $7\times10^{-12}$. The purity-subtracted seed-42 central value has no recorded run configuration. An independent rerun reproduces it to $1.4\times10^{-11}$, and so does a regeneration whose run configuration is recorded (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, \recentry{2d-central-value}). Against the ten purity-subtracted LightGBM central values at seeds 1--10 it is mildly over-dispersed (RMS standardized difference 1.28, maximum 4.25). Seed~42 was chosen on computing cost: building the statistical band at seed~42 was the cheaper of the two costed ways to give the central value and the statistical band a common seed.

\paragraph{Hyperparameter choice.} The learner settings were fixed \emph{a priori}; no hyperparameter search was performed on data, which avoids tuning on the result. They are the scikit-learn \texttt{GradientBoosting} defaults: 100 trees, learning rate $0.1$, and depth-3 capacity. The LightGBM learner of the central value and its ensembles uses matched capacity ($\texttt{num\_leaves}=8=2^{3}$), giving every learner the same deliberately modest capacity, one of OmniFold's regularizers (\S\ref{sec:method-algo}). The configuration is instead validated after the fact by closure and classifier-calibration tests (\S\ref{sec:validation}, \S\ref{sec:3d-classifier}), insensitivity to doubling the iteration count, measured for the exact-split cross-check (below), backend agreement in method-dependence studies (\S\ref{sec:results-tension}), and an explicit training-seed variation band for residual ML stochasticity (\S\ref{sec:systematics}).

Production uses $n=5$ iterations. Huang \emph{et al.}~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold} select 45 iterations for OmniFold-family methods (20 for their binned benchmark) using a binned $\chi^{2}$ against known mock-data truth, a criterion they note is infeasible on real data. We do not adopt that recommendation. Their mock data injects a large prior--data \emph{shape} discrepancy through an RPA-modified quasielastic model, requiring many iterations. Our prior already describes the data shapes well (\S\ref{sec:controlplots}); the data pull is dominantly a normalization. Cross-experiment production also typically uses ${\sim}5$ iterations~\cite{Canelli:2025guide}. Our $n=5$ is instead justified empirically: for the exact-split cross-check, the total cross section agrees to within \SI{0.026}{\percent} between five and ten iterations (\S\ref{sec:validation}), and the unfold reproduces an independently published binned measurement of the same data (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Cross-section extraction}
\label{sec:method-xsec}
The differential cross section follows from unfolded truth-level counts:
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{d^{n}\sigma}{d x_{1}\cdots d x_{n}}
  = \frac{U}{c\,\Phi\,N\,\mathrm{POT}\,\Delta x_{1}\cdots\Delta x_{n}}
    \times 10^{4},
\end{equation*}
where $U$ is the unfolded yield and $c$ the per-bin \emph{completeness}, the fraction of truth events visible to the unfolding input. The event loop explicitly appends truth-only misses, so every truth event enters and $c\equiv1$ bin-by-bin. The factor remains a monitored self-check; dropping misses would give $c<1$. $\Phi$ is the per-$\pT$ flux integral, broadcast over other axes, $N$ the fiducial nucleon count ($3.2353\times10^{30}$), and POT the data exposure ($1.057\times10^{21}$). The factor $10^{4}$ converts flux tabulated per square meter to the reported $\si{cm^2}$ ($\SI{1}{m^2}=10^{4}\,\si{cm^2}$), giving units of $\si{cm^2}$ per axis unit per nucleon. The implementation (App.~\ref{app:codebase}) is self-tested to reproduce stored lower-dimensional reference extractions to $<10^{-12}$. Since $\sigma\propto1/\Phi$, $\Phi$ varies per flux universe when building the systematic covariance. This variation dominates the Flux band (\S\ref{sec:systematics}).

\subsection{Binning}
\label{sec:method-binning}
% The reported binning follows \texttt{minerva\_paper\_anc/bin\_mapping.txt} (cited below).
The 2D grid has 14 $\pT$ $\times$ 16 $\pPar$ bins; 205 of 224 are reported. The 19 unreported diagonal cells have $\pT^{\mathrm{hi}}/\pPar^{\mathrm{lo}}>\tan20^{\circ}$. The extension axes use $\Eavail$: $[0,0.1,0.2,0.4,0.8,1.5,3.0,100]\,\si{GeV}$; $q_{3}$: $[0,0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8,1.2,2.0,100]\,\si{GeV}$; $W$: $[0,1.1,1.4,1.8,2.2,3.0,100]\,\si{GeV}$. Each ends in a wide catch bin to capture the full CC-inclusive tails in marginals; truncating the top edge would break the marginalization checks of \S\ref{sec:3d-anchor}. Binning after unfolding allows these choices to be revisited without re-running the unfold. App.~\ref{app:release} (\S\ref{app:release-edges}) gives all five axes' edges. \S\ref{sec:205bins} defines the 205 reported cells; the authoritative mapping is the bin-mapping file of the published data release~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}.

Neither underflow nor overflow appears in the reported product, by construction rather than by truncation. Truth-level muon edges coincide with the measurement phase space (\S\ref{sec:selection}); no signal is defined beyond them. Hadronic axes span $[0,100]\,\si{GeV}$, with catch-bin edges far beyond the kinematic reach at $\langle E_\nu\rangle\sim\SI{6}{GeV}$, so the truth grids are exhaustive. A selected reco event outside the grid is treated as unaccepted and excluded from the measured sample by \S\ref{sec:method-driver}'s masking convention. Its in-phase-space truth content is recovered by the miss treatment, as for any unreconstructed event. The unbinned unfold has no response-matrix under/overflow to track; reporting bins are applied once to events that all lie inside the grids.

Truth$\to$reco migration on the analysis binning (Fig.~\ref{fig:migration}) summarizes the smearing OmniFold must undo, although it uses no response matrix. Median diagonal purity is $\sim0.6$ on every axis. These substantial migrations require unfolding; a bin-by-bin correction would be badly biased.

The structure differs qualitatively between the muon and hadron sides. The spectrometer muon measurement makes the $\pT$ and $\pPar$ maps nearly tridiagonal, with migrations rarely exceeding one bin. Purity dips to ${\sim}0.45$--$0.5$ for $\pT$ and ${\sim}0.35$ for $\pPar$ where bins are finest, recovering in wide edge bins. The calorimetric axes have coarser bins but relatively broader, visibly asymmetric migrations. $\Eavail$ preferentially migrates \emph{downward} because energy carried by neutrons and other unresponsive particles is not collected. Diagonal purity falls from $0.61$ in the lowest bins to $0.43$ in the highest. $q_{3}$, built from $\Eavail$ and the muon, inherits a milder version of the same pattern. $W$ compounds $\Eavail$ and muon smearing and has the broadest migrations; two-bin shifts are common in the resonance region. The lowest-$W$ (quasi-elastic) bin remains purest at $0.71$.

\subsection{Related developments and estimator scope}
Profile OmniFold~\cite{Zhu:2025profile} extends classifier-based EM to forward-model nuisance parameters. Its distinct algorithmic construction complements earlier unbinned profiled unfolding~\cite{Chan:2023tbf}. Profiling nuisances with observed data would change our inference model, which propagates an external nuisance ensemble through a fixed procedure. Neither a profiled estimator nor its uncertainty is used here.

SPINUP learns a forward mapping~\cite{Butter:2025spinup}; AUSSIE replaces OmniFold's second step with a different loss and avoids the outer iteration~\cite{Ore:2026aussie}. Both offer possible future cross-checks with different algorithmic assumptions. Reduced simulation-prior dependence does not establish detector-model independence, adequate phase-space support, or coverage for this measurement. Comparisons would require matched signal, selection, normalization, and uncertainty definitions.
