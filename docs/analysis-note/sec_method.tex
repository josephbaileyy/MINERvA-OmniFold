\subsection{The unbinned OmniFold procedure}
\label{sec:method}

\subsubsection{Algorithm}
\label{sec:method-algo}

OmniFold~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk} unfolds by iterated reweighting with machine-learned likelihood ratios, operating on per-event observables rather than on a fixed histogram binning. Each iteration has two steps:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Step 1 (detector space).} A classifier separates measured data from reconstructed simulation weighted by the current push-weights. Its calibrated probability $p$ gives the likelihood-ratio weight $w = p/(1-p)$ for each reconstructed simulated event. Each simulated event pairs truth with reconstruction (reco). The weight is \emph{pulled back} directly to its truth record. See the records for acceptance details (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}).
  \item \textbf{Step 2 (truth space).} A second classifier separates the original truth-level simulation from the same sample carrying the pulled-back step-1 weights. Its smooth truth-space reweight becomes the next iteration's push-weight.
\end{enumerate}
After $n$ iterations, the push-weights define an efficiency-corrected truth-level reweighted simulation ensemble. Histogramming this ensemble in any binning chosen afterward gives the unfolded spectrum. The iteration count and learner capacity regularize the unfolding.

See the records for dimensional scaling (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}).

\paragraph{Training details.} Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, with no held-out or cross-fitted split and no early stopping. The two classes keep their physical weights, with no class balancing and no renormalization between iterations. The learned ratio $p/(1-p)$ therefore carries the normalization, with $p$ restricted to $[10^{-6},1-10^{-6}]$. Truth events without a reconstructed partner receive the prediction of an unweighted regressor of the step-1 weight on the truth observables. A regressor weighted with each event's current weight instead moves the sixteen-cell data result (\S\ref{sec:fived16}) by at most \fvval{rsn-wr-sig-max} of a cell's reported standard deviation. In noise-free, signal-only closures on the full simulation, it leaves the bias flags at the GiBUU hybrid truth and the $q_3$ tilt unchanged. Its effect on the flags from half-sample pseudo-data with background and noise was not tested. A held-out transfer probe among matched events at fixed truth places no cell beyond half its reported standard deviation, at the data or at those two truths. It cannot show that the missed events resemble matched events in what the five features omit. For the \estMultiD{}, the per-event Kish effective sample size and weight tails have been computed on the sixteen reported cells~\cite{UnfoldedWeightsESS2026}. \subsubsection{Cross-section extraction}
\label{sec:method-xsec}
In n dimensions, the differential cross section follows from unfolded truth-level counts:
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{d^{n}\sigma}{d x_{1}\cdots d x_{n}}
  = \frac{U}{c\,\Phi\,N\,\mathrm{POT}\,\Delta x_{1}\cdots\Delta x_{n}}
    \times 10^{4},
\end{equation*}
where $U$ is the unfolded yield and $c$ is per-bin \emph{completeness}, the fraction of truth events visible to unfolding. The event loop appends truth events without reconstructed partners. Thus the defined truth sample enters and $c\equiv1$ per bin. $\Phi$ is the flux integral by transverse muon momentum $\pT$. It is tabulated per square meter and broadcast over other axes. $N$ is the fiducial nucleon count ($3.2353\times10^{30}$). POT is the data exposure ($1.057\times10^{21}$). The factor $10^{4}$ converts flux tabulated per square meter to the reported $\si{cm^2}$ ($\SI{1}{m^2}=10^{4}\,\si{cm^2}$), giving units of $\si{cm^2}$ per axis unit per nucleon. See the records for extraction checks and flux universes (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}).
