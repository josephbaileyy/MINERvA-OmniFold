\subsection{Validation of the two-dimensional result}
\label{sec:validation}

In a simulated experiment, \emph{truth} is the known target distribution, the \emph{prior} is the starting simulated truth distribution, and the \emph{response} describes reconstruction and selection conditional on truth. A closure test compares the unfolded estimate with its known target under a specified deformation. Bias is the mean estimate minus that target over repeated experiments. A single closure residual also contains sampling fluctuations. Coverage is the fraction of repeated intervals containing the specified truth, and tests the estimator and interval procedure together. Table~\ref{tab:validation-questions} separates these questions before the results. Repeating a numerical calculation checks neither the coverage and width of the intervals nor the adequacy of the physical model.

\paragraph{Comparison of injected-bump and generator-shaped closure tests.} The injected-bump and generator-shaped tests differ in deformation, metric and iteration dependence. Their results do not contradict each other.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Deformation.} The closing injections are single smooth bumps in one variable the classifiers see directly. A generator ratio changes correlated structure across $\Eavail$, $W$ and $q_3$, including directions the reconstructed observables constrain weakly (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}, comparison with exact binned unfolding).
  \item \emph{Metric.} The 3D, 4D and 5D closures are judged on one-dimensional marginals, where per-cell errors average out. The 3D test's per-bin spread is already \SI{3.2}{\percent}, and the 5D test's is \SI{3.7}{\percent}. The generator-shaped failures are measured in joint $(\Eavail,W)$ cells.
  \item \emph{Iterations.} At five iterations the estimator retains part of the prior wherever the reconstructed data constrain the truth weakly. Thirty iterations reduce the $\Eavail$ residual by \SI{26}{\percent} while the $q_3$ residual grows (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}).
  \item \emph{Five-dimensional $W$ test.} A $W$ bump at the $\Delta$ peak is recovered to a ratio of 0.180 against a threshold of 0.20, a narrow pass in the marginal only. The same unfolding result leaves joint $(\Eavail,W)$ cells in the peak column up to \SI{9.4}{\percent} low and moves part of the bump into the neighboring $W$ bins. A single smooth bump in a marginal is therefore weaker evidence than the generator-shaped tests, which remain the main five-dimensional recovery evidence of \S\ref{sec:5d}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Held-out-tune coverage of the 2D covariance}
\label{sec:coverage-tunes}
A second coverage test, specified before it ran, asks whether the analysis procedure's 2D covariance covers truths held out of its construction. A pseudo-experiment is inside a cell when the difference between its unfolded result and the held-out truth lies within that cell's standard deviation under the remade covariance, and inside the joint region when its $\chi^{2}$ on that covariance, with the degrees of freedom printed below, is at most the $\chi^{2}$ quantile at the one-standard-deviation Gaussian content. The null-based threshold rule was fixed before any pseudo-experiment was read. This containment is why the test is called a coverage test. Section~\ref{sec:limitations-2d} says why the result is not a coverage rate of the reported intervals. At each of two held-out GENIE~v3 tunes, \fvval{f1-tune-ar23} and \fvval{f1-tune-g18}, with \lrval{cov2d-ntoys} pseudo-experiments each and the analysis procedure's 2D covariance remade at each tune's truth (model term included, run-period term excluded), the test reads \emph{under-covers}. The joint in-region count is \lrval{cov2d-ar23-joint} of \lrval{cov2d-ntoys} at both tunes, against thresholds of \lrval{cov2d-ar23-kjoint} (\fvval{f1-tune-ar23}) and \lrval{cov2d-g18-kjoint} (\fvval{f1-tune-g18}). Per cell, \lrval{cov2d-ar23-nflagged} cells are flagged at \fvval{f1-tune-ar23} and \lrval{cov2d-g18-nflagged} at \fvval{f1-tune-g18}, each attributed to bias (Table~\ref{tab:coverage-tunes}). The mean $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ of the pseudo-experiments is $\lrval{cov2d-ar23-chi2ndf}\pm\lrval{cov2d-ar23-chi2ndf-se}$ ($\mathrm{ndf}=\lrval{cov2d-ar23-ndf}$) at \fvval{f1-tune-ar23} and $\lrval{cov2d-g18-chi2ndf}\pm\lrval{cov2d-g18-chi2ndf-se}$ ($\mathrm{ndf}=\lrval{cov2d-g18-ndf}$) at \fvval{f1-tune-g18}. The test's false-flag rate under its null's law is at most \lrval{cov2d-ar23-size} and \lrval{cov2d-g18-size} per tune. In a descriptive study made after the test's result, truncating the joint test to its \lrval{t2a-r1} or \lrval{t2a-r2} largest-eigenvalue directions still finds none of the \lrval{cov2d-ntoys} pseudo-experiments in its region at either tune, so truncation to the better-determined directions does not remove the joint under-coverage. In the same study, the mean $\chi^{2}$ splits into a part from the pseudo-experiments' mean error $\bar e$, $\bar e^{\top}C^{+}\bar e$, and a part from their spread $S$, $\mathrm{tr}(C^{+}S)$, with $C$ the remade covariance. At \fvval{f1-tune-ar23} and \fvval{f1-tune-g18}, the mean-error part is \fvval{t2a-bias-share-ar23}\,\% and \fvval{t2a-bias-share-g18}\,\% of the mean $\chi^{2}$, or \fvval{t2a-bias-ndf-ar23} and \fvval{t2a-bias-ndf-g18} per degree of freedom; the spread part is \fvval{t2a-spread-ndf-ar23} and \fvval{t2a-spread-ndf-g18} per degree of freedom, where 1 would mean that the covariance describes the spread. In the covariance's eigenbasis, the \fvval{t2a-ntop} directions with the largest eigenvalues carry \fvval{t2a-top-ar23}\,\% and \fvval{t2a-top-g18}\,\% of the mean-error part, and the directions beyond the \fvval{t2a-r1} largest carry \fvval{t2a-beyond1-ar23}\,\% and \fvval{t2a-beyond1-g18}\,\%. \subsubsection{Other validation diagnostics}
\label{sec:val-other}

\paragraph{Unbinned diagnostic (C2ST).} In the classifier two-sample test (C2ST), a classifier is trained to separate the data from the (reweighted) simulation in the full unbinned feature space, and its held-out area under the ROC curve (AUC) or accuracy is compared with the chance value $0.5$. The AUC drops from $\approx0.535$ before unfolding to $\approx0.501$ after. On this training sample, that drop is a discrimination diagnostic. It does not show that the mismatch was removed, and it is not evidence of generalization or of calibrated agreement. This binning-free check is descriptive, and no calibrated $p$-value is quoted. As run, the C2ST trains and evaluates on the same sample without cross-fitting. A valid null would require re-running the full cross-fitted OmniFold pipeline per pseudoexperiment. Calibrated unbinned goodness-of-fit remains open~\cite{Canelli:2025guide}.
