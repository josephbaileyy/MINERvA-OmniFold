\section{Validation of the two-dimensional measurement}
\label{sec:validation}

In a simulated experiment, \emph{truth} is the known target distribution, the \emph{prior} is the starting simulated truth distribution, and the \emph{response} describes reconstruction and selection conditional on truth. A closure test compares the unfolded estimate with its known target under a specified deformation. Bias is the mean estimate minus that target over repeated experiments. A single closure residual also contains sampling fluctuations. Coverage is the fraction of repeated intervals containing the specified truth, and tests the estimator and interval procedure together. Table~\ref{tab:validation-questions} separates these questions before the results. Repeating a numerical calculation checks neither the coverage and width of the intervals nor the adequacy of the physical model.

\paragraph{Injected-feature recovery.} The 2D truth-reweighting closure with a Gaussian bump passes the recovery test of the unbinned-unfolding guide~\cite{Canelli:2025guide}. In the feature bins the residual from the injected truth is \SI{1.69}{\percent} for an injected \SI{17.2}{\percent} feature (ratio $0.098$), about ten times smaller. The test requires the residual left after recovering a deviation from the prior to be far smaller than the deviation itself. Unfolding cannot create discriminating power (3D: \S\ref{sec:3d-closure}).

A data-versus-prior $\chi^2$ with a diagonal covariance of statistical variances only is larger at truth level than at reco level. This is expected at high resolution: detector smearing lowers the reco-level value, and the diagonal covariance omits the unfolding covariance. The comparisons that use a full covariance are the $\chi^2$ against the published result (\S\ref{sec:results-tension}) and the generator comparisons.

These closure tests recover injected features and are not a formal statistical validation of the estimator. No non-closure uncertainty is assigned from them. Assigning one would require a suite of injection shapes and amplitudes specified before the tests are run. Calibration to a single Gaussian bump would leave that requirement unmet.

None of these tests changes the simulated detector. The hidden-variable closure does change the effective response: it reweights reconstructed events by a function of $\Delta\pT$ and leaves the truth weights unchanged (\S\ref{sec:closure-hv}). Appendix~\ref{app:response-mismatch} defines a separate test, a closure that rescales only the reconstructed $\Eavail$ of the pseudo-data, together with a more complete leading-nucleon follow-up. No result from it is reported here. The five-dimensional estimator, including its background subtraction, is evaluated under the technical closure checks of \S\ref{sec:val-fiveaxis}. Its coverage is unvalidated.

\paragraph{Requirements for validated total uncertainties.} The follow-up assessment~\cite{TwoDFollowup2026} distinguishes a proposed split-sample width check from repeated-sampling coverage. With the MC sample, binning maps and background subtraction held fixed, such a check could establish only the precision conditional on them, at its tested exposure. Nonlinear unfolding does not guarantee a simple scaling from half to full exposure. Nor can the check supply the variation across independent MC populations. With the current inputs, validated total uncertainties for all 205 bins, the integral and the projections therefore cannot be obtained. They would require independent populations or a validated generative law, a continuous nuisance generator for tests of total intervals, and an untouched generator-truth domain for bias checks (\S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}). This is a limit of the available inputs, not a general impossibility result for 2D unfolding.

\subsection{Closure tests across dimensions}
\label{sec:closure-map}

Only the MINERvA simulation supplies a detector response, the conditional distribution of reconstructed events given truth. Other generators (GiBUU, NuWro, GENIE variants) supply truth only. Every closure test here therefore keeps the simulated MINERvA response and changes the truth distribution, by reweighting the simulated events, or changes the analysis chain around it. Reweighting to another generator's shape is a valid known-truth test of the unfolding. It does not test the detector model. An ordinary closure, whose pseudo-data are the simulation itself, is an identity check that a constant estimator would also pass. Table~\ref{tab:closure-map} lists, for each test, what it changes relative to the unfolding's simulation and what its result can detect.

\paragraph{Comparison of injected-bump and generator-shaped closure tests.} The injected-bump and generator-shaped tests differ in deformation, metric and iteration dependence. Their results do not contradict each other.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Deformation.} The closing injections are single smooth bumps in one variable the classifiers see directly. A generator ratio changes correlated structure across $\Eavail$, $W$ and $q_3$, including directions the reconstructed observables constrain weakly (\S\ref{sec:coverage-5d}, response-information diagnostic).
  \item \emph{Metric.} The 3D, 4D and 5D closures are judged on one-dimensional marginals, where per-cell errors average out. The 3D test's per-bin spread is already \SI{3.2}{\percent}, and the 5D test's is \SI{3.7}{\percent}. The generator-shaped failures are measured in joint $(\Eavail,W)$ cells.
  \item \emph{Iterations.} At five iterations the estimator retains part of the prior wherever the reconstructed data constrain the truth weakly. Thirty iterations reduce the $\Eavail$ residual by \SI{26}{\percent} while the $q_3$ residual grows (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}).
  \item \emph{Five-dimensional $W$ test.} A $W$ bump at the $\Delta$ peak is recovered to a ratio of 0.180 against a threshold of 0.20, a narrow pass in the marginal only. The same unfolding result leaves joint $(\Eavail,W)$ cells in the peak column up to \SI{9.4}{\percent} low and moves part of the bump into the neighbouring $W$ bins. A single smooth bump in a marginal is therefore weaker evidence than the generator-shaped tests, which remain the main five-dimensional recovery evidence of \S\ref{sec:5d}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Fixed-truth coverage of the 2D statistical band}
\label{sec:coverage}
This test asks whether a 2D statistical band covers a \emph{fixed} truth at its nominal rate. The test scored the \emph{completeness-divided band}, the first-built statistical band, quoted when the test ran, whose replicas divide by a bootstrapped completeness. The \emph{undivided band} is the rebuilt band, without the division, since superseded. The quoted statistical block (\S\ref{sec:uq-boot}) is a separate ensemble of 300 replicas at the central value's training seed. The design, criteria and positive control of the test were specified before any toy was generated (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, \recentry{coverage-fixed-truth}).

Each toy draws pseudo-data event by event as Poisson counts with means equal to the simulated reconstructed weights. It resamples the simulation with the per-event Poisson(1) bootstrap of the statistical band and unfolds with that band's estimator, LightGBM with five iterations at a fixed training seed, the learner of the quoted central value at a different seed (\S\ref{sec:method-config}). The reference $T$ is the unfluctuated simulated truth, identical in every toy. The no-fluctuation configuration reproduces both the production closure output and $T$ exactly.

In reported bin $b$ of toy $t$, $z_{tb}=(U_{tb}-T_b)/(r_b T_b)$, where $r_b$ is the production band's relative spread. Coverage $C_k$ is the fraction of all (toy, bin) pairs with $|z|\le k$, with intervals from resampling whole toys. The band counts as nominal if its ratio to the true scatter lies in $[0.9,1.1]$, which gives the windows $C_1\in[\covTwoDWinOneLo,\covTwoDWinOneHi]$ and $C_2\in[\covTwoDWinTwoLo,\covTwoDWinTwoHi]$. A failure at the interim analysis of 100 toys, with 99.5\% intervals, stops the study.

At the interim analysis ($N_{\text{toy}}=\covTwoDNtoy$, 205 bins) the completeness-divided band undercovers in its tails. Its one-standard-deviation coverage $C_1=\covTwoDCOne$ $[\covTwoDCOneLo,\covTwoDCOneHi]$ is nominal (Gaussian 0.683), but its two-standard-deviation coverage $C_2=\covTwoDCTwo$ $[\covTwoDCTwoLo,\covTwoDCTwoHi]$ lies entirely below its window (Gaussian 0.954). The pull RMS is $\covTwoDPullRMS$, and the mean pull is consistent with zero. By the criteria above this is a failure by undercoverage, and the study stopped at the interim analysis. An independent reimplementation of the scoring reproduces the pooled coverages, their intervals, the controls and the secondary analysis below.

The positive control rescales the band on the same toys. At $\times0.7$ and $\times1.3$, $C_2$ moves to $\covTwoDCTwoLowCtl$ and $\covTwoDCTwoHighCtl$, and the test flags both directions.

The failure is in the width alone. The per-bin pattern, which is descriptive and post hoc, is uneven in both directions. The band is too wide in $\covTwoDNwide$ bins (pull RMS below 0.9) and too narrow in $\covTwoDNnarrow$ (above 1.1). The pooled $C_1$ is nominal partly through this cancellation. The median bin's pull RMS is $\covTwoDBinRMSmed$. $\covTwoDNlowCTwo$ bins have a per-bin $C_2$ below 0.85. Of these, 21 form a block at $\pPar\ge\SI{15}{GeV/c}$ and $\pT<\SI{0.55}{GeV/c}$. Six more lie in the highest-$\pT$ row (\SIrange{2.5}{4.5}{GeV/c}) at $\pPar$ \SIrange{6}{20}{GeV/c}. The $\covTwoDNedge$ bins with pull RMS above~2 carry \SI{\covTwoDDeficitShare}{\percent} of the $C_2$ deficit.

Unlike the band's replicas, the central value has completeness exactly~1. A secondary analysis, added after the pilot, applies the band's division to the toys. Scored against the same band, the divided toys overcover: $C_1=\covTwoDRepCOne$, with its interval above the window. The attribution rule was fixed before the full run, but its clause for the undercoverage outcome was written after the pilot pulls had been seen. Under this rule the primary undercoverage is attributed to the completeness term of the band. The attribution is partial: in the replica form, the $\pPar$ \SIrange{40}{60}{GeV/c} column still undercovers.

The band was then rebuilt without this term. The resulting undivided band was rescored only post hoc (below), and the quoted statistical block has not been tested.

\subsection{Held-out-tune coverage of the 2D covariance}
\label{sec:coverage-tunes}
A second coverage test, specified before it ran, asks whether the analysis procedure's 2D covariance covers truths held out of its construction. A pseudo-experiment is inside a cell when the difference between its unfolded result and the held-out truth lies within that cell's standard deviation under the remade covariance, and inside the joint region when its $\chi^{2}$ on that covariance, with the degrees of freedom printed below, is at most the $\chi^{2}$ quantile at the one-standard-deviation Gaussian content. The thresholds were fixed from the null before any pseudo-experiment was read. This containment is why the test is called a coverage test. The scope paragraph says why the result is not a coverage rate of the reported intervals. At each of two held-out GENIE~v3 tunes, \fvval{f1-tune-ar23} and \fvval{f1-tune-g18}, with \lrval{cov2d-ntoys} pseudo-experiments each and the analysis procedure's 2D covariance remade at each tune's truth (model term included, run-period term excluded), the test reads \emph{under-covers}. The joint in-region count is \lrval{cov2d-ar23-joint} of \lrval{cov2d-ntoys} at both tunes, against thresholds of \lrval{cov2d-ar23-kjoint} (\fvval{f1-tune-ar23}) and \lrval{cov2d-g18-kjoint} (\fvval{f1-tune-g18}). Per cell, \lrval{cov2d-ar23-nflagged} cells are flagged at \fvval{f1-tune-ar23} and \lrval{cov2d-g18-nflagged} at \fvval{f1-tune-g18}, each attributed to bias (Table~\ref{tab:coverage-tunes}). The mean $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}$ of the pseudo-experiments is $\lrval{cov2d-ar23-chi2ndf}\pm\lrval{cov2d-ar23-chi2ndf-se}$ ($\mathrm{ndf}=\lrval{cov2d-ar23-ndf}$) at \fvval{f1-tune-ar23} and $\lrval{cov2d-g18-chi2ndf}\pm\lrval{cov2d-g18-chi2ndf-se}$ ($\mathrm{ndf}=\lrval{cov2d-g18-ndf}$) at \fvval{f1-tune-g18}. \subsection{Other validation diagnostics}
\label{sec:val-other}

\paragraph{Unbinned diagnostic (C2ST).} In the classifier two-sample test (C2ST), a classifier is trained to separate the data from the (reweighted) simulation in the full unbinned feature space, and its held-out area under the ROC curve (AUC) or accuracy is compared with the chance value $0.5$. The AUC drops from $\approx0.535$ before unfolding to $\approx0.501$ after. On this training sample, that drop is a discrimination diagnostic. It does not show that the mismatch was removed, and it is not evidence of generalization or of calibrated agreement. This binning-free check is descriptive, and no calibrated $p$-value is quoted. As run, the C2ST trains and evaluates on the same sample without cross-fitting. A valid null would require re-running the full cross-fitted OmniFold pipeline per pseudoexperiment. Calibrated unbinned goodness-of-fit remains open~\cite{Canelli:2025guide}.
