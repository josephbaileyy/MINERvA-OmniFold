\subsection{Which source of error is being tested}
\label{sec:val-fiveaxis}
Prior reweighting changes the frequencies of existing simulated truth events while retaining their detector-response simulation. A response mismatch instead changes how a given truth is reconstructed. Finite classifier capacity and iteration count approximate the inverse and can leave residuals in either case. Coverage asks whether the resulting intervals contain their specified target at the stated rate (\S\ref{sec:coverage-5d}). These mechanisms are separate: an incorrect generator prediction alone does not diagnose an interval failure. Each test below isolates one intervention.

\subsection{Background subtraction and generator-sized truth changes}
\label{sec:val-negweight}

\paragraph{Background subtraction.}

End-to-end five-dimensional pseudo-experiments add background-simulation events and rebuild per-bin purity weights as in the data unfolding. At nominal truth they return the highest-$W$ column of $(\Eavail,W)$ \SIrange{3.5}{4.1}{\percent} low and the highest-$\Eavail$, highest-$W$ cell \SI{1.7}{\percent} high. Without background, the same experiments recover the truth to \SI{0.22}{\percent}. Relative to these cells' total uncertainty in the configuration-(a) covariance, the bias is 0.36--1.07 standard deviations. The bias comes from the purity-based subtraction (\S\ref{app:negweight}), enters no quoted covariance, and has no established size on data. Refined unbinned negative-weight subtraction (App.~\ref{app:negweight}), applied with a deterministic variant of the five-dimensional LightGBM production estimator and a refinement of 100 trees and 8 leaves, brings the highest-$W$ column to \SIrange{0.11}{0.24}{\percent}, the corner cell to \SI{0.24}{\percent} and every other $(\Eavail,W)$ cell to at most \SI{0.39}{\percent} over sixty nominal-truth experiments, which are not paired with the purity-based ones (Fig.~\ref{fig:bkg-residual-note}). Some joint cells remain up to \SI{1.0}{\percent} high, up to 14 standard errors, against \SI{-0.2}{\percent} without background. The mechanism is not established. On data, the two treatments differ by a median of \SI{0.36}{\percent} and at most \SI{1.4}{\percent} over the reported functionals. These differences measure sensitivity to the method. They do not measure bias. Negative-weight subtraction is not used for the configuration-(a) five-dimensional central value. The sixteen-cell result uses it (\S\ref{sec:fived16}).

\paragraph{Higher-capacity background refinement and data stability.}

The refinement classifier underfits, which causes the residual background bias (App.~\ref{app:negweight}). A candidate with raised refinement capacity, configuration (e) of the sixteen-cell result (\S\ref{sec:fived16}), passes the nominal background-inclusive closure on 40 fresh pseudo-experiments (largest deviation 2.7 standard errors) and a pooled test of its statistical intervals (pull width 1.02). The pooled test does not establish coverage and width for every functional.

The candidate fails the data-stability test, whose criterion was set before it was run: input perturbations within floating-point rounding move functionals by a median of 0.8 and at most 2.6 statistical standard deviations. The baseline estimator's shifts, a median of 0.8 and at most 2.5, place this instability in the estimator family. The instability also explains the difference of at most \SI{1.2}{\percent} on data between the driver and the development pipeline. How the data bootstrap contains this variability, and what it means for the reproducibility of the central values, is in \S\ref{sec:limitations-5d}. The paired perturbation study of \S\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence} tests whether the bootstrap contains this variability, for this candidate only.

A probe on simulated pseudo-data of two-fold cross-fitting and of two tree regularizations, specified before it ran, found no variant that met the data-stability requirement. The estimator is unchanged. A study of the mechanism on one pseudo-experiment, specified before it ran, found that the perturbation reaches the estimator by breaking ties among equal float32 values, which changes LightGBM's histogram bins. A perturbation that keeps the ties removes the spread. Perturbing only the refinement stage's input carries the full spread, so the refinement is a sufficient entry. Perturbing only the OmniFold loop also carries the full spread on the 153 quantities and is inconclusive on the sixteen cells, so neither stage alone is established as the mechanism. The stage tests and the tie test were separate, and no test linked their findings; whether the ties act at the refinement stage was not tested. Neither finding is a statement about the data's spread, and equal float32 values are what float32 storage of these kinematics produces, not a defect of the inputs.

\subsection{Model dependence, variability and reporting resolution}
\label{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}

The scalar five-dimensional estimator studied here uses production LightGBM.

These studies measure three different quantities: truth-dependent responses to iterations and capacity, small repeat variability at one setting, and appreciable prior sensitivity. Unfolding benchmarks also assess these quantities separately~\cite{Brenner2020Unfolding}. The studies do not establish a bias--variance tradeoff across estimator settings, and regularization bias can invalidate intervals based solely on estimator variability~\cite{Kuusela2015Unfolding}; neither reference supplies the variance measurement that is missing for this estimator.

The noise-free study unfolds the full simulation's reweighted reconstructed events without background. Baseline classifiers and the missed-event regressor use 100 trees and 8 leaves; the capacity probe uses 400 trees and 31 leaves.
