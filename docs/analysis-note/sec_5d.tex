\section{Five-dimensional estimator: validation and uncertainty}
\label{sec:5d}

This section gives the validation of the five-dimensional estimator behind the
central values of \S\S\ref{sec:3d}--\ref{sec:eavailw}: what each validation
test isolates (\S\ref{sec:val-fiveaxis}), background subtraction and
generator-sized truth changes (\S\ref{sec:val-negweight}), why the estimator does
not follow such changes (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}), model dependence and the
reporting definitions it rules out (\S\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}), the
failed five-dimensional coverage test (\S\ref{sec:coverage-5d}), and the status
of the one five-axis covariance used in this note (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-construction}). The
calibrated generator tests built on a variant of this estimator follow in
\S\ref{sec:joint5d}. The unbinned negative-weight background method itself is
derived in App.~\ref{app:negweight}.

\subsection{Which source of error is being tested}
\label{sec:val-fiveaxis}
Prior reweighting changes the frequencies of existing simulated truth events
while retaining their detector-response simulation; a response mismatch
instead changes how a given truth is reconstructed. Finite classifier capacity and
iteration count approximate the inverse and can leave residuals in either case. Calibration asks whether the resulting intervals
contain their specified target at the stated rate, and correcting a nominal
closure bias does not establish coverage under other truths. These mechanisms
are separate: an incorrect generator prediction alone does not diagnose an
interval failure. Each test below isolates one intervention. Improved background
refinement repairs the nominal recovery, while the failure of signal-only
experiments to follow a truth departure shows that background subtraction does
not explain all the residuals.


All 2D closures in \S\ref{sec:validation} unfold reweighted simulated signal
without background, so they do not test background subtraction.

\subsection{Background subtraction and generator-sized truth changes}
\label{sec:val-negweight}

\paragraph{Background subtraction.}

End-to-end five-dimensional pseudo-experiments add background-simulation events
and rebuild per-bin purity weights as in the measurement. At nominal truth they return
% (scalar-5D campaign, development study D1)
the highest-$W$ column of $(\Eavail,W)$ \SIrange{3.5}{4.1}{\percent} low and
the highest-$\Eavail$, highest-$W$ cell \SI{1.7}{\percent} high; without
background, the same experiments close to \SI{0.22}{\percent}. Relative to
these cells' quoted total uncertainty, the bias is 0.36--1.07 standard deviations.
It comes from the purity-based subtraction (\S\ref{app:negweight}), enters no quoted
covariance, and has no established size on data.
% Sources: docs/orchestration/OUTCOME-20260925-s5c-purity-background-bias-at-high-W.md,
% docs/orchestration/state/s5c/d1/d1_summary.json, docs/orchestration/state/s5c/d1/bias_vs_adopted.json.
With a deterministic variant of the five-dimensional LightGBM production
estimator, refined unbinned negative-weight subtraction (\texttt{negweight-refined},
App.~\ref{app:negweight}) brings the highest-$W$ column to
\SIrange{0.11}{0.24}{\percent} and the corner cell to \SI{0.24}{\percent}
over sixty nominal-truth experiments.

% non-negative weights before the first OmniFold step; injection and independent
% subtraction halves of the background simulation) is in the negative-weight appendix (app:negweight).
Some joint cells remain up to \SI{1.0}{\percent} high, up to 14 standard
errors, against \SI{-0.2}{\percent} without background; the mechanism is not
established. On data, the two treatments differ by a median of \SI{0.36}{\percent}
and at most \SI{1.4}{\percent} over the reported functionals. These differences
measure method sensitivity, not bias. Negative-weight subtraction
is not used for the quoted five-dimensional central value.

\subsection{Why the estimator does not follow a generator-sized change}
\label{sec:val-departure}
% An instrumented rerun recorded every OmniFold iteration of these experiments. It
% reproduced the development results bitwise and repeated them through the unmodified

% \SI{73.8}{\percent} against \SI{73.5}{\percent}).

Both OmniFold steps behave as intended: the first matches the reco
pseudo-data within statistical noise, and the second reproduces the truth-level
weight sums to \SI{0.5}{\percent}. Yet the unfolded result folds back onto the data less well than the
true model does. With noise-free pseudo-data it recovers more than \SI{99}{\percent}
of the departure's reco signal, while $(\Eavail,W)$ residuals remain median
\SIrange{5}{11}{\percent} and up to \SI{74}{\percent} in the worst cell.
The remaining reco imprint is small relative to the departure but statistically
significant at the analysis exposure.

Thirty iterations reduce the $\Eavail$ residual by \SI{26}{\percent}; it
continues falling slowly, while the $q_3$ residual grows with iterations.
A larger classifier improves the
reco-level fit without improving the $\Eavail$ truth residual, and removing the regression
for events that fail reconstruction does not help it. No single cause is established, and no estimator change that the
study's rules allow meets the recovery requirement of the GiBUU-to-GENIE departure screen, which was set before the test.

of an exact binned iterative unfolding. It uses the 109 J cells, noise-free
signal-only pseudo-data, and the fixed historical truth deformations D1, W1, W3
and the $q_3$ stress (labels as in \S\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}), pooled

unchanged without it. The binned unfolding unfolds the same simulated

\paragraph{Higher-capacity background refinement and data stability.}

The refinement classifier underfits, which causes the residual background bias
(App.~\ref{app:negweight}). A candidate with raised refinement capacity passes the
nominal background-inclusive closure on 40 fresh pseudo-experiments (largest deviation
2.7 standard errors) and a pooled calibration test of its statistical intervals (pull width 1.02),
which does not establish calibration for every functional.
The candidate fails the predeclared data-stability test: input perturbations within
floating-point rounding move functionals by a median of 0.8 and at most 2.6
statistical standard deviations. The baseline estimator's shifts, 0.8 and 2.5, place this
instability in the estimator family, and it explains the difference on data
between the driver and the development pipeline, at most \SI{1.2}{\percent}.
The data bootstrap, which refits everything per replica, already contains this
spread: each replica carries the same spread (median
\SI{0.23}{\percent} per joint cell), about \SI{37}{\percent} of the bootstrap
variance and below a tenth of any total uncertainty. Central values are
reproducible only to this size.

containment for this candidate only.

\subsection{Model dependence, variability and reporting resolution}
\label{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}

The scalar five-dimensional estimator studied here uses production LightGBM.

These studies measure three different quantities: truth-dependent responses
to iterations and capacity, small repeat variability at one setting, and
appreciable prior sensitivity. Unfolding benchmarks
also assess these quantities separately~\cite{Brenner2020Unfolding}. The studies
do not establish a bias--variance tradeoff across estimator settings, and
regularization bias can invalidate intervals based solely on estimator
variability~\cite{Kuusela2015Unfolding}; neither reference supplies the
variance measurement that is missing for this estimator.

The noise-free study unfolds the full simulation's reweighted reco events without
background. Baseline classifiers and the missed-event regressor use 100 trees
and 8 leaves; the capacity probe uses 400 trees and 31 leaves. Repeated experiments
use estimator $R$ with five iterations, baseline OmniFold estimators and a separate
400-tree, 31-leaf background-refinement classifier. With seed 42, its 40 nominal
experiments and 20 at each of five departures include Poisson pseudo-data, changing
MC splits and background refinement. Because these samples were used during
development, they provide no independent validation here.

Saved fine-grid outputs are integrated into 109 supported joint cells (J), 27
supported coarser joint cells (H2) and 39 supported $(\Eavail,W)$ cells (EW).
Both joint partitions subdivide all five axes; H2 is not nested within J.
Unsupported cells remain in the full-domain rate. The interval assessment
keeps all 42 EW cells, including the three that the support rule excludes
(definitions and normalization checks in App.~\ref{sec:gbdt-diagnostic-definitions}).

\paragraph{Variability and interval behavior at five iterations.}

Figure~\ref{fig:gbdt-bias-spread} compares ensemble residual means and repeat
standard deviations at the same setting. At historical W3 truth, EW29 has
mean residual \SI{23.67}{\percent} and repeat SD \SI{0.56}{\percent}; EW7 has
\SI{-6.81}{\percent} and \SI{0.30}{\percent}. Small spread does not imply
small bias. Coarser cells can have smaller
residuals and spread, but they estimate different integrals; this is not an improvement
in precision on an unchanged quantity. No matching repeat ensembles exist at
more iterations or larger OmniFold capacity, so no tradeoff can be inferred.

\paragraph{Prior sensitivity is not a measured real-data bias.}

Real-data prior shifts correlate with minus the noise-free residual at the same
historical truth (Fig.~\ref{fig:gbdt-prior}). W3's J correlation is 0.92, yet
$|\mathrm{shift}|\geq|\mathrm{residual}|$ holds in only 71\% of supported
cells (52\% on H2, 54\% on EW). Maximum shifts over five historical vertices
have medians 12.0\%, 9.6\% and 13.4\% on J, H2 and EW; excluding D1 gives
11.2\%, 4.3\% and 9.9\%. These envelopes are sensitivities: they provide no
probabilities, covariance components or guaranteed bias bounds, and the truth
of the data has not been shown to lie within their span.

\subsection{The five-axis covariance used in this note and its $(\Eavail,W)$ projection}
\label{sec:eavailw-construction}

\paragraph{Status.}
\label{sec:eavailw-adopted}

The five-axis covariance used in this note is the variant centered on the
central value (\texttt{cv}); its $(\Eavail,W)$ projection has \zprojCells\ cells.
The file and the projection are identified by their SHA-256 checksums
(Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, identities already fixed). The covariance does not
meet the acceptance criteria of its specification. The

was not fixed before this covariance was produced, so the requirement cannot be
met for it, and the file's own metadata records it as not passing and as not to be used.
It is used under an exception that covers only this file and its projection, not any
other covariance built to the same specification, including the two companion
covariances of the measurements below.

\paragraph{Construction.} The covariance projects the corrected five-axis
statistical covariance as $M C_{5D}M^{\top}$, without rebuilding it in the
destination bins, and uses corrected systematic endpoints. Re-selected event loops bound the selection migrations that the single-pass shifted observables omit (\S\ref{sec:uq-higherdim}). On the
$\latBins$ reported cells of $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$, the covariance is

\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:zassembly}
  C_{Z} \;=\; D\,\Big(\textstyle\sum_{V} C_{b}\Big)\,D
  \;+\; \textstyle\sum_{R} C_{b}
  \;+\; \textstyle\sum_{A} L_{b}
  \;+\; C_{\mathrm{stat}}
  \;+\; C_{\mathrm{ML}},
\end{equation}
with the inflation $D=\diag(g)$,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:zinflation}
  g_{i} \;=\;
  \sqrt{\frac{\max\!\big(v^{\mathrm{cv}}_{\mathrm{uni},i},\,
                         v_{\mathrm{blk},i}\big)}
             {v_{\mathrm{blk},i}}}
  \;\ge\; 1,
  \qquad
  g_{i}\equiv 1 \ \text{ where } v_{\mathrm{blk},i}=0 .
\end{equation}

Here $v_{\mathrm{blk}}=\diag\big(\sum_{V}C_{b}\big)$ is the vertical block-sum
diagonal; $v^{\mathrm{cv}}_{\mathrm{uni}}$ is the unified-throw covariance
diagonal \emph{centered on the central value}. Where the denominator is zero,
$g=1$ is set by definition, not computed. The band groups $V$ (vertical), $R$
(residual) and $A$ (re-selected lateral) must be disjoint and exhaust the 45 bands.
The same information can be transferred onto a sweep block,
$\mathbf{C}^{\syst}\!\to\!(\mathbf{C}^{\syst}-\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{vert}})+\mathbf{G}\,\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{vert}}\mathbf{G}$
($\mathbf{G}=\operatorname{diag}g$), only with the low-rank treatment, the mean
shift and the selection-support bound above documented, a condition set in July 2026.
This transfer is positive-semidefinite by construction
and never falls below the block baseline, whereas a direct rank-$N$ swap is not positive-semidefinite.

\paragraph{Centering, explicitly.} Per-band blocks $C_{b}$ use the MAT-conformant
mean-centered $\pm1\sigma$ estimator of \S\ref{app:pair}. Inflation in the CV-centered variant uses $v^{\mathrm{cv}}_{\mathrm{uni}}$. The unified-throw centerings
differ by the outer product of measured joint mean shift $\mathbf{ms}$
(joint throw mean minus central value),

\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:zcentering}
  C^{\mathrm{cv}}_{\mathrm{uni}}
  = C^{\mathrm{mean}}_{\mathrm{uni}} + \mathbf{ms}\,\mathbf{ms}^{\top}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  v^{\mathrm{cv}}_{\mathrm{uni},i}-v^{\mathrm{mean}}_{\mathrm{uni},i}
  = \mathbf{ms}_{i}^{2},
\end{equation}

so CV centering retains the mean shift as variance and mean centering removes it.
The covariance used here is the CV-centered variant, which retains the shift
deliberately; the mean-centered variant (\texttt{mean}) has a $\sqrt{\Tr C}$ lower by
\SI{7.13}{\percent}. A file's variant is identified by its metadata, not by its
path.

\paragraph{The projection to $(\Eavail,W)$.} The quoted 42-cell covariance is
$C_{EW}=M\,C_{Z}\,M^{\top}$, with $M$ of shape $\zprojCells\times\latBins$
over kept axes $(\Eavail,W)$. Each entry of $M$ is the product of the dropped axes' bin widths,
giving a differential density in \si{cm^2/GeV^2/nucleon}. Integrating a
destination cell requires its own $\Delta\Eavail\,\Delta W$. Rows follow the
$7\times6$ $(\Eavail,W)$ grid in measured C order. No source cell is dropped, and
the result is exactly symmetric. Support and
pinned masks and row-index arrays accompany the covariance and are byte-identical
in the two variants. The covariance is paired with the central values of
Fig.~\ref{fig:eavailWexcess} \emph{by checksum identity}: the projected source's
central-value array and the figure's are the same bytes, and their numerical
agreement is only a corroborating check.

\paragraph{Conditioning, and why no rank is quoted.}
Measured extremes $\lambda_{\min}=+4.359104\times10^{-92}$ and
$\lambda_{\max}=1.488215\times10^{-77}$ give
$\lambda_{\min}/\lambda_{\max}=2.9\times10^{-15}$ and condition number
$\approx3.4\times10^{14}$, at double precision's edge. No eigenvalue is negative,
but the smallest lie at the floating-point noise floor of a $\latBins$-term
weighted sum. Their sign carries no information: $n_{\mathrm{negative}}=0$
describes this computation and does not show that the source's negative
directions were cured. Rank is a property of the cutoff, not of the matrix. The 42 eigenvalues decay
smoothly across about fifteen orders of magnitude with no plateau. Retained
counts are 26 at relative cutoff $10^{-6}$, 36 at the code default $10^{-12}$,
41 at \texttt{numpy.linalg.matrix\_rank}'s default $n\epsilon=9.3\times10^{-15}$,
and 42 without a cutoff. No rank is therefore quoted for this covariance; in
particular, 36 is the count at one cutoff, not a number of degrees of freedom.
An inverse requires a declared regularization, and none is applied
implicitly.

\paragraph{What the covariance supports.}

Four measurements limit what the covariance supports. Items 1, 2 and 4 use two
companion covariances built at two estimator-seed settings; only item 3 describes this file.

\begin{enumerate}

\item Estimator-seed sensitivity exceeds its limit. Companion
  covariances at seed offsets $\{0,1200\}$ give the correlation-sensitive measure
  $s_{\mathrm{proj}}=\sprojMeasured\,\%$, against the
  \sprojBound\,\% movement limit set before production, on 2026-09-18, as a
  direct movement tolerance at the scale of the
  ensemble's own precision. A 160-throw ensemble determines a standard deviation
  only to a relative precision of about $1/\sqrt{2(N-1)}\approx\SI{5.6}{\percent}$
  for independent Gaussian throws, so a smaller seed-induced drift could not be
  resolved against the width it would change. The limit is a declared stability
  tolerance, not a derived confidence threshold for a maximum over correlated
  projections. The diagonal-only measures pass
  ($s_{\mathrm{agg}}=\saggMeasured\,\%$, $s_{\mathrm{med}}=\smedMeasured\,\%$);
  the failing one probes the off-diagonal structure used by projections.
