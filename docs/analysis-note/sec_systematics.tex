\section{Uncertainty quantification: constructing the covariance blocks}
\label{sec:systematics}

The uncertainty budget sums four blocks: systematic universes, statistical bootstraps, ML training-seed variation, and a run-period block (\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}). Their independence is an assumption: distinct random streams and physics sources do not by themselves establish it after unfolding. The universe, statistical and training-seed blocks are computed with the LightGBM estimator of the quoted 2D central value (\S\ref{sec:method-config}); the universe and statistical blocks use its training seed (seed~42), and the training-seed block uses five seed settings that include it. A 3D budget built with the analogous 3D grid construction is not quoted, and no 4D uncertainty magnitude is reported (\S\ref{sec:4d}). App.~\ref{app:statmethods} gives the full mathematics and worked examples using the reported numbers (N-D declarations in App.~\ref{app:statmethods-nd}). The resulting budget and its agreement with the published total are in \S\ref{sec:results-uq}; what the covariance includes and omits, and the conventions it follows, are in \S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}.

\subsection{Systematic universes}
\label{sec:uq-universes}
Detector and interaction-model systematics use the July universe sweep: the 44 error bands and 187 universes of the MINERvA Analysis Toolkit (MAT), identical to the standard systematics set of the published analysis. Each universe is re-unfolded with LightGBM at seed~42 and compared with the quoted 2D central value, unfolded with the same estimator and seed. The five lateral bands, which shift reconstructed kinematics rather than event weights, are taken from re-unfolds of re-selected universes (\S\ref{sec:uq-higherdim}) at the same seed. The band sum omits the nonresonant single-pion band Rvp1pi, which encodes the same shift as Rvn1pi (\S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}); it holds 43 bands plus the flat normalization. Per-band covariances follow MAT's universe-mean centering and biased $1/N$ normalization. For a $\pm1\sigma$ pair the covariance is the outer product of the half-difference of the two shifts, excluding any common endpoint displacement from the central value (CV). The 100 PPFX hadron-production flux universes use the same mean-centered $1/N$ formula.

\subsection{Statistical uncertainty: Poisson bootstrap}
\label{sec:uq-boot}
The statistical block jointly bootstraps data and simulation with 300 per-event Poisson(1) weight replicas, each fully re-unfolded with the LightGBM estimator at the central value's training seed (seed~42), and is their sample covariance about the replica mean (Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}). Each replica draws its weights in three independent streams, for data, signal simulation and background simulation; possible shared events between the two simulations were not assessed. Because the background simulation is resampled in every replica, the separable background-only term is contained in the statistical block. The reconstructed-level and truth-level weights of a simulated event share one draw, which preserves within-event migration correlations. Fixing the GBDT seed per replica separates bootstrap and training-seed variances. With 300 replicas the Gaussian formula $1/\sqrt{2(N-1)}$ gives a relative standard error of about \SI{4.1}{\percent} on each per-bin $\sigma$, and the per-bin $\sigma$ from a nested half (150) of the replicas is a median 1.000 times that from all 300. The replica mean differs from the central value by a per-bin median of 4.37 standard errors of the mean ($\sigma/\sqrt{300}$), or 0.25 of the statistical standard deviation. Because the block is the covariance about the replica mean, it does not include this offset. The bootstrap block is smaller than the published statistical block (ratio of square-root traces 0.407). This \emph{observed} covariance difference does not demonstrate a causal efficiency advantage (App.~\ref{sec:rank}).

\subsection{ML/optimization uncertainty: training-seed variation}
\label{sec:uq-ml}
The training-seed block is the sample covariance, over five training-seed settings, of the mean of 20 bootstrap replicas at each setting. Each setting is a triplet $(s,s{+}1,s{+}2)$ of seeds for the step-1 classifier, step-2 classifier and miss regressor, with $s\in\{42,45,48,51,54\}$; the block has rank~4 (\S\ref{sec:seedscan}). \subsection{Combination and the unified-throw test}
\label{sec:uq-combine}
The nominal covariance is the block sum $C = C_{\mathrm{syst}} + C_{\mathrm{stat}} + C_{\mathrm{ML}} + C_{\mathrm{PL}}$, which assumes the blocks are independent and the unfolding responds linearly to simultaneous perturbations, so that per-band covariances add. The \emph{unified throw} tests linearity directly (App.~\ref{sec:universes}; \S\ref{sec:eavailw-construction}) by perturbing all reweightable interaction-model bands and the flux universes simultaneously in each event and re-unfolding jointly. The procedure interpolates asymmetrically between actual $-1\sigma$ and $+1\sigma$ event-weight endpoints, fixes the estimator seed for every throw, and centers the joint covariance on the throw mean. That mean's displacement from CV is stored separately as a per-bin vector and its norm; per-band endpoint displacements are not stored. The matched block-sum comparator re-unfolds both endpoints of each band using MAT's universe-mean centering and biased $1/N$ normalization. A single-throw superposition probe is insufficient to test nonlinear response. No scalar run-to-run jitter floor is subtracted. The configuration-(a) 5D result uses the strict joint ensemble and matched-endpoint comparator.

In the five-axis covariance of the configuration-(a) central value, $C_Z$, the per-source decomposition is retained for attribution and the joint throws enter through the inflation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:zinflation} (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-construction}).

The run-period block $C_{\mathrm{PL}}$ is an uncertainty assigned for an observed inconsistency between run periods whose cause is unknown; it is not a measured systematic uncertainty from an identified source. Data taken in different run periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties. After unfolding, the double ratio (data over simulated truth) of the data-taking periods (playlists) 1G--1M to the earliest periods, 1A--1F, has a $\pPar$ step of $(+6.89\pm0.46)\%$ at \SI{8}{GeV/c}. Diluted into the combined result, the raw shift of those playlists is small in each bin: every marginal $z$ is below 0.7. Its $\pPar$ $\chi^{2}$ against the earlier 2D budget is nevertheless 34.4, where noise would give 2.0. These data cannot establish which period, if any, is correct.
% Note register round n3, batch n3-B (2026-10-27): the unknown cause is stated once, in this paragraph's first sentence (R-N3b).

The block is built from separate seed-42 unfolds of three run periods, the playlist groups 1A--1F, 1G--1M and 1N--1P, each unfolded through its own simulated detector response. The block uses each period's unfolded result over that period's own simulated truth. Each period is compared with the exposure-weighted mean of the three, so no reference period is needed; the spread, applied to the central value, gives the block. It is added after the other blocks. It has rank two, and with three period groups its own size is poorly determined. Its per-bin median fractional standard deviation is \SI{2.09}{\percent} (84th percentile \SI{3.34}{\percent}, maximum \SI{6.48}{\percent}); it is \SIrange{1.17}{3.36}{\percent} on the $\pPar$ projections and \SI{1.97}{\percent} on the integrated cross section. Added to the other blocks, it raises the per-bin $\sigma$ by a median of \SI{5.8}{\percent} (at most \SI{39.1}{\percent}), the integral by \SI{9.9}{\percent} and the $\pPar$ projections by at most \SI{21}{\percent}; it carries a median \SI{10.6}{\percent} of the per-bin variance and \SI{17.1}{\percent} of the integral's. Taking 1A--1F as the reference would give a smaller block (per-bin median \SI{1.38}{\percent}, integral \SI{1.09}{\percent}).

The block's interpretation is limited by the small number of period groups, by possible shrinkage from the regularization, and by the statistical and training noise it retains. Because the block is built from the observed differences among the periods, it contains them by construction; this carries no information about its coverage, that is, about whether its size is right. For the same reason, the double-ratio $\chi^{2}$ against any total that contains the block is bounded whatever the data, so it does not test the block. The raw 1G--1M $\pPar$ $\chi^{2}$ is 34.8 against the other blocks alone and 2.59 with the block added; these values indicate sensitivity only.
