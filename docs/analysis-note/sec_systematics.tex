\section{Uncertainty quantification: constructing the covariance blocks}
\label{sec:systematics}

The uncertainty budget sums four blocks: systematic universes, statistical
bootstraps, ML training-seed variation, and a run-period block
(\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}). Independence is an assumption;
distinct RNGs and physics sources alone do not establish it after unfolding.
The universe, statistical and training-seed blocks are computed with the
LightGBM estimator of the quoted 2D central value (\S\ref{sec:method-config});
the universe and statistical blocks use its training seed (seed~42), and the
training-seed block five seed settings that include it.

\subsection{Statistical uncertainty: Poisson bootstrap}
\label{sec:uq-boot}
The statistical block jointly bootstraps data and simulation with 300 per-event
Poisson(1) weight replicas, each fully re-unfolded with the LightGBM estimator at the
central value's training seed (seed~42), using their sample covariance about the
replica mean (Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}). The central value and its statistical block
are therefore matched in estimator and seed. Each replica draws its weights in three
independent streams: data, signal simulation and background simulation. Statistical
fluctuations of the background simulation are drawn independently of those of the
signal simulation; possible shared events between the two were not assessed. A
separable background-only term (a median 0.26 of the statistical $\sigma$) is
contained in the statistical block and reported separately. MC reco and
truth weights share a \emph{single} event
draw to preserve within-event migration correlations. Pinning the GBDT seed per replica
separates bootstrap and training-seed variances. With 300 replicas the Gaussian formula
$1/\sqrt{2(N-1)}$ gives a relative standard error of about \SI{4.1}{\percent} on each per-bin
$\sigma$, and the per-bin $\sigma$ from a nested half (150) of the replicas is a median 1.001
times that from all 300. The replica mean
differs from the central value by a per-bin median of 4.43 standard errors of the
mean ($\sigma/\sqrt{300}$), that is 0.26 of the statistical standard deviation; the
block is the covariance about the replica mean, so this offset is not included in it. The bootstrap block is
genuinely smaller than the published statistical block (square-root-trace ratio
0.428). This \emph{observed} covariance difference does not
demonstrate a causal efficiency advantage (App.~\ref{sec:rank}); the split data/MC
streams of the earlier, rebuilt bootstrap are in
Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, superseded values: retired material, and \texttt{coverage-fixed-truth}. The fixed-truth coverage test (\S\ref{sec:coverage}) found
that the statistical band quoted when it ran, before that rebuild, undercovered in its
tails, unevenly across bins.

\subsection{ML/optimization uncertainty: training-seed variation}
\label{sec:uq-ml}
The ML block is the sample covariance, over five training-seed settings, of the mean
of 20 bootstrap replicas at each setting; each setting is a triplet $(s,s{+}1,s{+}2)$ of
seeds for the step-1 classifier, step-2 classifier and miss regressor, with
$s\in\{42,45,48,51,54\}$, and the block has rank~4 (\S\ref{sec:seedscan}). It is
the smallest block, with median \SI{0.057}{\percent} per bin.
The block measures LightGBM seed variation, with the bootstrap\,$\times$\,seed
interaction at its $1/20$ share. With four degrees of freedom, its variance has a
Gaussian relative standard error of about \SI{71}{\percent}. It is 0.30 of the earlier
ten-seed block (median ratio of the per-bin $\sigma$): the bootstrap\,$\times$\,seed
interaction, which dominated the earlier scan, is already inside the fixed-seed
statistical block, and this block carries it only at its $1/20$ share, a median 0.685
of its diagonal. The interaction itself (a median 0.076 of the statistical variance)
and the universe\,$\times$\,retraining term (\S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}) are reported and not
added. The dependence on the learner, between the LightGBM central value and the
exact-split cross-check (a per-bin median of \SI{0.98}{\percent}, maximum
\SI{12.2}{\percent}; \S\ref{sec:method-config}), is in no block. The mean of the earlier ten-seed scan
(Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, superseded values: retired material) differs from the exact-split cross-check by a median of
\SI{0.99}{\percent} per bin, a backend difference the block does not cover
(\S\ref{sec:method-config}).

earlier 2D budget was built~\cite{TwoDFollowup2026}. The May systematic

\subsection{Combination and the unified-throw test}
\label{sec:uq-combine}
The nominal covariance is the block sum
$C = C_{\mathrm{syst}} + C_{\mathrm{stat}} + C_{\mathrm{ML}} + C_{\mathrm{PL}}$, which assumes
the blocks are independent \emph{and} the unfolding responds linearly to
simultaneous perturbations, allowing per-band covariances to add. The
\emph{unified throw} directly tests linearity (App.~\ref{sec:universes};
\S\ref{sec:eavailw-construction}) by jointly re-unfolding all reweightable
knob bands and the flux multisim, perturbed simultaneously per event.
The corrected procedure interpolates asymmetrically between actual
$-1\sigma$ and $+1\sigma$ event-weight endpoints, fixes the estimator seed
for every throw, and centers the joint covariance on the throw mean.
That mean's displacement from CV is stored separately as a per-bin vector
and its norm; per-band endpoint displacements are not stored. The matched
block comparator re-unfolds both endpoints using MAT's universe-mean
centering and biased $1/N$ normalization. A single-throw superposition probe
is insufficient to test nonlinear response. No scalar run-to-run jitter floor is
subtracted. The 5D result uses the strict joint ensemble and matched-endpoint
comparator.
% Implementation: nd-unfolding/unified_throw_cov.py

The run-period block $C_{\mathrm{PL}}$ is an uncertainty assigned for an observed
inconsistency between run periods whose cause is unknown; it is not a measured
systematic uncertainty from an identified source.
Subsets of the data taken in different run periods are not mutually consistent within
the other uncertainties: after unfolding, the double ratio (data over simulated truth) of
playlists 1G--1M to the earliest periods, 1A--1F, has a $\pPar$ step of
$(+6.94\pm0.41)\%$ at \SI{8}{GeV/c}. Diluted into the combined result, the raw shift of
those playlists is small bin by bin, with every marginal $z$ below 0.7, but its $\pPar$
$\chi^{2}$ against the earlier budget is 33.8, where noise would give 1.8. The cause is not known, and these data cannot establish which
period, if any, is correct. The block is built from separate seed-42 unfolds of
three run periods (groups of playlists; 1N--1P is treated in the same way): each
period's unfolded-to-simulated ratio is compared with the exposure-weighted mean of the
three, without choosing any period as the reference, and the spread, applied to the
central value, gives the block. It is added after the other blocks. It has rank at most
two and about 1.8 effective degrees of freedom: with few period groups, its own size is
poorly determined, at the order of \SI{100}{\percent}. Per bin its median is
\SI{2.14}{\percent} (84th percentile \SI{3.31}{\percent}, maximum \SI{5.69}{\percent}), and
it is \SIrange{1.24}{3.36}{\percent} on the $\pPar$ projections and \SI{1.97}{\percent} on the
integrated cross section. Added to the other blocks, it raises the per-bin $\sigma$ by a
median of \SI{5.4}{\percent} (at most \SI{34.5}{\percent}), the integral by \SI{9.9}{\percent}
and the $\pPar$ projections by at most \SI{21}{\percent} (at 6--\SI{7}{GeV/c}); it carries a
median \SI{9.9}{\percent} of the per-bin variance and \SI{17.1}{\percent} of the integral's.
Taking 1A--1F as the reference would give a smaller block (median \SI{1.45}{\percent}, integral
\SI{1.09}{\percent}). The block uses each period's unfolded result over that period's own
simulated truth, with each period unfolded through its own simulated detector response.

Each period is compared with the exposure-weighted mean of the three, so the block needs
no reference period. It does not identify which period is correct. Its interpretation is limited by the small number of
period groups, by possible shrinkage from the regularization, and by the statistical and
training noise it retains. Because the block is built from the observed differences among
the periods, it contains them automatically; this says nothing about its coverage, that
is, about whether its size is right. For the same reason, the block's own double-ratio
$\chi^{2}$ against any total that contains it is bounded whatever the data, so it is not a
test. The raw 1G--1M $\pPar$ $\chi^{2}$, 34.8 against the other blocks alone and 1.97 with
the block added, is a sensitivity reading only.

\subsection{Scope, conventions and limitations of the 2D covariance}
\label{sec:uq-2d-scope}

\paragraph{Status of the 2D result.}
We report a reproducible two-dimensional central value, with a covariance computed
with the central value's own estimator, under the conventions stated below. The result's intervals, bias and covariance have
not been independently validated. In particular, the coverage of its intervals has not
been validated on independent simulated samples, nor simultaneously over all reported
bins and projections.
% plain-language pass, review fix m1 (DECISION-20261018-joseph-plain-language-pass)
The covariance also gives no calibrated
significance for comparisons with generator predictions. Such validation would need
an independent simulation sample of production size, a validated generator of
pseudo-data with continuously varied nuisance parameters, and samples from independent
event generators beyond the truth-level ones already examined; none is available. We
therefore make no claim about coverage, and goodness-of-fit values computed with this
covariance are indicative only. The following are not included in the covariance:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item the approximation in how the systematic-universe unfolds weight events that
        fail reconstruction (below); beyond the weak test described below, its effect
        has not been measured;
  \item the dependence on the machine-learning estimator: a per-bin median of
        \SI{0.98}{\percent} (maximum \SI{12.2}{\percent}) between the LightGBM
        central value and the exact-split cross-check (\S\ref{sec:uq-ml});
  \item the dependence on the unfolding model and its regularization, whose
        estimate requires independent-generator samples, and departures of the
        detector response from its parametrized family, which we assume absent;
        we assign no uncertainty for either.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Conventions.}
Each systematic band's covariance is computed about the mean of its universes
(constraint-weighted for the flux), with $1/N$ normalization, not about the
central value. This follows the MAT treatment of universes (\S\ref{sec:uq-universes}),
which we use as a stated convention. The band centres lie a median \SIrange{0.12}{0.50}{\percent} of the
central value from it, or 0.08--2.26 of the band's $\sigma$. The statistical
block is the covariance about the replica mean (\S\ref{sec:uq-boot}). A
\SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization uncertainty is included as a rank-one block.

\paragraph{Approximations and omitted terms.}

\paragraph{Seed-related terms reported, not added.}
Two seed-related terms are reported and not added to the covariance. The
bootstrap\,$\times$\,seed interaction is a median 0.076 of the statistical
variance; the training-seed block includes it at its $1/20$ share. For the
universe\,$\times$\,retraining term, in the six non-null universes tested (of
187), the seed standard deviation of each universe's shift is a median
1.24--1.33 times the $\sigma$ of the earlier ten-seed block, with four degrees of
freedom each. In those universes the universe block, built at one training seed,
therefore carries single-seed training noise of about the earlier seed block's
size; it is part of the block as built and is not separated from the physical
variation. In two of them the noise is comparable to or larger than the shift:
in \texttt{2p2h:0} the shift is \SI{0.114}{\percent} against a seed standard
deviation of \SI{0.235}{\percent}, and in \texttt{MaCCQE:0} \SI{0.266}{\percent}
against \SI{0.220}{\percent} (per-bin medians). In expectation such noise adds
variance to a band rather than removing it (inferred), and these two bands are
largely training noise (inferred from the medians). The
universe\,$\times$\,retraining values and the band-centring numbers were not
recomputed independently: they cannot be recomputed from the saved covariance
products, and the verification compared only the checksums of their inputs.

\subsection{What changes for the higher-dimensional products}
\label{sec:uq-higherdim}
Two event-loop modes supply the lateral bands. The single-pass event loop, which writes every universe in one pass, stores each lateral universe's shifted observables only for events that pass the selection at the central value, so it omits events that the shift moves into the selection. Re-selected event loops re-run the selection with one lateral universe active at a time. For the five kinematic bands (BeamAngleX/Y, MuonResolution, and Muon\_Energy\_MINERvA/MINOS), production treats selection migration with the re-selected event loops. They supply the lateral-band block of the five-axis covariance used in this note. Higher-dimensional products built from the single-pass shifted observables are not used for any quoted uncertainty. Replacing the single-pass lateral-band block by the re-selected one moves that block's square-root trace by
\SI{\latTraceMove}{\percent}. That is a change in a square-root trace and is
\emph{not} a bound on any individual bin: across the \latBins\ reported bins
the per-bin ratio of re-selected to single-pass standard deviation runs \latRatioMin\ to
\latRatioMax, and within the top \SI{1}{\percent} of bins by single-pass variance
--- which carry \SI{\latTopVarShare}{\percent} of the total --- it runs
\latRatioTopMin\ to \latRatioTopMax. A small share of the aggregate variance
does not make a bin irrelevant to a quoted result.

\paragraph{Scope of the re-selected replacement.} The replacement covers
the five detector bands using shifted reconstructed kinematics. MINOS
efficiency and the three GEANT
hadron-interaction bands (GEANT\_Neutron, GEANT\_Pion, GEANT\_Proton) retain
their weight-based treatment and are thereby excluded from the kinematic
replacement. This classification does not independently validate
hadronic-response model completeness for the $\Eavail$ and $W$ measurement.

The corrected 5D ML ensemble fixes the estimator seed at 42 and varies the
train/test split seed. It therefore measures split response and does not measure full
estimator stochasticity. A dedicated scan of twelve estimator seeds at fixed
data and split closes this scope. Its covariance has
$\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}\,C}=\SI{\gbdtAiEstTrace}{cm^2/nucleon}$,
\SI{\gbdtAiEstFrac}{\percent} of the candidate split-varied band
(\SI{\gbdtMlSplitTrace}{cm^2/nucleon}). Genuine estimator stochasticity therefore
dominates the band used in this note over effects of the particular train/test partition.
The scan remains an auxiliary robustness check; it is not an independent
matrix, since split and estimator responses are not assumed uncorrelated. Both
bands are minor components, about three percent of the combined budget in
$\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}\,C}$.

An earlier 5D covariance construction used both mean-centered and CV-centered unified throws (\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}); no magnitude from either is quoted, and
neither is used. The covariance used in this note is a later, separate build in which the unified-throw covariance is centered on the central value (variant \texttt{cv})
(\S\ref{sec:eavailw-construction}; Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, superseded values: the superseded 5D candidate constructions).
