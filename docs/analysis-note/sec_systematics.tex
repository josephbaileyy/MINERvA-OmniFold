\section{Uncertainty of the two-dimensional cross section}
\label{sec:systematics}

The uncertainty budget sums four blocks: systematic universes, statistical bootstraps, ML training-seed variation, and a run-period block (\S\ref{sec:uq-combine}). Their independence is an assumption: distinct random streams and physics sources do not by themselves establish it after unfolding. The universe, statistical and training-seed blocks are computed with the LightGBM estimator of the quoted 2D central value (\S\ref{sec:method-config}); the universe and statistical blocks use its training seed (seed~42), and the training-seed block uses five seed settings that include it. A 3D budget built with the analogous 3D grid construction is not quoted, and no 4D uncertainty magnitude is reported (\S\ref{sec:4d}). App.~\ref{app:statmethods} gives the full mathematics and worked examples using the reported numbers (N-D declarations in App.~\ref{app:statmethods-nd}). The resulting budget and its agreement with the published total are in \S\ref{sec:results-uq}; the conventions the covariance follows and the model-dependence component reported beside it are in \S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}, and what it omits and its validation status are in \S\ref{sec:limitations-2d}.

\subsection{Systematic universes}
\label{sec:uq-universes}
Detector and model systematics use the July universe sweep: the 44 error bands and 187 universes of the MINERvA Analysis Toolkit (MAT), identical to the standard systematics set of the published analysis. Each universe is re-unfolded with LightGBM at seed~42 and compared with the quoted 2D central value, unfolded with the same estimator and seed. The five lateral bands, which shift reconstructed kinematics rather than event weights, are taken from re-unfolds of re-selected universes (\S\ref{sec:uq-higherdim}) at the same seed. The band sum omits the nonresonant-pion band Rvp1pi, which encodes the same shift as Rvn1pi (\S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}); it holds 43 bands plus the flat normalization. Per-band covariances follow MAT's universe-mean centering and biased $1/N$ normalization. For a $\pm1\sigma$ pair the covariance is the outer product of the half-difference of the two shifts, excluding any common endpoint displacement from the central value (CV). The 100 PPFX hadron-production flux universes use the same mean-centered $1/N$ formula.

\subsection{Statistical uncertainty: Poisson bootstrap}
\label{sec:uq-boot}
The statistical block jointly bootstraps data and simulation with 300 per-event Poisson(1) weight replicas, each fully re-unfolded with the LightGBM estimator at the central value's training seed (seed~42), and is their sample covariance about the replica mean (Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}). Each replica draws its weights in three independent streams, for data, signal simulation and background simulation; possible shared events between the two simulations were not assessed. Because the background simulation is resampled in every replica, the separable background-only term is contained in the statistical block. The reconstructed-level and truth-level weights of a simulated event share one draw, which preserves within-event migration correlations. Fixing the GBDT seed per replica separates bootstrap and training-seed variances. With 300 replicas the Gaussian formula $1/\sqrt{2(N-1)}$ gives a relative standard error of about \SI{4.1}{\percent} on each per-bin $\sigma$, and the per-bin $\sigma$ from a nested half (150) of the replicas is a median 1.000 times that from all 300. The replica mean differs from the central value by a per-bin median of 4.37 standard errors of the mean ($\sigma/\sqrt{300}$), or 0.25 of the statistical standard deviation. Because the block is the covariance about the replica mean, it does not include this offset. The bootstrap block is smaller than the published statistical block (ratio of square-root traces 0.407; App.~\ref{sec:rank}).

\subsection{ML/optimization uncertainty: training-seed variation}
\label{sec:uq-ml}
The training-seed block is the sample covariance, over five training-seed settings, of the mean of 20 bootstrap replicas at each setting. The quoted central value is one unfold at one seed. Its statistical block is the bootstrap at that seed and already contains the training noise at that seed, so the added component is this covariance of the per-seed means. A spread of single unfolds on fixed data is a different statistic. Each setting is a triplet $(s,s{+}1,s{+}2)$ of seeds for the step-1 classifier, step-2 classifier and miss regressor, with $s\in\{42,45,48,51,54\}$; the block has rank~4 (\S\ref{sec:seedscan}). It is the smallest block, with a per-bin median fractional standard deviation of \SI{0.048}{\percent}. With four degrees of freedom, its variance has a Gaussian relative standard error of about \SI{71}{\percent}. Its per-bin $\sigma$ is a median 0.27 of that of the earlier ten-seed block. The reason is that the bootstrap\,$\times$\,seed interaction, which dominated the earlier scan, is already inside the fixed-seed statistical block. The training-seed block carries it only at its $1/20$ share, a median 0.763 of its diagonal. The interaction itself and the universe\,$\times$\,retraining term are reported and not added (\S\ref{sec:limitations-2d}).

\subsection{Combination and the unified-throw test}
\label{sec:uq-combine}
The nominal covariance is the block sum $C = C_{\mathrm{syst}} + C_{\mathrm{stat}} + C_{\mathrm{ML}} + C_{\mathrm{PL}}$. This sum assumes independent blocks and a linear response to simultaneous perturbations. Under those assumptions, the per-band covariances add. The \emph{unified throw} tests linearity directly (App.~\ref{sec:universes}; \S\ref{sec:eavailw-construction}) by perturbing all reweightable bands and the flux universes simultaneously in each event and re-unfolding jointly. The procedure interpolates asymmetrically between actual $-1\sigma$ and $+1\sigma$ event-weight endpoints, fixes the estimator seed for every throw, and centers the joint covariance on the throw mean. That mean's displacement from CV is stored separately as a per-bin vector and its norm. Per-band endpoint displacements are not stored. The matched block-sum comparator re-unfolds both endpoints of each band using MAT's universe-mean centering and biased $1/N$ normalization. A single-throw superposition probe is insufficient to test nonlinear response. No scalar run-to-run jitter floor is subtracted. The configuration-(a) 5D result (Table~\ref{tab:config-key}) uses the strict joint ensemble and matched-endpoint comparator. No two-dimensional comparison of a joint throw with the block sum is reported, and the stored mean-shift norm is not quoted.

The run-period block $C_{\mathrm{PL}}$ is an uncertainty assigned for an observed inconsistency between run periods whose cause is unknown. The block does not measure a systematic uncertainty from an identified source. Data taken in different run periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties. After unfolding, the double ratio (data over simulated truth) of the data-taking periods (playlists) 1G--1M to the earliest periods, 1A--1F, has a $\pPar$ step of $(+6.89\pm0.46)\%$ at \SI{8}{GeV/c}. The step is already present in the reconstructed data, at $+\fvval{rp-pb-reco}\,\%$ relative to 1A--1F (data minus background over each period's signal simulation, scaled to its exposure; Fig.~\ref{fig:runperiod-steps}(a)). It therefore survives unfolding at about its reconstruction-level size. The 1N--1P group shows a smaller step, $+\fvval{rp-pc-reco}\,\%$ reconstructed and $+(\fvval{rp-pc-unf}\pm\fvval{rp-pc-unf-se})\,\%$ unfolded, and lies above 1A--1F in every $\pPar$ bin below \SI{8}{GeV/c}, in central value, by \SIrange{\fvval{rp-pc-lev-reco-lo}}{\fvval{rp-pc-lev-reco-hi}}{\percent} at reconstruction level and \SIrange{\fvval{rp-pc-lev-unf-lo}}{\fvval{rp-pc-lev-unf-hi}}{\percent} after unfolding, an excess of unknown cause. Diluted into the combined result, the raw shift of those playlists is small in each bin: every marginal $z$ is below 0.7. The block is built from separate seed-42 unfolds of three run periods, the playlist groups 1A--1F, 1G--1M and 1N--1P, each unfolded through its own simulated detector response. The block uses each period's unfolded result over that period's own simulated truth. Each period is compared with the exposure-weighted mean of the three. This avoids choosing a reference period. \paragraph{Generator-shape model dependence.} The dependence of the result on the generator shape of the prior is reported beside the covariance, as a separate covariance $C_{\mathrm{model}}=\sum_k s_k s_k^{\top}$. For each of three generators $k$ (GENIE with meson-exchange currents, NuWro and GiBUU), the prior's truth distribution is reweighted by the ratio of that generator's $(\pT,\pPar)$ shape to GENIE's, with its normalization kept. The ratio is constant on each reported $(\pT,\pPar)$ cell. A relative-error gate, fixed before the reweighted unfolds were run, decides each cell's ratio. A cell whose own ratio passes the gate keeps it. Otherwise the cell takes the ratio pooled over a coarser cell, fixed before the measurement, when that pooled error passes the same gate. When neither passes, the cell leaves the prior unchanged. A row outside the grid, an unreported cell, and GiBUU where that sample is not defined also leave the prior unchanged. The data are unfolded again with the central value's estimator; $s_k$ is the per-cell relative change of the result. That sum is a fractional covariance. In cross-section units it is $\mathrm{diag}(x)\,C_{\mathrm{model}}\,\mathrm{diag}(x)$, with $x$ the cell-integrated cross sections of this result.

Each generator enters as one fully correlated component, unscaled, so $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ has rank three. Its median fractional standard deviation over the 205 reported cells is \SI{\cmodelMedian}{\percent} (84th percentile \SI{7.10}{\percent}, maximum \SI{21.10}{\percent}). For the sum of the combined covariance and $C_{\mathrm{model}}$, the total for users who include this dependence, the per-cell median is \SI{\qrCmodelMedian}{\percent} (84th percentile \SI{12.26}{\percent}, maximum \SI{24.60}{\percent}). On the integrated cross section $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ gives \SI{\cmodelIntegral}{\percent}, and the total stays at \SI{\uqIntegral}{\percent}: the reweightings change only the prior's shape, so $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ does not test the model dependence of the normalization.
