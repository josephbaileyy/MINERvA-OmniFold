## The species

> **Verification effort is allocated by suspicion, and suspicion tracks DISAGREEMENT rather than FRAGILITY.**
> Every instance caught today was a claim someone was sceptical of — a cost that looked too cheap, an absence
> too clean, a seed map too convenient. **The instances missed arrived as SUPPORT.**

**And a supporting citation is more dangerous than a contested number**, because nobody downstream has a
reason to check it: it is doing rhetorical work, so it reads as settled. A contested number attracts the
scrutiny that would kill it. A supporting one is load-bearing precisely where nobody is standing.

That is now **measured rather than asserted** — the party who named the species committed it in the next turn.
Which means the remedy cannot be awareness, and has to be procedural:

> **When you advocate a position, state its cost the way an opponent would** — enumerate what it makes harder,
> slower, or more serial, *before sending.*

## Why this stands alone rather than merging into `BEN-392`

`BEN-392` says the qualifier gets dropped in transport. **This says WHICH claims get their qualifier dropped:
the ones nobody is motivated to check, which are disproportionately the supporting ones.** So it explains the
*selection*, not the mechanism.

That distinction is not academic, because **the remedies are different kinds of thing.** `BEN-392`'s remedy is
a check on a claim — *re-derive the number from its stated operands.* This row's remedy is an **allocation
rule** — it tells you *which claims to spend the check on*, and its answer is counter-intuitive: the ones you
already believe. A row carrying both would carry one headline naming neither. Same reasoning as the
three-bucket split in `BEN-391`: **different reflex, different row.**

## The corrective is mechanical, not attitudinal

> **Resolve every citation you repeat — including the ones that agree with you, and especially the ones that
> arrive as support.**

Attitudinal correctives ("be more careful with citations") have failed all day; mechanical ones have worked.
And the cost is already known: **resolving it was one command — the same command run a dozen times that day on
other people's numbers.** That is the whole finding in one clause. The capability was present, in use, and
aimed outward.
