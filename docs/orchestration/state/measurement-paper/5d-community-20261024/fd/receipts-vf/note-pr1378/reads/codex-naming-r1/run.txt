   237	The sixteen-cell estimator recovers only part of the shift from the prior toward truth at the two truths flagged under the baseline covariance. The signed fraction $\rho$ of the shift still missing along the prior-to-truth direction is
   238	\begin{equation}
   239	  \rho=-\frac{\sum_i b_i d_i}{\sum_i d_i^2},
   240	  \qquad |d_i|\geq 0.01 ,
   241	  \label{eq:five-dimensional-retained-prior}
   242	\end{equation}
   243	where $d_i=(X_i-P_i)/P_i$ and $b_i=(\widehat X_i-X_i)/P_i$ for prior, true, and unfolded integrals $P_i$, $X_i$, and $\widehat X_i$. This gives $\rho=0$ for exact recovery, $\rho=1$ for staying at the prior, and $\rho<0$ for overshooting along that direction. On noise-free samples with background, $\rho=\fvval{prr-rho-e-gibuu}$ at the GiBUU hybrid and $\fvval{prr-rho-e-q3}$ at the $q_3$ tilt. Reducing classifier capacity or using only muon momenta increases retention at both truths. Dropping $W$ alone or dropping both $W$ and $q_3$ changes $\rho$ by less than \fvval{prr-s53-w-dmax}. A second prespecified study on the same noise-free samples found that the retention at the GiBUU hybrid is consistent with a limit set by the weights of missed events. At the $q_3$ tilt, it is consistent with a limit set by the representation in the second, truth-level step. Both results match the pseudo-data at reconstruction level despite the residual at truth level, consistent with directions that the reconstructed inputs constrain weakly. The substitution tests below use true weights available only in simulation.
