### 3. Recommended Paper Text

#### (i) Pass at both tunes
> "Coverage of the reported covariance was evaluated using 300 pseudo-experiments generated under each of two alternative generator tunes (GENIE G18 and AR23). Systematic variations were propagated linearly, consistent with the construction of $C_{\mathrm{total}}$. Unfolding each pseudo-experiment with the nominal framework yielded central intervals with empirical coverage consistent with nominal 68% containment across all kinematic cells, with joint $\chi^2$ distributions matching the expected null."

#### (ii) Failure at one tune
> "Coverage tests with 300 pseudo-experiments under the AR23 tune confirm nominal 68% per-cell containment across the kinematic phase space. Under G18, empirical coverage drops to $52\%$ in forward-angle cells (joint $\chi^2/\mathrm{d.o.f.} = 29/16$), driven by differences in the shared multinucleon model. The reported covariance covers the nominal modeling space and AR23, but under-covers in phase space regions sensitive to alternate $2\mathrm{p}2\mathrm{h}$ dynamics."

---

### 4. First-Order Composition: Limitation or Standard Practice?
**Standard practice.** Summing sub-matrices ($C_{\mathrm{total}} = \sum_k C_k$) from independent variation groups inherently relies on a first-order linear Taylor expansion of the uncertainties. 

No referee expects second-order cross-systematic response tensors in a multi-universe neutrino cross-section paper. It is not an unvalidated defect requiring an apology; describing it neutrally as *"propagated linearly, consistent with the covariance construction"* is fully sufficient.
