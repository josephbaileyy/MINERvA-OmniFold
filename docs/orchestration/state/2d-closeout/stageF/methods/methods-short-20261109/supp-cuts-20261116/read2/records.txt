### B. The paper's iteration paragraph (paper_body.tex:119, section sec:unfolding)
Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The pairing of reconstructed and true events transfers that weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Because events that fail reconstruction enter as truth events without a reconstructed partner, no separate efficiency correction is applied. Their step-1 weight comes from a regressor of that weight on the truth coordinates, fitted to the events that have a partner. Each simulated event contributes equally to the regressor fit, which therefore estimates the mean weight at fixed truth without the events' simulation weights. Events without a reconstructed partner carry \SIrange{\fvval{ess-nrp-min}}{\fvval{ess-nrp-max}}{\percent} of a cell's final weight (\ssecc{S4}{supp:estimators}).

\section{The unbinned OmniFold procedure}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Algorithm}
\label{sec:method-algo}

OmniFold~\cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk} unfolds by iterated reweighting with machine-learned likelihood ratios, operating on per-event observables rather than on a fixed histogram binning. Each iteration has two steps:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Step 1 (detector space).} A classifier separates measured data from reconstructed simulation weighted by the current push-weights. Its calibrated output $p$ gives the likelihood-ratio reweight $w = p/(1-p)$ for each reconstructed simulated event. Each simulated event is a (truth, reco) pair, allowing the weight to be \emph{pulled back} directly to its truth record. Truth events without a reconstructed partner (``misses'') receive step-1 weights from a regressor trained on the reconstructed subsample, $w(\mathrm{truth\ features})$. This native OmniFold acceptance treatment requires no separate efficiency correction.
  \item \textbf{Step 2 (truth space).} A second classifier separates the original truth-level simulation from the same sample carrying the pulled-back step-1 weights. Its smooth truth-space reweight becomes the next iteration's push-weight.
\end{enumerate}
After $n$ iterations, the push-weights define an efficiency-corrected truth-level reweighted simulation ensemble. Histogramming this ensemble in any binning chosen afterwards gives the unfolded spectrum. The iteration count and learner capacity regularize the unfolding.

### I. The Supplement's S4.1 step list (unchanged by the cuts), which defines the push weights, pull weights and the cross-section histogramming
\paragraph{The reported estimator, step by step.} The five-observable estimator runs as follows. Table~\ref{tab:codemap} gives the code that implements each step.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Refinement of the measured side.} The signed sample is every measured event in the analysis window at weight $+1$ and every background-simulation event in the window at $-w^{\mathrm{bkg}}$, its exposure-scaled weight. A LightGBM classifier $g$ (400 trees, 31 leaves, deterministic, seed plus three) separates the positive- from the negative-weight rows, each weighted by $|w|$, and every row receives $\nu=|w|\max(0,\,2g-1)$, with $g$ restricted to $[10^{-6},1-10^{-6}]$.
  \item \emph{Iterations} (five). The push weights start at $\omega_i=1$ on the truth-selected simulated events. In each iteration:
  \begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0pt, topsep=1pt]
    \item Step~1: a LightGBM classifier (100 trees, 8 leaves, learning rate 0.1, deterministic) separates the reconstructed simulated events that have a reconstructed partner, weighted $\omega_i w^{\mathrm{reco}}_i$, from the refined measured rows, weighted $\nu$, and each such event receives $u_i=p/(1-p)$ at its reconstructed values, with $p$ restricted as above.
    \item Events without a reconstructed partner receive $u_i$ from a LightGBM regressor of the same capacity, fitted with equal event weights to $u$ on the truth coordinates of the events that have one. The regressed $u_i$ therefore estimates the mean weight at fixed truth over simulated events, each counted equally.
    \item The pull weight is $\pi_i=\omega_i u_i$.
    \item Step~2: a second classifier separates the truth coordinates weighted $w^{\mathrm{truth}}_i$ from the same coordinates weighted $\pi_i w^{\mathrm{truth}}_i$, and $\omega_i=p/(1-p)$ at each event's truth coordinates.
  \end{enumerate}
  % Amended 2026-11-16 (independent referee read of d30df1ce9 (2026-11-15, via the director, 2026-11-16), item D6; Joseph's critique of 2026-11-12, item 2, at paper_body.tex:131): the completeness c, 1 by construction, leaves S4.1 as it left the paper's equation.
  \item \emph{Cross section.} The truth coordinates of the truth-selected events, including those without a reconstructed partner, are histogrammed on the fine grid with weight $\omega_i w^{\mathrm{truth}}_i$ and divided by the flux, the exposure, the number of nucleons and the cell volume.
  \item \emph{Reported cells.} Each reporting cell's integral is the volume-weighted sum of the fine cells it contains.
\end{enumerate}
