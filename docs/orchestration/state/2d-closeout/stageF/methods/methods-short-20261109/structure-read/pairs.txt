This paper is a benchmark and diagnostic study of a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}, in the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta $\pT$ and $\pPar$, the available hadronic energy $\Eavail$, the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and the hadronic invariant mass $W$. Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five observables are unfolded simultaneously but are not independent. The event weights allow the same unfolded sample to be histogrammed in projections or cells chosen afterwards. An uncertainty for a new choice must be constructed and checked for that choice, and a practical gain over binned unfolding remains to be shown.
We report three findings and the tests supporting them, including tests that failed (table~\ref{tab:outcomes}). First, a benchmark against the published double-differential cross section $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$ from the same data~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq} shows agreement in normalization and a correlated shape difference that accompanies the unfolding method on fixed inputs and whose mechanism has not been identified (section~\ref{sec:bench}). Second, controlled changes of the simulated truth show that the five-observable result retains part of its prior on the sixteen reporting cells, while individual $(\Eavail,W)$ cells can move opposite to a generator-sized change or overshoot it (section~\ref{sec:prior}). Third, the estimator is reproducible but numerically unstable, and the calibration of its uncertainties has not been validated: these are distinct properties (section~\ref{sec:stability}).
The findings apply to one OmniFold implementation on one data set, for the observables, configurations and deformations tested. The sixteen-cell cross section that the estimator gives on the data illustrates the method and is not a measurement; the supplementary material gives it with its covariance and descriptive comparisons with generator predictions (\ssec{S9}{supp:output}).

The first coverage test of the five-observable covariance did not run to a result. The second test of section~\ref{sec:ll5d} grades the procedure's covariance at two truths. The procedure's two-dimensional covariance under-covers at both held-out tunes (section~\ref{sec:calib}). Neither the coverage of $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ at truths held out of its construction nor the size of either run-period block is independently validated (\ssec{S5}{supp:cov16}, and \ssecc{S6}{supp:cov2d}). The per-cell coverage readings cited are pointwise, for each reported quantity separately; simultaneous coverage of all of them is not graded. The joint readings grade the fraction of pseudo-experiments whose $\chi^2$ lies in a region fixed in advance.
\item The systematic-universe unfoldings of the two-dimensional covariance approximate the weighting of events that fail reconstruction. The approximation is not corrected. Tested in two universes, its effect is excluded only above twice the spread of an earlier training-seed block (section~\ref{sec:cov16}; \nsec{sec:uq-2d-scope}).
\item \emph{Data and response.} The data show a run-period inconsistency, present before unfolding, of unknown cause (section~\ref{sec:closure2d}).
\item The simulated detector response is not varied in the closure, deformation and fake-data tests (sections~\ref{sec:strategy} and~\ref{sec:fd16}), beyond the descriptive closures with a shifted recoil response of section~\ref{sec:fd16}; the coverage tests vary it only through the detector-systematic uncertainties they draw (section~\ref{sec:calib}). All use simulated pseudo-data, so none tests the detector model against data.
\end{enumerate}
