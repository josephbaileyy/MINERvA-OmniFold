We test what unbinned unfolding with OmniFold delivers on neutrino data, unfolding MINERvA
medium-energy open data simultaneously in five observables: muon transverse and longitudinal
momenta, available hadronic energy, three-momentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass. Three
findings follow. As a benchmark, the unfolding reproduces the published muon-kinematic crosssection integral within about 1%, but differs from it in shape, for a reason not identified. In controlled
studies the five-observable result retains part of its prior: fake-data studies at a GiBUU-like truth
and at a synthetic momentum-transfer tilt find residual bias exceeding uncertainty-based criteria that
were fixed before the tests were graded. [PENDING: the study of what changes the retained
prior] The estimator is reproducible, bitwise independent of its training seed in the unfolds we
ran, but not numerically stable: rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by up to 2.6
statistical standard deviations, beyond a stability requirement fixed in advance, and by a median
of 0.133 % of the cross section on the 16 reported cells, and an average of 96 fits that meets the
requirement is worse in bias at one of 17 truths. The procedure’s two-dimensional covariance, rebuilt
at each of two held-out generator tunes, under-covers at both; coverage of the reported five-observable
intervals is not established. [PENDING: the like-for-like five-observable coverage test] The
five-observable output on data is therefore given only as an illustration.

1.

INTRODUCTION

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their interpretation depends on correcting detector response and
on assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding
procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration matrix [1] is widely used, but its response tensor
grows rapidly as observables are added. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions [2–5]. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned
feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation [6, 7]. This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional
unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned
response tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been applied to collider data, for example to
eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [8]
and to twenty-four Z+jets observables at ATLAS [9] (see
ref. [10] for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been
demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector
sample [11].
This paper is a benchmark and diagnostic study of a
simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on
neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA mediumenergy νµ data from the open-data release [12], in the
transverse and longitudinal muon momenta pT and p∥ ,
the available hadronic energy Eavail , the three-momentum

transfer q3 , and the hadronic invariant mass W . Reconstructed W is fixed by the reconstructed (pT , p∥ , q3 ), so
the five observables are simultaneous but not independent. Event weights let the same unfolded sample be
histogrammed in projections or cells chosen afterwards.
An uncertainty for a new choice must be constructed and
checked for that choice; this study does not establish a
practical gain over binned unfolding. The study reports
three findings, each with the tests that establish it, including those that failed (table 1). First, a benchmark
against the published double-differential cross section
d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) from the same data [13] shows agreement
in normalization and a correlated shape difference whose
source has not been identified (section 4). Second, controlled changes of the simulated truth show that the
five-observable result retains part of its prior (section 5).
Third, reproducibility, numerical stability and the calibration of the uncertainties are distinct properties: the
estimator has the first, fails the second, and the third is
not established (section 6).
Interval coverage has not been validated. The findings
are those of one OmniFold implementation on one data
set, for the observables, configurations, and deformations
tested, rather than of multidimensional OmniFold in general. The sixteen-cell cross section the estimator gives
on the data is therefore not a measurement; the supplementary material gives it as illustrative output, with its
covariance and descriptive comparisons with generator
predictions (supplementary material, section S10).
Section 2 describes the data and the observables, section 3 the unfolding, the covariances and the test design,
sections 4–6 the three findings, and section 7 what they

show; the supplementary material gives the definitions,
and the companion analysis note [14] the full record.

A coarse 35 grid in (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ) has its edges at
the fine edges nearest the simulated tertiles. A coarse cell
is kept if its expected reconstructed signal and its truth
weight at the data exposure are both at least 1000 events.
We call this requirement the support rule. The 109 coarse
cells that meet it are the joint cells. The others, which
hold 0.47 % of the expected signal, stay in the total only
(supplementary material, section S7).
The five-observable results are reported on 16 cells,
R00–R15. They come from the 32 boxes formed by one
interior edge per axis, at pT 0.7, p∥ 4.5 and q3 2 GeV/c,

each supported box (one that meets the support rule) with
purity, the fraction of the simulated signal reconstructed
in it that was generated in it, below 0.1 is merged into a
neighbor, 4 boxes remain unreported, and every reported
cell passes the support rule. The diagonal fraction of a cell
is the fraction of the simulated signal generated in it and
reconstructed that is reconstructed in the same cell. Each
cell’s box is listed in supplementary material, table S2, and
the construction of the cells is given in section S3; other
reporting definitions, with their precision, are studied in
ref. [14], section 9.4.

3.

UNFOLDING METHOD AND TEST DESIGN
3.1.

Unfolding

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The
event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including
unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates
a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth
events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated
flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate
efficiency correction is applied.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin b
carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1−Bb /Db ), where Db
and Bb are the data and predicted background counts in

that bin, so the classifier’s target density is D(x) wpur (b).
The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds
simulated background events with negative weight to the
data, so that the target is D(x) − B(x) pointwise. A classifier refinement [18], clipped at zero, converts the signed
sample to non-negative weights before the first iteration.
The two targets share bin integrals wherever Bb < Db
but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction
varies within it (supplementary material, section S4).
The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees [19, 20] of
nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold
steps, and negative-weight background subtraction with
a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves. The
two-observable estimator, LightGBM [20] at a fixed seed,
gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance
(section 4.1); its central value is the one its systematicuniverse ensemble is built around, so the covariance uses
the same estimator. The five-observable results use the
five-observable estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with
the same background treatment and refinement capacity;
the note’s calibrated joint tests of generator predictions
use it too (supplementary material, section S7). Its seeds
are fixed, so its training is reproducible. The two estimators differ in the observables their classifiers see, two
or five, and in LightGBM’s training mode, seeded or deterministic (supplementary material, section S4). This
estimator removes most of the bias of purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-W cells (ref. [14],
section 9.2). Results from the two estimators are not
combined. The five-observable estimator is the baseline
this study characterizes (configuration (e) of section S4):
the stability probe and the ensemble average of section 6.1
were tested against it, and neither replaced it.
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the
unfolding. No learner setting, whether capacity, learning
rate, refinement capacity or iteration count, was tuned on
data. The five-observable estimator keeps five iterations
because 10, 15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model
dependence over the truth changes used in its development
(supplementary material, section S4). Two choices for the
two-dimensional result were made with the comparison to
the published result in view: the learner implementation
and the background-subtraction treatment (section 4.1).
That comparison is therefore not an independent test of
the method. Each classifier is trained on the full sample
whose weights it then sets, so no held-out or cross-fitted
split is used; it is trained without early stopping, and the
two classes keep their physical weights (supplementary
material, section S4).

3.2.

The reported covariances

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is
Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D . The term Ctotal sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization
uncertainties of the five-observable estimator, whose sum

is Ce , and Cmodel ; the lateral uncertainties shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than
reweighting events (supplementary material, section S10).
The model term Cmodel is built from the change in the
unfolded data when the prior is reweighted to GENIE
+ MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn, with GENIE CV
as the common reference. It is a measure of unscaled
generator-shape dependence, not a calibrated bound (supplementary material, section S5). The response term Cresp
is the response of the result to a one-standard-deviation
scale of the reconstructed recoil energy in the simulation.
The scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic
and other deposits. Cresp does not cover neutron or muon
deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution
effects beyond a scale. The unfolded results of the three
run-period groups, each relative to its own simulation,
differ by more than their bootstrap scatter. The cause is
not known, and these data cannot establish which period
is correct. The run-period block CPL,5D includes this
inconsistency in the reported covariance. It is built from
the double ratios among the groups, each group’s unfolded result over its own simulated truth, with no group
chosen as the reference, so it contains the differences by
construction. It was added after these differences were
seen. It has rank 2 and is not corrected for the bootstrap
noise it contains, and with three groups its size is poorly
determined: the 68 % interval on the ratio of its true to its
estimated size is 0.73–2.56. The median per-cell relative
uncertainty is 7.86%, 7.29% without CPL,5D , and 6.74%
without CPL,5D or Cresp . The fake-data tests are given
with and without it (section 5.2; ref. [14], section 10.4).
The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four
blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central
value’s estimator: systematic universes (187 universes,
including the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap
replicas), run period (three period groups), and training
seed (five seed settings, so that its own size is poorly determined). The resulting relative uncertainty has a median
of 6.41 % per bin and is 4.76 % on the integrated cross section, compared with 6.85 % and 4.61 % for the publication.
The covariance omits the approximation in the systematicuniverse unfoldings for events that fail reconstruction, the
learner dependence of section 4.1, and the dependence on
the regularization (supplementary material, section S6).
The dependence on the prior’s generator shape is reported
beside it as a separate covariance Cmodel , built from the
change of the result when the prior is reweighted to each
generator’s (pT , p∥ ) shape; its median is 3.71 % per bin,
and it is a measure of model dependence, not a calibrated
bound on the unfolding bias.
Every χ2 of the five-observable result inverts the full
covariance of the cells compared, 16 × 16, or 8 × 8 for
GiBUU’s eight cells with p∥ < 4.5 GeV/c, by Cholesky
decomposition and without truncation, so its number of
degrees of freedom is the number of cells. The blocks,
their ensembles and their normalizations are given in the
supplementary material, section S5.

3.3.

Test design

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the
prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated
experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The closure, deformation, and coverage
studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events
used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed
and true distributions together and keeps the detector
response at fixed truth. Throughout, the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune v1. Every study uses the analysis
simulation’s detector response.
The five-observable tests use one set of truths. 12
are graded (table 2): the simulation’s prior; five hybrid
truths, each equal to a generator prediction in every
cell of the fine grid and to the Tune v1 simulation’s
event composition within each cell; four generator shape
changes, among them the GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail ratio (the GiBUU Eavail shape); a noise-free closure at the
NuWro shape; and a synthetic tilt of the q3 distribution at fixed (Eavail , W ). 6 further truths, given in the
note, are descriptive. The truths are not held out of the
analysis. Two GENIE v3 tunes, G18_10a_02_11b and
AR23_20i_00_000, were generated for these tests and
held out of it; each reweights the simulated truth by its
shape ratio to GENIE CV. The bias is graded on Poisson pseudo-experiments, whose mean is compared with
the truth, or on noise-free pseudo-data, in which every
Poisson draw is replaced by its mean; the comparison of
the ensemble average with the baseline (section 6.1) uses
noise-free pseudo-data at all 17 distinct truths, graded
and descriptive, since the Tune v1 hybrid equals the prior.
The grading rules and their timing are stated with
the individual tests; comparisons without a grading rule
are descriptive. A truth is flagged when the bias gives
p < 0.01 in the χ2 over the 16 cells, or when a cell has
|z| > 2, where z is the cell’s bias divided by its uncertainty
under the same covariance. An estimator is stable when
rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by at
most 0.3 statistical standard deviations in the median and
1.0 at most. A coverage test grades the fraction of pseudoexperiments whose result lies within its interval, per cell
and jointly, against a threshold set from the test’s own null
distribution. The two-dimensional benchmark (section 4)
concerns the two-observable estimator; the truth-recovery
and stability findings (sections 5 and 6.1) concern the
five-observable estimator, whose rounding sensitivity is
carried in its statistical uncertainty (section 6.1).

BENCHMARK AGAINST THE PUBLISHED
TWO-DIMENSIONAL MEASUREMENT

4.1.

Normalization, per-bin agreement and the
covariance-weighted distance

The two-dimensional result benchmarks the unfolding
against a measurement from the same data. Its integrated cross section is 3.069 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon, compared with 3.039 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published
analysis, a ratio of 1.010. The standardized bin differences
formed with the published uncertainties have an RMS of
0.535 (figure 1). The two results share the data and the
flux, detector and generator systematics, and their crosscovariance is not available, so no difference between them
can be given a calibrated significance. The published
covariance has a condition number of 1.5 × 1012 , and 73 of
its 205 eigenvalues exceed 10−4 of the largest. Against it
alone, the covariance-weighted distance is χ2 /ndf = 2.27,
computed with the pseudo-inverse over the 205 reported
bins, which keeps all 205 eigenmodes, with ndf = 205,
the number of reported bins, since no parameter is fitted.
The value is descriptive, and no p-value is quoted for it.
It indicates a correlated shape difference, not a normalization difference: its normalization part is χ2 = 0.04,
against 465 for the shape. Keeping the 73 directions
with λ/λmax > 10−4 gives χ2 = 136, and the 139 with
λ/λmax > 10−6 give χ2 = 285. Most of the χ2 therefore
lies outside the best-determined directions, and those directions also contribute to it (figure 2; section S6). The
source of the shape difference has not been identified.
We quote the LightGBM result as the central value because its covariance uses the same estimator. The learner
implementation was chosen after three had been compared
with the published result: with purity-based subtraction,
their χ2 /ndf values were 3.66 (scikit-learn exact-split),
2.65 (LightGBM) and 2.70 (scikit-learn histogram, a mean
over ten seeds), so the distance depends on the estimator.
The refined negative-weight background treatment was
chosen on a closure test in simulation, before the refined
result’s comparison with the published result was computed, but was adopted in the analysis only after that
comparison was known, and the change lowered χ2 /ndf
from 2.65 to 2.27 (supplementary material, section S4).
The values quoted for the two-dimensional comparison are
therefore conditional on both choices, and the comparison
should be read as a descriptive comparison, not a blind
validation.
Two comparisons, each specified before it ran, put the
two-dimensional result beside other unfoldings of its own
inputs. Each compares a statistic T , formed from the
difference of the two results and its covariance over paired
replicas, with bars from a null of exact agreement (supplementary material, section S8), so a reading of “differ”
means that a difference is detectable at this noise, not that
it is large. A binned D’Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU) at ten iterations differs from it: T = 382.8,

cross term taken as a common fraction, from zero to one,
of its fully correlated value, over which the agreement
holds. The five-observable result therefore agrees with the
two-dimensional normalization in each muon-kinematic
quadrant; the benchmark does not test its distribution in
the three hadronic observables.

5.4.

Algorithm dependence and per-cell validity

On the sixteen cells, an IBU of the same data, with the
same simulation, background template and weight-only
systematic universes as the five-observable estimator, at
five iterations, differs from it: T = 194.4, against the
95th and 99.7th percentiles of its null, 28.12 and 39.04.
The reported cells therefore depend on the unfolding algorithm: the difference exceeds the null’s 99.7th percentile.
The covariance of that comparison holds the statistical,
universe and normalization blocks; the model block and
the lateral, Cresp and run-period blocks are not in it. The
comparisons use this analysis’s inputs: agreement between
the methods would not show that these inputs equal the
publication’s, and a difference does not show that either
method is correct.
Two per-cell validity rules, one based on the usage
recommendations of ref. [9] and one comparing the spread
of each cell’s bias over the graded truths with the spread of
the generator predictions, each leave 14 of the 16 cells valid
and 12 under both (supplementary material, section S11).
A third rule asks that a cell’s bias be at most half of
the truth’s departure from the prior, |bc | ≤ 0.5 |dc | in the
notation of section 5.3, at each graded truth where that
departure is at least 1 %; a cell where it applies at no truth
is reported as not assessed, and one whose class changes
within the noise of the estimate as unresolved. All three
rules classify the bias under simulated truths: none is
a coverage statement, none assesses the detector model,
and none excludes a cell from the output. [PENDING:
the per-cell bias-half validity rule]

6.1.

REPRODUCIBILITY, STABILITY AND
UNCERTAINTY CALIBRATION
Reproducibility and numerical stability

The five-observable estimator is unstable under floatingpoint rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, the reported quantities scatter
by a median of 0.23 % per joint cell, against a median
data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty of 0.37 %, and each
bootstrap replica of the data shows a scatter of the same
size. Over the 153 quantities defined below, rounding-level
changes of its inputs move its results by a median of 0.8
and up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of
the lower-capacity variant by up to 2.5, so the estimator
fails a data-stability requirement of at most 0.3 (median)
and 1.0 (maximum) statistical standard deviations. The
training-seed block of section 3.2 is zero because, in the
unfolds we ran, the result is bitwise independent of the
training seed and of input row order; this is distinct from
the sensitivity to rounding-level input changes quoted
above. That sensitivity enters the reported statistical
block by construction: each of its 50 bootstrap replicas
refits the whole unfolding with its own rounding-level
perturbation of the inputs. On the finer joint cells, the
rounding spread is about 37 % of the statistical variance
and below a tenth of any total uncertainty (ref. [14]); on
the 16 reported cells, over 20 rounding-level perturbations
of the data, it is a median 0.133 % of the cross section
(0.055 % to 0.252 %); the quoted 2.6 statistical standard
deviations is the largest shift, over 153 quantities (the
joint cells, the (Eavail , W ) cells and two totals) under two
such perturbations of the data, not the typical one. A
probe specified in advance, on simulated pseudo-data, of
two-fold cross-fitting and of two tree regularizations found
no variant that met the data-stability requirement, so the
estimator is unchanged (ref. [14]). Cross-fitting also gives
a larger fake-data bias than the current estimator at three
of the 17 truths it was graded on.
A study of the mechanism specified in advance, on
one simulated pseudo-experiment, examined six candidate causes, five of them testable, by the ratio of the
spread under a modified perturbation to that under the
unmodified one. On this pseudo-experiment, the perturbation reaches the estimator by breaking ties among equal
float32 values, which changes LightGBM’s histogram bins;
a perturbation that keeps the ties removes the spread,
leaving a ratio of 6.2 × 10−7 and 1.07 × 10−6 on the 153
quantities and the 16 cells. Equal float32 values are what
float32 storage of these kinematics produces. Perturbing
only the input of the refinement stage reproduces the
full spread, so the perturbation can enter there, though
whether the ties act there was not tested; finer binning,
float64 inputs and a permutation of the input rows do
not reduce the spread (table 1; section S4).
An ensemble-averaged variant of the estimator, the
mean of 96 fits, specified before the mechanism study
was read, meets the stability requirement on the pseudo-

data used to test stability (median 0.08 and at most 0.76,
against 0.3 and 1.0), where the reported estimator does
not. On noise-free fake-data closures at the 17 distinct
truths it is no worse than the reported estimator at 16
and worse at one, the GiBUU Eavail shape, by a criterion
fixed before the test, so it is not adopted and the estimator is unchanged. Whether a small bias increase is an
acceptable price for the stability gain is a bias–variance
question, which a separate study, specified in advance at
truths reserved from this one, is to address; this paper’s
conclusions do not depend on it. It is a tested mitigation
of the instability, not a replacement.

6.2.

Calibration of the uncertainties

The procedure’s two-dimensional covariance was tested
for coverage, in a test specified in advance, with 300
pseudo-experiments at each of the two held-out GENIE v3
tunes, the uncertainties propagated linearly as in the covariance’s construction, and the covariance rebuilt by the
same procedure at each tune, with Cmodel included; the
run-period block was not drawn. It under-covers at both
tunes. At AR23_20i_00_000, no pseudo-experiment
of the 300 lies in the joint region, a range of the χ2
fixed in advance from the test’s null distribution, which
flags under-coverage when 190 or fewer lie in it, and the
mean χ2 /ndf is 16.55 ± 0.17 for 204 degrees of freedom;
at G18_10a_02_11b, none lies in it, against 192, and
χ2 /ndf = 24.17 ± 0.37 for 205; the degrees of freedom are
the rank of each tune’s rebuilt covariance above 10−12
of its largest eigenvalue, which drops one direction at
AR23_20i_00_000. Per cell, three flags are raised, each
attributed to bias: one cell at both tunes and one more at
AR23_20i_00_000 (section S6). The test’s false-flag rate
under its null distribution is at most 0.0348 and 0.0363
per tune. G18_10a_02_11b shares its 2p2h model family
with one of Cmodel ’s generators. The result concerns the
procedure’s covariance at those tunes, not the reported
intervals at the data.
Statistical intervals of an earlier estimator of the five
observables, with purity-based subtraction, corrected for
its closure bias at nominal truth, miscover at the tested
truths, most strongly when the true signal rate is lowered
by about 10 %, with coverage as low as 0.06 for 68%
intervals (ref. [14], section 9.5).
A first coverage test of the five-observable covariance
at the two tunes, specified in advance, was stopped at
one of its gates before any coverage pseudo-experiment
ran, because the gate compared unlike objects, pseudoexperiments that move only the detector response at fixed
truth against blocks built from the prior and the response
together, and a staged redesign stopped at its first stage
under stopping rules fixed in advance, so the test gave no
coverage result in either direction (ref. [14], section 10.5;
section S9).

6.3.

A like-for-like five-observable coverage test

The design of the two-dimensional test has been carried
to the sixteen cells, at the same two tunes, with each
drawn uncertainty group’s pseudo-data built as its covariance block is built, and with the covariance rebuilt at each
tune by the reported procedure. It has two variants, fixed
in advance and correlated by construction: a primary one
without the GEANT4 neutron and proton bands, and one
with them. Its covariance is rebuilt once per truth, not per
pseudo-experiment; the response and run-period terms
are not drawn, and the lateral uncertainties are carried
by a surrogate. It grades each of the two truths and each
variant separately, and it tests the reported estimator, not
the ensemble average. The tunes are held out of the analysis but not independent of it: they share a quasielastic
model, and G18_10a_02_11b shares its 2p2h model family with one of the generators of Cmodel . [PENDING:
the like-for-like five-observable coverage test]

7.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

The method provides a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables, without a separate binned response
tensor for each projection. Its two-dimensional result
agrees with the published measurement in normalization
and differs from it in shape, for a reason not identified, and
its five-observable result agrees with the two-dimensional
result in each muon-kinematic quadrant. Its reported
cells depend on the unfolding algorithm. It does not yet
provide validated interval coverage, numerical stability
or recovery of every generator-sized truth change tested
within the reported uncertainty (table 1), and the data
show a run-period inconsistency, present before unfolding,
of unknown cause.
Two findings point to the unfolded result retaining part
of its prior. The first is the residual under a generatorsized change of the truth in section 5.1, which for the
GiBUU-like change is consistent with finite-iteration regularization. The second is on the data. When the simulation’s prior is changed, the data result shifts in correlation with minus the noise-free residual at the same truth
(ref. [14], section 9.4); these shifts are sensitivities, not a
measured bias on data.
These results bound what the study shows. The benchmark establishes normalization agreement and a qualified
shape comparison in two dimensions, not the validity
of the five-observable distribution. The validity rules
grade the estimator’s bias under simulated truths; they
do not identify where the detector model is inadequate.
Whether the two-dimensional shape difference arises in
the hadronic observables is not established: a test of that
would need contrasts specified in advance within fixed
muon-kinematic regions, with their joint uncertainty and
a treatment of multiplicity, and a demonstration that
plausible changes of the unfolding and of the detector
response cannot produce the observed pattern. That test

is a next step.
The studies of section 3–6 and of the companion note
show what must be checked before unfolded intervals
are interpreted: closure with the backgrounds included;
truth changes of generator size, not only nominal closure;
coverage under changes of the truth, not only at nominal
truth; robustness to training seeds and rounding-level
input changes; and run-period consistency.
Until the coverage, the prior dependence, the numerical stability, and the run-period effect are resolved,
the sixteen-cell output is an illustration of the method.

7.1.

Limitations

1. No reported interval has validated coverage. The
fake-data tests assess bias, not coverage; the first
coverage test of the five-observable covariance did
not run to a result; the like-for-like test of section 6.3, whatever its outcome, grades the procedure at two truths, not the reported intervals; and
the procedure’s two-dimensional covariance undercovers at both held-out tunes, a result about the
procedure’s covariance at those tunes, not the reported intervals at the data (section 6.2). Neither
the coverage of Cmodel at truths held out of its construction nor the size of either run-period block is
independently validated (supplementary material,
section S5, and section S6). The per-cell coverage readings cited are pointwise, for each reported
quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous
coverage of all of them; the joint readings grade the
fraction of pseudo-experiments whose χ2 lies in a
region fixed in advance.
2. Four of the sixteen cells, R02, R06, R10 and R15,
have a diagonal fraction (section 2) of only 0.16–
0.25.
