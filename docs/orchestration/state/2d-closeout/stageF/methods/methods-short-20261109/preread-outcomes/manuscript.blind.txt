We test unbinned unfolding with OmniFold on MINERvA medium-energy open data in five
observables simultaneously: muon transverse and longitudinal momenta, available hadronic energy,
three-momentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass. Three findings follow. As a benchmark,
the unfolding reproduces the published muon-kinematic cross-section integral within about 1%, but
differs in shape for an unidentified reason. In controlled studies the five-observable result retains
part of its prior: fake-data studies at a GiBUU-like truth and at a synthetic momentum-transfer tilt
find residual bias exceeding uncertainty-based criteria that were fixed before the tests were graded.
[PENDING: the study of what changes the retained prior] The estimator is reproducible:
in the unfolds we ran, its result is bitwise independent of its training seed. It is numerically unstable:
rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations,
beyond a stability requirement fixed in advance. On the 16 reported cells, these changes move
the results by a median of 0.133 % of the cross section. An average of 96 fits meets the stability
requirement but has greater bias at one of 17 truths. The procedure’s two-dimensional covariance,
rebuilt at each of two held-out generator tunes, under-covers at both; coverage of the reported
five-observable intervals is not established. [PENDING: the second five-observable coverage
test] We therefore present the five-observable output on data only as an illustration.

1.

INTRODUCTION

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their
interpretation requires corrections for detector response
and an assessment of the bias and uncertainty of the
unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on
a binned migration matrix [1] is widely used, but its
response tensor grows rapidly as observables are added.
Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach
three dimensions [2–5]. OmniFold learns event weights
in an unbinned feature space through classifier-based
density-ratio estimation [6, 7]. This allows simultaneous
higher-dimensional unfolding without a separate binned
response tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding
has been applied to collider data, for example to eight
observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [8] and
to twenty-four Z+jets observables at ATLAS [9] (see
ref. [10] for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been
demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector
sample [11].
This paper is a benchmark and diagnostic study of a
simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on
neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA mediumenergy νµ data from the open-data release [12], in the
transverse and longitudinal muon momenta pT and p∥ ,
the available hadronic energy Eavail , the three-momentum

transfer q3 , and the hadronic invariant mass W . Reconstructed W is fixed by the reconstructed (pT , p∥ , q3 ), so
the five observables are unfolded simultaneously but are
not independent. The event weights allow the same unfolded sample to be histogrammed in projections or cells
chosen afterwards. An uncertainty for a new choice must
be constructed and checked for that choice; this study
does not establish a practical gain over binned unfolding.
We report three findings and the tests supporting them,
including tests that failed (table 1). First, a benchmark
against the published double-differential cross section
d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) from the same data [13] shows agreement
in normalization and a correlated shape difference whose
source has not been identified (section 4). Second, controlled changes of the simulated truth show that the
five-observable result retains part of its prior (section 5).
Third, the estimator is reproducible but numerically unstable, and its uncertainty calibration is not established:
these are distinct properties (section 6).
Interval coverage has not been validated. The findings
are those of one OmniFold implementation on one data
set, in the cases tested. The sixteen-cell cross section that
the estimator gives on the data illustrates the method
and is not a measurement; the supplementary material
gives it with its covariance and descriptive comparisons
with generator predictions (supplementary material, section S10).
Section 2 describes the data and observables, and section 3 describes the unfolding, covariances and test design.
Sections 4–6 report the three findings, and section 7 discusses their implications. The supplementary material
gives the definitions, and the companion analysis note [14]

gives the full record.

the fine edges nearest the simulated tertiles. A coarse
cell is kept if its expected reconstructed signal and its
truth weight at the data exposure are both at least 1000
events. This support rule selects the 109 coarse cells used
in the joint tests, called the joint cells. The remaining
cells contain 0.47 % of the expected signal and contribute
only to the total (supplementary material, section S7).

R00–R15. They come from the 32 boxes formed by one

diagonal fraction is the fraction reconstructed in that cell
among the simulated signal events generated in it and
reconstructed anywhere. Each cell’s box is listed in supplementary material, table S2, and the cell construction
is described in section S3; other reporting definitions and
their precision are studied in ref. [14], section 9.4.

3.

UNFOLDING METHOD AND TEST DESIGN
3.1.

Unfolding

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps.
A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from
simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight.
The pairing of reconstructed and true events transfers that
weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a truthlevel classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to the
next iteration. We form cross sections by histogramming
the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and
dividing by integrated flux, target count and bin width.
Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth
events, so no separate efficiency correction is applied.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin b
carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1−Bb /Db ), where Db
and Bb are the data and predicted background counts in
that bin, so the classifier’s target density is D(x) wpur (b).
The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds

simulated background events with negative weight to the
data, so that the target is D(x) − B(x) pointwise. A classifier refinement [18], clipped at zero, converts the signed
sample to non-negative weights before the first iteration.
The two targets share bin integrals wherever Bb < Db
but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction
varies within it (supplementary material, section S4).
The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees [19, 20] of
nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold
steps, and negative-weight background subtraction with
a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves. The
two-observable estimator, LightGBM [20] at a fixed seed,
gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance
(section 4.1); its central value is the one its systematicuniverse ensemble is built around, so the covariance uses
the same estimator. The five-observable results use the
five-observable estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with
the same background treatment and refinement capacity;
the note’s calibrated joint tests of generator predictions
use it too (supplementary material, section S7). Its seeds
are fixed, so its training is reproducible. The two estimators differ in the observables their classifiers see, two
or five, and in LightGBM’s training mode, seeded or deterministic (supplementary material, section S4). This
estimator removes most of the bias of purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-W cells (ref. [14],
section 9.2). Results from the two estimators are not
combined. The five-observable estimator is the baseline
this study characterizes (configuration (e) of section S4):
the stability probe and the ensemble average of section 6.1
were tested against it, and neither replaced it.
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the
unfolding. No learner setting was tuned on data, including
capacity, learning rate, refinement capacity and iteration
count. The five-observable estimator uses five iterations
because 10, 15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model
dependence for the truth changes used in its development
(supplementary material, section S4). Two choices for the
two-dimensional result were made with the comparison to
the published result in view: the learner implementation
and the background-subtraction treatment (section 4.1).
That comparison is therefore not an independent test of
the method. Each classifier is trained on the full sample
whose weights it then sets, without a held-out or crossfitted split or early stopping. The two classes retain their
physical weights (supplementary material, section S4).

3.2.

The reported covariances

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is
Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D . The term Ctotal combines the
statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization uncertainties of the five-observable estimator,
whose sum is Ce , and Cmodel ; the lateral uncertainties
shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than reweighting events (supplementary mate-

rial, section S10). The model term Cmodel is constructed
from the change in the unfolded data when the prior is
reweighted to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in
turn, with GENIE CV as the common reference. It measures the result’s unscaled dependence on the generator
shape and is not calibrated as a bound (supplementary
material, section S5). The response term Cresp is the
response of the result to a one-standard-deviation scale
of the reconstructed recoil energy in the simulation. The
scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and
other deposits. Cresp does not cover neutron or muon
deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution
effects beyond a scale. The unfolded results of the three
run-period groups, each relative to its own simulation,
differ by more than their bootstrap scatter. The cause is
unknown, and these data cannot establish which period
is correct. The run-period block CPL,5D includes this
inconsistency in the reported covariance. It was added
after the differences were observed and is constructed
from the double ratios among the groups: each group’s
unfolded result divided by its own simulated truth, with
no group chosen as the reference. It therefore contains
the differences by construction. It has rank 2 and is not
corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains. With three
groups its size is poorly determined: the 68 % interval
on the ratio of its true to its estimated size is 0.73–2.56.
The median per-cell relative uncertainty is 7.86%, 7.29%
without CPL,5D , and 6.74% without CPL,5D or Cresp . The
fake-data tests are reported with and without this block
(section 5.2; ref. [14], section 10.4).
The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four
blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central
value’s estimator: systematic universes (187 universes,
including the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap
replicas), run period (three period groups), and training
seed (five seed settings, so its size is poorly determined).
The resulting relative uncertainty has a median of 6.41 %
per bin and is 4.76 % on the integrated cross section, compared with 6.85 % and 4.61 % for the publication. The
covariance omits the approximation in the systematicuniverse unfoldings for events that fail reconstruction, the
learner dependence of section 4.1, and the dependence on
the regularization (supplementary material, section S6).
The dependence on the prior’s generator shape is reported
separately as a covariance Cmodel , constructed from the
change in the result when the prior is reweighted to each
generator’s (pT , p∥ ) shape. Its median is 3.71 % per bin.
It measures model dependence and is not calibrated as a
bound on the unfolding bias.
For every χ2 of the five-observable result, we invert the
full covariance of the cells compared, 16 × 16, or 8 × 8 for
GiBUU’s eight cells with p∥ < 4.5 GeV/c, by Cholesky
decomposition and without truncation, so its number of
degrees of freedom is the number of cells. Each block is
defined in supplementary material, section S5.

3.3.

Test design

A closure test measures how well the unfolding recovers
a specified deformation of the known simulated truth
while holding the prior (the initial truth distribution)
fixed. Coverage is the frequency with which intervals
contain their target over repeated experiments; it tests
the estimator and the interval construction together. The
closure, deformation, and coverage studies reweight the
true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data,
which changes their reconstructed and true distributions
together and keeps the detector response at fixed truth.
The unfolding prior remains Tune v1 throughout. Every
study uses the analysis simulation’s detector response.
The five-observable tests use one set of truths. 12 are
graded (table 2): the simulation’s prior, five hybrid truths,
four generator shape changes, a noise-free closure at the
NuWro shape, and a synthetic tilt of the q3 distribution
at fixed (Eavail , W ). Each hybrid truth equals a generator
prediction in every cell of the fine grid and the Tune v1
simulation’s event composition within each cell. The
shape changes include the GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail ratio
(the GiBUU Eavail shape). 6 further truths, given in the
note, are descriptive. The truths are not held out of the
analysis. Two GENIE v3 tunes, G18_10a_02_11b and
AR23_20i_00_000, were generated for these tests and
held out of it; each reweights the simulated truth by its
shape ratio to GENIE CV. The bias is graded on Poisson
pseudo-experiments, whose mean is compared with the
truth, or on noise-free pseudo-data, in which every Poisson
draw is replaced by its mean; the comparison of the
ensemble average with the baseline (section 6.1) uses
noise-free pseudo-data at all 17 distinct truths, graded
and descriptive, since the Tune v1 hybrid equals the prior.
The grading rules and their timing are stated with
the individual tests; comparisons without a grading rule
are descriptive. A truth is flagged when the bias gives
p < 0.01 in the χ2 over the 16 cells, or when a cell has
|z| > 2, where z is the cell’s bias divided by its uncertainty under the same covariance. An estimator meets the
stability requirement when rounding-level changes of its
inputs move its results by at most 0.3 statistical standard
deviations in the median and 1.0 at maximum. A coverage test grades the fraction of pseudo-experiments whose
result lies within its interval, per cell and jointly, against
a threshold set from the test’s own null distribution. The
two-dimensional benchmark (section 4) concerns the twoobservable estimator; the truth-recovery and stability
findings (sections 5 and 6.1) concern the five-observable
estimator, whose rounding sensitivity is carried in its
statistical uncertainty (section 6.1).

BENCHMARK AGAINST THE PUBLISHED
TWO-DIMENSIONAL MEASUREMENT

4.1.

Normalization, per-bin agreement and the
covariance-weighted distance

We benchmark the two-dimensional unfolding against
a measurement from the same data. Its integrated cross
section is 3.069 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon, compared with
3.039 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published analysis, a ratio of 1.010. The standardized bin differences formed with
the published uncertainties have an RMS of 0.535 (figure 1). The two results share the data and the flux, detector and generator systematics, and their cross-covariance
is unavailable, so the significance of their difference cannot
be calibrated. The published covariance has a condition
number of 1.5 × 1012 , and 73 of its 205 eigenvalues exceed 10−4 of the largest. Using this covariance alone, the
covariance-weighted distance is χ2 /ndf = 2.27, computed
with the pseudo-inverse over the 205 reported bins, retaining all 205 eigenmodes. Here ndf = 205 is the number of
reported bins; no parameter is fitted. The value is descriptive, and no p-value is quoted for it. The difference is in
shape: its normalization part is χ2 = 0.04, against 465 for
the shape. Keeping the 73 directions with λ/λmax > 10−4
gives χ2 = 136, and the 139 with λ/λmax > 10−6 give
χ2 = 285. Most of the χ2 therefore lies outside the
best-determined directions, although those directions also
contribute (figure 2; section S6). The source of the shape
difference has not been identified.
We quote the LightGBM result as the central value
because its covariance uses the same estimator. The
learner implementation was chosen after three had been
compared with the published result: with purity-based
subtraction, their χ2 /ndf values were 3.66 (scikit-learn
exact-split), 2.65 (LightGBM) and 2.70 (scikit-learn histogram, a mean over ten seeds), so the distance depends
on the estimator. We chose the refined negative-weight
background treatment from a closure test in simulation,
before computing the refined result’s comparison with the
published result. We adopted it in the analysis only after
that comparison was known. The change lowered χ2 /ndf
from 2.65 to 2.27 (supplementary material, section S4).
The two-dimensional comparison is therefore conditional
on both choices and is descriptive.
Two comparisons, each specified before it ran, test
the two-dimensional result against other unfoldings of
the same inputs. Each compares a statistic T , formed
from the difference of the two results and its covariance
over paired replicas, with thresholds from a null of exact
agreement (supplementary material, section S8), so “differ”
means that a difference is detectable at this noise level.
A binned D’Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU)
at ten iterations differs from it: T = 382.8, against a null
95th percentile of 123.6 and maximum of 138.5 over 300
paired replicas. The ratio r of the IBU’s distance from the
published result to that of the two-dimensional result is

5.3.

What moves the retained prior

A study specified in advance tests which settings
of the five-observable estimator change the fraction
of a truth change that remains unrecovered. It uses
noise-free pseudo-data with backgrounds at one split

of the simulation
P
Pand measures the retained fraction
ρ = − c bc dc / c d2c at each graded truth. Here
dc = (Xc − Pc )/Pc is the truth’s departure from the
prior in cell c and bc = (X̂c − Xc )/Pc is the bias, with X̂,
X and P the unfolded, true and prior cross sections. The
sums run over cells where |dc | is at least 1 %; ρ = 1 if the
result is the prior and ρ = 0 if the departure is recovered.
The study changes one setting at a time: the iteration
count, classifier capacity, training statistics or observables
used by the classifiers. It also runs an exact binned IBU
on the same pseudo-data. At the two flagged truths, each
setting is classified according to whether any tested level
reduces or increases ρ by more than a threshold fixed in
advance. This threshold is the larger of 0.05 and 9.03
times the standard deviation of the paired change of ρ
over five splits of the simulation. The factor 9.03 is the
two-sided Student-t quantile on four degrees of freedom
at a familywise rate of 0.01 over the study’s twelve primary comparisons. The IBU at five iterations is classified
according to whether it retains less or more of the prior
than the five-observable estimator, using the same rule
with its own threshold. A separate test asks whether its
retention falls or rises by at least a fixed margin of 0.05,
distinct from that threshold, between five and 200 iterations. The retained fraction is undefined at a truth where
fewer than two cells depart from the prior by at least 1 %.
A tested level clears a flagged truth if the truth is no
longer flagged under the criterion of section 3.3 applied to
the noise-free pseudo-data. The results are combined into
labels fixed in advance: consistent with finite-iteration
regularization (more iterations reduce ρ, and so do longer
IBU runs); consistent with an information limit at these
settings (neither more iterations nor more capacity reduces ρ, nor do longer IBU runs); and consistent with
a share specific to the implementation (the IBU retains
less of the prior, or more capacity reduces ρ). The study
selects no estimator; its results describe these truths only
(supplementary material, section S9). [PENDING: the
study of what changes the retained prior]

5.4.

Algorithm dependence and per-cell validity

On the sixteen cells, an IBU of the same data, with the
same simulation, background template and weight-only
systematic universes as the five-observable estimator, at
five iterations, differs from it: T = 194.4, against the 95th
and 99.7th percentiles of its null, 28.12 and 39.04. The
reported cells therefore depend on the unfolding algorithm:
the difference exceeds the null’s 99.7th percentile. The
covariance of that comparison contains the statistical,
universe and normalization blocks; the model block and
the lateral, Cresp and run-period blocks are not in it.
These comparisons use this analysis’s inputs: agreement
between the methods would not show that these inputs
equal the publication’s, and a difference does not show
that either method is correct.
Two per-cell validity rules, one based on the usage

recommendations of ref. [9] and one comparing the spread
of each cell’s bias over the graded truths with the spread of
the generator predictions, each leave 14 of the 16 cells valid
and 12 under both (supplementary material, section S11).
A third rule asks that a cell’s bias be at most half of
the truth’s departure from the prior, |bc | ≤ 0.5 |dc | in the
notation of section 5.3, at each graded truth where that
departure is at least 1 %; a cell is reported as not assessed
if the rule applies at no truth, and as unresolved if its
class changes within the noise of the estimate. All three
rules classify the bias under simulated truths: none is
a coverage statement, none assesses the detector model,
and none excludes a cell from the output. [PENDING:
the per-cell bias-half validity rule]

6.1.

REPRODUCIBILITY, STABILITY AND
UNCERTAINTY CALIBRATION
Reproducibility and numerical stability

The five-observable estimator is unstable under floatingpoint rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, the reported quantities scatter
by a median of 0.23 % per joint cell, against a median
data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty of 0.37 %, and each
bootstrap replica of the data shows a scatter of the same
size. Over the 153 quantities defined below, rounding-level
changes of its inputs move its results by a median of 0.8
and up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of
the lower-capacity variant by up to 2.5, so the estimator
fails a data-stability requirement of at most 0.3 (median)
and 1.0 (maximum) statistical standard deviations. The
training-seed block of section 3.2 is zero because, in the
unfolds we ran, the result is bitwise independent of the
training seed and of input row order; this is distinct from
the sensitivity to rounding-level input changes quoted
above. That sensitivity enters the reported statistical
block by construction: each of its 50 bootstrap replicas
refits the whole unfolding with its own rounding-level
perturbation of the inputs. On the finer joint cells, the
rounding spread is about 37 % of the statistical variance
and below a tenth of any total uncertainty (ref. [14]); on
the 16 reported cells, over 20 rounding-level perturbations
of the data, it is a median 0.133 % of the cross section
(0.055 % to 0.252 %); the quoted 2.6 statistical standard
deviations is the largest shift, over 153 quantities (the
joint cells, the (Eavail , W ) cells and two totals) under two
such perturbations of the data, not the typical one. A
probe specified in advance tested two-fold cross-fitting
and two tree regularizations on simulated pseudo-data.
No variant met the data-stability requirement, so we retained the estimator (ref. [14]). Cross-fitting also gives a
larger fake-data bias than the current estimator at three
of the 17 truths it was graded on.
A study of the mechanism specified in advance, on
one simulated pseudo-experiment, examined six candidate causes, five of them testable, by the ratio of the

spread under a modified perturbation to that under the
unmodified one. On this pseudo-experiment, the perturbation reaches the estimator by breaking ties among equal
float32 values, which changes LightGBM’s histogram bins;
a perturbation that keeps the ties removes the spread,
leaving a ratio of 6.2 × 10−7 and 1.07 × 10−6 on the 153
quantities and the 16 cells. Equal float32 values are what
float32 storage of these kinematics produces. Perturbing
only the input of the refinement stage reproduces the
full spread, so the perturbation can enter there, though
whether the ties act there was not tested; finer binning,
float64 inputs and a permutation of the input rows do
not reduce the spread (table 1; section S4).
An ensemble-averaged variant of the estimator, the
mean of 96 fits, specified before the mechanism study
was read, meets the stability requirement on the pseudodata used to test stability (median 0.08 and at most 0.76,
against 0.3 and 1.0), where the reported estimator does
not. On noise-free fake-data closures at the 17 distinct
truths it is no worse than the reported estimator at 16
and worse at one, the GiBUU Eavail shape, by a criterion
fixed before the test, so it is not adopted and the estimator is unchanged. Whether a small bias increase is an
acceptable price for the stability gain is a bias–variance
question, which a separate study, specified in advance at
truths reserved from this one, is to address; this paper’s
conclusions do not depend on it. We report the ensemble
average as a tested mitigation of the instability.

6.2.

Calibration of the uncertainties

The procedure’s two-dimensional covariance was tested
for coverage, in a test specified in advance, with 300
pseudo-experiments at each of the two held-out GENIE v3
tunes, the uncertainties propagated linearly as in the
covariance’s construction, and the covariance rebuilt by
the same procedure at each tune, with Cmodel included;
the run-period block was not drawn. It under-covers
at both tunes. At AR23_20i_00_000, none of the 300
pseudo-experiments lies in the joint region, a range of χ2
fixed in advance from the test’s null distribution. The
test flags under-coverage when 190 or fewer lie in this
region. The mean χ2 /ndf is 16.55 ± 0.17 for 204 degrees
of freedom. At G18_10a_02_11b, none lies in the region,
against a threshold of 192, and χ2 /ndf = 24.17 ± 0.37
for 205 degrees of freedom. The degrees of freedom are
the rank of each tune’s rebuilt covariance above 10−12
of its largest eigenvalue, which drops one direction at
AR23_20i_00_000. The per-cell tests raise three flags,
each attributed to bias: one cell at both tunes and one
more at AR23_20i_00_000 (section S6). The test’s falseflag rate under its null distribution is at most 0.0348
and 0.0363 per tune. G18_10a_02_11b shares its 2p2h
model family with one of Cmodel ’s generators. The result
concerns the procedure’s covariance at those tunes, not
the reported intervals at the data.
Statistical intervals of an earlier estimator of the five

observables, with purity-based subtraction, corrected for
its closure bias at nominal truth, miscover at the tested
truths, most strongly when the true signal rate is lowered
by about 10 %, with coverage as low as 0.06 for 68%
intervals (ref. [14], section 9.5).
A first coverage test of the five-observable covariance at
the two tunes, specified in advance, gave no coverage result: one of its gates, which compared pseudo-experiments
that move only the detector response at fixed truth with
blocks built from the prior and the response together,
stopped it before any coverage pseudo-experiment ran,
and a staged redesign stopped at its first stage under
stopping rules fixed in advance (ref. [14], section 10.5;
section S9).

6.3.

A second five-observable coverage test

A second test carries the design of the two-dimensional
test to the sixteen cells: at each of the same two tunes,
300 pseudo-experiments in each variant, each drawn uncertainty group’s pseudo-data built as its covariance block
is built, the model, response and run-period terms not
drawn, the lateral uncertainties carried by a surrogate,
and the covariance rebuilt once per tune by the reported
procedure. The graded covariance includes the model
term, whose role is to cover each tune’s departure from
the simulation, and leaves out the response and run-period
terms. The test has two variants, fixed in advance and
correlated by construction: a primary one without the
GEANT4 neutron and proton bands, and one with them.
Each tune and each variant is graded separately, per cell
and jointly, against thresholds set from the test’s null
distribution. A pass means that no under-coverage is
detected at these thresholds, whose resolution is about
0.10 in coverage per cell and 0.08 jointly. Only undercoverage counts as a failure; over-coverage is reported.
In one variant at one tune, the cell tests raise a false
flag with probability at most 0.025, and so does the joint
test. Because the response and run-period terms are left
out of the graded covariance, a cell that passes at a tune
also passes under the procedure’s full covariance there,
and the joint reading carries over only if both matrices
have 16 degrees of freedom. The test uses the reported
estimator. The tunes are held out of the analysis but
not independent of it: they share a quasielastic model,
and G18_10a_02_11b shares its 2p2h model family with
one of the generators of Cmodel . The result concerns the
procedure’s covariance at those tunes, not the reported intervals at the data. The construction of the pseudo-data,
and the checks made on it before the main run, are given
in supplementary material, section S9. [PENDING: the
second five-observable coverage test] The data result
remains an illustration.

7.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

This implementation performs simultaneous unbinned
unfolding in five observables, without a separate binned
response tensor for each projection. The two-dimensional
result agrees with the published measurement in normalization but differs in shape for an unidentified reason. The
five-observable result agrees with the two-dimensional result in each muon-kinematic quadrant. The reported cells
depend on the unfolding algorithm. The implementation
has not achieved validated interval coverage, numerical
stability or recovery within the reported uncertainty of
every generator-sized truth change tested (table 1), and
the data show a run-period inconsistency, present before
unfolding, of unknown cause.
Two findings indicate that the unfolded result retains
part of its prior. The first is the residual under a generatorsized change of the truth in section 5.1, which for the
GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail change is consistent with finiteiteration regularization. The second comes from the data.
When the simulation’s prior is changed, the data result
shifts in correlation with minus the noise-free residual at
the same truth (ref. [14], section 9.4); these shifts measure
the result’s sensitivity to the prior.
Whether the two-dimensional shape difference arises
in the hadronic observables is not established. Testing
this would require contrasts specified in advance within
fixed muon-kinematic regions, with their joint uncertainty
and a treatment of multiplicity. It would also require
demonstrating that plausible changes of the unfolding and
detector response cannot produce the observed pattern.
That test is a next step.
The studies of section 3–6 and of the companion note
show what must be checked before unfolded intervals are
interpreted: closure with the backgrounds included, recovery of truth changes of generator size, coverage at changed
truths, robustness to training seeds and rounding-level
input changes, and run-period consistency.
Until the coverage, the prior dependence, the numerical stability, and the run-period effect are resolved,
the sixteen-cell output is an illustration of the method.

7.1.

Limitations

1. No reported interval has validated coverage. The
fake-data tests assess only bias. The first coverage
test of the five-observable covariance did not run
to a result. The second test of section 6.3 grades
the procedure’s covariance at two truths. The procedure’s two-dimensional covariance under-covers
at both held-out tunes (section 6.2). Neither the
coverage of Cmodel at truths held out of its construction nor the size of either run-period block is
independently validated (supplementary material,
section S5, and section S6). The per-cell coverage readings cited are pointwise, for each reported
quantity separately. The joint readings grade the
fraction of pseudo-experiments whose χ2 lies in a
region fixed in advance.
2. Four of the sixteen cells, R02, R06, R10 and R15,
have a diagonal fraction (section 2) of only 0.16–
0.25.
