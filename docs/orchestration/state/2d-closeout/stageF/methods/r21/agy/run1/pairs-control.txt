BEFORE (Sec. 1 of the paper, accepted as correct): \section{Introduction} \label{sec:intro} Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction models across muon and hadronic observables. Their interpretation depends on correcting detector response and assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration matrix~\cite{DAgostini:1994fjx} is widely used, but the response tensor grows rapidly when additional observables are reported. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space through classifier-based density-ratio estimation \cite{Andreassen:2019cjw,Andreassen:2021zzk}. This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding possible without specifying a separate binned response tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been applied to collider data, for example eight observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1~\cite{H1:2021wkz} and twenty-four $Z$+jets observables at ATLAS~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}; see Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} for a survey. In neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector sample~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions~\cite{MINERvA:2022qelike3d,MINERvA:2026qeleme,MicroBooNE:2023inc3d,NOvA:2026inc3d}. We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_{\mu}$ data from the MINERvA open-data release~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData}. The result is a simultaneous charged-current inclusive cross section in five observables, the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta $\pT$ and $\pPar$, available hadronic energy $\Eavail$, three-momentum transfer $q_{3}$, and hadronic invariant mass $W$, reported in sixteen cells with its covariance (Sec.~\ref{sec:result16}) and compared with five generator predictions (Sec.~\ref{sec:gen16}). We first reproduce the published double-differential cross section in transverse and longitudinal muon momentum, $d^{2}\sigma/(d\pT\,d\pPar)$~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, as an end-to-end test of the unfolding, and we also give three- and four-observable central values. Reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five observables are simultaneous but not independent. The validation studies assess the result through the two-dimensional reproduction, background-subtraction tests, recovery of generator-sized truth changes, a coverage test and a fake-data study of estimator bias. They examine whether the unfolding gives reliable multidimensional cross sections when the simulation mismodels the truth by generator-sized amounts. The coverage of unfolded intervals is the rate at which they contain the true cross section. Interval coverage has not been validated. The bias tests, the fake-data study and the dependence of the generator $\chi^{2}$ on the flux correlations are given with the result (Secs.~\ref{sec:fd16} and~\ref{sec:gen16}). \emph{Scope.} The findings are those of one OmniFold implementation on one data set, for the observables, configurations, and deformations tested, rather than of multidimensional OmniFold in general. The limitations are listed in Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the data, the simulation and the observables, and Sec.~\ref{sec:method} the unfolding and the reporting cells. Section~\ref{sec:validation} gives the uncertainties and the validation studies, from the reproduction of the published two-dimensional measurement to the fake-data tests of the sixteen-cell result, and Sec.~\ref{sec:result16} gives the result and its comparison with generator predictions. Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} discusses what limits the estimator, and Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations} summarizes the principal limitations. The companion analysis note~\cite{AnalysisNote2026} gives the full validation record, the construction of the uncertainties, and the tests with their assumptions, and is cited below by section.

BEFORE (Sec. 2 of the paper, accepted as correct): \section{Data, simulation, and observables} \label{sec:data} The analysis uses the twelve MINERvA medium-energy, forward-horn-current run periods, corresponding to $1.057\times10^{21}$ protons on target and an average neutrino energy near \SI{6}{GeV}~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}, from an earlier production of the MINERvA open-data release of pre-selected analysis ntuples (AnaTuples)~\cite{MINERvA:OpenData} (see Data availability). The detector, including the downstream MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2013zvz}. The signal is a $\nu_{\mu}$ charged-current interaction, with any final state, whose true vertex lies in the tracker fiducial volume ($5980<z<\SI{8422}{mm}$, inside a hexagon of apothem \SI{850}{mm}) and whose muon has $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<\pPar<\SI{60}{GeV/c}$, and $\pT\le\SI{4.5}{GeV/c}$. Selected simulated events outside this definition, including charged-current $\nu_{\mu}$ events whose muon lies outside these limits, are background. The selection requires a reconstructed vertex in the same volume and a reconstructed muon with $\theta_{\mu}<20^{\circ}$, matched to a MINOS track of negative charge, with a dead-time requirement, as in Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. The flux treatment follows Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2016iqn}. After selection, the sample contains $4.12$ million selected data events. The simulation contains $32.8$ million unweighted true signal events and $20.5$ million selected simulated events. The simulation is scaled to the data exposure. The simulated events are generated with GENIE~2.12.6~\cite{Andreopoulos:2009rq} and the MINERvA Tune~v1, which reweights GENIE with a non-resonant pion reduction, a random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil two-particle--two-hole (2p2h) enhancement~\cite{MINERvA:2016lowrecoil}. Event selection is held fixed as the classifier inputs and unfolded observables grow from $(\pT,\pPar)$ to $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_3,W)$. Adding features does not add selected events or relax the signal definition. The unfolded observables are defined at truth level. Besides the muon momentum components $\pT=p_{\mu}\sin\theta_{\mu}$ and $\pPar=p_{\mu}\cos\theta_{\mu}$, they are the three-momentum transfer $q_{3}=|\vec{q}\,|$ from the generator's four-vectors, the hadronic invariant mass $W=\sqrt{M^{2}+2M(E_{\nu}-E_{\mu})-Q^{2}}$ with the struck-nucleon mass $M$, and the available energy \begin{equation} \Eavail=\sum_{p}(E_{p}-m_{p})+\sum_{\pi^{\pm}}(E_{\pi}-\SI{135}{MeV}) +\sum_{\pi^{0}}E_{\pi}+\sum_{\gamma}E_{\gamma}, \label{eq:eavail} \end{equation} with a defined constant of \SI{135}{MeV}, rather than the charged-pion mass, in the charged-pion term. All other final-state particles, including neutrons, contribute nothing. The sums run over the generator's final-state particles after final-state interactions, in which neutral pions appear undecayed. The \SI{135}{MeV} constant is inherited from the analysis framework's definition. It defines the reported observable and is used consistently by the analysis and every prediction. Reconstructed $\Eavail$ is $1.17$ times the calorimetric recoil energy in the tracker and electromagnetic calorimeter, with muon deposits removed. Reconstructed $q_{3}$ and $W$ instead combine the muon kinematics with a separate calorimetric estimate $q_{0}$ of the energy transfer. Reconstructed $W$ is an exact function of the reconstructed $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$, so the five reconstructed inputs share information and do not provide five independent constraints. The relation holds because $W_{\mathrm{reco}}^{2}=(\bar M+q_{0})^{2}-q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}^{2}$, with $\bar M$ an average nucleon mass, and $q_{3,\mathrm{reco}}$ increases with $q_{0}$ at fixed muon kinematics. This relation is exact apart from a clamp at $W^{2}=0$. Only reconstructed $W$ is fixed by the other inputs. Reconstructed $\Eavail$ comes from its own calorimetric estimator and is not a function of them. At truth level, $\theta_{\mu}$, and with it $\pT$ and $\pPar$, is measured from a fixed beam axis rather than from the true neutrino direction. Truth $W$ is therefore fixed by $(\pT,\pPar,q_{3})$ only up to that event-by-event angle and the choice of $M$. The definitions are detailed in Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}, Secs.~\ref*{N-sec:eavail-definition} and~\ref*{N-sec:eavailw-definitions}. The external predictions for CH are the untuned GENIE~2.12.10 without (``GENIE CV'') and with (``GENIE + MEC'') the Valencia 2p2h component~\cite{Nieves:2011pp}, which models meson-exchange currents (MEC). The predictions for carbon are NuWro~21.09~\cite{Golan:2012wx} and GiBUU~2019~\cite{Buss:2011mx}. All are per nucleon of their target. The GENIE and NuWro predictions are reweighted exactly, event by event, to the analysis flux. The GiBUU sample stops at $E_{\nu}\approx\SI{20}{GeV}$. The predictions compute $W$ with the neutron mass for every event and measure $\pT$ about their own neutrino axis. Hydrogen has no quasielastic, 2p2h, or coherent $\nu_{\mu}$ charged-current channel, so normalizing per nucleon does not remove the difference between the targets. In GENIE's own CH sample, the carbon-only cross section per nucleon exceeds that of CH by \SI{8.3}{\percent} for $W<\SI{1.1}{GeV}$. The NuWro and GiBUU comparisons contain a target difference of this kind, which the comparison of Sec.~\ref{sec:gen16} does not correct. The comparisons of the predictions with the analysis definitions are given in Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}, Secs.~\ref*{N-sec:eavailw-axis} and~\ref*{N-sec:eavailw-generators}. The fine grid is the analysis binning: the published $14\times16$ muon-momentum bins times 7 $\Eavail$, 7 $q_{3}$, and 6 $W$ bins. A coarse $3^{5}$ grid in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$ has its edges at the fine edges nearest the simulated tertiles. A coarse cell is kept if its expected reconstructed signal and its truth weight at the data exposure are both at least 1000 events. Of the coarse cells, \jtNcells{} pass; these are the joint cells. The others, which hold \SI{0.47}{\percent} of the expected signal, stay in the total only. The construction of the coarse grid is given in \nsec{sec:joint5d-design}.

BEFORE (Sec. 3 of the paper, accepted as correct): \section{Cross-section extraction} \label{sec:method} Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification steps. A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate efficiency correction is applied. Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through the density the classifier learns to match. In the purity-based subtraction every data event in reconstructed bin $b$ carries the weight $w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)=\max(0,\,1-B_{b}/D_{b})$, where $D_{b}$ and $B_{b}$ are the data and predicted background counts in that bin, so the target density is $D(x)\,w_{\mathrm{pur}}(b)$. The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative weight to the data, so the target is $D(x)-B(x)$ pointwise; a classifier refinement~\cite{StayPositive2025}, clipped at zero, converts the signed sample into non-negative weights before the first iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever $B_{b}<D_{b}$ but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction varies within it (\napp{app:negweight}). Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, without a held-out or cross-fitted split, early stopping, or class balancing; the training details are in \nsec{sec:method-algo}. Both estimators use five OmniFold iterations with gradient-boosted decision-tree classifiers~\cite{Pedregosa:2011sklearn,Ke:2017lightgbm}. The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding. The nominal settings are the scikit-learn defaults, with LightGBM matched to their capacity. The refinement capacity of the joint-test estimator was chosen in simulation closure studies. No hyperparameter search was performed on data. Table~\ref{tab:estimators} lists the two estimators and the results each is used for. The two-observable central value uses LightGBM at seed~42 with negative-weight background subtraction and a refinement of 400 trees and 31 leaves. This reproducible central value is that of its own systematic-universe ensemble, so its covariance is built with the same estimator. The three- and four-observable central values and the sixteen-cell five-observable result (Sec.~\ref{sec:result16}) use the joint-test estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with the same background treatment and refinement capacity, which removes most of the bias of purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-$W$ cells (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}); the note's simulation-calibrated tests use the same estimator. The variants used in development, and the results that rest on them, are described in \nsec{sec:method-config}. The joint-test estimator keeps five iterations because 10, 15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model dependence, measured as the largest residual proxy over the truth changes used in its development. Because the block is built from the observed differences among the periods, its size is not validated and its own consistency check cannot fail.
