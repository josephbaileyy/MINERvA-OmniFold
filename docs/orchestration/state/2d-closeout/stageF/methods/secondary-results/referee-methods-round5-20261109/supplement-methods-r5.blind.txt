This Supplement gives the definitions that the paper’s results depend on: the event selection,
the observables, the reporting cells and their machine-readable files, the estimators, the reported
covariances, the joint tests and the comparisons with binned unfolding, and the designs of the
coverage and fake-data tests. Its text is taken from the companion analysis note [1], which gives the
full record; where a detail stays in the note, the Supplement points to it by section.

S1

Signal definition and event selection

The signal definition and phase space follow Ref. [2] exactly. Truth-level signal events are chargedcurrent νµ interactions in the central-tracker fiducial volume with muon phase space
θµ < 20◦ ,

1.5 < p∥ < 60 GeV/c,

pT < 4.5 GeV/c.

The fiducial volume spans 5980 < z < 8422 mm within an 850 mm apothem and contains 3.2353×1030
nucleons, the fixed tracker-geometry constant.
Reconstructed events require a vertex in the same fiducial volume and apothem, a muon track
with θµ < 20◦ matched to a track in the MINOS near detector whose MINOS track fit succeeded, no
dead-time overlap, and a neutrino (rather than antineutrino) candidate.
Genuine backgrounds are reconstructed events whose true interaction fails the signal definition:
charged-current events of other flavors or wrong sign, neutral-current interactions, and out-of-fiducialvertex events. Their predicted rate is 0.35 % of selected events, evaluated on playlist 1A. This
is the same sub-percent scale as the 8655 (0.2 %) predicted background events of Ref. [2], which
counts only the wrong-flavor, wrong-sign and neutral-current components; exact agreement is not
expected given the differences in definition and selection, and neither prediction carries an uncertainty.
Out-of-phase-space signal, reconstructed inside the phase space with its true muon outside it, is
treated separately. Both are POT-scaled and subtracted from the reconstructed-data target before
unfolding (Ref. [1], Sec. 3).

S4

Algorithm, background treatment and estimator configurations

OmniFold [3, 4] unfolds by iterated reweighting with machine-learned likelihood ratios, operating on
per-event observables rather than on a fixed histogram binning. Each iteration has two steps:
1. Step 1 (detector space). A classifier separates measured data from reconstructed simulation
weighted by the current push-weights. Its calibrated output p gives the likelihood-ratio reweight
w = p/(1 − p) for each reconstructed simulated event. Each simulated event is a (truth, reco)
pair, allowing the weight to be pulled back directly to its truth record. Truth events without
a reconstructed partner (“misses”) receive step-1 weights from a regressor trained on the
reconstructed subsample, w(truth features). This native OmniFold acceptance treatment
requires no separate efficiency correction.
2. Step 2 (truth space). A second classifier separates the original truth-level simulation from
the same sample carrying the pulled-back step-1 weights. Its smooth truth-space reweight
becomes the next iteration’s push-weight.
After n iterations, the push-weights define an efficiency-corrected truth-level reweighted simulation
ensemble. Histogramming this ensemble in any binning chosen afterwards gives the unfolded spectrum.
The iteration count and learner capacity regularize the unfolding.

Training details. Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, with no
held-out or cross-fitted split and no early stopping. The two classes keep their physical weights, with
no class balancing and no renormalization between iterations, so the learned ratio p/(1 − p), with p
restricted to [10−6 , 1 − 10−6 ], carries the normalization. Truth events without a reconstructed partner
receive the prediction of an unweighted regressor of the step-1 weight on the truth observables. In
the refined configurations, a classifier separating positive from negative weights, trained with |w|,
gives each event the weight |w| max(0, 2g − 1). No effective sample size of the unfolded ensemble is
computed.
Background subtraction. In the binned purity subtraction, all selected data events enter step 1;
none is discarded and no negative-weight events are injected. Each receives a reco-bin purity weight.
The POT-scaled background prediction includes genuine backgrounds and out-of-phase-space signal
(Sec. S1) and is binned on the reconstructed analysis grid. A data event in reco bin b receives

Hyperparameter choice. The learner settings were fixed a priori; no hyperparameter search was
performed on data, which avoids tuning on the result. They are the scikit-learn GradientBoosting
defaults: 100 trees, learning rate 0.1, and depth-3 capacity. The LightGBM learner of the central
value and its ensembles uses matched capacity (num_leaves = 8 = 23 ), giving every learner the same
deliberately modest capacity, one of OmniFold’s regularizers (Sec. S4). The configuration is instead
validated after the fact by closure and classifier-calibration tests (Ref. [1], Sec. 5, Ref. [1], Sec. 7.5),

insensitivity to doubling the iteration count, measured for the exact-split cross-check (below), backend
agreement in method-dependence studies (Ref. [1], Sec. 6.4), and an explicit training-seed variation
band for residual ML stochasticity (Ref. [1], Sec. 4).
Production uses n = 5 iterations. Huang et al. [5] select 45 iterations for OmniFold-family
methods (20 for their binned benchmark) using a binned χ2 against known mock-data truth, a
criterion they note is infeasible on real data. We do not adopt that recommendation. Their mock
data injects a large prior–data shape discrepancy through an RPA-modified quasielastic model,
requiring many iterations. The simulation prior already describes the measured shapes well (Ref. [1],
Sec. 2.5); the correction the data impose on it is dominantly one of normalization. Cross-experiment
production also typically uses ∼5 iterations [6]. Our n = 5 is instead justified empirically: for
the exact-split cross-check, the total cross section agrees to within 0.026 % between five and ten
iterations (Ref. [1], Sec. 5), and the unfolded result reproduces an independently published binned
measurement of the same data (Ref. [1], Sec. 6).
Binned purity subtraction (Sec. S4) is the only step that uses analysis binning before final
histogramming. Each data event in reconstructed bin b receives the purity weight wpur (b) =
max(0, Ndata (b) − Nbkg (b))/Ndata (b). Injecting background simulation on the measured side with
negative weight gives a fully unbinned correction with the same bin-integrated estimand. Its agreement
with the binned correction is quantified on the 2D measurement below. It is the continuous version
of the purity weight recommended for nontrivial backgrounds in Ref. [6], and the natural treatment
for the larger backgrounds of the extended-fiducial analysis.

Equations (S3) and (S4) differ as functions of x. A bin-constant rescaling reproduces D(x) − B(x)

The floor. Where D − B < 0, binned purity floors the correction at zero through max(0, ·). The
unbinned classifier’s bounded output p ∈ (0, 1) forces r = p/(1 − p) ≥ 0. Non-negativity is structural,
while the value at the floor follows from the bounded output and ordinary regularization rather than
from a minimizer of the fit (Ref. [1], Sec. C.2). Locally negative class-1 mass makes the fit ill-posed
and calls for a positive-target prescription. We apply the “Stay Positive” refinement of Ref. [7], then
explicitly zero-clip the learned refinement factor before step-1 training. Agreement between plain
injection and clipped refinement depends on the classifier.
Refined negative-weight subtraction. Refinement used the unfold’s deterministic GBDT
configuration, independent injection and subtraction halves of background simulation, and bootstrap
replicas that individually resampled every pseudo-event and refit refinement. It preserves the signed
total to 3 × 10−6 and clips 0.01 % to 0.05 % of events. Corner bias falls from 1.66 % to 0.24 %,
but residuals in some joint cells fail the nominal closure criterion. The criterion allows none of
the 153 reported quantities a mean residual beyond 3.6 standard errors of the mean (|t| > 3.6,
the two-sided 5% level, Bonferroni-corrected over the 153), or beyond half its bootstrap standard
deviation with |t| > 3. The data difference’s sign pattern matches simulated purity bias at an order
of magnitude smaller size; the difference is method sensitivity, not a measured bias. With either
treatment, the estimator does not follow generator-sized Eavail shape changes. No coverage test
was run. Reducing one bias does not itself qualify an uncertainty, so this result does not close the
revisit of the background treatment of the configuration-(a) five-dimensional central value (Ref. [1],
Sec. C.6).

The reported five-observable covariance

The reported covariance is Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D , with Ctotal = Ce + Cmodel ; the run-period block
CPL,5D is described in Sec. S5. Ce sums the statistical bootstrap of configuration (e), 50 replicas,
each of which refits the whole unfolding with its own rounding-level perturbation of the inputs; the
PPFX flux universes, weighted by the neutrino–electron and inverse-muon-decay constraint; the
interaction-model and detector bands, which reweight simulated events; the lateral bands, which
shift reconstructed kinematics (Ref. [1], Sec. 4); the normalization; and the training-seed block. With
full-sample binning and deterministic training, the unfolds at the other seeds are bitwise equal to the
central one, so the measured training-seed block is zero in every cell. Table S4 gives each group’s
per-cell relative σ.
For Cmodel , the prior is reweighted in turn by the ratio of GENIE + MEC, NuWro or GiBUU to
GENIE CV, and the data are unfolded again. Each generator’s shift is the relative change of the
data result, and Cmodel is the unscaled sum of their outer products. It is generator-shape model
dependence, not a calibrated bound; its coverage at truths held out of its construction has not
been validated. The GiBUU reweighting is applied only below p∥ = 10 GeV/c; above it the prior
is unchanged. Between p∥ = 6 and 10 GeV/c, the GiBUU sample lacks the contribution from
Eν > 20 GeV, which is at least 4.9–5.3% of the cross section there in the GENIE CV and NuWro
samples (which stop at Eν = 50 GeV). The GiBUU shift contributes 0.890 of the trace of Cmodel , the
NuWro shift 0.095 and the GENIE + MEC shift 0.014. In every cell, 77–100% of the prior weight
lies next to a discontinuity larger than 0.1 in the reweighting ratio, created by the ratio’s statistical
gate or by a cut; the shifts are used unmodified.
For Cresp , the simulation’s reconstructed recoil energy is scaled by one standard deviation
separately for four deposit categories: protons, mesons, electromagnetic deposits and other deposits.
Cresp sums the covariance contributions of the four resulting shifts of configuration (e)’s data result.

It does not cover neutron or muon deposits, the effects of particle species, energy dependence and
resolution beyond a scale, the null distributions of the joint tests, the configuration-(a) products or
the two-, three- and four-dimensional results. The median per-cell relative σ of Cresp is 2.14%, and
adding Cresp to Ctotal raises the per-cell σ by a factor of 1.011–1.220 (median 1.048). A cross-talk
term, reported beside Cresp and not summed into it, has a median per-cell relative σ of 0.36%. Of
the trace of (Ctotal + Cresp )/f f T , Ctotal contributes 0.9262 and the four deposit categories 0.0168,
0.0436, 0.0071 and 0.0062.

and 1N–1P), each over its own simulated truth (the double ratio), differ from one another by more
than the scatter of their bootstrap replicas. The offsets of the twelve playlists differ between the
three period groups by more than expected from their playlist-to-playlist scatter. The cause is not
known, and these data cannot establish which period, if any, is right. As for the two-dimensional
result (Sec. S6, Ref. [1], Table 3), the block CPL,5D is built from these double ratios with no reference
period: each group’s offset from the exposure-weighted mean of the three, applied to the central
value, with a reliability-weight factor for the weighted spread. It has rank 2 and is not debiased for
the replica noise it contains. With three groups its own size is poorly determined: the 68 % interval
on the ratio of its true to its estimated σ is 0.73–2.56, and in cells R00, R08 it is dominated by that
noise.
Each of the twelve playlists was also unfolded on its own, with its own simulation and bootstrap
replicas, in a study whose tests, bounds and readings were fixed before any playlist result was read.
A playlist’s offset is its double ratio on the sixteen cells relative to the exposure-weighted mean
of the twelve. The statistic F of a division of the playlists into groups is the share of the offsets’
exposure-weighted squared size, measured with the inverse of the relative Ctotal + Cresp , that the
groups’ exposure-weighted mean offsets carry. For the three run-period groups F = 0.70, the largest
of all 9,240 divisions of the twelve playlists into groups of six, three and three, so the permutation
p-value is 1/9,240 = 1.08 × 10−4 , the smallest the test can give. The permutation is approximate:
the playlists differ in exposure and hence in noise, so their labels are not exchangeable, and the
test’s validity rests on a simulated null. In each of 2,000 simulated datasets the playlists’ offsets
are drawn independently from one covariance, estimated from the twelve playlists and scaled by 0,
0.5, 1 or 2, with each playlist’s own bootstrap and simulation noise added; the share of datasets
with p < 0.05 is 0.0395–0.0565 over the four scales, within the bound of 0.0646. For an alternative
chronological division into groups of five, two, two and three playlists (1A–1E, 1F and 1G, 1L and
1M, and 1N–1P), the same statistic gives p = 5.17 × 10−3 over its 83,160 divisions; this comparison
is descriptive.
The block was chosen after the period differences had been seen. Before it was added, an audit
examined its overlap with the existing blocks, especially the flux and detector-response ones; its noise
treatment, with bootstrap replicas of each period; why its cell pattern differs from the projection
of the two-dimensional period offsets, although their sizes agree; and the stability of its rank-two

construction. The audit’s stopping condition was fixed before any of its numbers were read, and
it did not fire. The addition therefore followed the data. Passing the audit establishes neither the
block’s size nor the absence of overlap: descriptively, one detector-response pair, a pion-interaction
band with the meson response shift, reproduces one period’s offset pattern within noise and part of
the other two. Built from the observed differences, the block contains them by construction, and its
size and coverage have not been independently validated. The per-cell uncertainties, the fake-data
tests and the generator comparison are given here with and without it.

S6

The two-dimensional covariance

The nominal covariance is the block sum C = Csyst + Cstat + CML + CPL , which assumes the
blocks are independent and the unfolding responds linearly to simultaneous perturbations, so that
per-band covariances add. The unified throw tests linearity directly (Ref. [1], App. A.2.4; Ref. [1],
Sec. 9.6) by perturbing all reweightable bands and the flux universes simultaneously in each event
and re-unfolding jointly. The procedure interpolates asymmetrically between actual −1σ and +1σ
event-weight endpoints, fixes the estimator seed for every throw, and centers the joint covariance
on the throw mean. That mean’s displacement from CV is stored separately as a per-bin vector
and its norm; per-band endpoint displacements are not stored. The matched block-sum comparator
re-unfolds both endpoints of each band using MAT’s universe-mean centering and biased 1/N
normalization. A single-throw superposition probe is insufficient to test nonlinear response. No
scalar run-to-run jitter floor is subtracted. The configuration-(a) 5D result uses the strict joint
ensemble and matched-endpoint comparator.
The run-period block CPL is an uncertainty assigned for an observed inconsistency between run
periods whose cause is unknown; it is not a measured systematic uncertainty from an identified
source. Data taken in different run periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties.
After unfolding, the double ratio (data over simulated truth) of the data-taking periods (playlists)
1G–1M to the earliest periods, 1A–1F, has a p∥ step of (+6.89 ± 0.46)% at 8 GeV/c. Diluted into
the combined result, the raw shift of those playlists is small in each bin: every marginal z is below
0.7. Its p∥ χ2 against the earlier 2D budget is nevertheless 34.4, where noise would give 2.0. These
data cannot establish which period, if any, is correct.
The block is built from separate seed-42 unfolds of three run periods, the playlist groups 1A–1F,
1G–1M and 1N–1P, each unfolded through its own simulated detector response. The block uses each
period’s unfolded result over that period’s own simulated truth. Each period is compared with the
exposure-weighted mean of the three, so no reference period is needed; the spread, applied to the
central value, gives the block. It is added after the other blocks. It has rank two, and with three
period groups its own size is poorly determined. Its per-bin median fractional standard deviation
is 2.09 % (84th percentile 3.34 %, maximum 6.48 %); it is 1.17 % to 3.36 % on the p∥ projections
and 1.97 % on the integrated cross section. Added to the other blocks, it raises the per-bin σ by
a median of 5.8 % (Ref. [1], Table 3). Taking 1A–1F as the reference would give a smaller block
(per-bin median 1.38 %, integral 1.09 %).
The block’s interpretation is limited by the small number of period groups, by possible shrinkage
from the regularization, and by the statistical and training noise it retains. Because the block is
built from the observed differences among the periods, it contains them by construction; this carries
no information about its coverage, that is, about whether its size is right. For the same reason, the
double-ratio χ2 against any total that contains the block is bounded whatever the data, so it does
not test the block. The raw 1G–1M p∥ χ2 is 34.8 against the other blocks alone and 2.59 with the
block added; these values indicate sensitivity only.

S7

Joint tests: grid, support rule, statistics, calibration and conditions

For each of five predictions G (the MINERvA Tune v1, GENIE 2.12.10 without and with MEC,
NuWro 21.09 and GiBUU 2019; Ref. [1], Sec. 8.5), the null H0 (G) is that the true cross section
equals G on every cell of a fine five-dimensional grid. Below that grid, the null truth has the
MINERvA Tune v1 simulation’s event composition, varied in each pseudo-experiment by the drawn
interaction-model universes. Every fine-cell integral is held fixed in that variation. The background
is the Tune v1 simulation’s, with its uncertainty bands. This hybrid null is the hypothesis the
calibration simulates. For the Tune v1, which is the analysis simulation itself, no external prediction
is combined with the Tune v1 composition. Three requirements are distinct. The hybrid null is
defined exactly on the finite grid, and no convergence is needed to define it. Whether it represents the
physical generator prediction depends on condition (i) (Sec. S7). Whether the calibration reproduces
the analysis of the data is calibration fidelity (conditions (ii)–(iv)). Each prediction is tested in
total (rate and shape together) and in shape alone: 10 tests. The fine grid is the analysis binning of
Sec. S3, 14 × 16 × 7 × 7 × 6 bins in (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ); the test cells below are its integrals.
The tests are evaluated on a coarse 35 grid in (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ) with edges pT ∈
[0, 0.55, 0.85, 4.5] GeV/c, p∥ ∈ [1.5, 3.5, 6, 60] GeV/c, Eavail ∈ [0, 0.4, 1.5, 100] GeV, q3 ∈
[0, 1.2, 2, 100] GeV and W ∈ [0, 1.4, 2.2, 100] GeV. Each inner edge is the fine-grid edge
nearest a tertile of the weighted simulated truth distribution of the signal sample in that coordinate,
so the cells keep physical bin widths. That distribution is taken from the development simulation.
The tests are restricted to the 109 supported cells (J). For GiBUU, whose sample lacks high-energy
neutrinos, the domain is the 72 cells with p∥ < 6 GeV/c. The grid specifies a finite hybrid hypothesis
and a reporting statistic. It does not turn OmniFold’s event-level training into binned unfolding.
Two statistics are formed from the unfolded cell values F and the prediction µG . The total
(rate-and-shape) statistic is Ttot = r⊤ Σ−1 r with r = F − µG . The shape statistic Tsh has the same
form on the normalized cell fractions, with the normalization Jacobian applied to Σ and one cell
dropped. The metric Σ = V + diag Var(µG ) adds the prediction’s own finite-sample variance to V .
Here V is a Ledoit–Wolf-shrunk covariance [8] of 194 pilot pseudo-experiments at the Tune v1 null.
The metric defines how hypotheses are compared, and so it affects power. The null calibration below,
not the metric, sets each test’s size.
The prediction variance is diagonal because each simulated event of a prediction falls in one cell.
The pilot’s seeds are disjoint from those of the calibration, but it reuses the same simulated events
and omitted the MINOS-efficiency band. The diagonal of V matches the calibration’s null variances
(median ratio 1.00). The shrinkage, of intensity 0.015, floors about 80 of the 109 nearly degenerate
correlation directions. At the Tune v1 null the statistic therefore has a whitened trace of about 47
rather than 109. Since V was fixed before the calibration, this affects power only (Ref. [1], Sec. 11.5).
The power at other shrinkage intensities has not been measured.
The null distribution of each statistic is obtained from pseudo-experiments that run the full
analysis chain at the null truth, using the joint-test estimator (Ref. [1], Sec. 9.3). That estimator
is deterministic LightGBM OmniFold with five iterations and the negative-weight background
treatment, whose refinement classifier has 400 trees and 31 leaves. Each pseudo-experiment
• draws Poisson pseudo-data from one half of the simulation together with an independent
background source, refits the background-refinement classifier, and unfolds with the estimator
trained on the other half;
• draws one of 100 flux universes and every one of the 34 interaction-model bands at one of its
universes;

• draws the weight-based detector bands (MINOS muon-matching efficiency and the GEANT4
neutron, pion and proton interaction reweights) and the five muon and beam-angle lateral
bands through a linear surrogate;
• draws a 1.4 % normalization, the data’s rounding noise (taken from 20 real-data re-unfolds
with rounding-level input changes), and the prediction’s finite-sample residual.
The data are unfolded once, with the full simulation. The Monte Carlo p value of a test is
(k + 1)/(B + 1), where k of B null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic. This p value
is never zero. The p value on which each decision rests, the claim p value, is the largest over the
declared claim variants, which shift the null. The variants move it by an upper bound on the
simulation-size difference between the pseudo-experiments and the data unfold (condition (iv) below),
and by ±2 δfine (condition (i)). The external nulls are strongly non-central, with non-centralities λ
of 318–6344 in these units. They are non-central because the calibration reproduces the estimator’s
pull toward its prior (Ref. [1], Sec. 9.3). Each observed statistic is compared with that displaced
distribution (Ref. [1], Fig. 31).
Generative model of one pseudo-experiment. Each calibration pseudo-experiment splits the
simulation at random into two halves (Table S6) and refits the background refinement. Its truth
equals G on every fine cell that the source half populates. Cells it leaves empty lower a coarse cell
by at most 0.2 %, or about 4 % of the null standard deviation (the per-experiment spread is below
1.5 % of it). On the signal, each interaction-model band is renormalized within each fine truth cell,
so it moves only the composition within the cell, and with it the efficiency and the migrations. The
flux universe and the band universes are drawn independently, and their P
weight ratios multiply.
The muon-energy, muon-resolution, and beam-angle lateral bands enter as b zb ∆b , with ∆b from
endpoint unfoldings of the data. The detector weight bands include the GEANT4 hadron-interaction
reweights. Because the ∆b come from the observed data, the null distributions are conditional on
these data-derived inputs, which would differ in a repeated experiment. That conditioning has not
been validated.
Each decision holds under six conditions of the declared model. They are restated here, numbered
(i)–(vi) as in the test specification, and fall into three kinds.
Numerical convergence. Condition (i) concerns the null truth below the fine grid. There it has
the Tune v1 composition of the hypothesis definition (Sec. S7), varied by the drawn interaction-model
universes, with every fine-cell integral held fixed at that of G, including the finite-sample fluctuations
of G’s simulation. The fine-grid convergence check has not shown convergence. In the L2 norm of the
per-cell relative changes, the last refinement, from a twofold-merged grid to the fine grid, is 0.50–0.86
of the whole change from the coarse test grid to the fine grid. In that norm the successive refinement
changes are therefore not shown to shrink, and the residual below the fine grid is unmeasured and
potentially material. The design assumes that each twofold refinement of the grid multiplies the
refinement change, measured in a proxy of the test metric, by a factor of 0.45–0.76. This factor
comes from the review of the design, and its derivation is not given. If the change kept shrinking
geometrically at that rate, the residual would be about one to three times the last refinement change,
δfine . Here δfine is the change of the noise-free unfolded result when the null truth is refined from the
twofold-merged grid to the fine grid. That estimate sets the claim variants at ±2 δfine , the central
extrapolated estimate, and the robustness variants, for reporting only, at ±3 δfine . The extrapolation
assumes geometric convergence. The measured L2 ratios also allow slower rates and larger residuals.

modelling choice of this kind.

S8

Comparisons with binned iterative Bayesian unfolding

The paper’s three comparisons with a binned iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU) were each declared,
with their statistic, null and readings, before they ran. IBU is a cross-check in all three: it enters
neither the reported result nor its covariance.
The sixteen-cell comparison. The IBU is D’Agostini’s, with the background template folded in
as a known additive term in reconstructed space. It uses the same data, simulation, background
template and weight-only systematic universes as the joint-test estimator. Its reconstructed bins
are the fine five-observable cells inside the reconstruction window, its truth grid is the coarse grid
whose unions are the cells R00–R15, its prior is the simulation’s own truth, and it runs at five
iterations (iterations 1–4, 10 and 20 are printed and read by nothing). Each of its 50 bootstrap
replicas and each systematic universe is paired with the joint-test estimator’s product made from the
same draw or the same weights. The difference ∆ = fIBU − f on the sixteen cells has the covariance

The two-dimensional comparisons. The second comparison runs a D’Agostini IBU (RooUnfold’s

and it is empirical: leave-one-out over the N replicas, each replica’s T computed about the mean

and its maximum, so “differ” (above the maximum) means that a difference is detectable at this
noise, not that it is large. For the IBU comparison the descriptive ratio ρ is the IBU result’s χ2 /ndf

result, computed on each paired replica as well as on the data.

S9

Designs of the coverage and fake-data tests

This test asks whether a 2D statistical band covers a fixed truth at its nominal rate. The test scored
the completeness-divided band, the first-built statistical band, quoted when the test ran, whose
replicas divide by a bootstrapped completeness. The undivided band is the rebuilt band, without the
division, since superseded. The quoted statistical block (Ref. [1], Sec. 4.2) is a separate ensemble of
300 replicas at the central value’s training seed. The design, criteria and positive control of the test
were specified before any toy was generated (Ref. [1], Sec. 5.2).

Each toy draws pseudo-data event by event as Poisson counts with means equal to the simulated
reconstructed weights. It resamples the simulation with the per-event Poisson(1) bootstrap of the
statistical band and unfolds with that band’s estimator, LightGBM with five iterations at a fixed
training seed, the learner of the quoted central value at a different seed (Sec. S4). The reference T is
the unfluctuated simulated truth, identical in every toy. The no-fluctuation configuration reproduces
both the production closure output and T exactly.
In reported bin b of toy t, ztb = (Utb − Tb )/(rb Tb ), where rb is the production band’s relative
spread. Coverage Ck is the fraction of all (toy, bin) pairs with |z| ≤ k, with intervals from resampling
whole toys. The band counts as nominal if its ratio to the true scatter lies in [0.9, 1.1], which gives
the windows C1 ∈ [0.632, 0.729] and C2 ∈ [0.928, 0.972]. A failure at the interim analysis of 100 toys,
with 99.5% intervals, stops the study.
Scope. The test concerns the statistical band alone and changes no central value or quoted
uncertainty. It tests the LightGBM statistical estimator at the band’s training seed (seed 1), not
at the quoted central value’s seed 42, and does not address systematic, total or five-dimensional
coverage. Its simulation-side scatter reuses the resampling that built the band, so only the data-side
fluctuation is tested by an independent mechanism. Because T equals the prior, the test cannot
detect regularization bias toward the prior.
The fake-data tests compare the estimator’s bias at truths other than the simulation with the
reported covariance. Pseudo-data are generated at 12 truths and unfolded with configuration (e).
The mean unfolded result is compared with each truth on the 16 cells, through χ2 with 16 degrees
of freedom and the per-cell pull z. The truths are the simulation’s prior; the five hybrid truths of
the generator tests (Ref. [1], Sec. 11), each a prediction on the fine grid; the historical shapes W1,
W2 and W3 of Ref. [1], Sec. 9.4; a GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail shape; a noise-free closure at the NuWro
shape; and a synthetic tilt of the q3 distribution at fixed (Eavail , W ). A truth from a Cmodel generator
is compared with the covariance without that generator’s shift. A truth is listed as failing when
p < 0.01 or when any cell has |z| > 2; these criteria were fixed before any of the tests was graded
against them. These are not held-out truths, and the detector response is not probed. Ref. [1],
Table 17 gives each truth under the reported covariance, without CPL,5D , and under Ctotal alone.
The bias of each truth is the same in all three; only the covariance differs.
The truths form three groups, evaluated separately, not pooled: the 8 truths from the Cmodel
generators; the other truths, the GENIE CV hybrid and the q3 tilt; and the prior truths, the
simulation prior and the Tune v1 hybrid. Under the reported covariance, the Cmodel -generator truths
have 2 of 128 cells with |z| > 2 (Gaussian reference 5.82) and 1 of 8 truths with p < 0.01 (reference
0.08); the other truths 0 of 32 and 1 of 2; and the prior truths 0 of 32 and 0 of 2. The Gaussian
references are context only, because the bias is deterministic.
Two truths are held out of the analysis: the GENIE v3 tunes G18_10a_02_11b and AR23_20i_00_000, generated for this test. Each tune enters as its normalized shape ratio to GENIE CV
on the fine grid, constructed as the reweighting ratios of the Cmodel shifts are. Where the tune’s
sample fails the ratio’s statistical gate, the ratio is pooled over a coarser cell or set to one. The
ratio is applied to the simulated truth, under the simulation’s detector response. At each tune,
20 Poisson pseudo-experiments on half the simulation and a noise-free closure are tested against
the full reported covariance, as truths with no shift in Cmodel , in a fourth group, not pooled with
the others. The bias meets both criteria in all four records, the pseudo-experiment mean and the
closure at each tune. The mean of the pseudo-experiments gives χ2 = 9.26 (p = 0.90; largest |z|
0.61, in R11) at G18_10a_02_11b and 14.06 (p = 0.59; 0.61, in R07) at AR23_20i_00_000; the
closures give 9.14 (p = 0.91; 0.60, in R11) and 13.43 (p = 0.64; 0.57, in R07). In every cell of the
four records, the bias is below 0.62 of the cell’s reported σ; in cell R11 at G18_10a_02_11b, the
bias lies below the reported uncertainty only with Cmodel included. This group has 0 of 64 cells

with |z| > 2 (Gaussian reference 2.91) and 0 of 4 records with p < 0.01 (reference 0.04). The four
records are the pseudo-experiment and closure results for two truths, so the Gaussian references,
which count them as independent, are context only.
