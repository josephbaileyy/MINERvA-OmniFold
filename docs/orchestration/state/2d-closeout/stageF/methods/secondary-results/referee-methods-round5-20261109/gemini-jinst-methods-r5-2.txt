# REFEREE REPORT

**Journal:** Journal of Instrumentation (JINST)  
**Manuscript Title:** [Methods and validation study of five-observable unbinned OmniFold unfolding on MINERvA medium-energy data]  
**Subject Area:** Neutrino cross-section measurements, multidimensional unfolding, machine learning in instrumentation and data analysis  

---

### 1. Summary of What the Paper Claims and Does

The manuscript presents a methods and validation study of multidimensional, unbinned unfolding using the machine-learning algorithm OmniFold, applied to the MINERvA medium-energy $\nu_\mu$ charged-current open data release. The authors perform a simultaneous unfolding in five kinematic observables ($p_T, p_\parallel, E_\text{avail}, q_3, W$) and compare the two-dimensional $(p_T, p_\parallel)$ projection against the published MINERvA double-differential cross section, finding agreement in total normalization within ~1% but an unexplained, statistically significant shape discrepancy ($\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ across 205 bins) spanning multiple covariance eigenmodes. Across an extensive validation program, the authors test the five-observable estimator on 16 coarse kinematic cells, observing that the unfolding fails pre-declared numerical stability criteria under floating-point input rounding (shifting results by up to $2.6\sigma_\text{stat}$ due to float32 ties in decision-tree splitting), retains prior dependence that exceeds pre-declared bias thresholds on generator-sized truth changes (GiBUU hybrid truth and synthetic $q_3$ tilt), and exhibits an unexplained ~7% run-period discrepancy across data-taking playlists. Crucially, because the two-dimensional covariance severely under-covers at held-out generator tunes and the declared five-observable coverage test was aborted prior to execution due to test-design failures, the authors refrain from claiming a physics measurement, presenting their 16-cell result strictly as an uncalibrated, illustrative demonstration of the method.

---

### 2. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Major Revision**

This manuscript represents an unusually candid, rigorous, and technically sophisticated examination of the pitfalls and limitations of unbinned machine-learning unfolding when applied to complex, high-dimensional neutrino scattering data. While the prevailing literature on machine-learning unfolding often showcases idealized closures on synthetic data, this work performs a vital service to the experimental neutrino community by demonstrating how unbinned reweighting estimators can fail in realistic settings—specifically regarding prior retention, numerical instability from floating-point binning ties, unvalidated interval coverage, and sensitivity to unmodeled, time-dependent detector/flux drifts. However, in its present form, the paper suffers from a critical structural conflict: it repeatedly emphasizes that the 16-cell result "is therefore not a measurement" and gives "illustrative output, not for fitting or for constraining models" (Sec. V, Appendix A), yet it tabulates cell cross sections (Table III), a full correlation matrix (Table V), and generator $\chi^2$ values (Table VI) showing dramatic rejections of standard models (e.g., GENIE+MEC $\chi^2 = 229$ for 16 ndf) based on an admittedly uncalibrated covariance. Furthermore, the declared five-observable coverage test completely collapsed due to flawed test-gate design, the baseline estimator remains vulnerable to machine-precision rounding instabilities despite a viable ensemble mitigation being identified, and the text leans far too heavily on an inaccessible companion note (Ref. [14]). To be suitable for publication in JINST, the manuscript requires a major revision to either resolve these methodological failures or appropriately reframe the presentation so that unvalidated cross sections cannot be extracted and misused by the neutrino phenomenology community.

---

### 3. Major Issues

#### 1. Presentation of Unvalidated Cross Sections and Misleading Generator Comparisons
* **What is wrong or missing:** The authors state clearly in the abstract, Sec. I, Sec. V, and Appendix A that the 16-cell cross-section output is "illustrative" and "not for fitting or for constraining models." Nevertheless, Appendix A provides exact numerical cross sections (Table III), an explicit $16\times 16$ correlation matrix (Table V), and formal $\chi^2$ goodness-of-fit values comparing five generator predictions against the data (Table VI), reporting rejections with $\chi^2$ values between 48 and 262.
* **Why it matters physically:** In neutrino physics, published tables of cross sections and covariances are routinely harvested for global event-generator tuning (e.g., in GENIE, GiBUU, NuWro, NEUT) and nuclear-model fits. Publishing these numbers—when the authors know that coverage is unvalidated, that the estimator retains significant prior bias on non-nominal truths (e.g., GiBUU hybrid truth bias yields $\chi^2 = 171$, $p < 10^{-27}$, with pulls $|z| > 4.5$), and that $C_\text{model}$ is an uncalibrated bound constructed from the very models being evaluated—will lead to erroneous physics conclusions in the wider community.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must either remove the numerical cross sections and generator $\chi^2$ values from the manuscript entirely or convert them strictly into qualitative diagnostic ratio figures. If numerical tables are retained in an appendix for algorithmic benchmarking, every table caption, figure, and machine-readable data file must carry an unequivocal warning label ("UNVALIDATED METHOD BENCHMARK: NOT FOR MODEL CONSTRAINTS"). Furthermore, Table VI should be removed or thoroughly qualified to explain that these $\chi^2$ values are descriptive metrics of method discrepancy, not physical exclusions of physics models.

#### 2. Complete Failure and Non-Execution of the Five-Observable Coverage Test
* **What is wrong or missing:** The manuscript acknowledges that "the coverage of the reported uncertainty intervals has not been validated" (Abstract) and that "no reported interval has validated coverage" (Sec. VI A). The declared five-observable coverage test was aborted at a preliminary sanity gate because the test compared "unlike objects" (fixed-truth pseudo-experiments moving only response versus full-universe reported blocks moving both prior and response). A staged redesign subsequently aborted because "892 of its 900 declared draws gave a non-positive composite denominator" from a first-order linear Taylor expansion of systematic uncertainties.
* **Why it matters physically:** For an instrumentation and measurement journal like JINST, an uncertainty interval is meaningless unless its coverage properties are understood. A measurement cannot claim an uncertainty budget of ~7.9% if the intervals cover the true value at an unknown (or deficient) rate. The two-dimensional test already demonstrated that the procedure's covariance under-covers at held-out generator tunes (0 of 300 pseudo-experiments falling in the joint confidence region; mean $\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 16.55$ and $24.17$). An uncalibrated covariance in 5D leaves the reader with no way of knowing whether the reported uncertainties are over- or under-estimated.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must design and successfully execute a viable coverage test for the five-observable estimator, even if evaluated on a restricted domain, a coarser subset of cells, or under a well-behaved toy model that enforces positive event weights and non-negative composite denominators. If full systematic coverage is computationally intractable or impossible due to the breakdown of linear expansion methods, the authors must explicitly demonstrate statistical coverage under controlled pseudo-experiments and honestly document where non-linear error propagation fails in high dimensions.

#### 3. Unresolved Numerical Instability Under Floating-Point Rounding
* **What is wrong or missing:** Rounding-level changes in the input data move the unfolded results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations ($2.6\sigma_\text{stat}$), causing the joint-test estimator to fail the pre-declared stability requirement (median $\le 0.3$, maximum $\le 1.0\sigma_\text{stat}$). The authors diagnosed the mechanism on a simulated pseudo-experiment: perturbations break ties among equal float32 values, causing LightGBM to assign events to different histogram bins during gradient boosting. Remarkably, an ensemble-averaged estimator (averaging 96 fits) was tested and successfully met the stability requirement (median 0.08, maximum 0.76), but the authors chose *not* to adopt it because its fake-data closure was slightly worse at a single truth (Stage 0c GiBUU) out of 17 tests.
* **Why it matters physically:** A primary requirement of scientific instrumentation and data analysis software is numerical stability. If an analysis pipeline outputs results that fluctuate by multiple standard deviations under machine-precision noise or tie-breaking of identical kinematic floats, the extracted physics depends on arbitrary computational artifacts. Retaining an unstable estimator when a viable ensemble-averaging solution exists undermines the paper's methodological validity.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must adopt a numerically stable configuration as their primary estimator. The decision to discard the 96-fit ensemble estimator based on a single failing test out of 17 should be revisited: numerical stability is a hard prerequisite for any estimator, whereas closure pull on an extreme, un-held-out generator variant can be accounted for in the systematic model uncertainty. Alternatively, the authors must resolve the float32 tie-breaking issue at the root level (e.g., casting inputs to float64, jittering ties systematically, or modifying the tree-building splitting algorithm).

#### 4. Ad-Hoc Run-Period Covariance ($C_\text{PL,5D}$) Masking Experimental/Detector Drifts
* **What is wrong or missing:** The data exhibit an unexplained ~7% step in $p_\parallel$ (around 8 GeV/c) between run periods (playlists 1G–1M vs 1A–1F) that is already present at the reconstruction level (+6.10%) and persists after unfolding (+(6.94 ± 0.41)%). The authors incorporate this discrepancy by adding an ad-hoc, rank-2 covariance block $C_\text{PL,5D}$ constructed post hoc from the observed double ratios of the three run-period groups. The authors admit that this block is poorly determined (68% CI on its scale is 0.73–2.56), contains uncorrected bootstrap replica noise, and passes its own consistency check by construction.
* **Why it matters physically:** In neutrino beam experiments, a 7% step in reconstructed muon momentum distributions between run periods points to an unmodeled physical or instrumental change—such as horn current drifts, target degradation, beam alignment variations, scintillator aging, or MINOS tracking efficiency shifts. Sweeping an unresolved time-dependent detector or beam systematic into an empirical rank-2 covariance block does not solve the instrumentation problem. It obscures whether OmniFold itself is robust against such drifts or whether it amplifies them.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must investigate the experimental origin of the playlist discrepancy (e.g., checking neutrino energy spectra, MINOS muon matching efficiency across run periods, or beam-line monitoring). If the open-data release lacks the auxiliary metadata required to diagnose the issue, the authors must state clearly that data quality defects in the underlying sample preclude complete validation across run periods, rather than treating $C_\text{PL,5D}$ as a standard systematic uncertainty. The impact of unfolding each run-period group independently should be transparently displayed.

#### 5. Unresolved Shape Difference in the Two-Dimensional Benchmark
* **What is wrong or missing:** When benchmarked against the published MINERvA 2D measurement [13], the OmniFold unfolding reproduces the integrated cross section to within 1% (ratio 1.010), but yields a highly significant shape discrepancy ($\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ across 205 bins, with shape $\chi^2 = 465$). The authors find that the discrepancy spans both poorly determined directions and the 73 best-determined directions of the published covariance ($\chi^2/205 = 0.66$ in those directions alone). Crucially, a binned Iterative Bayesian Unfolding (IBU) of the same inputs also differs from OmniFold ($T = 382.8$), but the localization test is "unresolved" ($\rho = 0.452$ with a 90% interval of 0.701–1.128). The manuscript concludes: "The source of the shape difference has not been identified."
* **Why it matters physically:** The central thesis of unbinned unfolding in neutrino physics is that it eliminates binning artifacts while faithfully recovering multidimensional distributions. If OmniFold applied to real data departs from the established binned IBU and published results across well-constrained eigenmodes, and the authors cannot determine whether the discrepancy originates from the machine-learning architecture, regularization bias, background subtraction, or input ntuple processing, the method cannot yet be recommended as a reliable alternative to binned unfolding.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must isolate the origin of this shape difference. They should test whether the difference is driven by: (i) the regularizing effect of finite iterations ($n=5$) versus tree capacity; (ii) the unbinned negative-weight background refinement versus binned purity subtraction; (iii) the handling of events failing reconstruction ("misses"); or (iv) differences between the pre-selected open-data AnaTuples and the original ntuples used in Ref. [13].

#### 6. Over-Reliance on Inaccessible Companion Material (Ref. [14])
* **What is wrong or missing:** The manuscript cites Ref. [14] (an internal or unpublished companion analysis note) dozens of times for essential technical details, including the full validation record, background closure tests, systematic construction, test-gate failure mechanisms, and mathematical definitions of test statistics. 
* **Why it matters methodologically:** JINST requires published papers to be self-contained and reproducible. Neither the referee nor future readers have access to Ref. [14]. When critical methodological justifications (such as why hyperparameter tuning was frozen or why specific fail-closed rules fired) are outsourced to an unreviewed, private document, the manuscript fails JINST's archival standards.
* **What the authors must do:** All essential definitions, derivation of test criteria, and critical diagnostic figures from Ref. [14] must be incorporated directly into the manuscript’s Supplemental Material, or Ref. [14] must be deposited simultaneously on an open repository (such as arXiv or Zenodo) with a permanent DOI.

### 4. Evaluation of Stated Limitations and Qualifications

The manuscript is commendable for its extensive catalog of qualifications. Below is a systematic assessment of each limitation noted in the paper, categorized as requested:

#### 1. Unvalidated Coverage
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reasoning & Required Analysis:** A statistical measurement method cannot publish confidence intervals without demonstrating their coverage properties. The fact that the 2D covariance severely under-covers at held-out tunes and 5D coverage was never evaluated means the uncertainty intervals have unknown statistical meaning. The authors must carry out a tractable coverage study—at minimum testing coverage under nominal truth and under controlled smooth deformations—to quantify the true interval containment rate.

#### 2. The Run-Period Uncertainty ($C_\text{PL,5D}$)
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reasoning & Required Analysis:** Absorbing an unexplained 7% step in $p_\parallel$ into an empirical, rank-2 covariance matrix constructed from only three points (with an error scale uncertain by a factor of three) is an ad-hoc fix. The authors should analyze the playlist dependence of the input data to identify whether the drift originates from detector calibration (e.g., MINOS matching), beam composition, or background normalization. If unresolved, the analysis should present the period groups separately rather than convolving an unknown instrumental shift into an unvalidated covariance block.

#### 3. Fake-Data Bias Exceeding Pre-Declared Thresholds (GiBUU Hybrid Truth and Synthetic $q_3$ Tilt)
* **Classification:** **(b) Acceptable, honestly stated limitation.**
* **Reasoning:** In a methods paper, discovering that OmniFold regularized with 5 iterations retains a strong memory of its prior when exposed to large hadronic or momentum-transfer deformations (e.g., GiBUU hybrid truth giving $p < 10^{-27}$, $|z| > 2$ in R02 and R06; $q_3$ tilt giving $p < 10^{-5}$) is an important, high-value scientific finding. It alerts the community that unbinned reweighting classifiers cannot magically pull data across large physics model gaps without sufficient iterations, unconstrained capacity, or tailored priors.

#### 4. The Stopped Five-Observable Coverage Test
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reasoning & Required Analysis:** The stopping of the declared test was caused by two analytical design flaws: (i) setting a preliminary gate that compared unlike objects (pseudo-experiments varying response only versus full-universe blocks varying prior and response); and (ii) a first-order uncertainty composition that collapsed into negative denominators in 892 of 900 draws. This is not a fundamental limitation of the physical data, but a failure of test design. The authors should correct the mathematical formulation of the test (e.g., by using exact log-normal or non-negative parameterizations for nuisance draws) and execute the test to completion.

#### 5. Low-Stability Cells (R02, R06, R10, R15)
* **Classification:** **(b) Acceptable, honestly stated limitation.**
* **Reasoning:** In high-dimensional neutrino kinematics, severe detector smearing and coarse energy resolution in hadronic calorimetry inevitably cause some phase-space cells to suffer from low reconstruction stability (0.16–0.25). The authors honestly flag these cells (Table III) and demonstrate that these exact cells coincide with the large fake-data biases. This is standard, well-documented physics behavior that JINST readers expect to see analyzed.

#### 6. Target Differences (Carbon vs. CH in NuWro and GiBUU Comparisons)
* **Classification:** **(b) Acceptable, honestly stated limitation.**
* **Reasoning:** External generator predictions often exist only on pure nuclear targets (like $^{12}\text{C}$), whereas MINERvA's tracker is scintillator (CH). The authors explicitly correct for hydrogen with a 1/13 free-proton weighting, quantify the remaining target discrepancy (~8.3% for $W < 1.1$ GeV in GENIE), and detail the impact on $\chi^2$ in Table VII. This is an honestly stated, standard limitation when dealing with legacy generator outputs.

#### 7. Definitional Departures ($E_\text{avail}$ 135 MeV Constant, Beam vs. Neutrino Axis, Nucleon Mass in $W$)
* **Classification:** **(b) Acceptable, honestly stated limitation.**
* **Reasoning:** The authors rigorously quantify the impact of using 135 MeV instead of the charged-pion mass (139.57 MeV), showing it shifts cell cross sections by at most $0.19\sigma$ (and $0.42\sigma$ coherently). Similarly, measuring muon angles relative to the beam axis rather than the neutrino axis shifts predictions by $< 0.07\sigma$. Because these definitions are applied consistently across data, simulation, and external predictions, they represent fully acceptable, documented conventions.

#### 8. Sensitivity to Input Rounding (Float32 Ties)
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reasoning & Required Analysis:** Allowing machine-level rounding to shift unfolded results by up to $2.6\sigma_\text{stat}$ violates fundamental computational stability requirements. The authors already proved that an ensemble-averaged estimator (96 fits) resolves this issue. The authors must replace the single-fit estimator with the ensemble estimator or resolve the float32 tie-breaking behavior before publication.

#### 9. Kinematic Redundancy of the Five Observables ($W$ Fixed by $p_T, p_\parallel, q_3$)
* **Classification:** **(b) Acceptable, honestly stated limitation.**
* **Reasoning:** The authors openly explain that $W_\text{reco}$ is an algebraic function of $(p_T, p_\parallel, q_3)_\text{reco}$ and calorimetric $q_0$, meaning the five inputs populate a lower-dimensional manifold. Making this transparent is a commendable contribution to the literature, cautioning others against treating simultaneous machine-learned observables as independent degrees of freedom.

#### 10. GiBUU Phase-Space Truncation ($E_\nu < 20$ GeV) and Generator Deficiencies
* **Classification:** **(c) Detail that belongs in the companion note, not the paper.**
* **Reasoning:** The minutiae regarding GiBUU's free-proton sample failing sampling closures, its deep-inelastic scattering events violating energy conservation, and NuWro generator aborts at 50–100 GeV (footnotes to Table VI) are distracting technical sidebars that belong in the companion technical note or an appendix, rather than cluttering the main text.

### 5. Referee's Perspective on Limitations, Publishability, and Disqualifying Defects

* **Should authors resolve these limitations or move them out?**  
  The authors should **not** move out the limitations that constitute legitimate scientific findings. The discoveries that OmniFold retains prior bias under generator-sized truth deformations, that kinematic cells with low stability suffer large pulls, and that five correlated observables exhibit redundancy are the very findings that make this paper valuable to JINST readers. However, limitations that stem from *analytical or procedural incompletions*—specifically the aborted coverage test, the numerical instability under float32 rounding, and the reliance on an ad-hoc rank-2 run-period covariance—must be **resolved with further analysis**, rather than simply cataloged as admitted defeats.

* **Is the paper publishable in JINST with its current set of limitations?**  
  In its current state, **no**. The paper occupies an untenable middle ground: it provides all the trappings of a published cross-section measurement (unfolded tables, error matrices, model $\chi^2$ values), while disclaiming in the text that it is not a measurement and cannot be used to constrain models. A paper cannot publish an unvalidated, biased cross section and expect the community to ignore the numbers.

* **Is any limitation disqualifying?**  
  For a physics measurement submitted to a journal like *Physical Review D*, the lack of validated coverage, the failure of fake-data closures, and the unresolved run-period drift would be absolutely disqualifying. For JINST, as a *methods and validation study*, these issues are **not disqualifying in themselves**, because documenting how and why a method fails is within the journal's scope. What *is* currently disqualifying is publishing numerical cross-section values and generator $\chi^2$ exclusions alongside these failures. If the authors reframe the paper strictly around methodology, resolve the numerical instability, and complete a valid coverage evaluation, the work will be fully publishable.

### 6. Minor Issues and Presentation

1. **Tone and Procedural Jargon:** The manuscript frequently reads like an internal post-mortem or technical audit rather than a peer-reviewed journal article. Terms such as "fail-closed rule," "preliminary gate," "development variant," and "noise-free study" reflect the procedural mechanics of an internal analysis review. The authors should adopt a more standard scientific register.
2. **Dense Abstract:** The abstract is packed with dense, unexplained quantitative findings (e.g., "$p < 0.01$, or any cell with $|z| > 2$," "shifts by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations," "stopped at a preliminary check that... compared unlike objects"). It should be rewritten to clearly state the overarching goals, primary conclusions, and key methodological takeaways.
3. **Figure 1 Presentation:** In the top panels, the ratio plots below the distributions are difficult to read due to heavy overlap of error bands. In the bottom-left panel, the 2D bin residual map is informative, but the color scale and bin-numbering convention should be made larger and clearer.
4. **Figure 2 Diagnostics:** Figure 2 displays results from a "development variant" (100 trees, 8 leaves) and uses an "earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance" that was subsequently rejected. Showing obsolete configurations in the main text is confusing. Figure 2 should be updated to display the final reported estimator and covariance, or moved to an appendix documenting algorithm development.
5. **Figure 3 Interpretation:** The caption notes that per-bin contributions $\Delta_i (C^{-1} \Delta)_i$ to the $\chi^2$ can be negative due to off-diagonal covariance elements. A brief sentence explaining how off-diagonal anticorrelations produce these negative values in high-dimensional matrix inversions would help readers unfamiliar with generalized distances.
6. **Figure 4 and Table VI Inconsistency:** Figure 4 presents data/prediction ratios for GiBUU only on the 8 cells with $p_\parallel < 4.5$ GeV/c, while other models are shown across all 16 cells. The visual break between the 16-cell panel and 8-cell panel should be made more prominent. More importantly, Table VI quotes total $\chi^2$ values that are dominated by off-diagonal correlations, yet the text notes that removing correlations drops the $\chi^2$ from 229 to 41. This extreme sensitivity to matrix condition numbers should be discussed with caution in the caption.
7. **Table I Usability:** Table I provides an excellent roadmap of the validation program. However, the "Status" column mixes methodological outcomes ("Differ," "Flagged") with procedural outcomes ("Stopped," "Descriptive"). It would be much clearer if categorized into "Physics Finding," "Method Limitation," and "Test Aborted."
8. **Undefined Model Labels:** In Sec. IV D and Sec. IV H, generator tunes are cited by their internal GENIE strings (e.g., `AR23_20i_00_000` and `G18_10a_02_11b`) without describing what physics models they represent. A brief parenthetical sentence describing their physical ingredients (e.g., axial form factors, Valencia 2p2h, Nieves MEC) is essential for readers.
9. **Catch-Bin Labeling:** In Table III and Table S2, the kinematic upper bounds of 100 GeV (e.g., $E_\text{avail} \in [0, 100]$ GeV) represent inclusive overflow/catch bins. They should be explicitly labeled as "$> 0.8$ GeV" or "inclusive catch bin" rather than implying that the MINERvA detector reconstructs 100 GeV hadrons.
10. **Data Availability / Open Code:** The manuscript references a candidate release package of ~16 MB that has "not yet been deposited." For a methods paper in JINST, the analysis code, Python scripts, and Jupyter notebooks implementing the OmniFold training and validation tests must be deposited in a public repository (e.g., GitHub/Zenodo) upon acceptance.

---

### 7. Scores and Concluding Evaluations

SCORE: 5/10

**Main Contribution:**  
The paper provides a rigorous, highly transparent stress-test of multidimensional unbinned machine-learning unfolding on real neutrino data, revealing that unbinned reweighting estimators can suffer from severe prior retention, numerical instability under floating-point rounding, and uncalibrated coverage under realistic experimental conditions.

**Alternative Journal Assessment:**  
JINST is precisely the right venue for this manuscript because it focuses on data analysis methodology, software instrumentation, and systematic validation; a primary physics journal such as Physical Review D would reject it outright due to the absence of a validated physics measurement.
