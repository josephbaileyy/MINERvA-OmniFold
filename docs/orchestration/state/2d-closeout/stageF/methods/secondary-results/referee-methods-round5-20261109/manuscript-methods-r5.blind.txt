We test what unbinned unfolding with OmniFold delivers on neutrino data, unfolding MINERvA
medium-energy open data simultaneously in five observables: muon transverse and longitudinal
momenta, available hadronic energy, three-momentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass. The
unfolding reproduces the published muon-kinematic cross-section integral within about 1%, but differs
from it in shape, over many eigenmodes of the published covariance, for a reason not identified. A
binned iterative Bayesian unfolding of the same inputs differs from the method in two dimensions and
on the five-observable cells, without localising the shape difference. The five-observable result retains
part of its prior: fake-data studies at a GiBUU-like truth and at a synthetic momentum-transfer
tilt find residual bias exceeding uncertainty-based criteria (p < 0.01, or any cell with |z| > 2) that
were fixed before the tests were graded. The estimator is bitwise independent of its training seed,
yet rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations,
beyond a stability requirement fixed in advance. A coverage test declared in advance was stopped at
a preliminary check that, for the interaction uncertainties, compared unlike objects; the coverage of
the reported uncertainty intervals has not been validated, and the run periods differ for a cause not
known. The five-observable output on data is therefore given only as an illustration.

I.

INTRODUCTION

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their
interpretation depends on correcting detector response
and on assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned
migration matrix [1] is widely used, but its response
tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space
through classifier-based density-ratio estimation [2, 3].
This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding
possible without specifying a separate binned response
tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been
applied to collider data, for example to eight observables
in deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [4] and to twenty-four
Z+jets observables at ATLAS [5] (see Ref. [6] for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on
a public simulated T2K near-detector sample [7]. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions [8–11].
This paper is a methods and validation study of a
simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on
real neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA
medium-energy νµ data from the open-data release [12], in
the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta pT and p∥ ,
the available hadronic energy Eavail , the three-momentum
transfer q3 , and the hadronic invariant mass W . Reconstructed W is fixed by the reconstructed (pT , p∥ , q3 ), so

the five observables are simultaneous but not independent. The unfolding is benchmarked against the published
double-differential cross section d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) from the
same data [13], and the five-observable estimator is tested
for bias, numerical stability, coverage, and run-period
consistency.

II.

DATA, SIMULATION, AND OBSERVABLES

The data come from the twelve MINERvA mediumenergy, forward-horn-current run periods (playlists 1A–1G
and 1L–1P), corresponding to 1.057 × 1021 protons on
target, with an average neutrino energy near 6 GeV [13].

−0.9×10−5 in the data and −1.8×10−5 in the simulation,
of order 10−6 on the combined sample. This compares
selected samples, not unfolded results, and not these
inputs with those of the published analysis.

III.

CROSS-SECTION EXTRACTION

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The
event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including

unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates
a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth
events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated
flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate
efficiency correction is applied.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin b
carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1−Bb /Db ), where Db
and Bb are the data and predicted background counts in
that bin, so the classifier’s target density is D(x) wpur (b).
The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds
simulated background events with negative weight to the
data, so that the target is D(x) − B(x) pointwise. A
classifier refinement [21], clipped at zero, converts the
signed sample to non-negative weights before the first
iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever
Bb < Db but differ inside a bin wherever the background
fraction varies within it (Supplemental Material, Sec. S4).
The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees [22, 23]
of nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps, and negative-weight background subtraction
with a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves.
The two-observable estimator, LightGBM [23] at a fixed
seed, gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance (Sec. IV B). This reproducible central value is
that of its own systematic-universe ensemble, so its covariance is built with the same estimator. The threeand four-observable central values and the sixteen-cell
five-observable result (Sec. V) use the joint-test estimator,
a deterministic LightGBM with the same background
treatment and refinement capacity, named for the note’s
calibrated joint tests of generator predictions, which use
it (Supplemental Material, Sec. S7). Its seeds are fixed, so
its training is reproducible, and it also gives the held-out
shape tests of Fig. 2b. This estimator removes most of
the bias of purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in
the highest-W cells (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.2). Results from
the two estimators are not combined. The other configurations used below, and their results, are described in
Supplemental Material, Sec. S4.
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the
unfolding. No learner setting, whether capacity, learning
rate, refinement capacity or iteration count, was tuned on
data. The nominal settings are the scikit-learn defaults,
with LightGBM matched to their capacity. The refinement capacity, which the two estimators share, was chosen
in simulation closure studies, and so were the joint-test
estimator’s five iterations (below). The two-observable
estimator uses five iterations following common practice;
this choice was checked on data, against ten iterations
with purity-based subtraction and against the published
result, without a search over the iteration count (Ref. [14],
Sec. 3.2). Two other choices for the two-dimensional result
were made with the comparison to the published result

in view: the learner implementation and the backgroundsubtraction treatment (Sec. IV B). That comparison is
therefore not an independent test of the method. Each
classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it
then sets, so no held-out or cross-fitted split is used; it is
trained without early stopping, and the two classes keep
their physical weights (Supplemental Material, Sec. S4).
The joint-test estimator keeps five iterations because 10,
15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model dependence,
measured as the largest residual proxy over the truth
changes used in its development. In the noise-free study,
this measure on the 27 coarser joint cells is 0.314 at five
iterations and at least 0.384 at 10, 15 or 20; a classifier of
400 trees and 31 leaves lowers it by 8 %, short of the 20 %
the rule required for a change (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4).
A.

Reporting cells

The joint-test estimator’s output on the data is given,
as illustrative output with total uncertainties, on 16 cells,
R00–R15 (Appendix A). Other reporting definitions of
a five-observable measurement, with their precision, are
studied in Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4. They come from the 32 boxes
formed by one interior edge per axis, at pT 0.7, p∥ 4.5 and
q3 2 GeV/c, and at Eavail 0.8 and W 1.8 GeV. In forming
the cells, each supported box (one that meets the support
rule) with purity, the fraction of the simulated signal
reconstructed in it that was generated in it, below 0.1 is
merged into a neighbour, 4 boxes remain unreported, and
every reported cell passes the support rule. The result’s
covariance is given in Sec. IV A.
IV.

UNCERTAINTIES AND THE VALIDATION
PROGRAMME

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the
prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated
experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The closure, deformation, and coverage
studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events
used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed
and true distributions together and keeps the detector
response at fixed truth. Throughout, the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune v1. Every study uses the analysis
simulation’s detector response.
A.

The reported covariance

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is
Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D . The term Ctotal sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization
uncertainties of the joint-test estimator and Cmodel ; the

lateral uncertainties shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than reweighting events
(Appendix A 2). The model term Cmodel is built from the
change in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted
to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn. Its contributions come from those generators’ samples in their
own definitions, with NuWro and GiBUU on carbon and
GENIE CV as the common reference, not from the predictions compared in Sec. A 3. The comparisons there with
GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU therefore use covariance contributions derived from the same generators.
It is a measure of unscaled generator-shape dependence,
not a calibrated bound (Supplemental Material, Sec. S5).
The response term Cresp is the response of the result to a
one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil
energy in the simulation. The scale is applied to proton,
meson, electromagnetic and other deposits. Cresp does
not cover neutron or muon deposits, nor species, energydependence or resolution effects beyond a scale. It enters
only this result, its fake-data tests and its comparison in
Sec. A 3. The unfolded results of the three run-period
groups, each relative to its own simulation, differ by more
than their bootstrap scatter. The offsets of the twelve
playlists differ between the three period groups by more
than expected from their playlist-to-playlist scatter (Supplemental Material, Sec. S5). The cause is not known,
and these data cannot establish which period is correct.
The run-period block CPL,5D includes this inconsistency
in the reported covariance. It is built from the double
ratios among the groups, each group’s unfolded result
over its own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the
reference, so it contains the differences by construction.
It was added after these differences were seen. It has rank
2 and is not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains,
and with three groups its size is poorly determined: the
68 % interval on the ratio of its true to its estimated size
is 0.73–2.56. The median per-cell relative uncertainty is
7.86%, 7.29% without CPL,5D , and 6.74% without CPL,5D
or Cresp . The block raises the median by about 7.8 %,
and the uncertainty of the most affected cell, R04, by
14.1 %. The fake-data tests and the generator comparison
are given with and without it (Sec. IV H, Appendix A 3;
Ref. [14], Sec. 10.4).
Every χ2 of this result inverts the full covariance of the
cells compared, 16×16 or 8×8, by Cholesky decomposition
and without truncation, so its number of degrees of freedom is the number of cells (one fewer for the shape-only
χ2 ). Of the blocks, the statistical block is the unbiased
sample covariance of 50 bootstrap replicas, and the flux
block a covariance of 100 weighted universes (effective
number 19), normalized by the sum of the weights. The
others are a 2p2h band, two-point shifts, chosen models,
the normalization, the run-period block, built from three
exposure-weighted periods, and the training-seed block,
which is zero; each comparison with a prediction adds
Vg , the diagonal covariance from the finite size of the
prediction’s own simulated sample. No finite-ensemble
correction is applied. Appendix A 2 lists the systematic

groups and gives the per-group uncertainties and the correlation matrix of the reported covariance; the note gives
each block’s ensemble and normalization (Supplemental
Material, Sec. S5).
B.

Two-dimensional benchmark

The two-dimensional result benchmarks the unfolding
against a measurement from the same data. Its integrated
cross section is 3.069×10−38 cm2 /nucleon, compared with
3.039 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published analysis, a
ratio of 1.010. The standardized bin differences formed
with the published uncertainties have an RMS of 0.535.
Against the full published covariance, the descriptive
covariance-weighted distance is χ2 /ndf = 2.27. It is computed with the pseudo-inverse over the 205 reported bins,
which keeps all 205 eigenmodes of the published covariance (condition number 1.5×1012 ), and ndf is the number
of reported bins. The value is descriptive, and no p-value
is quoted for it. It indicates a correlated shape difference,
not a normalization difference: its normalization part is
χ2 = 0.04, against 465 for the shape. In the eigenbasis of the published covariance, the largest standardized
component is 4.8 and the next nine 3.4–3.8, and the ten
smallest-eigenvalue modes carry 9.6 % of the χ2 . Keeping
only the 73 best-determined directions (λ/λmax > 10−4 )
gives χ2 /205 = 0.66, and the 139 with λ/λmax > 10−6
give 1.39, against 2.27 for all 205; the divisor is the number of bins in each case, not the number of directions kept.
Most of the χ2 therefore lies outside the best-determined
directions, and those directions also contribute to it. Of
the six leading modes, three are poorly determined directions (λ/λmax ≲ 4 × 10−9 ) in the highest-p∥ bins, and
three are moderate-λ modes (4 × 10−6 to 6 × 10−5 ) below
them (Fig. 3; Ref. [14], Sec. 6.4). The source of the shape
difference has not been identified.
We quote the LightGBM result as the central value
because its covariance uses the same estimator. LightGBM, rather than the scikit-learn exact-split estimator
at the same settings, was chosen after both, and a third
implementation, the scikit-learn histogram estimator, had
been compared with the published result. With puritybased subtraction and with ndf the 205 reported bins,
their χ2 /ndf values were 3.66 for exact-split, 2.65 for
LightGBM and 2.70 for the histogram estimator (a mean
over ten seeds), and the distance depends on the estimator (Ref. [14], Sec. 6.4). The refined negative-weight
background treatment was chosen on a closure test in simulation. With the simulation’s own truth as the target,
the 20-seed mean residual of the purity-based estimate
reached a maximum over the 205 bins of 5.9, in units
of the data result’s per-bin statistical uncertainty; the
corresponding maximum for the refined estimate was 0.44.

one such unit in 14 of the 205 bins. That choice was made
on the closure test, before the refined result’s comparison
with the published result was computed. It was adopted

D.

Two-dimensional covariance

The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four
blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central
value’s estimator: systematic universes (187 universes,
including the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap
replicas), run period (three period groups), and training
seed (five seed settings, so that its own size is poorly determined). The resulting relative uncertainty has a median
of 6.41 % per bin and is 4.76 % on the integrated cross
section, compared with 6.85 % and 4.61 % for the publication. The published flux uncertainty (median 4.01 %)
is larger than ours (3.71 %), for a reason the released
inputs do not let us determine. The covariance omits the

approximation in the systematic-universe unfoldings for
events that fail reconstruction, the learner dependence
of Sec. IV B, and the dependence on the regularization,
while the dependence on the prior’s generator shape is reported beside it (below). Details are in the Supplemental
Material, Sec. S6, and Ref. [14], Secs. 6.3 and 4.6.
The statistical block, with a median statistical uncertainty of 0.676 %, is a bootstrap of 300 replicas drawn at
the central value’s training seed, in which the background
templates are also resampled.
In the two-dimensional result, data from different run
periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties: one period group’s unfolded p∥ distribution,
relative to its own simulation, differs from that of the
earliest periods by a step of about 7 % near 8 GeV/c, of
unknown cause (Sec. IV A). Its run-period block, built
from these differences in the same way, includes this inconsistency, amounts to 2.0 % on the integrated cross section
and passes its own consistency check by construction.
The step is already in the reconstructed data: there, the
1G–1M group against 1A–1E has a step of +6.10 %, and
in a per-period unfolding the double ratio of each period’s
result to its own simulated truth gives +(6.94 ± 0.41) %,
so the step survives unfolding at about its reconstructionlevel size. These data cannot establish which period is
right or the cause.
A declared benchmark compares each run-period
group’s existing two-dimensional unfolding with the published result, in the directions of the published covariance
used in Sec. IV B. In the 73 best-determined directions,
removing the 1G–1M group does not change the distance
to the published result beyond noise; the smallest change
resolved is 3σ = 0.29 per direction, about 15 % of the
three-group value, and no group’s distance differs from
another’s beyond noise. The run-period step does not
account for the distance in the best-determined directions,
at the resolved size. In the 132 poorly determined directions, the combination of the other two run-period groups
is farther from the published result than the three-group
combination, by 0.69 per direction, a fraction 0.31 of
the three-group value, beyond noise estimated from 10
replicas. Of the six leading modes of the χ2 , all outside
the best-determined directions, three are independent of
the period group at this resolution, two are unresolved,
and one differs between the 1A–1F and 1G–1M groups
beyond noise, among 18 tests at 3σ. Each group’s p∥
high-to-low ratio matches the published one, whose own
uncertainty (4.1–4.6 %) is wider than the differences between groups. Each σ comes from 10 bootstrap draws
and one seed draw per group, so it is uncertain by about
±24 %, and the noise excludes the background template
and the systematic universes, so it is a lower bound.

shape as a separate

k . Here sk is the per-bin relative

Added to the four-block covariance, it gives a total with

The procedure’s two-dimensional covariance was tested
for coverage, in a test declared in advance, with 300
pseudo-experiments at each of the two held-out GENIE v3 tunes of Sec. IV H, the uncertainties propagated
linearly as in the covariance’s construction, and the covariance rebuilt by the same procedure at each tune, with
Cmodel included; the run-period block was not drawn.
It under-covers at both tunes. At AR23_20i_00_000,
no pseudo-experiment of the 300 lies in the declared

the mean χ2 /ndf is 16.55 ± 0.17 for 204 degrees of freedom; at G18_10a_02_11b, none lies in it, against 192,

and 0.0363 per tune. G18_10a_02_11b shares its 2p2h
model family with one of Cmodel ’s generators. The result
concerns the procedure’s covariance at those tunes, not
the reported intervals at the data.
E.

Normalization and closure

Summed over each of the four (pT , p∥ ) quadrants, the
sixteen-cell result agrees with the two-dimensional result:
the sums differ by −1.5 to +0.7 per cent, and with the
covariance between the two results evaluated from the
systematic variations they share, the four differences give
χ2 ≤ 1.25 for four degrees of freedom (p ≥ 0.87), with
no quadrant beyond one standard deviation. The statistical correlation, and that of four systematic terms
that could not be evaluated directly, are bounded within
a one-parameter family of valid covariances, each such
cross term taken as a common fraction, from zero to one,
of its fully correlated value, over which the agreement
holds. Of the joint-test estimator’s other results, only
the three-observable marginal has been compared with
the two-dimensional result, agreeing with it to 0.141 % in
area-weighted total over all 205 reported cells (Ref. [14],

Sec. 10.2).
For the joint-test estimator, a background-inclusive
closure at nominal truth on 40 pseudo-experiments, not
held out from its development, has a largest deviation of
2.7 standard errors and a largest (Eavail , W ) residual of
0.09 % (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.3); on the sixteen cells, the closures of Table II meet the bias criteria. Closure tests with
backgrounds included, comparing purity-based with unbinned negative-weight subtraction, are given in Ref. [14],
Sec. 9.2 and Ref. [14], App. C.6.
F.

Generator-sized truth changes

Figure 2 shows that the unfolding recovers only part of
a change of the Eavail shape as large as the difference between generators. When the true shape is reweighted by
the GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio, the (Eavail , W ) cells unfolded
with a development variant of the joint-test estimator (a
100-tree, 8-leaf refinement), not the reported estimator, deviate from it by several percent in the largest cells and by
up to 74 % at high W , in the cell 0.8 ≤ Eavail < 1.5 GeV,
W ≥ 3 GeV. These deviations occur with or without
background, so background subtraction alone does not
explain the residual. In a noise-free comparison in simulation on the 109 joint cells, an exact binned iterative
unfolding of the same development truth changes leaves
about the same median residual at five iterations: 8.3 %,
5.4 %, 4.9 % and 5.3 % for the GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail
change, a NuWro Eavail shape, a NuWro (pT , p∥ , Eavail )
shape and a q3 stress test, against 9.5 %, 5.4 %, 5.6 %
and 4.3 % for the boosted trees. Longer exact iteration
removes much of the residual of the GiBUU-to-GENIE
change (its median falls to 3.3 % at 200 iterations) and
part of the NuWro Eavail one, but not that of the other
two (4.4 % and 6.6 % at 200), and the long runs do not
converge. The residual is therefore consistent with finiteiteration regularization for the GiBUU-like change, but
not for every change tested, and no single cause is established (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.3). In that comparison, 121 of
the 144 residuals stable under the truth resolution lie in
quantities that the binned response constrains weakly at
the analysis exposure, with Cramér–Rao widths above
10 % (median 31 %), and the tree approximation dominates 15, of which 2 lie in identified quantities; this
holds for a binned, signal-only, noise-free comparison at
the simulation’s detector response. For other fixed generator shapes tested with the joint-test estimator, it is
16 % to 31 % in the most affected (Eavail , W ) cell and
lies within the quoted total uncertainty in about half
of those cells. Those uncertainties come from an earlier
purity-subtracted five-observable covariance that fails its
specified acceptance criteria (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.6). For the
sixteen-cell result, Sec. IV H reports the fake-data tests
of the estimator’s bias. The studies, their scope and their
seed sets are in Ref. [14], Secs. 9.2 and 9.3.
Statistical intervals of an earlier five-observable estimator with purity-based subtraction, corrected for its

closure bias at nominal truth, miscover at the tested
truths, most strongly when the true signal rate is lowered by about 10 %, with coverage as low as 0.06 for 68%
intervals (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.5).
G.

Sensitivity to input rounding

The joint-test estimator is unstable under floatingpoint rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, the reported quantities scatter
by a median of 0.23 % per joint cell, against a median
data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty of 0.37 %, and each
bootstrap replica carries this scatter at the same size.
Rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by
up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of the
lower-capacity development variant by up to 2.5, so the
estimator fails a data-stability requirement of at most
0.3 (median) and 1.0 (maximum) statistical standard deviations. The training-seed block of Sec. IV A is zero
because, in the unfolds we ran, the result is bitwise independent of the training seed and of input row order;
this is distinct from the sensitivity to rounding-level input changes quoted above. That sensitivity enters the
reported statistical block by construction: each of its
50 bootstrap replicas refits the whole unfolding with its
own rounding-level perturbation of the inputs. On the
finer joint cells, the rounding spread is about 37 % of
the statistical variance and below a tenth of any total
uncertainty (Ref. [14]); on the 16 reported cells, over 20
rounding-level perturbations of the data, it is a median
0.133 % of the cross section (0.055 % to 0.252 %); the
quoted 2.6 statistical standard deviations is the largest
shift, over 153 quantities (the joint cells, the (Eavail , W )
cells and two totals) under two such perturbations of the
data, not the typical one. A declared probe on simulated
pseudo-data of two-fold cross-fitting and of two tree regularisations found no variant that met the data-stability
requirement, so the estimator is unchanged (Ref. [14]).
Cross-fitting also gives a larger fake-data bias than the
current estimator at three of the 17 truths it was graded
on.
A declared study of the mechanism, on one simulated
pseudo-experiment, examined six candidate causes, five
of them testable, comparing the spread under a modified
perturbation with that under the unmodified one, as a
median ratio over the 153 quantities and over the 16 cells.
On this pseudo-experiment, the perturbation reaches the
estimator by breaking ties among equal float32 values,
which changes LightGBM’s histogram bins; a perturbation
that keeps the ties removes the spread, leaving a ratio of
6.2 × 10−7 and 1.07 × 10−6 . Equal float32 values are what
float32 storage of these kinematics produces. Separately,
jittering only the input of the refinement stage carries the
full spread (ratios 0.959 and 0.890), so the refinement is a
sufficient entry; jittering only the OmniFold loop carries it
on the 153 quantities (0.947) and is inconclusive on the 16
cells, and whether the ties act at the refinement was not

tested. Finer binning does not reduce the spread (0.950
and 0.945), the float32 and float64 inputs give bitwiseequal results, and permuting the input rows moves the
result by about 10−10 of the spread. The spread grows
over the five iterations, to 1.35 times its size after the
first.
A declared ensemble-averaged variant of the estimator,
the mean of 96 fits, declared before the mechanism study
was read, meets the stability requirement on the declared
stability input (median 0.08 and at most 0.76, against 0.3
and 1.0), where the reported estimator does not. On the
declared noise-free fake-data closures it is no worse than
the reported estimator at 16 of the 17 truths and worse
at one, the Stage 0c GiBUU truth, by the criterion fixed
before the test, so it is not adopted and the estimator is
unchanged.
H.

Fake-data tests of the sixteen-cell result

To compare the estimator’s bias with the reported
covariance when the truth differs from the simulation, we
unfold pseudo-data at 12 truths and compare the mean
unfolded result with each truth on the 16 cells through χ2
with 16 degrees of freedom. The truths are not held out:
they are the simulation’s prior; five hybrid truths, each
equal to a generator prediction in every cell of the fine
grid and to the MINERvA Tune v1 simulation’s event
composition within each cell; NuWro shape changes in
Eavail and in (pT , p∥ , Eavail ); a GENIE + MEC shape
change in (Eavail , W ); a GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail ratio; a
noise-free closure at the NuWro shape; and a synthetic
tilt of the q3 distribution at fixed (Eavail , W ). For a
truth from a generator that contributes to Cmodel , the
covariance omits that generator’s component (the Cov.
column of Table II). Two of the truths, the simulation
prior and the Tune v1 hybrid, are the simulation’s own
truth, so they are closure tests (marked ∗ in Table II). A
truth is flagged when p < 0.01 or when a cell has |z| > 2,
where z is the cell’s bias divided by its uncertainty under
the same covariance; these criteria were fixed before any
of the tests was graded against them; Table II lists all
12 truths. Table II gives the results under the reported
covariance and under Ctotal alone, and 6 further truths,
given in the note, are descriptive and not judged against
these criteria.
Under the reported covariance, without the GiBUU
component for the two GiBUU truths, the flagged truths
(p < 0.01, or a cell with |z| > 2) are the GiBUU hybrid
truth (p < 10−27 ; cells R02, R06) and the synthetic q3
tilt, by p alone (p < 10−5 ). Under Ctotal + Cresp , the
covariance without CPL,5D , which Table II does not show,
the GiBUU Eavail shape also meets the flagging criterion,
in cell R06 (z = 2.01, against 1.76 with the block). Thus
the fake-data bias exceeds the stated thresholds at the
GiBUU hybrid truth and the q3 tilt under the reported
covariance, and also at the GiBUU Eavail shape without
CPL,5D . The bias is the same in both; only the covariance

differs. Under Ctotal alone, the right-hand columns of Table II, the flagged truths are the GiBUU hybrid truth, the
GiBUU Eavail shape (cell R06, p = 0.0031), the synthetic
q3 tilt and the NuWro (pT , p∥ , Eavail ) shape, the last by
p alone (p = 0.0027). At the other 10 truths the bias
passes both criteria under the reported covariance, with
no cell beyond 2σ and p ≥ 0.01. Both cells flagged at
the GiBUU hybrid truth, R02 and R06, are low-stability
cells. On the eight cells with p∥ < 4.5 GeV/c, which leave
out R06, that truth still gives χ2 = 132.4 for 8 degrees of
freedom under the reported covariance; the comparison is
descriptive, and selecting cells after these outcomes would
be a post hoc change.
Two validity rules, labelled separately, classify each cell.
The first applies per cell the thresholds of the usage recommendations of Ref. [5]: at least 5000 effective simulated
events, here those of the simulation’s weights, a deviation
from that definition; a data statistical uncertainty below
15 %; and |z| ≤ 2 at each of the 12 graded truths under
the reported covariance. The first two are met in every
cell (the smallest effective number is 331 614, in R07, and
the largest statistical uncertainty 0.368 %, in R05), so
the rule rests on the third, and its recommended test of
correlation assumptions on pseudo-data is not met here.
Fourteen cells are valid under it; R02 and R06 fail, each
from the GiBUU hybrid truth alone, at z = +4.19 and
+4.53. The second rule is ours, not taken from Ref. [5]:
a cell is valid if the spread of its fake-data bias over the
graded truths is less than half the spread of the generator
predictions, ρ < 0.5, in each of four noise treatments,
which agree in every cell. Fourteen cells are valid under
it; R08 (ρ = 0.522–0.534) and R15 (0.564–0.565) are not,
and the largest ρ among the valid cells is 0.444, in R10.
The threshold of 0.5 is our choice: at 1/3, 11 cells are
valid, and at 1, all 16. Twelve cells are valid under both
rules. The two rules test different things, the first one
truth’s closure pull and the second the size of the bias
spread relative to the generator spread, and neither validates an interval, since the fake-data tests grade bias, not
coverage.

APPLICATION TO DATA

Applied to the data, the joint-test estimator gives a
cross section on the sixteen cells of Sec. III, with the
covariance of Sec. IV A. Appendix A gives the cells, the

covariance and descriptive comparisons with five generator
predictions. They are illustrative output, not for fitting
or for constraining models: no interval has validated
coverage, the fake-data bias exceeds the stated criteria
at two truths, four cells have low reconstruction stability,
and the run-period block was added after the period
differences were seen. Figure 4 shows the output over
each prediction, cell by cell.
VI.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

The method provides a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables, without a separate binned response
tensor for each projection, whose normalization agrees
with the two-dimensional result. Its reported cells depend
on the unfolding algorithm, by the binned comparison of
Sec. IV C. It does not yet provide validated interval coverage, numerical stability, recovery of every generator-sized
truth change tested within the reported uncertainty, runperiod consistency, or an identified source of the shape
difference from the published result (Table I).
Two validation findings point to the unfolded result
retaining part of its prior. The first is the residual under a
generator-sized change of the truth in Sec. IV F, which for
the GiBUU-like change is consistent with finite-iteration
regularization. The second is the shift of the data result
when the simulation’s prior is reweighted to generatorsized changes of the truth, which tracks the same bias
(Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4). The note’s calibrated tests of the
predictions show the same bias toward the prior (Ref. [14],
Sec. 11.5).
The studies of Sec. IV and of the companion note
show what must be checked before unfolded intervals
are interpreted: closure with the backgrounds included;
truth changes of generator size, not only nominal closure;
coverage under changes of the truth, not only at nominal
truth; robustness to training seeds and rounding-level
input changes; and run-period consistency.
Until the coverage, the prior dependence, the numerical stability, and the run-period effect are resolved, the
sixteen-cell output is a demonstration of the method.
A.

Limitations

Apart from the validation of the uncertainties and
the low stability of four of the sixteen cells (below), the
limitations of these results are stated with the results they
qualify, in Secs. III–V, and those of the calibrated tests
in the companion note. The principal ones are these.
1. No reported interval has validated coverage. The
fake-data tests of Sec. IV H assess bias, not coverage,
so interval widths cannot be tested, and the coverage test declared for the sixteen-cell covariance was
stopped by one of its preliminary checks before any
coverage pseudo-experiment ran, and its staged redesign stopped at its first stage, so it did not run to

a result; that check compared unlike objects for the
interaction group at the nominal truth and mostly
for the flux band, two detector bands depart for a
cause not established, and the blocks’ non-additivity
and the truth dependence stay open (Sec. IV H). Neither the coverage of Cmodel at truths held out of
its construction nor the size of either run-period
block is independently validated (Supplemental Material, Sec. S5, and Sec. S6). The two-dimensional
covariance gives no calibrated significance for generator comparisons: an earlier statistical band of
that result undercovered in its tails in a coverage
test at fixed truth (Supplemental Material, Sec. S9),
and the procedure’s covariance, rebuilt at each of
two held-out tunes without the run-period block,
under-covers at both (Sec. IV D), a result about
the procedure’s covariance at those tunes, not the
reported intervals at the data. The coverage tests
cited are pointwise, for each reported quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage of all
of them. We make no coverage claim on independent simulated samples or simultaneously over all
reported bins and projections.
2. Four of the sixteen cells, R02, R06, R10 and R15,
have a reconstruction stability of 0.16–0.25: of the
simulated signal events generated in such a cell and
reconstructed, only that fraction is reconstructed in
the same cell.
3. The note’s calibrated tests reject hybrid nulls, conditionally on the simulation’s hadronic response and
on the simulated chain reproducing the analysis of
the data. A rejection extends to a generator prediction only if the residual below the fine grid is
bounded, and no such bound has been shown. They
are not physical exclusions, and show no evidence
of additional discrimination beyond matched coarse
projections (Supplemental Material, Sec. S7).

Uncertainty tables

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result
(Sec. IV A) is a sum of blocks, grouped as follows.
• Statistical : the bootstrap of the data, 50 replicas.
• Flux : 100 hadron-production (PPFX) flux universes,
weighted by the neutrino–electron and inverse-muondecay constraint (effective number 19).
• Interaction: the 33 interaction-model bands of the
MINERvA systematic set, which reweight simulated
events.
• Detector : the 4 detector-model bands, which
reweight simulated events: the MINOS muonmatching efficiency and the GEANT4 neutron, pion
and proton reweights.

• Lateral : the 5 bands that shift the reconstructed
kinematics of each simulated event rather than
reweighting it: the beam angle in two directions, the
muon momentum resolution, and the muon energy
scales in MINERvA and in MINOS.
• Normalization: 1.40 % in every cell.
• Cmodel , Cresp and CPL,5D , as defined in Sec. IV A.
The statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization groups, with a training-seed block that is zero
in every cell, form Ce , and Ctotal = Ce + Cmodel . Table IV

DATA AVAILABILITY

The input data and simulation are the MINERvA opendata AnaTuples, released under CC0 [12]. The sixteen-cell
cross section, its covariance matrix and the cell definitions
are provided as machine-readable files in the Supplemental
Material. This analysis used an earlier production of that
release; its 2,374 input files are identified by SHA256
checksum in the records cited in Ref. [14], App. G. The
analysis note’s figures of the joint-test null distributions
and the (Eavail , W ) map are regenerated by a candidate
release package of about 16 MB, not yet deposited, that
requires only numpy, scipy, and matplotlib. The paper’s
quoted numbers and the event-level processing require
the archived analysis products, the open-data AnaTuples,
and the generator samples produced for this analysis;
Ref. [14], App. G states what each part of the release
does and does not reproduce.
