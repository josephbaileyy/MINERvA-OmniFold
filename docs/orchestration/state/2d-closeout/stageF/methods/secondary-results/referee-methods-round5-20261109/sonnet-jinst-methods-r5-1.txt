# Referee report for JINST

## 1. Summary of the submission

The manuscript applies OmniFold, an unbinned ML unfolding, to MINERvA medium-energy νµ open data. It unfolds five observables simultaneously: pT, p∥, Eavail, q3 and W. The authors compare a two-dimensional (pT, p∥) result with the published binned cross section. The integral agrees to about 1%, but the shape differs over many eigenmodes of the published covariance, for a cause not identified. They then run a validation programme on a sixteen-cell five-observable output: binned IBU cross-checks, fake-data bias tests, rounding and seed stability, run-period consistency, and a coverage test. Several of these fail or were stopped (fake-data bias at two truths, rounding stability, the coverage test, unexplained run-period differences). The authors therefore present the five-observable result only as "illustrative output", and the paper is framed as a negative-or-mixed methods study.

## 2. Recommendation: major revision

The honesty and pre-registration discipline are unusual and welcome. However, as written the manuscript is a catalogue of failed and stopped tests, with no resolved methodological conclusion. Its central claims are the 2D benchmark and the five-observable output. The 2D benchmark has an unexplained shape discrepancy, and the authors concede it is not an independent test (learner and background treatment were chosen with the comparison in view). The five-observable output has no validated coverage and fails the authors' own bias and stability criteria. The few positive findings (rounding-level tie-breaking as a mechanism, integral agreement, prior retention under generator-sized changes) are buried under about forty tests, much of the detail belonging in the note. The paper is also very hard to read, relying on a companion note the referee cannot see for most of its substantive content. I would want a substantially restructured and shortened paper, with the tractable deficiencies resolved, before publication.

## 3. Major issues

**1. The 2D benchmark does not validate anything, and its shape discrepancy is unexplained.**
- χ²/ndf = 2.27 against the published covariance (the shape part is 465 against a normalization part of 0.04). The largest eigen-component is 4.8σ and the next nine are 3.4–3.8σ.
- The authors show it is not the run-period step (Sec. IV D), not clearly the algorithm (ρ = 0.452, interval 0.701–1.128, "unresolved"), and not the learner (exact-split gives 3.66, LightGBM 2.65). The inputs are also a different production than the published analysis's, and this is not assessed.
- Why it matters: the paper's sole external anchor is therefore a descriptive comparison with a distance that varies by 40% across implementations. The learner choice and the background treatment were made with the comparison in view, so the χ² values are conditional and effectively tuned.
- Required: (a) a genuinely blind-style check, such as unfolding the simulation with a published-analysis-like binned response and comparing, or running OmniFold on pseudo-data generated at the published result's shape and showing it recovers that shape; (b) a quantitative answer on whether the input-production difference (playlist 1A only) can matter; (c) clarity on the 205-bin pseudo-inverse with condition number 1.5×10¹², where "all 205 eigenmodes" are kept. Modes at λ/λmax ≲ 4×10⁻⁹ cannot support a χ² interpretation. The paper should report the χ² truncated at a defensible rank and ideally with the published covariance's own uncertainty in its small eigenvalues.

**2. Retained prior dependence (regularization bias) is the central methodological finding but is not resolved or quantified.**
- At the GiBUU hybrid truth the χ² is 171 for 16 degrees of freedom, with cell z = 4.5. At the q3 tilt p < 10⁻⁵. Individual (Eavail, W) cells recover only part of an injected change, with a residual up to 74%.
- The authors show that binned IBU at five iterations behaves similarly, that 200 iterations help for some changes but not others, and that "no single cause is established".
- Why it matters: this is the property that determines whether unbinned multidimensional OmniFold is usable on data. The paper's conclusion ("retains part of its prior") is stated, but the fix is not tested. The reported Cmodel is explicitly "unscaled" and not calibrated. At the GiBUU hybrid truth it does not cover the bias at all.
- Required: an iteration/regularization scan with a pre-declared selection criterion, evaluated against held-out truths (not the same truths used to develop). The authors should show whether more iterations, early stopping, cross-fitting or higher-capacity classifiers reduce the bias at fixed variance, or state plainly that the method as configured cannot unfold a generator-sized change in Eavail/W. The 12 "graded" truths are largely not held out, and only the two GENIE v3 tunes are. The tunes pass, but the authors acknowledge they share model families with Cmodel components and so are "not independent".

**3. Numerical instability under rounding-level input changes is a fatal property for a measurement tool and is only partially diagnosed.**
- Changes of up to 2.6 statistical σ against the authors' own requirement of 1.0 (median 0.8 against 0.3). The ensemble-averaged variant (mean of 96 fits) meets the stability requirement. It was not adopted because it is worse in bias at a single truth (Stage 0c GiBUU), under a pre-fixed criterion.
- The mechanism study is on one pseudo-experiment, finds tie-breaking among equal float32 values, but the paper states that the refinement stage is "a sufficient entry" and that "whether the ties act at the refinement was not tested". Finer bins do not help.
- Why it matters: a result that moves by up to 2.6σ under bitwise-trivial input changes has a hidden noise source that is not in the stated uncertainties. Folding it into the bootstrap by construction partly handles it, but it means the statistical block is not a pure Poisson fluctuation.
- Required: (a) repeat the mechanism study on more than one pseudo-experiment and on the data; (b) test the obvious remedy, deduplicating or jittering ties deterministically before training, or quantizing features, and show the effect on both stability and bias; (c) reconsider adopting the 96-fit ensemble. Rejecting the variant that fixes the stated failure because of one truth, by a rule fixed beforehand, is defensible procedurally but scientifically unhelpful. The authors should report the ensemble result prominently and discuss the one bias worsening quantitatively.

**4. The coverage of reported uncertainties is unvalidated, and the key test was stopped by a design error.**
- The declared coverage test was stopped at a gate comparing unlike objects. The redesign stopped at its first stage (892 of 900 draws gave non-positive composite denominators). The only coverage result obtained (2D covariance at two held-out tunes) under-covers (mean χ²/ndf = 16.6 and 24.2; none of 300 pseudo-experiments in the joint region).
- Why it matters: the paper's title-level claim is a "test" of what OmniFold delivers. If the intervals are not shown to cover and the one coverage test that ran under-covers, the uncertainty component of the study is absent. The interaction-group block is shown to differ from the pseudo-experiment reproduction by a median factor of 2.72 (range 0.49–6.51), meaning the linearly propagated universe covariance is not the covariance of the actual procedure under the nominal truth. Linearity, or additivity of blocks, is therefore not established.
- Required: a working coverage test. The like-for-like control already reproduces the interaction block (0.9–1.1 in all cells), so a coverage test in which pseudo-data carry the full universe weights is feasible. The authors should run it (fixed truth, nuisance draws, held-out tunes), at least for the interaction, flux and detector groups, and report the cell-wise and joint coverage. The positive-denominator failure suggests a simple remedy, such as drawing a smaller number of bands, using a log-normal parameterization, or composing the draws at the event-weight level instead of first order. If this cannot be done, the paper should not claim anything about its uncertainties beyond "propagated linearly".

**5. The run-period inconsistency is a data-quality issue that is unresolved and handled ad hoc.**
- Three period groups differ by a p∥ step of about 7% near 8 GeV/c, present already at reconstruction level (+6.10%) and surviving unfolding (+6.94 ± 0.41%). The authors do not know the cause. They add a rank-2 block built from the observed differences after seeing them, with a 68% interval on its size of 0.73–2.56.
- Why it matters: a ~7% step, ten times the 0.7% statistical error, in a published open-data sample is either a real detector/flux/calibration effect or an artefact of the open-data production. Either way it confounds everything. The paper itself notes the block contains the differences by construction, so it cannot be tested.
- Required: (a) check whether the step is in the published analysis's own run-period breakdowns (the authors do not say whether the published analysis reports this); (b) check the reconstruction-level step against POT normalization, flux per playlist, the muon energy-scale and MINOS-matching systematics, or the earlier-production-vs-current-release difference for playlist 1A (only 1A was compared); (c) show whether the step appears in simulation-only comparisons across playlists. This is analysis, not note material, because it bears on whether any result from these data can be trusted. Given the open-data context, the authors should contact the data release maintainers.

**6. The five-observable setup carries redundancy and definitional departures that are not assessed.**
- Reconstructed W is an exact function of the reconstructed (pT, p∥, q3), apart from a clamp. Truth W is nearly so. Yet the paper says "the information added by W has not been measured". Eavail uses a 135 MeV constant, omits kaons and electrons, and is neither the published definition nor a single standard one. Reconstructed Eavail is 1.17 times the calorimetric recoil with an energy scale that is only partly varied (Cresp covers proton, meson, EM and other deposits, not neutrons or muons). The authors note, however, that "no recoil-energy-scale band" exists in the analysis systematic set.
- Why it matters: the headline of "five observables" is partly a four-observable problem. The 1.17 factor and the neutron-blind truth definition mean the hadronic observables' response depends on simulated neutron and unmodelled-deposit behaviour that is not in the uncertainties (GEANT4 neutron band departs in 6 cells in the coverage diagnosis).
- Required: demonstrate, with a four-observable (omit W) unfolding of the same cells, whether W adds anything. Compare the five-observable cells with the three- and four-observable marginals beyond the single 0.141% area-weighted number. Treat the Eavail definition and neutron blindness explicitly as a limitation on interpretability in the abstract. The first-order shift for the 135 MeV convention (≤0.19σ per cell, 0.42σ coherent) is reassuring but done without retraining.

**7. Target and generator-comparison treatment.**
- Hydrogen has no QE/2p2h/coherent channel. The NuWro/GiBUU carbon predictions are converted to CH with weight 1/13 per nucleon from free-proton samples. The NuWro free-proton supplement at 50–100 GeV has unquantified generator-abort losses. The GiBUU free-proton sample failed a sampling-closure check and 46% of its DIS events do not conserve energy. GiBUU truncates at Eν = 20 GeV and is compared only on 8 cells.
- Why it matters: the Table VI χ² values (e.g. 262 for Tune v1, 229 for GENIE + MEC) are not interpretable physically and the paper says so. They then serve no purpose in a methods paper beyond illustrating a pipeline.
- Required: either drop the generator χ² comparison from the main text, or restrict it to predictions with a clean CH target, such as GENIE and a native CH NuWro, and give no GiBUU entry. Appendix A's comparisons with a known-defective GiBUU CH correction should be removed.

**8. Statistics and covariance construction.**
- χ² inversions use full 16×16 covariances with no finite-ensemble correction, from blocks of 50 bootstrap replicas (with a 16-dim covariance, so the inverse is strongly biased), a flux block with effective number 19, a three-universe 2p2h band, and a rank-2 run-period block. A pseudo-inverse over 205 bins with condition number 10¹² is used elsewhere. Each χ² is stated as "descriptive".
- Why it matters: with 50 replicas in 16 dimensions the χ² is inflated by an O(p/N) factor (about 1.5 on top of the Hotelling-type correction), and the authors acknowledge that such a correction is not derived for sums of unlike blocks. Several headline numbers (the fake-data χ² of 52 and 171) depend on this.
- Required: apply a Hartlap-type or bootstrap-based correction (or increase replica numbers for the statistical block, which is cheap, given the bitwise-reproducible estimator), and state how conclusions change. The fake-data χ² uses the same covariance as the quoted uncertainty, so the bias significance inherits this.

**9. Non-additivity and linearity of systematic propagation.**
The authors find that the full-universe interaction block is not the response-only block plus a cell-mean truth-reweight block. The sum "misses it by about the response block's own size". This is the physics of the problem: in an unfolding with a prior-dependent regularization, the systematic response depends on both the response and the prior reweighting. Yet the reported covariance is a linear sum of such blocks. The resulting uncertainty is neither shown to be right nor labelled as an approximation in the abstract. A direct test (throw all bands simultaneously, which the Supplement says exists for the 2D case in Ref. [1]) should be shown for the 16-cell result.

## 5. Resolve or move out? Is the paper publishable in JINST?

I would prefer the authors resolve the (a) items that are cheap and within their means. These are the stability remedy, a working coverage test, the held-out regularization scan, the W-information test and the run-period trace. They should move all of the (c) material out. As things stand, the paper tries to be a validation record and a methods paper at once, and does neither well. JINST accepts methods and validation studies, and a frank negative-result study is publishable in principle. However, a paper whose conclusion is that the central estimator fails its own stability and bias requirements, whose coverage test never ran, and whose only external benchmark is descriptive and partly tuned, does not yet deliver a reproducible methodological lesson. The lessons it does offer (retained prior, rounding sensitivity, the need to test closure under generator-sized changes) are valuable and could be the whole paper.

No single limitation is disqualifying on its own. The combination is. Specifically, unvalidated coverage, a failed stability requirement and an unexplained run-period step together mean nothing in the five-observable output can be used, which the authors concede. If the paper is reframed as a validation-and-failure-modes study, centred on the three or four tractable findings with the five-observable cross section reduced to a small illustrative table, it would be publishable after revision. It is not publishable as submitted.

## 6. Minor issues and presentation

1. The abstract is a single dense paragraph of about 300 words, with undefined jargon ("joint-test estimator", "stability requirement", "fixed in advance"). Rewrite it with three or four findings.
2. The paper depends heavily on a companion note ([14]) that is cited about 60 times, including for numbers that carry the argument (e.g. Secs. 9.2–9.6, 10.5–10.7). Readers cannot evaluate claims that rest on the note. Deposit the note, or include the essential results.
3. Table I has about 20 rows and is partly illegible in its text-extracted form. Its "status" labels (Differ, Same size, Not adopted, Diagnostic, Miscover) are inconsistent in kind. Reduce it to roughly eight rows with a uniform pass/fail/not-run vocabulary.
4. The register is repeatedly hedged to the point of obscuring results ("at the resolved size", "declared before it ran", "reads unresolved"). Use plain statements and leave pre-registration detail to the Supplement.
5. Section references are inconsistent. The Introduction describes the discussion as "Sec. VI", yet Secs. IV and V contain much of it. The "Sec. A 3" reference is unclear, and Sec. IV D contains a long passage on the test that belongs in IV H.
6. The Supplemental Material has errors. Section S4 contains "(Sec. S4)" self-references, a "Ref. [1]" that is the note rather than the paper's [14], and an exact-split date ("2026-10-11"). Table S2 is cited as both the cell table and the uncertainty table. Reference numbering differs between the paper and the Supplement.
7. Configuration labels (a)–(f) are introduced only in the Supplement. The main text uses "joint-test estimator", "two-observable estimator", "development variant" and "reported estimator" interchangeably. One naming scheme should be adopted in the paper.
8. Sec. III says that "No learner setting … was tuned on data" while Sec. IV B discloses that the learner and background treatment were selected with the published comparison in view. Reconcile this wording.
9. Fig. 2 mixes a development variant (panel a) and the joint-test estimator (panel b) with different uncertainty bands from an earlier, failed covariance. This invites misreading. Use one estimator and the reported covariance, or drop the grey band.
10. Fig. 1: the caption says the two bands are correlated, "so their overlap is not a compatibility test", yet the figure invites that reading. Add the residual panel with the published-covariance eigenmode decomposition (currently only the Fig. 3 per-bin contribution map).
11. The statement that the sixteen-cell result "is therefore not a measurement" followed by a full appendix with tables, a correlation matrix and machine-readable files sends mixed signals. The figures and tables of Appendix A are largely redundant with the Supplement (Table III repeats Table S2; Table V repeats Table S5).
12. The Eavail definition: Eq. (1) subtracts 135 MeV from the pion energy where the text calls this the "charged-pion term", while the Supplement writes this as Eπ± − 135, a total energy, not a kinetic energy. State explicitly that this is non-standard, and drop the line saying it "keeps the species" of the closed list of Ref. [15].
13. The coarse and fine grids, the 109 joint cells, the 32 boxes and the 16 cells are four definitions for one study. The 109-cell joint tests are not a result of this paper. Remove them from the main text.
14. The reference to a "candidate release package … not yet deposited" in the Data availability statement is not acceptable for a paper presenting a reproducibility-oriented validation. Deposit code and configuration before publication, including the seeds and LightGBM determinism settings that the stability result depends on.
15. The phrase "bitwise independent of training seed" needs context. Deterministic LightGBM with full-sample binning ignores the seed when no subsampling is used, so this is a property of the configuration, not evidence of robustness. The training-seed block is thus identically zero and gives no information about ML stochasticity. State this plainly.
16. Language points: "is therefore given only as an illustration" should read "is presented only as an illustration"; "miscover" is not standard; "a mean over ten seeds" attached to a single value in a list of three is ambiguous.
17. The paper's length (about 11 pages of dense text plus a large Supplement) is out of proportion to its definite results. A 6–8 page paper plus a short supplement would serve readers better.

## 7. Scores

(i) SCORE: 3/10

(ii) Main contribution as I read it: a candid, pre-registered stress test of a five-observable OmniFold unfolding on real MINERvA open data, showing that the estimator retains prior information, is numerically sensitive to rounding-level input changes, and cannot be given validated uncertainties with the tools tried so far.

(iii) A better venue: a computational-physics or data-analysis methods journal such as Computer Physics Communications or Machine Learning: Science and Technology, or a reframed version for the European Physical Journal C (Techniques), would suit this better than JINST once the validation story is made coherent.
