You are an independent referee with a working knowledge of how neutrino cross-section measurements and unfolded
measurements are published in Physical Review D, including how their uncertainties are validated. You have no stake
in this paper.

## The files in this directory (all read-only)

- `paper.txt`: the printed text of the paper as it stands today, about 8,500 words. It is a measurement paper led by a
  sixteen-cell five-observable cross section unfolded with OmniFold.
- `coverage-options.md`: the authors' costed scoping of one of its stated limitations, "interval coverage is not
  validated": what exists, the designs considered (1C, 1A, 1B, 1B′) and their costs. Since it was written, option 1A
  (a held-out GENIE v3 bias test) has been run; its results are in the paper's Sec. IV (the paragraph on two GENIE v3
  tunes).

## The question

How important would running a nuisance-drawn coverage test (option 1B or 1B′) be to the quality and credibility of this
measurement as a PRD publication?

## Answer in this form

1. **Your rating, on this scale,** with one sentence of justification:
   - 1 = cosmetic: few readers or referees would notice its absence;
   - 2 = a nice addition that strengthens a secondary point;
   - 3 = a real improvement a careful referee might suggest, but not request;
   - 4 = a referee would likely request it, or the paper is noticeably weaker without it;
   - 5 = a referee would likely require it before acceptance, or the headline result is not credible without it.
2. **Standard practice:** do comparable published PRD unfolded neutrino measurements validate interval coverage, and
   how (fake-data studies, mock-data tests, coverage of toy ensembles, none)? Name the papers you rely on, with arXiv
   identifiers if you know them, and mark any you are not sure of as UNSURE. They will be checked.
3. **This paper's case:** what in its text makes the test more or less important than it is for those papers. Consider
   what the paper already shows (closure, fake-data bias tests, the bias test at GENIE v3 truths) and how it states the
   limitation.
4. **What the paper gains** if the test passes, and what it shows if the test fails, given that the result's intervals
   would not be changed after the fact.
5. **Design and cost:** given the costs in `coverage-options.md` (about 37–146 node-hours, with a development phase),
   which design you would run (1B, 1B′ or another), and whether the cost is proportionate. Name any cheaper design
   that would give most of the value, and any risk that could make the test uninformative.
6. **Anything that matters more** for this paper's credibility than this test, if you think something does.
