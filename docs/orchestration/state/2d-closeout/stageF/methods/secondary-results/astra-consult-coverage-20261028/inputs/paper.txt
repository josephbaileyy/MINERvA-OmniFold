We report a simultaneous measurement of the νµ charged-current inclusive cross section in five
observables, the muon transverse and longitudinal momenta, available hadronic energy, threemomentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass, unfolded without binning with OmniFold from
MINERvA medium-energy open data and reported in sixteen cells with a covariance whose median
per-cell uncertainty is 7.29%. Three external generator predictions compared on all sixteen cells give
χ2 of 109–272 for 16 degrees of freedom, and GiBUU, on 8 cells, 58. The coverage of the intervals
has not been validated; a bias test at two held-out GENIE v3 truths finds the estimator’s bias below
the reported uncertainty in every cell but does not test interval widths. In a fake-data study, the
reported uncertainty does not cover the estimator’s bias at GiBUU-like truths or at a synthetic q3 tilt.
The sixteen-cell χ2 values fall by 24–48% when only the flux correlations are weakened by 10%. The
same unfolding reproduces the integral of the published muon-kinematic cross section within about
1%, with a correlated shape difference. Pseudo-experiments measure a background-subtraction bias
that negative-weight subtraction largely removes, a partial recovery of generator-sized truth changes,
and statistical undercoverage of a deterministic purity-subtracted variant at the tested truths. As an
illustration, simulation-calibrated tests of hybrid null hypotheses for five generator predictions all
reject at the 5% familywise level, with no evidence of gain over matched coarse projections.

I.

INTRODUCTION

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their interpretation depends on correcting detector response and
assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration
matrix [1] is widely used, but the response tensor grows
rapidly when additional observables are reported. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space
through classifier-based density-ratio estimation [2, 3].
This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding
possible without specifying a separate binned response
tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been
applied to collider data, for example eight observables in
deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [4] and twenty-four Z+jets
observables at ATLAS [5]; see Ref. [6] for a survey. In
neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public
simulated T2K near-detector sample [7]. Multidifferential
neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions [8–
11].
We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy
νµ data from the MINERvA open-data release [12]. The
result is a simultaneous charged-current inclusive cross
section in five observables, the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta pT and p∥ , available hadronic energy Eavail , three-momentum transfer q3 , and hadronic

Interval coverage has not been validated. At two heldout GENIE v3 truths, a bias test finds the estimator’s

II.

DATA, SIMULATION, AND OBSERVABLES

The analysis uses the twelve MINERvA mediumenergy, forward-horn-current run periods, corresponding
to 1.057 × 1021 protons on target and an average neutrino
energy near 6 GeV [13], from an earlier production of
the MINERvA open-data release of pre-selected analysis
ntuples (AnaTuples) [12] (see Data availability). The
detector, including the downstream MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref. [15]. The signal is a νµ charged-current interaction,
with any final state, whose true vertex lies in the tracker
fiducial volume (5980 < z < 8422 mm, inside a hexagon
of apothem 850 mm) and whose muon has θµ < 20◦ ,
1.5 < p∥ < 60 GeV/c, and pT ≤ 4.5 GeV/c. Selected simulated events outside this definition, including chargedcurrent νµ events whose muon lies outside these limits,
are background. The selection requires a reconstructed
vertex in the same volume and a reconstructed muon with
θµ < 20◦ , matched to a MINOS track of negative charge,
with a dead-time requirement, as in Ref. [13]. The flux
treatment follows Ref. [16]. After selection, the sample
contains 4.12 million selected data events. The simulation
contains 32.8 million unweighted true signal events and
20.5 million selected simulated events. The simulation is
scaled to the data exposure. The simulated events are
generated with GENIE 2.12.6 [17] and the MINERvA
Tune v1, which reweights GENIE with a non-resonant
pion reduction, a random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil two-particle–two-hole
(2p2h) enhancement [18]. Event selection is held fixed as
the classifier inputs and unfolded observables grow from

(pT , p∥ ) to (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ). Adding features does
not add selected events or relax the signal definition.

III.

CROSS-SECTION EXTRACTION

Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification
steps. A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data
from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight.
The event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including
unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates
a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth
events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated
flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate
efficiency correction is applied.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction every data event in reconstructed bin b
carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1 − Bb /Db ), where
Db and Bb are the data and predicted background counts
in that bin, so the target density is D(x) wpur (b). The
unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative weight to the data,
so the target is D(x) − B(x) pointwise; a classifier refinement [22], clipped at zero, converts the signed sample
into non-negative weights before the first iteration. The
two targets share bin integrals wherever Bb < Db but
differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction varies
within it (Ref. [14], App. C).
Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose
weights it then sets, without a held-out or cross-fitted split,
early stopping, or class balancing; the training details are
in Ref. [14], Sec. 3.1.
Both estimators use five OmniFold iterations with
gradient-boosted decision-tree classifiers [23, 24]. The
iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding. The nominal settings are the scikit-learn defaults,
with LightGBM matched to their capacity. The refinement capacity of the joint-test estimator was chosen in
simulation closure studies. No hyperparameter search was
performed on data.

A.

Reporting definitions and the precision target

To include model dependence, a dedicated study defines
the envelope of a cell as the largest shift of its data
result when the simulation’s prior is reweighted to each
of five generator-sized changes of the truth, and adds
it linearly to the probabilistic half-width. The envelope
is a sensitivity diagnostic that tracks the regularization
bias, not an interval. Our precision target is a 10 %
median half-width for the total 68% interval. With the
envelope added, none of the reporting definitions of a
five-observable measurement tested in the study meets
this target. With the joint-test estimator, the envelope
alone exceeds it for the 109 coarse joint cells and for the
fallback definition, the 39 supported (Eavail , W ) cells. For
a coarser, non-nested partition of all five observables into
27 cells, the same estimator’s envelope alone does not
exceed it, but the 1.4 % normalization contained in every
probabilistic half-width lifts the median above it. The
study is described in Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4.
Over these definitions, the envelope has a median of

4
about 10–13% per cell, comparable to the generator differences that such a measurement would need to resolve.

B.

Reporting cells

A five-observable cross section with total uncertainties
is reported separately from those reporting definitions:
the joint-test estimator’s data result on 16 cells, R00–R15.
They come from the 32 boxes formed by one interior edge
per axis (pT 0.7, p∥ 4.5 and q3 2 GeV/c; Eavail 0.8 and
W 1.8 GeV): each supported box with purity below 0.1 is
merged into a neighbour, 4 boxes remain unreported, and
every reported cell passes the support rule. The result’s
covariance is given in Sec. IV A.

IV.

UNCERTAINTIES AND VALIDATION

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the
prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated
experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together; the coverage tested here is pointwise, for
each reported quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them. The closure, deformation,
and coverage studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data, which changes their
reconstructed and truth distributions together and keeps
the detector response at fixed truth; the prior used to
unfold them stays at Tune v1. The envelope of Sec. III A
instead reweights the prior used to unfold the data. Every
study uses the analysis simulation’s detector response.

A.

The reported covariance

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is
Ctotal + Cresp . Unlike the envelope of Sec. III A, its model
term is a covariance built from three generator shifts.
Ctotal sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization uncertainties of the joint-test estimator and Cmodel . Cmodel is built from the change in the
unfolded data when the prior is reweighted to GENIE +
MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn; it represents unscaled
generator-shape dependence, not a calibrated bound, and
its coverage at truths held out of its construction has not
been validated. Cresp is the response of the result to a
one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil
energy in the simulation, for protons, mesons, electromagnetic and other deposits; it does not cover neutron or
muon deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale, and it enters only this result,
its fake-data tests and its comparison in Sec. V B. The
median per-cell relative uncertainty is 7.29% (6.74% without Cresp ). The note gives the cells, the central values,

the per-group uncertainties and the correlation matrix of
the reported covariance.

B.

Two-dimensional benchmark

The two-dimensional result is an end-to-end test of the
unfolding against a measurement from the same data. Its
integrated cross section is 3.069×10−38 cm2 /nucleon, compared with 3.039 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published
analysis, a ratio of 1.010. The standardized bin differences formed with the published uncertainties have an
RMS of 0.535. Against the full published covariance, the
descriptive covariance-weighted distance is χ2 /ndf = 2.27.
It is computed with the pseudo-inverse over the 205 bins,
which keeps all 205 eigenmodes (rank 205, condition number 1.5 × 1012 ), so that ndf is that rank. It indicates a
correlated shape difference, not a normalization difference,
spread over many eigenmodes of the published covariance.
The source of the shape difference has not been identified.
Figure 1 shows the principal one-dimensional projections.
We quote the LightGBM result as the central value
because its covariance uses the same estimator. The distance depends on the estimator (Ref. [14], Sec. 6.4). The
learner was selected after the LightGBM and exact-split
comparisons with the published result, so this benchmark
is a reproduction rather than an independent validation.
The purity-subtracted exact-split result differs from the
central value by a median of 1.03 % per bin (maximum
17.6 %); see Ref. [14], Sec. 3.2.

C.

Two-dimensional covariance

The covariance of our two-dimensional result is the
sum of four blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with
the central value’s estimator: systematic universes (187
universes), statistical (300 bootstrap replicas), training
seed (five seed settings, so that its own size is poorly
determined), and run period (three period groups). The
resulting relative uncertainty has a median of 6.41 % per
bin and is 4.76 % on the integrated cross section, compared with 6.85 % and 4.61 % for the publication. The
published flux uncertainty (median 4.01 %) is larger than
ours (3.71 %), for a reason the released inputs do not let
us determine. The covariance omits the approximation
in the systematic-universe unfoldings for events that fail
reconstruction, the learner dependence of Sec. IV B, and
the dependence on the regularization; the dependence on
the prior’s generator shape is reported beside it (below).
The covariance has not been independently validated. It
provides no calibrated significance for generator comparisons. The block sizes, the assumptions of the block sum,
and the tests of the approximation are given in Ref. [14],
Secs. 6.3, 4.5, and 4.6.

D.

Normalization and closure

Of the joint-test estimator’s results, only the threeobservable marginal has been compared with the lowerdimensional result it extends: its area-weighted total
agrees with the two-dimensional result to 0.141 %, a comparison across the two estimators. The normalization
checks of the earlier purity-subtracted five-observable central value are described in Ref. [14], Sec. 8.1, and closure
checks of its estimator in three and four observables in
Ref. [14], Sec. 5.1.
Figure 2 shows that, with purity-based background
subtraction in a deterministic LightGBM estimator, endto-end pseudo-experiments at nominal truth reproduce the
input except in the highest-W cells, which are biased by
up to 4 % in simulation. In the same estimator, unbinned
negative-weight subtraction with a refinement of 100 trees
and 8 leaves reduces it to below 0.3 % in those cells (at
most 0.4 % elsewhere in the projection), leaving up to 1 %
in some joint cells. On data, the two treatments differ by
at most 1.4 %. Both kinds of closure study are described
in Ref. [14], Sec. 5.1.
In two observables, a closure test with backgrounds
(20 pseudo-experiments at nominal truth) puts the purity
and refined estimates more than one statistical standard
deviation apart in 14 of the 205 cells. The purity estimate
lies up to 5.9 statistical standard deviations from the truth
(more than one total standard deviation in one cell), and
the refined one lies within 0.44 in every cell. The twodimensional result therefore uses the refined estimator
(Ref. [14], App. C.6).

E.

Generator-sized truth changes

Figure 3 shows that the unfolding recovers only part of
a change of the Eavail shape as large as the difference between generators. When the true shape is reweighted by
the GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio, the (Eavail , W ) cells unfolded
with a development variant of the joint-test estimator using a 100-tree, 8-leaf refinement deviate from it by several
percent in the largest cells and by up to 74 % at high W ,
in the cell 0.8 ≤ Eavail < 1.5 GeV, W ≥ 3 GeV. These
deviations occur with or without background, so background subtraction alone does not explain the residual.
We interpret the residual as regularization bias, because it
persists over the tested iteration numbers and classifier capacities. For other fixed generator shapes tested with the
joint-test estimator, it is 16 % to 31 % in the most affected
(Eavail , W ) cell and lies within the quoted total uncertainty
in about half of those cells. Those uncertainties come

from an earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance that fails its specified acceptance criteria (Ref. [14],
Sec. 9.6). For the sixteen-cell result, the model term of
its reported covariance is the change in the unfolded data
when the prior is reweighted to each generator (GENIE
+ MEC, NuWro, GiBUU), unscaled and not a calibrated
bound; Sec. IV H reports the fake-data tests of the estimator’s bias. The deformation studies, the iteration and
capacity tests, the exact binned comparison and the jointtest-estimator shapes are described in Ref. [14], Secs. 9.2
and 9.3.
In a binned, signal-only and noise-free diagnostic on
the 109 joint cells and the truth changes used to develop
the estimator, with the detector response fixed at the
simulation’s, an exact binned iterative unfolding with
five iterations leaves about the same median residual as
the boosted-tree estimator. It covers only the residuals
whose diagnosis is stable under the truth resolution (38 %
of the joint-cell residuals above 2 %). What unbinned
information could recover, the coverage failure, and the
validity of any interval lie outside its scope. Within that
scope, the residual is consistent with the regularization of
the inverse problem at this binning and prior, rather than
with the tree approximation of the classifiers (Ref. [14],
Sec. 9.3).

F.

Coverage

Statistical intervals from a deterministic LightGBM
estimator with purity-based subtraction, corrected for
the estimator’s closure bias at nominal truth, miscover
at the tested truths, most strongly when the true signal
rate is lowered by about 10 %. Under that change, 51 of
the 153 reported cell and projection integrals have a 68%
coverage whose upper confidence bound lies below the
acceptance threshold of 0.66, with coverage as low as 0.06.
The end-to-end test unfolds 400 pseudo-experiments per
truth.
A diagnosis attributes the miscoverage to a residual bias
that grows with W and survives the nominal-truth bias
correction. The source of this residual bias has not been
established. Agreement in the marginal normalizations
coexists with substantial residual bias under the tested
deformations. The interval construction and the diagnosis
are described in Ref. [14], Sec. 9.5.

G.

Estimator stability

The joint-test estimator is unstable at the level of rounding. Rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results
by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of
its lower-capacity development variant by up to 2.5, so it

H.

Fake-data tests of the sixteen-cell result

To compare the estimator’s bias with the reported
covariance when the truth differs from the simulation, we
unfold pseudo-data at 12 truths and compare the mean
unfolded result with each truth on the 16 cells through
χ2 with 16 degrees of freedom. The truths are not held
out: they are the simulation’s prior; five hybrid truths,
each a generator prediction on the fine grid with the
MINERvA Tune v1 composition below it; NuWro shape
changes in Eavail and in (pT , p∥ , Eavail ); a GENIE + MEC
shape change in (Eavail , W ); a GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail
ratio; a noise-free closure at the NuWro shape; and a
synthetic tilt of the q3 distribution at fixed (Eavail , W ).
For a truth from a generator that contributes to Cmodel ,
the covariance omits that generator’s component, as the
Cov. column of Table II shows. A truth is listed when
p < 0.01 or when a cell has |z| > 2. Table II gives the
results under the reported covariance and under Ctotal
alone; 6 further truths are ungraded and given in the
note.
Under the reported covariance, without the GiBUU
component for the two GiBUU truths, the bias is listed
at the GiBUU Eavail shape (cell R06, z = 2.01), at the
GiBUU hybrid truth (p = 8.0 × 10−29 ; cells R02, R06,

R07) and at the synthetic q3 tilt (p = 6.4 × 10−6 ): the
reported uncertainty does not cover the fake-data bias at
these GiBUU-like truths or at the q3 tilt. At the other
9 truths the bias passes both criteria: no cell beyond 2σ
and p ≥ 0.01.
Two GENIE v3 tunes, G18_10a_02_11b and
AR23_20i_00_000, were generated for this test and held
out of the analysis. Each reweights the simulated truth
by the tune’s shape ratio to GENIE CV, built as for the
Cmodel components; the detector response is the simulation’s and is not probed. For each tune, 20 Poisson
pseudo-experiments and a noise-free closure are compared
with the truth using the full reported covariance. Both
tunes satisfy both bias criteria: the pseudo-experiment
means give χ2 = 11.84 (p = 0.76) and 16.86 (p = 0.39),
respectively, with |z| ≤ 0.65 in every cell of the means
and closures. Both are GENIE tunes and use the same
quasielastic cross-section model; G18_10a_02_11b also
uses a Valencia-family MEC model, as does the GENIE +
MEC component of Cmodel . They provide partial evidence
and are not independent tests. Omitting that component
gives χ2 = 15.15 (p = 0.51) and 24.60 (p = 0.077), respectively, as a descriptive comparison. These tests assess
bias, not coverage: only event counts fluctuate, without
variation of systematic parameters, so interval widths
cannot be tested.

Weakening only the flux correlations by 10 % lowers the
χ2 of the 16-cell predictions by 24–48 %. The same change
to the detector or lateral correlations lowers it by at most
18 %. With the correlations of the interaction, flux, detector and lateral groups removed, every 16-cell prediction
keeps χ2 ≥ 45 for 16 degrees of freedom. The flux correlations are estimated from 100 hadron-production (PPFX)
flux universes [16], weighted by the neutrino–electron
and inverse-muon-decay constraint. Bootstrapping these
universes, with the flux variances kept, gives 16th–84th
percentile bands for each 16-cell χ2 , with edges within
6.2 % of the reported value and a median at most 1.0 %
above it. These bands describe the scatter of the χ2 from
estimating the correlations with these universes, not its
sensitivity to incorrect correlations, which the weakening
probes; they are not intervals for the χ2 under the true
correlations.

C.

Descriptive comparison of the central values

These comparisons use the three- and four-observable
central values of the joint-test estimator, without uncertainties. GENIE, the MINERvA Tune v1, NuWro, and
GiBUU all underpredict the unfolded central value at
low Eavail , between the quasielastic and ∆ peaks, the
region of the established low-recoil discrepancy [18, 25].
Comparisons with pion final-state-interaction variations,
GENIE’s Valencia 2p2h component, and the published
measurement are given in Ref. [14], Secs. 7.7 and 7.8.
The five-observable (Eavail , W ) map of the earlier
purity-subtracted central value, and its comparisons with
these generators, are given in Ref. [14], Secs. 8.4 and 8.5.

VI.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

The principal result is the sixteen-cell cross section of
Sec. V, with its covariance and generator comparison.
Two validation findings point to the unfolded result
retaining part of its prior. The first is the residual under
a generator-sized change of the truth in Sec. IV E, which
we interpret as regularization bias. The second is the
envelope of Sec. III A, which tracks the same bias and
alone exceeds the precision target in two of the three tested
reporting definitions. The note’s calibrated tests show
the same pull: their null distributions for the external
predictions are displaced upward by it, as the calibration
reproduces (Ref. [14], Sec. 11.5). The miscoverage of
Sec. IV F is also attributed to a residual bias, but whether
that bias has the same origin is not established.
The studies of Sec. IV show what must be checked
before unfolded intervals are interpreted: closure with the

A.

Limitations

The limitations of these results are stated with the
results they qualify, in Secs. III–V, and those of the calibrated tests in the companion note. The principal ones
are these. The two-dimensional covariance has not been
independently validated, and its coverage has not been
tested (Sec. IV C). The three- and four-observable central
values lack intervals of validated coverage (Sec. V C). The
sixteen-cell result also has total uncertainties, whose coverage is not validated; Sec. IV H reports the fake-data tests
of the estimator’s bias. The note’s calibrated tests reject
hybrid nulls, conditionally on the simulation’s hadronic
response, and the rejections carry over to the generator
predictions themselves only under a bound on the residual
below the fine grid, which has not been shown. Their
calibration is valid to the extent that the simulated chain

reproduces the analysis of the data (Ref. [14], Sec. 11.4).
