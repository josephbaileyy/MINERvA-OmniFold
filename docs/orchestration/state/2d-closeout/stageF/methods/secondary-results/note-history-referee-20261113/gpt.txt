**Your concern is justified: the note mixes a technical account with an audit trail.** Its unusually explicit limitations are valuable. The editing task is to preserve those disclosures while removing obsolete-product forensics, repeated chronology, and internal administrative language.

**1. Conventions**

What I can substantiate:

- **Public notes and internal supporting notes are different document classes.** ATLAS distinguishes public PUB notes from restricted internal material; CMS likewise distinguishes public notes from internal analysis documentation. “Technical note” does not imply that a development diary is expected. [ATLAS public notes](https://twiki.cern.ch/twiki/bin/view/AtlasPublic/PUBnotes), [CMS documentation categories](https://cms.org.tr/sites/cms.org.tr/files/constitution_061215.pdf).
- **Method/software reports organize around the implemented method, comparisons, validation, and limitations.** TUnfold follows that structure. RooUnfold also reports an error-propagation deficiency and its correction: adverse findings belong when they establish a technical point. [TUnfold](https://arxiv.org/html/1205.6201), [RooUnfold, §5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1105.1160).
- **OmniFold guidance emphasizes configuration choices, uncertainty construction, stress tests, and validation scope.** Those are appropriate technical content even when discovered during development. [A Practical Guide to Unbinned Unfolding](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09582v1).
- **Long documents need not be chronological.** MINERvA’s substantial NIM detector paper is organized around the detector, calibration, and performance. I have not verified a representative corpus establishing a separate convention for publicly released MINERvA technical notes. [MINERvA detector paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.5199).

My general editorial judgment—not an asserted collaboration rule—is that released supporting notes tolerate more diagnostic detail than journal papers, but usually present a **scientific rationale**, rather than the sequence of investigations and approvals. Design evolution belongs when it explains an enduring feature, failure mode, or limitation.

**2. An operational rule**

**Retain history if removing it would change a reader’s understanding of the reported object, its reproducibility, its evidential independence, or its limitations. Otherwise, use the ledger.**

| Material | Public note treatment |
|---|---|
| Alternatives tried and rejected | Keep a concise comparison if it explains the selected method or demonstrates sensitivity. Put exhaustive searches in the ledger. |
| Failed or stopped tests | Keep failures relevant to reported claims, including why a stopped test supplies no conclusion. Relocate detailed troubleshooting. |
| Superseded numbers/configurations | Keep only those used in a meaningful comparison or needed to identify which estimator a test assessed. Label them locally. |
| Choices relative to results | Keep consequential exposure and adaptation: what had been seen, what changed afterward, and which assessment was therefore exploratory. |
| Dates, rounds, record names | Keep release versions, data-taking periods, and identifiers needed for reproduction. Usually replace development dates and internal round names with functional descriptions. |

“Fixed before” needs an object: **before generating toys, inspecting pilot pulls, grading tests, or seeing observed statistics are different disclosures.** “Declared” alone establishes none of these.

An internal ledger may support the authors’ account, but essential protocols, limitations, and artifact identities must be available in the public note or deposit.

**4. Structure**

**Yes—a clean main text plus a compact “Development history” appendix is better**, provided the appendix is selective rather than a public copy of the ledger.

Use three layers:

- **Main text:** reported configurations and results, current validation, and limitations beside the claims they qualify. Retain a short disclosure paragraph identifying consequential choices made after results were inspected.
- **Technical appendices:** derivations and substantial diagnostic results, including relevant unsuccessful tests.
- **Development-history appendix:** perhaps two or three pages, with columns: configuration/test; purpose; outcome/reason superseded; result affected; applicability to the released result; information already inspected.

Include rows for estimator replacement, background-treatment changes, retired statistical bands, and consequential test adaptations. Exclude job chronology, internal review rounds, abandoned scripts, and repetitive regeneration checks.

Keep public-version changes in a separate brief changelog. In App. G, distinguish the deposited package from planned capabilities; preserve unresolved metadata as explicit status statements.
