**Referee report: manuscript on five-observable OmniFold unfolding of MINERvA medium-energy νμ open data**

---

## 1. Summary of the paper

The authors apply OmniFold, an unbinned ML unfolding method, to MINERvA medium-energy νμ open data. They unfold five observables at once: pT, p∥, Eavail, q3 and W. The two-observable (pT, p∥) result is benchmarked against the published double-differential cross section. It matches the integral to 1.0% but has χ²/ndf = 2.27 in shape, and the paper says the cause is not identified. The five-observable estimator is then put through a programme of closure, fake-data, numerical-stability, coverage and run-period tests, summarised in Table I. By the authors' own prespecified criteria it fails or stops several of these. The fake-data bias is flagged at a GiBUU-like hybrid truth and at a synthetic q3 tilt. The estimator fails a rounding-sensitivity stability requirement (2.6σ against a 1.0σ requirement). The coverage test was stopped before it ran, and the run periods disagree for an unknown cause. The sixteen-cell data result is therefore given only as "illustrative output, not for fitting or for constraining models" in an appendix.

## 2. Recommendation: reject in the present form (resubmission possible as a differently scoped paper)

The paper's honesty is its main virtue, and I want to credit it. Criteria were fixed in advance, failures are reported as failures, and the limitations are stated prominently. The difficulty is that the stated result is mostly negative or inconclusive, and several of the negative findings are unexplained when they could have been explained with feasible work.

- The two-dimensional benchmark disagrees in shape with the published result for an unknown reason.
- The method is shown to retain prior information at generator-sized truth changes.
- The estimator is numerically unstable at the level of floating-point rounding.
- The coverage test is untested because the pseudo-experiment machinery does not reproduce its own input covariance.
- The data show a run-period inconsistency of unknown origin.
- The central deliverable is explicitly "not a measurement".

PRD publishes measurements, validated methods and physics interpretation. A paper whose principal output is a list of failed internal acceptance tests, with the diagnosis deferred to an unpublished note (Ref. [14]), does not yet meet that bar. Many of the failures look like defects of the implementation or of the test harness, not intrinsic properties of OmniFold. Examples are the rounding instability, the pseudo-experiment ratios of 0.70–2.30, and the bias at the GiBUU truth. The authors could probably diagnose these. As written, the paper can neither be read as "OmniFold works on neutrino data" nor as "OmniFold fails for this reason". It records that one implementation did not pass the authors' tests.

## 3. Major issues

**1. The paper's headline finding, retention of the prior, is not diagnosed, and the diagnosis offered is inconsistent.**
- Section IV E reports residuals of up to 74% in the high-W cells at a GiBUU-like change. It says this is "consistent with finite-iteration regularization" for the GiBUU-like change only. It also says the residual is not removed by 200 iterations for two of the four changes tested (4.4% and 6.6% at 200 iterations) and that "the long runs do not converge", and "no single cause is established".
- The residual comparison between exact binned unfolding and boosted trees shows "about the same" median residuals. This points to a feature of the problem: sparse high-W cells with low reconstruction stability, in which any unfolding with a finite-iteration or smoothness regularizer must be prior-dominated. That is an information-content argument, not an OmniFold-specific one.
- The paper never separates (i) irreducible loss of information, (ii) iteration-count regularization, and (iii) classifier-induced smoothing or bias.
- Why it matters: the paper's stated purpose is "what unbinned unfolding delivers on neutrino data". The reader cannot tell whether the answer is "the same as binned IBU, with more machinery" or "something specific to the classifier".
- Required:
  - A controlled study in which the classifier is replaced by an oracle or an exact density ratio, to bound the classifier contribution.
  - A binned IBU at matching iteration count in the same five-dimensional coarse grid, compared cell by cell on the sixteen reporting cells, not only on the 109 joint cells.
  - A statement of the information content (for example the condition number or singular values of the coarse response) for the low-stability cells.

**2. Numerical instability under rounding-level perturbations is a defect to be fixed, not reported.**
- A result that moves by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations when inputs are perturbed at floating-point rounding level, while being bitwise independent of the training seed, is a sign of a discontinuous learner. The most likely mechanism is tree split thresholds on tied or near-tied feature values, with threshold selection flipping.
- The text says that each bootstrap replica carries this scatter, that it is "about 37% of the statistical variance" on the joint cells, and that this share "was not measured on the 16 reported cells". The headline cells are exactly where the share is unmeasured.
- Two-fold cross-fitting and two tree regularizations "found no variant that met the data-stability requirement". That is a narrow search. Plausible remedies include feature quantization or histogram binning with fixed edges, deterministic tie-breaking, larger `min_data_in_leaf`, bagging with an ensemble average, or averaging over a set of many rounding perturbations as part of the estimator.
- Averaging over perturbations would turn the instability into a noise term that can be propagated. Treating the instability as statistical noise that is "built in" to the bootstrap, without measuring it on the reported cells, is not adequate.
- Required:
  - Identify the mechanism.
  - Measure the rounding spread on the sixteen reporting cells.
  - Either fix it, or define the estimator as the ensemble mean over N perturbations and recompute the covariance with that estimator.
  - The authors also set a requirement of at most 1.0σ and then publish a result that fails by 2.6×. They should either meet it or explain why the requirement is not relevant to the claims.

**3. The coverage test was never run, so no uncertainty in the paper can be called an uncertainty.**
- The stopped test is a gate failure in the harness: reproduced-to-drawn ratio 0.70–2.30 in 11 of 16 cells. The authors state that "why the ratios depart from one has not been established".
- The most economical reading is a bug or a misspecification in the pseudo-experiment generator. Examples are the surrogate treatment of lateral and normalization uncertainties, the treatment of Cmodel and Cresp as "not at all" drawn, and the noted non-linearity of the unfolding in systematic shifts. That is a solvable problem. It should have been solved before submission, since a coverage test is the paper's own stated validation target.
- Without a coverage test, the 7–8% median uncertainties quoted throughout (Tables III and IV) are unvalidated. The authors also admit that the run-period block has a "68% interval on the ratio of its true to estimated size" of 0.73–2.56. This is a fair indicator of how uncertain the uncertainty is.
- Required: diagnose the pseudo-experiment mismatch (for example by a linear-response test on each of the 38 drawn bands separately) and complete the declared coverage test. If a full coverage test cannot be done, give a restricted but complete one, for example statistical-only coverage on the sixteen cells at nominal and shifted truths, so that the paper can say something positive about interval calibration.

**4. The benchmark against the published result is neither a validation nor a falsification, and its design is compromised.**
- Three analysis choices were made with the comparison in view: the learner implementation, the background-subtraction treatment, and in part the iteration count. The authors say so. The text then concludes that the comparison is "descriptive, not a blind validation". χ²/ndf was reduced from 2.65 to 2.27 by a post-hoc choice.
- χ²/ndf = 2.27 with a pseudo-inverse over 205 bins at a condition number of 1.5×10¹² is also not interpretable. The authors report that 73 best-determined directions give 0.66, which implies the 'excess' lies in poorly determined directions. They also report a single largest standardized component of 4.8 and nine more at 3.4–3.8. The paper cannot have it both ways: either the poorly determined modes are noise-dominated and the result is consistent, or they are not and the shape difference is real. The data presented support neither.
- The published analysis used ten iterations and a fake-data-driven choice, and the paper uses five. The authors decline to "make any statement" about the residual bias of the published analysis, which defeats the purpose of the benchmark.
- The flux uncertainty also differs (3.71% against 4.01%) "for a reason the released inputs do not let us determine".
- Required:
  - Report a truncated or regularized χ² with a pre-declared cutoff, or the χ² in the coarser, well-determined sub-space.
  - Show the benchmark at ten iterations with the published analysis' own subtraction, as an apples-to-apples comparison.
  - Show the benchmark with fixed (not selected) choices for the learner, with seed and implementation variance as the error.
  - Show the shape differences projected on pT and p∥ with a bin-by-bin statement of where the discrepancy sits (the high-p∥ poorly determined bins are already named). If the open-data release differs from the data used in the published analysis, say that explicitly and quantify what would be expected from it.

**5. The five-observable measurement has not been shown to add information, and the conceptual design of the observable set is partly redundant.**
- The paper acknowledges that reconstructed W is an exact function of reconstructed (pT, p∥, q3) (up to a clamp), that truth W is fixed by (pT, p∥, q3) only up to the beam-axis angle and M, and that the five inputs "do not provide five independent constraints". The effective dimensionality is therefore four, and one observable (Eavail) is largely independent.
- The paper's own words describe the added dimensions as difficult to constrain: low reconstruction stability (0.16–0.25) in four of sixteen cells, and cells flagged in the fake-data tests (R02, R06, R07).
- No result in the paper shows a quantity that the five-observable unfolding delivers and that a binned multidimensional unfolding of the same coarse grid does not. The introduction's claim that OmniFold makes higher-dimensional unfolding "possible without specifying a separate binned response tensor" is therefore not tested against the alternative, a coarse binned 5D IBU at the same sixteen cells. A coarse 5D response matrix of order 35 cells squared is perfectly tractable.
- Required: this direct comparison (binned IBU vs OmniFold on the same 16 cells, same fake-data tests), and a discussion of whether the benefit is more than a convenience in the choice of binning.

**6. The fake-data protocol has weaknesses that limit what failures and passes mean.**
- The truths are "not held out", they are the simulation prior or hybrids of it. Only two GENIE v3 tunes are held out. They share a quasielastic model with each other and share a Valencia-family MEC model with a Cmodel component, so the authors concede the tests are "not independent" and "partial evidence".
- Only the event counts fluctuate. The detector response is not varied in the fake-data tests. The recoil-response shift is tested only descriptively in separate closures, with no pass threshold and a median 0.50σ (max 0.97σ) under a ±4% scale.
- The reported covariance is tested against the bias with the truth's own generator component removed from Cmodel. This is a reasonable design. It makes the pass or fail depend on the weight placed on a component the paper itself calls uncalibrated ("unscaled generator-shape dependence, not a calibrated bound").
- Between the two covariance definitions, the flag status changes for GiBUU Eavail (z = 2.01 vs 1.76 with CPL) and for NuWro (pT, p∥, Eavail) (p = 0.0027 under Ctotal alone). Hence "pass" for 10 of 12 truths depends on the run-period block, which was added after the discrepancy was seen and is rank 2 with three groups. Passing is partly produced by an ad hoc covariance inflation.
- Required:
  - A fake-data test with varied detector response (at least recoil scale and the MINOS and muon-energy scales).
  - More than two held-out truths, preferably from independent models (for example a different generator family).
  - A statement of which of the 12 truths are independent of the development of the estimator and of Cmodel.

**7. The run-period inconsistency is an unresolved data-quality problem, not a covariance feature.**
- Groups differ "beyond their bootstrap scatter". In two dimensions there is a step of about 7% near 8 GeV/c in p∥ for one period group. The authors say they cannot establish which period is correct.
- A 7% step in a single-variable distribution between run groups, relative to each group's own simulation, points to a specific, identifiable cause: flux or beam-configuration modelling, a detector-calibration change or a MINOS-related efficiency, or a playlist-specific simulation mis-modelling. It would be visible in unfolding-independent quantities, for example reconstructed-level data/MC ratios per playlist and per p∥ bin.
- The paper offers no reconstruction-level diagnosis, which is the cheapest and most informative. It also does not show whether the published MINERvA analysis, which used the same playlists, saw the same effect.
- Folding the discrepancy into a rank-2 covariance estimated from three groups, then showing that the "reported" tests pass with it, gives a covariance that is, by the authors' own estimate, uncertain by a factor of about three. It also reduces the generator-comparison χ² by 3.6–29.3% (7.4–40.4% for shape).
- Required: a reconstruction-level run-period study (data/MC per playlist at reco level in the observables involved, and in the muon-matching efficiency as a function of p∥). Then either identify the cause and correct, or exclude the discrepant period group and present the result for the consistent set.

**8. The Eavail definition and treatment, and the target differences, depart from published conventions, and the comparisons across generators are weakened by the carbon vs CH correction.**
- Eavail uses a constant of 135 MeV rather than the charged-pion mass and is not a published definition. This is an acceptable choice. The authors show first-order sensitivity (0.19σ per cell, 0.42σ coherent), without retraining. A first-order estimate of a change that requires re-weighting of reconstructed energies is not a demonstration. The retrained result should be shown, at least for one variant.
- The CH correction to NuWro and GiBUU carbon predictions adds a free-proton sample with weight 1/13 per nucleon. The authors report that the NuWro free-proton supplement at 50–100 GeV is flagged because generator aborts were "not shown to be negligible", and that the GiBUU free-proton sample "failed its sampling-closure check" and has 46% of events with energy non-conservation. The GiBUU χ² sign even changes depending on the hydrogen sample (Table VI footnotes and Sec. A 3).
- A 1/13 weight per nucleon is the mass-number fraction, but the paper's own statement that hydrogen lacks QE, 2p2h and coherent channels shows a nucleon-count correction is not a faithful CH prediction. A native CH generation of NuWro was done. It agrees only "within a tolerance fixed in advance" at Σz² = 17.9 for 16 cells with largest |z| = 2.2, which is borderline.
- Required: use the native CH NuWro as the primary prediction. Drop the GiBUU CH comparison or generate a correct GiBUU CH sample. The comparison Table VI is otherwise carrying numbers the authors themselves flag as unreliable.

**9. The paper's scope and structure are unclear, and the evidence is largely outsourced to an unpublished note.**
- The abstract and Introduction say the paper is a "methods and validation study". The reader is then given a small number of derived numbers and pointed to Ref. [14] for the basis of nearly every claim. Roughly forty citations of Ref. [14] by section appear, many carrying essential content: the calibrated tests of predictions, the construction of Cmodel, the stopped coverage test, the seed and rounding studies, and the cross-fitting probe.
- A PRD referee must be able to judge the paper from its text. Several sentences in the paper cannot be evaluated without the note. Examples:
  - "the coverage of Cmodel at truths held out..."
  - the 'calibrated tests' null distributions "displaces upward and the calibration reproduces that displacement"
  - the "learner dependence" of the 2D covariance, which is omitted from it
- The validation programme in Table I is given as a table of results without enough information to repeat any item.
- Required: either include the essential material, or restrict the claims to what the paper shows. If the note is the validation record, the paper should be a short communication summarising a small number of clearly stated conclusions.

## 4. Stated limitations and qualifications: classification

(a) = real deficiency to resolve with further analysis; (b) = acceptable honestly stated limitation; (c) = belongs in the companion note.

1. **Unvalidated interval coverage (including the stopped coverage test).** (a). The paper treats coverage as the criterion for interpreting unfolded intervals. A stopped test with an unexplained pseudo-experiment mismatch is a harness defect. Resolution: find the cause of the 0.70–2.30 ratios (for example by band-by-band linearity tests) and run the test, at least for the statistical plus linearly propagated systematic part. Nothing else in the uncertainty discussion can be taken as established until this is done.
2. **Run-period inconsistency of unknown cause.** (a). A reconstruction-level study per playlist could localize it. The 7% step near 8 GeV/c may be a flux, selection or acceptance issue. An inflated covariance is not a resolution.
3. **Fake-data bias exceeding thresholds (GiBUU hybrid, q3 tilt).** Split.
   - (b) for the observation that the five-observable unfolding retains prior information at truth changes as large as the generator spread. That is an honest and useful statement, and it is a general property of regularized unfolding.
   - (a) for the claim that the quoted uncertainties contain this bias. The Cmodel term is "not a calibrated bound", and the q3 tilt (p < 10⁻⁵, χ² = 52) and the GiBUU hybrid (χ² = 171, 247 cells beyond 2σ under Ctotal) show it does not. The fake-data bias should be assigned as an explicit, truth-conditioned systematic, or the result restricted to cells where the bias is shown to be small. The mixed status of "pass at 10 of 12" under an ad hoc covariance does not suffice.
4. **Stability failure (2.6σ against 1.0σ, median 0.8σ against 0.3σ).** (a). This is the most clearly fixable defect. Identify the source and either remove it or define the estimator as an ensemble mean. As it stands, the headline result is not reproducible to within its statistical uncertainty under benign changes of input representation. The paper currently leaves the share of rounding variance on the reported cells unmeasured.
5. **Shape difference from the published result for an unidentified reason (χ²/ndf = 2.27).** (a) in its current form, because the χ² is dominated by poorly conditioned directions and the benchmark settings were chosen with the comparison in view (see Major Issue 4). It is (b) once a pre-declared, truncated statistic and an apples-to-apples comparison are provided. The integral agreement (1.010) is a fair positive result.
6. **Low-stability cells (R02, R06, R10, R15; stability 0.16–0.25).** (b) for the fact that low-purity cells exist, provided the cells are clearly flagged. They are flagged in Table III. But (a) for the fact that R02 and R06 are exactly the cells that fail the fake-data tests. The authors should either merge or drop these cells, or make clear that they are prior-dominated. A reporting scheme that retains cells for which the unfolding has little information is questionable.
7. **Target differences (carbon vs CH, free-proton correction).** (a) for the NuWro and GiBUU comparisons as presented. They use a weighted free-proton supplement whose sample is flagged by the authors as failing checks, and the correction changes the sign of the GiBUU χ² effect. Use the native CH sample for NuWro, and either generate a valid CH sample for GiBUU or drop it. (b) for the GENIE comparisons.
8. **Definitional departures (Eavail with 135 MeV, W with struck-nucleon mass, truth angle from the beam axis).** (b), since the definitions are stated and used consistently. The first-order sensitivity estimates are acceptable as an estimate but should be replaced by a retrained variant. The statement that truth W is "fixed by (pT, p∥, q3) only up to" an event-by-event angle and M is a real conceptual caveat for anyone comparing with theory predictions. The authors should say whether published theory predictions can be put in these definitions without a generator.
9. **Choices made with the benchmark in view (learner, background subtraction, iteration count).** (b) as stated, with the consequence that the benchmark is "not a blind validation". The paper handles this honestly. The remedy lies in Major Issue 4.
10. **No held-out or cross-fitted classifier training; classifiers trained on the sample they weight.** (a). This is a known source of overfitting and prior leakage in classifier-based unfolding, and could contribute to both the residual bias and the instability. The authors tried two-fold cross-fitting only in a probe on pseudo-data. A cross-fitted version should be carried through the whole analysis, on data, if it removes either failure.
11. **Cmodel not calibrated, with its size dominating the generator-comparison χ² (Table VII, removal changes χ² by up to +119).** (b) with the proviso that it is stated. It is (a) if any comparison with generators is to be given quantitative meaning. The two-dimensional covariance also omits learner dependence and regularization dependence. These should be quantified or the statement narrowed.
12. **Pointwise, not simultaneous, coverage tests.** (b).
13. **Calibrated tests of generator predictions are conditional on the simulated hadronic response and carry no physical exclusion.** (c). The paper itself says these tests are not physical exclusions, and they are tangential. Move to the note or drop.
14. **Earlier-estimator coverage failures (coverage as low as 0.06 for 68% intervals) and the earlier covariance that fails its own acceptance.** (c). They describe a superseded estimator. Their presence in Table I and Sec. IV E invites the reader to apply them to the reported result. Either remove them or explain what they imply for the current estimator.
15. **Neutron and muon deposits not covered by Cresp; response only a scale.** (a) for neutron deposits in the hadronic recoil, which are a leading uncertainty on Eavail and q0 at these energies. (b) for muon deposits. The response shift is also not varied in the fake-data tests. A neutron-response variation should be added.
16. **Flux uncertainty larger in the published analysis (4.01% vs 3.71%) for an undetermined reason.** (b). A one-line note is enough, but a comparison with the published flux treatment should be easy to do.
17. **Unmeasured share of rounding variance on the reported cells.** (a), as part of item 4.
18. **Data and code availability: a "candidate release package... not yet deposited", and results that cannot be regenerated without unreleased products.** (a) for a methods paper. Reproducibility is the claim, and the code should be deposited at submission.

## 5. Resolve or move out? Is the paper publishable? Is anything disqualifying?

I would prefer that the authors resolve items 1, 2, 3 (the bias assignment), 4, 5 (benchmark statistic), 7 (target treatment) and 10, and move items 13 and 14 out. As submitted, the paper is not publishable in PRD as a methods-and-validation paper. This is not because it reports failures, since a PRD paper may publish failures if they are diagnosed. It is because the failures are, in large part, not diagnosed. Several of them (rounding sensitivity, the coverage-gate mismatch, the run-period step, the shape difference) have specific, testable explanations the authors have not tested. Publishing as is would put a catalogue of open problems on the record without the contribution that would justify it.

No single limitation is disqualifying on its own. Together, the pairing of (i) no validated uncertainty and (ii) an unstable estimator is disqualifying for a paper whose output is a cross-section, which is why the authors rightly decline to call it a measurement. A paper that makes no measurement claim can be acceptable provided the methodological conclusion is firm. At present it is not firm: the paper cannot say whether the failures are due to OmniFold, the implementation, the data or the test design. The two conditions I would regard as necessary and sufficient for publication are a completed coverage test (or an explicit statistical-only version) and a stable (or ensemble-defined) estimator, plus the direct binned-IBU comparison that would put the negative findings in context.

## 6. Minor issues and presentation

1. Length and register: the paper reads as a compressed test log, with a very high density of numbers, conditionals and cross-references. The reader cannot hold the logic of the argument. Table I carries about 17 rows of results, each with a result string that needs three clauses of context. A shorter main text with a single, clearly structured table (test, criterion, result, pass or fail) would help a lot.
2. The abstract is a single 200-word paragraph with five findings joined by semicolons, and it uses "p < 0.01, or any cell with |z| > 2" without saying what is being tested. Rewrite for a general PRD reader.
3. Terminology: "joint cells", "joint-test estimator", "reporting cells", "coarse cells", "fine grid", "supported box", "support rule", "hybrid truth", "development variant" are used without a single consolidated definition. A glossary or a schematic of the grid hierarchy (35 coarse grid → 32 boxes → 16 cells; 109 joint cells; 205 bins; 153 quantities) is needed.
4. The multiple estimators (reproducible two-observable LightGBM; joint-test LightGBM; development variant with 100 trees and 8 leaves; earlier purity-subtracted estimator) are used for different results. The reader is told that "results from the two estimators are not combined". A table matching each reported number to its estimator would prevent misreading.
5. Section cross-references are wrong or inconsistent: the Introduction says "Sec. VI what the study shows... principal limitations in Sec. VI A", while the Sec. IV G material and Sec. V are not announced. The text refers to "Sec. A 3" and "Sec. VI A, item 1" in tables without clarity.
6. Figure 1 caption: "Here the two bands are correlated, so their overlap is not a compatibility test." If so, say what test is intended. The bottom-left panel (standardized residuals on a pT-p∥ grid) is hard to read. Figure 2 shows residuals up to ±100% and is labelled a "development diagnostic, not a validation". Its grey bands come from "an earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance, not the reported one". A figure that does not show the reported result with the reported covariance should not be a main-text figure. Figure 3 has an uninformative caption ("can be negative"). The figure panel text, as extracted, is not legible.
7. Table II: the column structure is hard to read (the Cov., χ², p, cell-count and max-|z| columns for the two covariances run together), and the cells beyond 2σ are listed as counts in one place and names in another. It is also unclear why "Ctotal" columns omit max |z|.
8. Statistical conventions: the paper quotes p < 10⁻²⁷ and p < 10⁻³⁰ from χ² with 16 dof under a covariance it has described as uncalibrated. Such tail probabilities are not meaningful. Quote χ² and cell-level z only.
9. The "no finite-ensemble correction is applied" for covariances from 50 bootstrap replicas and 100 flux universes (effective number 19) on 16×16 matrices should be addressed: the inverse of a covariance estimated from 50 replicas is biased (Hartlap-type factor), which matters for the χ² values in Tables II and VI. The flux effective number of 19 for a 16-dimensional covariance is borderline. This is also relevant to the Hotelling-versus-χ² distinction for any p-values.
10. The uncertainty on the normalization is a flat 1.40% in every cell; justify it.
11. The uncertainty tables are given to 0.1% with no indication of precision of the underlying estimates. Table V is garbled in the typeset version and should be provided as a machine-readable supplement only.
12. The language "not a measurement" appears repeatedly (abstract, Sec. I, V, VI, Appendix A, Table III and Fig. 4 captions). A single clear statement is enough.
13. Table VI gives χ² values of 107–262 for 16 dof, which the paper then says establish no exclusion. The reader will wonder why the comparison is included at all. If it carries no weight, drop it from the appendix; if it does, defend it.
14. The introduction cites H1 (eight observables) and ATLAS (twenty-four) unfolding results but does not discuss the closest neutrino precedent, namely the multidimensional MINERvA and T2K analyses, in the context of the novelty claim. The statement "Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions [8–11]" should be matched to what is claimed here (five, with only about four independent).
15. Data availability: the dependence on an "earlier production" of the open-data release should be stated with the difference from the current release. The 2,374 files are identified only in the note.
16. Typographical: "Sec. VI" in the roadmap should be Secs. V and VI. The description of the flux "Φ = 6.243 × 10⁻⁸ cm⁻² per POT from 0 to 100 GeV" is given without the corresponding published value.

## 7. Scores

(i) SCORE: 3/10

(ii) Main contribution as I read it: a carefully documented, preregistered-style negative-result study showing that one implementation of five-observable OmniFold on MINERvA open data reproduces the published integrated cross section within about 1% but retains prior information, is numerically unstable at rounding level and has unvalidated uncertainties.

(iii) Another journal: with the diagnoses completed, a methods-oriented venue such as Journal of Instrumentation, Computer Physics Communications, or Machine Learning: Science and Technology (or a data-analysis or ML-for-HEP workshop proceedings) would fit better than PRD.
