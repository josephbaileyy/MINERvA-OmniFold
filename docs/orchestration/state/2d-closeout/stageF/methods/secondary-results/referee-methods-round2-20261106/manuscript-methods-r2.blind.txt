We test what unbinned unfolding with OmniFold delivers on neutrino data, unfolding MINERvA
medium-energy open data simultaneously in five observables: muon transverse and longitudinal
momenta, available hadronic energy, three-momentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass. Four
findings result. The unfolding reproduces the published muon-kinematic cross-section integral within
about 1%, but differs from it in shape, over many eigenmodes of the published covariance, for a reason
not identified. The five-observable result retains part of its prior: fake-data studies at a GiBUU-like
truth and at a synthetic momentum-transfer tilt find residual bias exceeding uncertainty-based
criteria (p < 0.01, or any cell with |z| > 2) that were fixed before the tests were graded. The estimator
is bitwise independent of its training seed, yet rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by
up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, beyond a stability requirement fixed in advance. A coverage
test declared in advance was stopped at a preliminary check, because its pseudo-experiments did not
reproduce the covariance blocks they draw; the coverage of the reported uncertainty intervals has
not been validated, and the run periods differ for a cause not known. The five-observable output on
data is therefore given only as an illustration, not for fitting or for constraining models.

I.

INTRODUCTION

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their
interpretation depends on correcting detector response
and on assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned
migration matrix [1] is widely used, but its response
tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space
through classifier-based density-ratio estimation [2, 3].
This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding
possible without specifying a separate binned response
tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been
applied to collider data, for example to eight observables
in deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [4] and to twenty-four
Z+jets observables at ATLAS [5] (see Ref. [6] for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on
a public simulated T2K near-detector sample [7]. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions [8–11].
This paper is a methods and validation study of a
simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on
real neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA
medium-energy νµ data from the open-data release [12], in
the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta pT and p∥ ,
the available hadronic energy Eavail , the three-momentum
transfer q3 , and the hadronic invariant mass W . Reconstructed W is fixed by the reconstructed (pT , p∥ , q3 ), so

the five observables are simultaneous but not independent. The unfolding is benchmarked against the published
double-differential cross section d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) from the
same data [13], and the five-observable estimator is tested
for bias, numerical stability, coverage, and run-period
consistency.

DATA, SIMULATION, AND OBSERVABLES

The data come from the twelve MINERvA mediumenergy, forward-horn-current run periods (playlists 1A–1G
and 1L–1P), corresponding to 1.057 × 1021 protons on target, with an average neutrino energy near 6 GeV [13].
The input is an earlier production of the MINERvA
open-data release (see Data availability), in the form
of pre-selected analysis ntuples (AnaTuples) [12]. The
detector, including the downstream MINOS near detector that serves as a muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref. [15]. The signal is a νµ charged-current interaction, with any final state, whose true vertex lies in the
tracker fiducial volume (5980 < z < 8422 mm, inside
a hexagon of apothem 850 mm). The signal muon has
θµ < 20◦ , 1.5 < p∥ < 60 GeV/c, and pT ≤ 4.5 GeV/c. Selected simulated events outside this definition, including
νµ charged-current events whose muon lies outside these
limits, are background. The selection requires a reconstructed vertex in the same volume and a reconstructed
muon with θµ < 20◦ matched to a MINOS track of negative charge, together with a dead-time requirement, as
in Ref. [13]. The flux treatment follows Ref. [16]. For
the results in more than two observables, the integrated
flux is Φ = 6.243 × 10−8 cm−2 per proton on target, from
0 to 100 GeV, after the neutrino–electron-scattering constraint and averaged over the twelve playlists by exposure

(Ref. [14], Sec. 3.4). The analysis uses 4.12 million selected
data events. The simulation contains 32.8 million unweighted true signal events and 20.5 million selected simulated events, and it is scaled to the data exposure. The
simulated events are generated with GENIE 2.12.6 [17]
and reweighted with the MINERvA Tune v1, which applies a non-resonant pion reduction, a random-phaseapproximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil
two-particle–two-hole (2p2h) enhancement [18]. Event
selection is the same for every number of observables.

p four-vectors, and the hadronic invariant mass W =

The fine grid is the analysis binning: the published
14 × 16 muon-momentum bins, crossed with 7 Eavail , 7 q3 ,
and 6 W bins. A coarse 35 grid in (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W )
has its edges at the fine edges nearest the simulated tertiles.
A coarse cell is kept if its expected reconstructed signal
and its truth weight at the data exposure are both at
least 1000 events. We call this requirement the support

on which the note’s joint tests are evaluated. The others,
which hold 0.47 % of the expected signal, stay in the
total only. The construction of the coarse grid is given in
Ref. [14], Sec. 11.1.
III.

CROSS-SECTION EXTRACTION

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The
event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including
unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates
a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth
events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated
flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate
efficiency correction is applied.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin b
carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1−Bb /Db ), where Db
and Bb are the data and predicted background counts in
that bin, so the classifier’s target density is D(x) wpur (b).
The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds
simulated background events with negative weight to the
data, so that the target is D(x) − B(x) pointwise. A
classifier refinement [24], clipped at zero, converts the
signed sample to non-negative weights before the first
iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever
Bb < Db but differ inside a bin wherever the background
fraction varies within it (Ref. [14], App. C).
The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees [25, 26]
of nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps, and negative-weight background subtraction
with a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves.
The two-observable estimator, LightGBM [26] at a fixed
seed, gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance (Sec. IV B). This reproducible central value is
that of its own systematic-universe ensemble, so its covariance is built with the same estimator. The threeand four-observable central values and the sixteen-cell
five-observable result (Sec. V) use the joint-test estimator,
a deterministic LightGBM with the same background
treatment and refinement capacity, named for the note’s
calibrated joint tests of generator predictions, which use
it (Ref. [14], Sec. 11). Its seeds are fixed, so its training is

reproducible, and it also gives the held-out shape tests of
Fig. 2b. This estimator removes most of the bias of puritybased subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-W cells
(Ref. [14], Sec. 9.2). Results from the two estimators are
not combined. The other configurations used below, and
their results, are described in Ref. [14], Sec. 3.2.
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the
unfolding. No learner setting, whether capacity, learning
rate, refinement capacity or iteration count, was tuned on
data. The nominal settings are the scikit-learn defaults,
with LightGBM matched to their capacity. The refinement capacity, which the two estimators share, was chosen
in simulation closure studies, and so were the joint-test
estimator’s five iterations (below). The two-observable
estimator uses five iterations following common practice;
this choice was checked on data, against ten iterations
with purity-based subtraction and against the published
result, without a search over the iteration count (Ref. [14],
Sec. 3.2). Two other choices for the two-dimensional result
were made with the comparison to the published result
in view: the learner implementation and the backgroundsubtraction treatment (Sec. IV B). That comparison is
therefore not an independent test of the method. Each
classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it
then sets, so no held-out or cross-fitted split is used; it is
trained without early stopping, and the two classes keep
their physical weights (Ref. [14], Sec. 3.1).
The joint-test estimator keeps five iterations because 10,
15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model dependence,
measured as the largest residual proxy over the truth
changes used in its development. In the noise-free study,
this measure on the 27 coarser joint cells is 0.314 at five
iterations and at least 0.384 at 10, 15 or 20; a classifier of
400 trees and 31 leaves lowers it by 8 %, short of the 20 %
the rule required for a change (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4).
A.

Reporting cells

The joint-test estimator’s output on the data is given,
as illustrative output with total uncertainties, on 16 cells,
R00–R15 (Appendix A). Other reporting definitions of
a five-observable measurement, with their precision, are
studied in Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4. They come from the 32 boxes
formed by one interior edge per axis, at pT 0.7, p∥ 4.5 and
q3 2 GeV/c, and at Eavail 0.8 and W 1.8 GeV. In forming
the cells, each supported box (one that meets the support
rule) with purity, the fraction of the simulated signal
reconstructed in it that was generated in it, below 0.1 is
merged into a neighbour, 4 boxes remain unreported, and
every reported cell passes the support rule. The result’s
covariance is given in Sec. IV A.

IV.

UNCERTAINTIES AND THE VALIDATION
PROGRAMME

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the
prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated
experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The closure, deformation, and coverage
studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events
used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed
and true distributions together and keeps the detector
response at fixed truth. Throughout, the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune v1. Every study uses the analysis
simulation’s detector response.
A.

The reported covariance

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is
Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D . The term Ctotal sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization
uncertainties of the joint-test estimator and Cmodel ; the
lateral uncertainties shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than reweighting events
(Appendix A 2). The model term Cmodel is built from the
change in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted
to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn. Its
contributions come from those generators’ samples in
their own definitions, with NuWro and GiBUU on carbon and GENIE CV as the common reference, not from
the predictions compared in Sec. A 3. The comparisons
there with GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU therefore use covariance contributions derived from the same
generators. It is a measure of unscaled generator-shape
dependence, not a calibrated bound (Ref. [14], Sec. 10.3).
The response term Cresp is the response of the result to a
one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil
energy in the simulation. The scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and other deposits. Cresp
does not cover neutron or muon deposits, nor species,
energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale.
It enters only this result, its fake-data tests and its comparison in Sec. A 3. The unfolded results of the three
run-period groups, each relative to its own simulation,
differ by more than their bootstrap scatter. The offsets of
the twelve playlists differ between the three period groups
by more than expected from their playlist-to-playlist scatter (Ref. [14], Sec. 10.4). The cause is not known, and
these data cannot establish which period is correct. The
run-period block CPL,5D includes this inconsistency in the
reported covariance. It is built from the double ratios
among the groups, each group’s unfolded result over its
own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the reference, so it contains the differences by construction. It
was added after these differences were seen. It has rank 2
and is not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains,
and with three groups its size is poorly determined: the

68 % interval on the ratio of its true to its estimated size
is 0.73–2.56. The median per-cell relative uncertainty is
7.86%, 7.29% without CPL,5D , and 6.74% without CPL,5D
or Cresp . The block raises the median by about 7.8 %,
and the uncertainty of the most affected cell, R04, by
14.1 %. The fake-data tests and the generator comparison
are given with and without it (Sec. IV G, Appendix A 3;
Ref. [14], Sec. 10.4).
Every χ2 of this result inverts the full covariance of the
cells compared, 16×16 or 8×8, by Cholesky decomposition
and without truncation, so its number of degrees of freedom is the number of cells (one fewer for the shape-only
χ2 ). Of the blocks, the statistical block is the unbiased
sample covariance of 50 bootstrap replicas, and the flux
block a covariance of 100 weighted universes (effective
number 19), normalized by the sum of the weights. The
others are a 2p2h band, two-point shifts, chosen models,
the normalization, the run-period block, built from three
exposure-weighted periods, and the training-seed block,
which is zero; each comparison with a prediction adds
Vg , the diagonal covariance from the finite size of the
prediction’s own simulated sample. No finite-ensemble
correction is applied. Appendix A 2 lists the systematic
groups and gives the per-group uncertainties and the
correlation matrix of the reported covariance; the note
gives each block’s ensemble and normalization (Ref. [14],
Sec. 10.3).
B.

Two-dimensional benchmark

The two-dimensional result benchmarks the unfolding
against a measurement from the same data. Its integrated
cross section is 3.069×10−38 cm2 /nucleon, compared with
3.039 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published analysis, a
ratio of 1.010. The standardized bin differences formed
with the published uncertainties have an RMS of 0.535.
Against the full published covariance, the descriptive
covariance-weighted distance is χ2 /ndf = 2.27. It is
computed with the pseudo-inverse over the 205 reported
bins, which keeps all 205 eigenmodes of the published
covariance (condition number 1.5 × 1012 ), and ndf is the
number of reported bins. The value is descriptive, and
no p-value is quoted for it. It indicates a correlated
shape difference, not a normalization difference, spread
over many eigenmodes of the published covariance: its
normalization part is χ2 = 0.04, against 465 for the shape.
In the eigenbasis of the published covariance, the largest
standardized component is 4.8 and the next nine 3.4–3.8;
the ten smallest-eigenvalue modes carry 9.6 % of the χ2 ,
and the 73 best-determined directions (λ/λmax > 10−4 )
give χ2 /ndf = 0.66. Of the six leading modes, three are
poorly determined directions (λ/λmax ≲ 4 × 10−9 ) in the
highest-p∥ bins, and three are moderate-λ modes (4×10−6
to 6 × 10−5 ) below them (Fig. 3; Ref. [14], Sec. 6.4). The
source of the shape difference has not been identified.
We quote the LightGBM result as the central value
because its covariance uses the same estimator. Light-

GBM, rather than the scikit-learn exact-split estimator
at the same settings, was chosen after both, and a third
implementation, the scikit-learn histogram estimator, had
been compared with the published result. With puritybased subtraction and with ndf the 205 reported bins,
their χ2 /ndf values were 3.66 for exact-split, 2.65 for
LightGBM and 2.70 for the histogram estimator (a mean
over ten seeds), and the distance depends on the estimator (Ref. [14], Sec. 6.4). The refined negative-weight
background treatment was chosen on a closure test in simulation. With the simulation’s own truth as the target,
the 20-seed mean residual of the purity-based estimate
reached a maximum over the 205 bins of 5.9, in units
of the data result’s per-bin statistical uncertainty; the
corresponding maximum for the refined estimate was 0.44.
In the closure, the two estimates differed by more than
one such unit in 14 of the 205 bins. That choice was made
on the closure test, before the refined result’s comparison
with the published result was computed. It was adopted
in the analysis only after that comparison was known,
and the change lowered χ2 /ndf from 2.65 to 2.27. The
χ2 /ndf values quoted for the two-dimensional comparison
are therefore conditional on both choices, and the comparison should be read as a descriptive comparison, not a
blind validation. The purity-subtracted exact-split result
differs from the central value by a median of 1.03 % per
bin (maximum 17.6 %; Ref. [14], Sec. 3.2).
C.

Two-dimensional covariance

The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four
blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central
value’s estimator: systematic universes (187 universes,
including the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap
replicas), run period (three period groups), and training
seed (five seed settings, so that its own size is poorly determined). The resulting relative uncertainty has a median
of 6.41 % per bin and is 4.76 % on the integrated cross
section, compared with 6.85 % and 4.61 % for the publication. The published flux uncertainty (median 4.01 %)
is larger than ours (3.71 %), for a reason the released
inputs do not let us determine. The covariance omits the
approximation in the systematic-universe unfoldings for
events that fail reconstruction, the learner dependence of
Sec. IV B, and the dependence on the regularization, while
the dependence on the prior’s generator shape is reported
beside it (below). Details are in Ref. [14], Secs. 6.3, 4.5,
and 4.6.
The statistical block, with a median statistical uncertainty of 0.676 %, is a bootstrap of 300 replicas drawn at
the central value’s training seed, in which the background
templates are also resampled.

relative to its own simulation, differs from that of the

E.

Generator-sized truth changes

Figure 2 shows that the unfolding recovers only part
of a change of the Eavail shape as large as the difference
between generators. When the true shape is reweighted by
the GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio, the (Eavail , W ) cells unfolded
with a development variant of the joint-test estimator (a
100-tree, 8-leaf refinement), not the reported estimator,
deviate from it by several percent in the largest cells
and by up to 74 % at high W , in the cell 0.8 ≤ Eavail <
1.5 GeV, W ≥ 3 GeV. These deviations occur with or
without background, so background subtraction alone
does not explain the residual. In a noise-free comparison in
simulation on the 109 joint cells, an exact binned iterative
unfolding of the same development truth changes leaves
about the same median residual at five iterations: 8.3 %,

5.4 %, 4.9 % and 5.3 % for the GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail
change, a NuWro Eavail shape, a NuWro (pT , p∥ , Eavail )
shape and a q3 stress test, against 9.5 %, 5.4 %, 5.6 % and
4.3 % for the boosted trees. Longer exact iteration removes
much of the residual of the GiBUU-to-GENIE change (its
median falls to 3.3 % at 200 iterations) and part of the
NuWro Eavail one, but not that of the other two (4.4 %
and 6.6 % at 200), and the long runs do not converge.
The residual is therefore consistent with finite-iteration
regularization for the GiBUU-like change, but not for
every change tested, and no single cause is established
(Ref. [14], Sec. 9.3). For other fixed generator shapes
tested with the joint-test estimator, it is 16 % to 31 %
in the most affected (Eavail , W ) cell and lies within the
quoted total uncertainty in about half of those cells. Those
uncertainties come from an earlier purity-subtracted fiveobservable covariance that fails its specified acceptance
criteria (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.6). For the sixteen-cell result,
Sec. IV G reports the fake-data tests of the estimator’s
bias. The studies, their scope and their seed sets are in
Ref. [14], Secs. 9.2 and 9.3.
Statistical intervals of an earlier five-observable estimator with purity-based subtraction, corrected for its
closure bias at nominal truth, miscover at the tested
truths, most strongly when the true signal rate is lowered by about 10 %, with coverage as low as 0.06 for 68%
intervals (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.5).
F.

Sensitivity to input rounding

The joint-test estimator is unstable under floatingpoint rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, the reported quantities scatter
by a median of 0.23 % per joint cell, against a median
data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty of 0.37 %, and each
bootstrap replica carries this scatter at the same size.
Rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by
up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of the
lower-capacity development variant by up to 2.5, so the
estimator fails a data-stability requirement of at most 0.3
(median) and 1.0 (maximum) statistical standard deviations. The training-seed block of Sec. IV A is zero because,
in the unfolds we ran, the result is bitwise independent
of the training seed and of input row order; this is distinct from the sensitivity to rounding-level input changes
quoted above. That sensitivity enters the reported statistical block by construction: each of its 50 bootstrap
replicas refits the whole unfolding with its own roundinglevel perturbation of the inputs. On the finer joint cells
(this share was not measured on the 16 reported cells), the
rounding spread is about 37 % of the statistical variance
and below a tenth of any total uncertainty (Ref. [14]); the
quoted 2.6 statistical standard deviations is the largest
shift, over 153 quantities (the joint cells, the (Eavail , W )

cells and two totals) under two such perturbations of the
data, not the typical one. A declared probe on simulated
pseudo-data of two-fold cross-fitting and of two tree regularisations found no variant that met the data-stability
requirement, so the estimator is unchanged (Ref. [14]).
G.

APPLICATION TO DATA

Applied to the data, the joint-test estimator gives a
cross section on the sixteen cells of Sec. III, with the
covariance of Sec. IV A. Appendix A gives the cells, the
covariance and descriptive comparisons with five generator predictions, as the method’s illustrative output, not
for fitting or for constraining models: no interval has
validated coverage, the fake-data bias exceeds the stated
criteria at two truths, four cells have low reconstruction
stability (Sec. VI A), and the run-period block was added
after the period differences were seen. An unfolded result
can be compared with any prediction at truth level without simulating the detector; Fig. 4 shows the output over
each prediction, cell by cell.
VI.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

The method provides a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables, without a separate binned response
tensor for each projection, whose normalization agrees
with the two-dimensional result. It does not yet provide
validated interval coverage, numerical stability, recovery
of every generator-sized truth change tested within the
reported uncertainty, run-period consistency, or an identified source of the shape difference from the published
result (Table I).
Two validation findings point to the unfolded result retaining part of its prior. The first is the residual under a
generator-sized change of the truth in Sec. IV E, which for
the GiBUU-like change is consistent with finite-iteration
regularization. The second is the shift of the data result
when the simulation’s prior is reweighted to generatorsized changes of the truth, which tracks the same bias
(Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4). The note’s calibrated tests of the predictions, whose null distributions are simulated through

A.

Limitations

Apart from the validation of the uncertainties and
the low stability of four of the sixteen cells (below), the

limitations of these results are stated with the results they
qualify, in Secs. III–V, and those of the calibrated tests
in the companion note. The principal ones are these.
1. No reported interval has validated coverage. The
fake-data tests of Sec. IV G assess bias, not coverage, so interval widths cannot be tested, and the
coverage test declared for the sixteen-cell covariance
was stopped by one of its preliminary checks before
any coverage pseudo-experiment ran, for reasons
not established (Sec. IV G). Neither the coverage
of Cmodel at truths held out of its construction nor
the size of either run-period block is independently
validated (Ref. [14], Secs. 10.3, 10.4 and 4.5). The
two-dimensional covariance has not been independently validated and gives no calibrated significance
for generator comparisons; an earlier statistical band
of that result undercovered in its tails in a coverage test at fixed truth (Ref. [14], Sec. 5.2), and the
coverage of the current statistical block and of the
total covariance has not been tested. The coverage
tests cited are pointwise, for each reported quantity
separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage
of all of them. We make no coverage claim on inde-

pendent simulated samples or simultaneously over
all reported bins and projections.
2. Four of the sixteen cells, R02, R06, R10 and R15,
have a reconstruction stability of 0.16–0.25: of the
simulated signal events generated in such a cell and
reconstructed, only that fraction is reconstructed in
the same cell.
3. The note’s calibrated tests reject hybrid nulls conditionally on the simulation’s hadronic response, and
their calibration holds to the extent that the simulated chain reproduces the analysis of the data. A
rejection extends to the generator prediction only if
the residual below the fine grid is bounded, and no
such bound has been shown (Ref. [14], Sec. 11.4).
They are not physical exclusions of the generators,
and they show no evidence of additional discrimination beyond matched coarse projections.

Weakening only the flux correlations by 10 % lowers
the χ2 of the 16-cell predictions by 23–38 %, and the
same change to the detector or lateral correlations by
at most 18 %; with the correlations of the interaction,
flux, detector and lateral groups removed, every 16-cell
prediction keeps χ2 ≥ 41 for 16 degrees of freedom. Bootstrapping the 100 flux universes, with the flux variances
kept, gives 16th–84th percentile bands for each 16-cell
χ2 with edges within 3.8 % of the reported value and
a median at most 0.4 % above it (Ref. [14], Sec. 10.6).
These dependences calibrate neither set of values, and no

exclusion of a generator follows.

DATA AVAILABILITY

The input data and simulation are the MINERvA opendata AnaTuples, released under CC0 [12]. The sixteen-cell
cross section, its covariance matrix and the cell definitions
are provided as machine-readable files in the Supplemental
Material. This analysis used an earlier production of that
release; its 2,374 input files are identified by SHA256
checksum in the records cited in Ref. [14], App. G. The
analysis note’s figures of the joint-test null distributions
and the (Eavail , W ) map are regenerated by a candidate
release package of about 16 MB, not yet deposited, that
requires only numpy, scipy, and matplotlib. The paper’s
quoted numbers and the event-level processing require
the archived analysis products, the open-data AnaTuples,
and the generator samples produced for this analysis;
Ref. [14], App. G states what each part of the release
does and does not reproduce.
