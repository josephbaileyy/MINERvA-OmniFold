We test what unbinned unfolding with OmniFold delivers on neutrino data, unfolding MINERvA
medium-energy open data simultaneously in five observables: muon transverse and longitudinal
momenta, available hadronic energy, three-momentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass. Three
findings follow. As a benchmark, the unfolding reproduces the published muon-kinematic crosssection integral within about 1%, but differs from it in shape, over many eigenmodes of the published
covariance, for a reason not identified. In controlled studies the five-observable result retains part of
its prior: fake-data studies at a GiBUU-like truth and at a synthetic momentum-transfer tilt find
residual bias exceeding uncertainty-based criteria (p < 0.01, or any cell with |z| > 2) that were fixed
before the tests were graded[PENDING: FD-PRR, the prior-retention study (PR-1 to PR-3,
the composite labels)]. The estimator is reproducible, bitwise independent of its training seed, but
not numerically stable: rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by up to 2.6 statistical
standard deviations, beyond a stability requirement fixed in advance, and an average of 96 fits that
meets the requirement is worse in bias at one of 17 truths. The uncertainties are not calibrated: the
two-dimensional covariance under-covers at two held-out generator tunes[PENDING: LL5D, the
like-for-like five-observable coverage test]. The five-observable output on data is therefore
given only as an illustration.

1.

INTRODUCTION

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their
interpretation depends on correcting detector response
and on assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned
migration matrix [1] is widely used, but its response
tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space
through classifier-based density-ratio estimation [2, 3].
This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding
possible without specifying a separate binned response
tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been
applied to collider data, for example to eight observables
in deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [4] and to twenty-four
Z+jets observables at ATLAS [5] (see ref. [6] for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on
a public simulated T2K near-detector sample [7]. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions [8–11].
This paper is a methods and validation study of a
simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables on
real neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA
medium-energy νµ data from the open-data release [12], in
the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta pT and p∥ ,
the available hadronic energy Eavail , the three-momentum

transfer q3 , and the hadronic invariant mass W . Reconstructed W is fixed by the reconstructed (pT , p∥ , q3 ), so
the five observables are simultaneous but not independent.
The study reports three findings, each with the tests that
establish it, including those that failed (table 1). First,
a benchmark against the published double-differential
cross section d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) from the same data [13] shows
agreement in normalization and a correlated shape difference whose source has not been identified (section 4).
Second, controlled changes of the simulated truth show
that the five-observable result retains part of its prior (section 5). Third, reproducibility, numerical stability and
the calibration of the uncertainties are distinct properties:
the estimator has the first, fails the second, and the third
is not established (section 6).
Interval coverage has not been validated. The findings
are those of one OmniFold implementation on one data
set, for the observables, configurations, and deformations
tested, rather than of multidimensional OmniFold in general. The sixteen-cell cross section the estimator gives
on the data is therefore not a measurement; the supplementary material gives it as illustrative output, with its
covariance and descriptive comparisons with generator
predictions (supplementary material, section S10).
Section 2 describes the data and the observables, and
section 3 the unfolding, the reported covariance and the
validation strategy common to the three findings. Sections 4–6 give the findings, and section 7 what they show
about the method, with its limitations. The supplementary material gives the definitions on which the results
depend, and the companion analysis note [14], cited by
section, the full validation record.

3.

UNFOLDING METHOD AND VALIDATION
STRATEGY
3.1.

Unfolding

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The
event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including
unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates
a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth
events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated
flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate
efficiency correction is applied.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin b
carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1−Bb /Db ), where Db
and Bb are the data and predicted background counts in
that bin, so the classifier’s target density is D(x) wpur (b).
The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds
simulated background events with negative weight to the
data, so that the target is D(x) − B(x) pointwise. A classifier refinement [18], clipped at zero, converts the signed
sample to non-negative weights before the first iteration.
The two targets share bin integrals wherever Bb < Db
but differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction
varies within it (supplementary material, section S4).
The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees [19, 20] of
nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold
steps, and negative-weight background subtraction with
a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves. The
two-observable estimator, LightGBM [20] at a fixed seed,
gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance
(section 4.1). This reproducible central value is that of
its own systematic-universe ensemble, so its covariance
is built with the same estimator. The five-observable
results use the joint-test estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with the same background treatment and refinement
capacity, named for the note’s calibrated joint tests of
generator predictions, which use it (supplementary material, section S7). Its seeds are fixed, so its training is
reproducible. This estimator removes most of the bias of
purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highestW cells (ref. [14], section 9.2). Results from the two
estimators are not combined. The joint-test estimator is
the baseline this study characterizes (configuration (e) of
section S4): the stability probe and the ensemble average

of section 6.1 were tested against it, and neither replaced
it.
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the
unfolding. No learner setting, whether capacity, learning
rate, refinement capacity or iteration count, was tuned
on data. The joint-test estimator keeps five iterations
because 10, 15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model
dependence over the truth changes used in its development
(supplementary material, section S4). Two choices for the
two-dimensional result were made with the comparison to
the published result in view: the learner implementation
and the background-subtraction treatment (section 4.1).
That comparison is therefore not an independent test of
the method. Each classifier is trained on the full sample
whose weights it then sets, so no held-out or cross-fitted
split is used; it is trained without early stopping, and the
two classes keep their physical weights (supplementary
material, section S4).

3.2.

The reported covariance

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is
Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D . The term Ctotal sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization
uncertainties of the joint-test estimator and Cmodel ; the
lateral uncertainties shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than reweighting events
(supplementary material, section S10). The model term
Cmodel is built from the change in the unfolded data
when the prior is reweighted to GENIE + MEC, NuWro
and GiBUU in turn, with GENIE CV as the common
reference. It is a measure of unscaled generator-shape
dependence, not a calibrated bound (supplementary material, section S5). The response term Cresp is the response
of the result to a one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil energy in the simulation. The scale
is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and other
deposits. Cresp does not cover neutron or muon deposits,
nor species, energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale. The unfolded results of the three run-period
groups, each relative to its own simulation, differ by more
than their bootstrap scatter. The cause is not known,
and these data cannot establish which period is correct.
The run-period block CPL,5D includes this inconsistency
in the reported covariance. It is built from the double
ratios among the groups, each group’s unfolded result
over its own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the
reference, so it contains the differences by construction.
It was added after these differences were seen. It has rank
2 and is not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains,
and with three groups its size is poorly determined: the
68 % interval on the ratio of its true to its estimated size
is 0.73–2.56. The median per-cell relative uncertainty is
7.86%, 7.29% without CPL,5D , and 6.74% without CPL,5D
or Cresp . The fake-data tests are given with and without
it (section 5.2; ref. [14], section 10.4).
Every χ2 of this result inverts the full covariance of

the cells compared, 16 × 16 or 8 × 8, by Cholesky decomposition and without truncation, so its number of
degrees of freedom is the number of cells. The blocks,
their ensembles and their normalizations are given in the
supplementary material, section S5.

3.3.

Validation strategy

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the
prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated
experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The closure, deformation, and coverage
studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events
used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed
and true distributions together and keeps the detector
response at fixed truth. Throughout, the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune v1. Every study uses the analysis
simulation’s detector response.
The five-observable tests use one set of truths. 12
are graded (table 2): the simulation’s prior; five hybrid
truths, each equal to a generator prediction in every
cell of the fine grid and to the Tune v1 simulation’s
event composition within each cell; four generator shape
changes, among them the GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail ratio (the GiBUU Eavail shape); a noise-free closure at the
NuWro shape; and a synthetic tilt of the q3 distribution at fixed (Eavail , W ). 6 further truths, given in the
note, are descriptive. The truths are not held out of the
analysis. Two GENIE v3 tunes, G18_10a_02_11b and
AR23_20i_00_000, were generated for these tests and
held out of it; each reweights the simulated truth by its
shape ratio to GENIE CV. The bias is graded on Poisson pseudo-experiments, whose mean is compared with
the truth, or on noise-free pseudo-data, in which every
Poisson draw is replaced by its mean; the comparison of
the ensemble average with the baseline (section 6.1) uses
noise-free pseudo-data at all 17 distinct truths, graded
and descriptive, since the Tune v1 hybrid equals the prior.
Every criterion was fixed before the results it grades
were seen. A truth is flagged when the bias gives p < 0.01
in the χ2 over the 16 cells, or when a cell has |z| > 2, where
z is the cell’s bias divided by its uncertainty under the
same covariance. An estimator is stable when roundinglevel changes of its inputs move its results by at most
0.3 statistical standard deviations in the median and 1.0
at most. A coverage test grades the fraction of pseudoexperiments whose result lies within its interval, per cell
and jointly, against a threshold set from the test’s own null
distribution. The two-dimensional benchmark (section 4)
concerns the two-observable estimator; the truth-recovery
and stability findings (sections 5 and 6.1) concern the
joint-test estimator, whose rounding sensitivity is carried
in its statistical uncertainty (section 6.1).

4.

BENCHMARK AGAINST THE PUBLISHED
TWO-DIMENSIONAL MEASUREMENT

4.1.

Normalization, per-bin agreement and the
covariance-weighted distance

The two-dimensional result benchmarks the unfolding
against a measurement from the same data. Its integrated cross section is 3.069 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon, compared with 3.039 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published
analysis, a ratio of 1.010. The standardized bin differences
formed with the published uncertainties have an RMS
of 0.535 (figure 1). The two results share the data and
the flux, detector and generator systematics, and their
cross-covariance is not available, so no difference between
them can be given a calibrated significance. Against the
full published covariance alone, the covariance-weighted
distance is χ2 /ndf = 2.27. It is computed with the pseudoinverse over the 205 reported bins, which keeps all 205
eigenmodes of the published covariance (condition number 1.5 × 1012 ), and ndf = 205, the number of reported
bins, since no parameter is fitted. The value is descriptive, and no p-value is quoted for it. It indicates a correlated shape difference, not a normalization difference:
its normalization part is χ2 = 0.04, against 465 for the
shape. Keeping only the 73 best-determined directions
(λ/λmax > 10−4 ) gives χ2 = 136 (χ2 /205 = 0.66), and the
139 with λ/λmax > 10−6 give χ2 = 285 (χ2 /205 = 1.39);
the divisor is the number of reported bins, not the number
of directions kept. Most of the χ2 therefore lies outside
the best-determined directions, and those directions also
contribute to it (figure 2; section S6). The source of the
shape difference has not been identified.
We quote the LightGBM result as the central value because its covariance uses the same estimator. The learner
implementation was chosen after three had been compared
with the published result: with purity-based subtraction,
their χ2 /ndf values were 3.66 (scikit-learn exact-split),
2.65 (LightGBM) and 2.70 (scikit-learn histogram, a mean
over ten seeds), so the distance depends on the estimator.
The refined negative-weight background treatment was
chosen on a closure test in simulation, before the refined
result’s comparison with the published result was computed, but was adopted in the analysis only after that
comparison was known, and the change lowered χ2 /ndf
from 2.65 to 2.27 (supplementary material, section S4).
The values quoted for the two-dimensional comparison are
therefore conditional on both choices, and the comparison
should be read as a descriptive comparison, not a blind
validation.
Two comparisons, each specified before it ran, put the
two-dimensional result beside other unfoldings of its own
inputs. Each compares a statistic T , formed from the
difference of the two results and its covariance over paired
replicas, with bars from a null of exact agreement (supplementary material, section S8), so a reading of “differ”
means that a difference is detectable at this noise, not that
it is large. A binned D’Agostini iterative Bayesian un-

folding (IBU) at ten iterations differs from it: T = 382.8,
against a null 95th percentile of 123.6 and maximum of
138.5 over 300 paired replicas. The ratio ρ of the IBU’s
distance from the published result to that of the twodimensional result is 0.452, but its 90 % interval over the
replicas, 0.701–1.128, straddles a class boundary fixed in
advance, so the localisation reads unresolved; the point
lies below the interval, for a reason not tested. This reading attributes the shape difference neither to the inputs
nor to the algorithm. The joint-test estimator, run on the
two-dimensional inputs, reproduces the two-dimensional
central value within its paired noise: T = 13.87 over 50
paired replicas, 4 % below its 95th-percentile bar of 14.46.

4.2.

Run periods and the five-observable quadrant
sums

In the two-dimensional result, data from different run
periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties: one period group’s unfolded p∥ distribution,
relative to its own simulation, differs from that of the
earliest periods by a step of about 7 % near 8 GeV/c, of
unknown cause. The step is already in the reconstructed
data: there, the 1G–1M group against 1A–1E has a step
of +6.10 %, and in a per-period unfolding the double
ratio of each period’s result to its own simulated truth
gives +(6.94 ± 0.41) %, so the step survives unfolding
at about its reconstruction-level size. Compared with
the published result in the 73 best-determined directions
of its covariance, removing the 1G–1M group does not
change the distance to the published result beyond noise;
the smallest change resolved is 3σ = 0.29 per direction,
about 15 % of the three-group value. The run-period step
does not account for the distance in the best-determined
directions, at the resolved size (section S6).
Summed over each of the four (pT , p∥ ) quadrants, the
sixteen-cell result agrees with the two-dimensional result:
the sums differ by −1.5 to +0.7 per cent, and with the
covariance between the two results evaluated from the
systematic variations they share, the four differences give
χ2 ≤ 1.25 for four degrees of freedom (p ≥ 0.87), with
no quadrant beyond one standard deviation. The statistical correlation, and that of four systematic terms
that could not be evaluated directly, are bounded within
a one-parameter family of valid covariances, each such
cross term taken as a common fraction, from zero to one,
of its fully correlated value, over which the agreement
holds. The five-observable result therefore agrees with the
two-dimensional normalization in each muon-kinematic
quadrant; the benchmark does not test its distribution in
the three hadronic observables.

5.

RECOVERY OF CONTROLLED TRUTH
CHANGES
5.1.

Closure and generator-sized changes

For the joint-test estimator, a background-inclusive
closure at nominal truth on 40 pseudo-experiments, not
held out from its development, has a largest deviation
of 2.7 standard errors and a largest (Eavail , W ) residual
of 0.09 % (ref. [14], section 9.3); on the sixteen cells, the
closures of table 2 meet the bias criteria.
Figure 3 shows that the unfolding recovers only part of
a change of the Eavail shape as large as the difference between generators. When the true shape is reweighted by
the GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio, the (Eavail , W ) cells unfolded
with a lower-capacity variant of the joint-test estimator (a

5.2.

Fake-data tests of the sixteen-cell result

To compare the estimator’s bias with the reported
covariance when the truth differs from the simulation, we
unfold pseudo-data at the 12 graded truths of section 3.3
and compare the mean unfolded result with each truth on
the 16 cells through χ2 with 16 degrees of freedom. For
a truth from a generator that contributes to Cmodel , the
covariance omits that generator’s component (the Cov.
column of table 2). Two of the truths, the simulation
prior and the Tune v1 hybrid, are the simulation’s own
truth, so they are closure tests (marked ∗ in table 2).
Table 2 gives the results under the reported covariance
and under Ctotal alone.
Under the reported covariance, without the GiBUU
component for the two GiBUU truths, the flagged truths
(p < 0.01, or a cell with |z| > 2) are the GiBUU hybrid
truth (p < 10−27 ; cells R02, R06) and the synthetic q3 tilt,
by p alone (p < 10−5 ). Under Ctotal +Cresp , the covariance
without CPL,5D , which table 2 does not show, the GiBUU
Eavail shape also meets the flagging criterion, in cell R06
(z = 2.01, against 1.76 with the block). Thus the fake-data
bias exceeds the stated thresholds at the GiBUU hybrid
truth and the q3 tilt under the reported covariance, and
also at the GiBUU Eavail shape without CPL,5D . The bias

Ctotal alone, the right-hand columns of table 2, the flagged

0.0027). At the other 10 truths the bias passes both
criteria under the reported covariance, with no cell beyond
2σ and p ≥ 0.01. Both cells flagged at the GiBUU hybrid
truth, R02 and R06, are low-stability cells.
At the two held-out tunes, 20 Poisson pseudoexperiments and a noise-free closure are compared with
the truth using the full reported covariance. Both tunes
satisfy the bias criteria: the pseudo-experiment means give
χ2 = 9 (p = 0.90) and 14 (p = 0.59), respectively, with
|z| ≤ 0.62 in every cell of the means and closures. In cell
R11 at G18_10a_02_11b, the bias lies below the reported
uncertainty only with Cmodel included. The tunes share a
quasielastic cross-section model, and G18_10a_02_11b
and the GENIE + MEC component of Cmodel also use
Valencia-family MEC models. These tests provide partial
evidence and are not independent. The simulated detector
response is not varied in these tests; noise-free closures
with a shifted recoil response move the sixteen cells by
at most 0.40σ per deposit category of Cresp , without a
pass threshold (ref. [14], appendix D). These tests assess
bias, not coverage: only event counts fluctuate, with the
systematic parameters fixed.

5.3.

What moves the retained prior

A study specified in advance asks which settings of
the joint-test estimator change the part of a truth change
that it leaves unrecovered. On noise-free pseudo-data with
backgrounds, at one split of the simulation, it measures
at each graded truth the retained fraction ρ: the leastsquares coefficient of the bias on the truth’s departure
from the prior, over the cells where that departure is at
least 1 %, so that ρ = 1 if the result is the prior and ρ = 0
if the departure is recovered. It changes one setting at a
time, the iteration count, the classifier capacity, the training statistics and the observables the classifiers see, and
runs an exact binned IBU on the same pseudo-data. At
the two flagged truths, each setting is classed by whether
some level reduces or increases ρ beyond a bar fixed in
advance, and the IBU by whether it retains less of the
prior than the joint-test estimator. The study selects no
estimator, and its readings are descriptive of these truths,
not held-out results (supplementary material, section S9).

6.1.

REPRODUCIBILITY, STABILITY AND
UNCERTAINTY CALIBRATION
Reproducibility and numerical stability

The joint-test estimator is unstable under floatingpoint rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, the reported quantities scatter
by a median of 0.23 % per joint cell, against a median
data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty of 0.37 %, and each
bootstrap replica carries this scatter at the same size.
Rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by
up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of
the lower-capacity variant by up to 2.5, so the estimator
fails a data-stability requirement of at most 0.3 (median)
and 1.0 (maximum) statistical standard deviations. The
training-seed block of section 3.2 is zero because, in the
unfolds we ran, the result is bitwise independent of the
training seed and of input row order; this is distinct from
the sensitivity to rounding-level input changes quoted
above. That sensitivity enters the reported statistical
block by construction: each of its 50 bootstrap replicas
refits the whole unfolding with its own rounding-level
perturbation of the inputs. On the finer joint cells, the
rounding spread is about 37 % of the statistical variance
and below a tenth of any total uncertainty (ref. [14]); on
the 16 reported cells, over 20 rounding-level perturbations
of the data, it is a median 0.133 % of the cross section
(0.055 % to 0.252 %); the quoted 2.6 statistical standard
deviations is the largest shift, over 153 quantities (the
joint cells, the (Eavail , W ) cells and two totals) under two
such perturbations of the data, not the typical one. A
probe specified in advance, on simulated pseudo-data, of
two-fold cross-fitting and of two tree regularisations found
no variant that met the data-stability requirement, so the
estimator is unchanged (ref. [14]). Cross-fitting also gives
a larger fake-data bias than the current estimator at three
of the 17 truths it was graded on.
A study of the mechanism specified in advance, on one
simulated pseudo-experiment, examined six candidate
causes, five of them testable, by the ratio of the spread
under a modified perturbation to that under the unmodified one. On this pseudo-experiment, the perturbation
reaches the estimator by breaking ties among equal float32
values, which changes LightGBM’s histogram bins; a perturbation that keeps the ties removes the spread, leaving
a ratio of 6.2 × 10−7 and 1.07 × 10−6 on the 153 quantities
and the 16 cells. Equal float32 values are what float32
storage of these kinematics produces. Jittering only the
input of the refinement stage carries the full spread, so
the refinement is a sufficient entry, and whether the ties
act there was not tested; finer binning, float64 inputs and
a permutation of the input rows do not reduce the spread
(table 1; section S4).
An ensemble-averaged variant of the estimator, the
mean of 96 fits, specified before the mechanism study
was read, meets the stability requirement on its stability
input (median 0.08 and at most 0.76, against 0.3 and 1.0),

where the reported estimator does not. On noise-free
fake-data closures at the 17 distinct truths it is no worse
than the reported estimator at 16 and worse at one, the
GiBUU Eavail shape, by a criterion fixed before the test,
so it is not adopted and the estimator is unchanged. It is
a tested mitigation of the instability, not a replacement:
reproducibility, which the estimator has, does not imply
stability, which it lacks, and the variant that meets the
stability requirement is worse in bias at one of the truths
tested.

6.2.

Calibration of the uncertainties

The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four
blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central
value’s estimator: systematic universes (187 universes,
including the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap
replicas), run period (three period groups), and training
seed (five seed settings, so that its own size is poorly determined). The resulting relative uncertainty has a median
of 6.41 % per bin and is 4.76 % on the integrated cross section, compared with 6.85 % and 4.61 % for the publication.
The covariance omits the approximation in the systematicuniverse unfoldings for events that fail reconstruction, the
learner dependence of section 4.1, and the dependence on
the regularization (supplementary material, section S6).
The dependence on the prior’s generator shape is reported
beside it as a separate covariance Cmodel , built from the
change of the result when the prior is reweighted to each
generator’s (pT , p∥ ) shape; its median is 3.71 % per bin,
and it is a measure of model dependence, not a calibrated
bound on the unfolding bias.
The procedure’s two-dimensional covariance was tested
for coverage, in a test specified in advance, with 300
pseudo-experiments at each of the two held-out GENIE v3 tunes, the uncertainties propagated linearly as
in the covariance’s construction, and the covariance rebuilt by the same procedure at each tune, with Cmodel
included; the run-period block was not drawn. It undercovers at both tunes. At AR23_20i_00_000, no pseudoexperiment of the 300 lies in the joint region fixed in
advance, against a flagging threshold of 190, and the
mean χ2 /ndf is 16.55 ± 0.17 for 204 degrees of freedom;
at G18_10a_02_11b, none lies in it, against 192, and
χ2 /ndf = 24.17 ± 0.37 for 205. Per cell, three flags are
raised, each attributed to bias: one cell at both tunes
and one more at AR23_20i_00_000 (section S6). The
test’s false-flag rate under its null’s law is at most 0.0348
and 0.0363 per tune. G18_10a_02_11b shares its 2p2h
model family with one of Cmodel ’s generators. The result
concerns the procedure’s covariance at those tunes, not
the reported intervals at the data.
Statistical intervals of an earlier five-observable estimator with purity-based subtraction, corrected for its
closure bias at nominal truth, miscover at the tested
truths, most strongly when the true signal rate is lowered by about 10 %, with coverage as low as 0.06 for 68%

intervals (ref. [14], section 9.5).
A first coverage test of the five-observable covariance at
the two tunes was specified in advance, for the covariance
without the run-period block, with pseudo-experiments
that also draw the flux, interaction and detector uncertainties (the lateral and normalization ones as surrogates,
and Cmodel and Cresp not at all). It was stopped at one of
its gates, before any coverage pseudo-experiment ran: the
uncertainty of each cell reproduced through the pseudoexperiments, which move only the detector response at
fixed truth, departed from that of the drawn covariance,
whose blocks are built from the prior and the response
together, by more than the window 0.9–1.1 in 11 of the
16 cells, ranging from 0.70 to 2.30. The gate therefore
compared unlike objects, for the interaction uncertainties
and mostly for the flux, while two detector bands, the
GEANT4 neutron and proton reweights, depart for a
cause not established (ref. [14], section 10.5; section S9).
A staged redesign stopped twice at its first stage under
fail-closed rules fixed in advance, so the test did not run
to a result, and none of its readings is a coverage result,
in either direction.

6.3.

A like-for-like five-observable coverage test

The design of the two-dimensional test has been carried
to the sixteen cells, at the same two tunes, with each
drawn uncertainty group’s pseudo-data built as its covariance block is built, and with the covariance rebuilt at each
tune by the reported procedure. It has two readings, fixed
in advance and correlated by construction: a primary one
without the GEANT4 neutron and proton bands, and one
with them. Its covariance is rebuilt once per truth, not per
pseudo-experiment; the response and run-period terms
are not drawn, and the lateral uncertainties are carried
by a surrogate. With two truths it gives no coverage rate,
and neither outcome bears on the reported intervals at the
data or on the ensemble average. [PENDING: LL5D,

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

The method provides a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables, without a separate binned response
tensor for each projection. Its two-dimensional result
agrees with the published measurement in normalization
and differs from it in shape, for a reason not identified,
and its five-observable result agrees with it in each muonkinematic quadrant. Its reported cells depend on the
unfolding algorithm. It does not yet provide validated
interval coverage, numerical stability, recovery of every
generator-sized truth change tested within the reported
uncertainty, or run-period consistency (table 1).
Two validation findings point to the unfolded result
retaining part of its prior. The first is the residual under a generator-sized change of the truth in section 5.1,

which for the GiBUU-like change is consistent with finiteiteration regularization. The second is the shift of the
data result when the simulation’s prior is reweighted to
generator-sized changes of the truth, which tracks the
same bias (ref. [14], section 9.4). The note’s calibrated
tests of the predictions show the same bias toward the
prior (ref. [14], section 11.5).
These results bound what the study shows. The benchmark establishes normalization agreement and a qualified
shape comparison in two dimensions, not the validity
of the five-observable distribution. The validity rules
grade the estimator’s bias under simulated truths; they
do not identify where the detector model is inadequate.
Whether the two-dimensional shape difference arises in
the hadronic observables is not established: a test of that
would need contrasts specified in advance within fixed
muon-kinematic regions, with their joint uncertainty and
a treatment of multiplicity, and a demonstration that
plausible changes of the unfolding and of the detector
response cannot produce the observed pattern. That test
is the natural next step.
The studies of section 3–6 and of the companion note
show what must be checked before unfolded intervals
are interpreted: closure with the backgrounds included;
truth changes of generator size, not only nominal closure;
coverage under changes of the truth, not only at nominal
truth; robustness to training seeds and rounding-level
input changes; and run-period consistency.
Until the coverage, the prior dependence, the numerical stability, and the run-period effect are resolved, the
sixteen-cell output is a demonstration of the method.

7.1.

Limitations

Apart from the validation of the uncertainties and
the low stability of four of the sixteen cells (below), the
limitations of these results are stated with the results they
qualify, and those of the calibrated tests in the companion
note. The principal ones are these.
1. No reported interval has validated coverage. The
fake-data tests assess bias, not coverage; the first
coverage test of the five-observable covariance did

not run to a result; and the procedure’s twodimensional covariance under-covers at both heldout tunes, a result about the procedure’s covariance
at those tunes, not the reported intervals at the
data (section 6.2). Neither the coverage of Cmodel
at truths held out of its construction nor the size of
either run-period block is independently validated
(supplementary material, section S5, and section S6).
The coverage tests cited are pointwise, for each reported quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them.
2. Four of the sixteen cells, R02, R06, R10 and R15,
have a reconstruction stability of 0.16–0.25: of the
simulated signal events generated in such a cell and
reconstructed, only that fraction is reconstructed in
the same cell.
3. The note’s calibrated tests reject hybrid nulls, conditionally on the simulation’s hadronic response and
on the simulated chain reproducing the analysis of
the data. A rejection extends to a generator prediction only if the residual below the fine grid is
bounded, and no such bound has been shown. They
are not physical exclusions, and show no evidence
of additional discrimination beyond matched coarse
projections (supplementary material, section S7).
