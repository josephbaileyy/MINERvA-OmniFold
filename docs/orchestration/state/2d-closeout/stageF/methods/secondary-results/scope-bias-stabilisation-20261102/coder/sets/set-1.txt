Fake-data studies find residual bias exceeding predeclared uncertainty-based thresholds at a GiBUU-like truth and a synthetic momentum-transfer tilt.

The covariance includes an unfolding-prior term: the difference between the nominal result and the result unfolded with the simulation reweighted to the data at reconstruction level. Its recipe (variables, smoothing and amplitude) was fixed before the data-to-simulation ratio was computed. It measures prior dependence along the direction the data select; it is not a calibrated bound.

Under the reported covariance, without the GiBUU component for the two GiBUU truths, the listed truths (p<0.01, or a cell with |z|>2) are the GiBUU hybrid truth (p=5.3e-28; cells R02, R06) and the synthetic q3 tilt, by p alone (p=9.1e-6). Without C_PL,5D, the GiBUU Eavail shape also meets the listing criterion, in cell R06 (z=2.01, against 1.76 with the block). Thus the fake-data bias exceeds the stated thresholds at the GiBUU hybrid truth and the q3 tilt under the reported covariance, and also at the GiBUU Eavail shape without C_PL,5D. The bias is the same in both; only the covariance differs, so the change is not a smaller bias or a validated uncertainty. At the other 10 truths the bias passes both criteria under the reported covariance, with no cell beyond 2 sigma and p>=0.01.
