=== REFEREE 1 REPORT ===
**Referee report on a PRD manuscript: simultaneous five-observable inclusive νμ CC cross section from MINERvA open data with OmniFold**

---

## 1. Summary of the claims and measurements

The authors unfold MINERvA medium-energy νμ open data with OmniFold, an unbinned, classifier-based method. They do this in five observables at once: pT, p∥, Eavail, q3 and W. The headline result is a cross section integrated over 16 cells, with a full covariance and a median per-cell uncertainty of 7.86%. This includes a run-period block added after unexplained differences between run periods were seen. The authors check the unfolding against the published (pT, p∥) cross section. The integral agrees to about 1%, with a "correlated shape difference" (χ²/ndf = 2.27). They compare the 16-cell result with GENIE, NuWro, GiBUU and Tune v1 and find χ² of 105–262 for 16 degrees of freedom. The paper is unusually candid that coverage has not been validated. It reports residual bias beyond its own predeclared thresholds at two fake-data truths, and a stopped coverage test. It also reports instability under rounding-level input changes and four cells with low stability.

## 2. Recommendation

**Major revision.** As written, the paper does not establish a physics measurement. It establishes a methods demonstration with extensive negative validation findings.

Several things block publication as a cross-section measurement:
- The reported uncertainties are not shown to cover the true value. The only direct coverage test found large miscoverage, with coverage as low as 0.06.
- The covariance contains a block, CPL,5D, whose size is, in the authors' words, "poorly determined" and whose "consistency check cannot fail".
- The one fully end-to-end benchmark is a reproduction and is not independent (the learner was chosen after comparison with the published result). It shows an unexplained χ²/ndf of 2.27.
- Two of 12 fake-data truths fail the authors' own criteria, one of them catastrophically (p = 5×10⁻²⁸).
- The estimator's central values move by up to 2.6 statistical σ under floating-point rounding.
- The generator χ² values are not interpretable, because the NuWro/GiBUU target mismatch is uncorrected and the covariance is unvalidated.

The topic is of clear interest to PRD readers, and the candour is commendable. The paper needs either additional analysis (Section 3) or a substantial reframing: the result would be presented as a validated-in-part method study plus a result with explicitly restricted claims. It should not be presented as a measurement with a 7.86% uncertainty.

## 3. Major issues

**M1. The reported uncertainties are not validated, and the only coverage evidence is negative.**
- Sec. IV F: 51 of 153 cell and projection integrals have 68% coverage whose upper confidence bound is below 0.66, with coverage as low as 0.06. This is for a (different, purity-subtracted) estimator under a ~10% signal-rate change.
- The test that would probe the actual reported covariance (the two held-out GENIE v3 tunes with flux, interaction and detector draws) was "stopped at one of its gates", because "the uncertainties reproduced through its pseudo-experiments disagreed with the covariance blocks they draw, for reasons not established". So the covariance blocks are not even self-consistent with their own pseudo-experiment generators.
- Why it matters: a cross-section measurement is useful only if its quoted uncertainty is meaningful. The abstract states that the coverage "has not been validated", which concedes the central point.
- What to do:
  - Establish why the pseudo-experiment ensemble disagrees with the blocks (a bug, a nonlinearity, or a covariance misconstruction).
  - Complete a coverage test on the joint-test estimator (the one used for the result), with and without the run-period block, at held-out truths.
  - Alternatively, the authors must replace the "uncertainty" claim with a restricted statement, for example "statistical plus modelled systematic, no coverage claim", and not describe the quantity as a 68% interval. Calling this a 68% interval anywhere else in the paper would be unjustified.

**M2. The estimator carries significant, undiagnosed prior-dependence (regularization bias) that is not in the uncertainty budget at an adequate level.**
- Sec. IV E: under the GiBUU/GENIE Eavail reweighting the residual is up to 74% in a cell. Other fixed shapes give 16–31% in the worst (Eavail, W) cell, "lies within the quoted total uncertainty in about half of those cells", and this uses an earlier covariance that "fails its specified acceptance criteria".
- Sec. III A: the model-dependence envelope alone has a median of 10–13% per cell, above the authors' own 10% precision target, and "none of the reporting definitions … meets this target".
- Cmodel is "unscaled generator-shape dependence, not a calibrated bound". It is built from only three generator shifts and is derived from a (pT, p∥) reweighting only (Sec. IV C), so it does not obviously span Eavail, q3 or W shape freedom.
- The 16 cells appear to have been chosen so that the target would be met on a coarse partition (one interior edge per axis), whereas the finer partitions fail. This is a reporting definition selected, in effect, by what passes.
- The paper itself says the result "retains part of its prior".
- Why it matters: if the central values retain a prior-dependent component that is not covered, the quoted precision (7.86%) is smaller than the actual uncertainty. Generator comparisons are then biased toward the prior (Tune v1 and GENIE).
- What to do:
  - Fold an explicit regularization/unfolding-bias term into the covariance, with a validated size (for example from the full set of generator-sized deformations in the observables the cells depend on, not just (pT, p∥)).
  - Show that Cmodel covers the held-out truths. The two GENIE v3 tunes are partial evidence and are "not independent".
  - Alternatively, report the cells on a coarsening where the envelope is below the target, and state honestly that the finest structure is not constrained by the data.

**M3. Two of the 12 fake-data truths fail the authors' own predeclared criteria, and the paper gives no resolution.**
- Table I: the GiBUU hybrid truth gives χ² = 171 (p = 5×10⁻²⁸, cells R02 and R06), and the synthetic q3 tilt gives χ² = 52.5 (p = 9×10⁻⁶).
- Under Ctotal alone, further truths (NuWro Eavail, GiBUU Eavail, NuWro (pT, p∥, Eavail)) also fail or are marginal.
- The truths are not held out: they were used in the development of the estimator and the covariance. The paper says "Neither seed set was held out from the estimator's development", so the passing cases are optimistic. The failing ones are lower bounds on the real bias.
- A q3 tilt at fixed (Eavail, W) is a simple, physically-sized deformation that fails, in an observable of central interest to the paper.
- The text says "the bias is the same in both; only the covariance differs, so the change is not a smaller bias or a validated uncertainty". That is a correct statement, but it means the passes at the other 10 truths partly reflect covariance inflation by an unvalidated block.
- Why it matters: the paper's stated purpose is to deliver a result usable for testing generators, and a failing GiBUU hybrid is a direct statement that the result would misrepresent a GiBUU-like truth.
- What to do:
  - Diagnose the failures (the authors attribute them to "residual bias that grows with W", with "no single cause established").
  - Reduce the bias (for example with a cross-fitted classifier, a different regularization strategy, or iteration choices set on held-out truths).
  - Alternatively, include the bias in the uncertainty, or restrict the claims to the region of phase space where the tests pass.

**M4. The run-period inconsistency is unexplained, its contribution is poorly determined, and it was added post hoc.**
- Sec. IV C: a step of about 7% in unfolded p∥ near 8 GeV/c between period groups, with "cause is not known". The block has rank 2, is "not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains", and the 68% interval on the ratio of true to estimated size is 0.73–2.56. It was added after the differences were seen, and "its own consistency check cannot fail".
- This block raises the median uncertainty by about 7.8% and R04 by 14.1%. It lowers the generator χ² by 3.6–30.5% (total) and 7.3–39.5% (shape-only).
- A 7% period-to-period step near 8 GeV/c in p∥ is the size of the full uncertainty. It may signal a real data-quality, flux or detector (MINOS or beam-line) issue that would affect every MINERvA ME analysis, or a bug in the open-data release or the authors' treatment (the paper notes an "earlier production" of the release was used).
- Why it matters: if the cause is something that affects the absolute scale or shape, a covariance block of rank 2 built from three groups is not a model of it. It makes the generator χ² depend on a post hoc choice.
- What to do:
  - Investigate the cause. Candidates are the flux (horn current and target position per period), muon-momentum scale (MINOS), POT or exposure normalization, and the simulation per period.
  - Test whether the step appears in the published analysis's own period-by-period breakdowns and in a reco-level data/MC comparison with no unfolding (to isolate unfolding artefacts from data differences).
  - If the cause cannot be found, publish the result with the periods combined (or split) and an honestly quantified systematic. A rank-2 block from three groups cannot support a precise χ².

**M5. The estimator is numerically unstable and is trained and evaluated on the same events.**
- Sec. IV G: "rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations … the estimator fails a data-stability requirement" of at most 0.3 (median) and 1.0 (maximum). The authors argue this "lies inside the data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty", which does not make it benign: it is an unquantified uncertainty of the same order as the statistical one, which the paper's covariance excludes (the training-seed block is "zero").
- A zero training-seed block coexists with a rounding sensitivity of up to 2.6σ. These two facts are in tension, and the paper does not reconcile them.
- Sec. III: "Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, so no held-out or cross-fitted split is used; it is trained without early stopping". Self-prediction with boosted trees can overfit (memorization), particularly with few events per leaf, which is a plausible contributor to both the instability and the retained-prior or regularization behaviour. The memorization cannot be excluded from the text.
- What to do:
  - Show results with cross-fitting (K-fold, where the classifier predicts on events it was not trained on), and include a data-stability test on that variant.
  - Give a non-zero estimator-stability block (a replica or seed-ensemble covariance, with the instability spanned by perturbed inputs).
  - Reconcile the zero seed block with the observed instability.

**M6. Validation is performed on shifting estimators, and the reported result is not the estimator that was best validated.**
- The paper uses at least four distinct configurations: the two-observable LightGBM at a fixed seed, the "joint-test" deterministic LightGBM, "a development variant" (100 trees, 8 leaves), and an "earlier purity-subtracted five-observable central value".
- Fig. 3a is the development variant. Fig. 3b's grey band is the covariance of the earlier purity-subtracted estimator. Sec. IV E notes that "those uncertainties come from an earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance that fails its specified acceptance criteria". Coverage (IV F) uses purity-subtracted LightGBM. Closure (IV D) is partly for the purity-based estimator and partly for the refined estimator.
- The two-observable benchmark uses a different estimator from the headline result: "Of the joint-test estimator's results, only the three-observable marginal has been compared with the lowerdimensional result it extends … a comparison across the two estimators."
- Why it matters: the validation evidence does not transfer cleanly to the reported result. The reader cannot tell which claims rest on which estimator, and the paper repeatedly warns that results "are not combined". The headline 16-cell result has never been compared with the published measurement in the five-observable estimator.
- What to do: re-run the key validations (closure, deformation recovery, coverage, 2D benchmark) with the single final estimator, and tabulate which test used which configuration.

**M7. The benchmark with the published measurement is weaker than presented.**
- χ²/ndf = 2.27 on 205 bins using a pseudo-inverse. The authors give the condition number as 1.5×10¹², with "rank 205" kept. A pseudo-inverse at this condition number is dominated by numerical noise in the smallest eigenmodes, and χ²/ndf = 2.27 is described as "descriptive". The authors should give the spectrum of the contributions (cumulative χ² versus eigenmode) so the reader can see whether it comes from a few modes.
- "RMS 0.535" of standardized residuals with the published uncertainties looks reassuring, but the statistical uncertainties are shared (the same data), so the residuals are correlated and the RMS is not a compatibility test. Fig. 1 itself says "their overlap is not a compatibility test".
- The authors concede it is "a reproduction rather than an independent validation" and the learner was selected after seeing the comparison.
- The 2D covariance is shown to be unvalidated, and the flux uncertainty differs from the published one "for a reason the released inputs do not let us determine".
- The integral agrees to 1.0%, but this is the quantity most constrained by the normalization, and the shape disagreement is unexplained.
- What to do: identify the source of the shape difference (for example with a binned IBU unfolding of the same open-data sample with the published setup). Use a pre-registered benchmark estimator, or state clearly that the benchmark demonstrates consistency, not accuracy.

**M8. Generator comparisons are not interpretable as they stand.**
- NuWro and GiBUU are on carbon and are compared to CH data with "no correction". The paper itself shows an 8.3% carbon-vs-CH difference for W < 1.1 GeV in GENIE, and the comparison "does not correct" it. Hydrogen has no QE, 2p2h or coherent channel, and so the cross-target difference is non-uniform in the cell structure.
- The generators use a different W definition (neutron mass versus the analysis M) and a different pT axis (about their own neutrino direction versus the fixed beam axis), unlike the analysis definitions. The authors do not apply the analysis definitions to the predictions (the comparisons are "in Ref. [14]" and are not reproduced here).
- GiBUU stops at 20 GeV and is compared on 8 cells only. The cells with p∥ < 4.5 are not independent of the high-energy tail anyway.
- χ² of 105 to 262 for 16 degrees of freedom cannot be read as meaning anything given the unvalidated covariance, the weakening test (flux correlations weakened by 10% lowers χ² by 23–38%), and the post hoc run-period block. The text then adds that the paired columns "calibrate neither set of values and establish no exclusion of a generator". The comparison therefore has no stated purpose.
- The Cmodel term is derived from GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU, and these same generators are then compared. The comparison is partly circular, and the text handles this only by "omitting that generator's component" in the fake-data tests, not in Table III.
- What to do: apply the analysis's W and pT definitions to the generator samples, handle the target (generate CH samples, or hydrogen-corrected carbon), and either validate the covariance or drop χ² as a quantitative statement.

**M9. Incomplete observable definitions and truth-reco mismatch (the "definitional departures").**
- Eavail is defined nonstandardly (135 MeV constant in the charged-pion term; no electrons or positrons; no kaons or strange baryons; neutrons contribute nothing). The reconstructed Eavail is "1.17 times the calorimetric recoil energy", which is a tuning factor, and it is described with no further information about how it was validated. The effect of this definition on the published comparison is only reported "to first order … without retraining".
- Truth W and q3 are computed from generator four-vectors, while reconstructed W is derived from q0, q3 and M̄. The notional relationship between truth and reco W is not exact (the fixed beam-axis angle versus the true neutrino direction). Reco W is a deterministic function of (pT, p∥, q3), which means the five observables only carry four independent reco-level pieces of information, plus Eavail, which is itself correlated with q0.
- Why it matters: "five-observable" overstates the information content. Since the results are reported on cells that separate W at 1.8 GeV and Eavail at 0.8 GeV, the resolution and migration across these boundaries are crucial. The low stability (0.16–0.25) of R02, R06, R10 and R15 indicates that the cell boundaries cut through the resolution.
- What to do: either state clearly that the measurement is four-dimensional plus a derived variable, or show that W adds information beyond (pT, p∥, q3, Eavail). Show the resolution and migration matrices at the cell boundaries.

**M10. The headline claim is hard to isolate from the statements in the abstract and the conclusions.**
The abstract gives "7.86% median uncertainty", while the same abstract and Sec. IV state that the coverage is unvalidated, that the run-period term is poorly determined, and that bias exceeds thresholds. The headline number is then not a measurement of the precision of the result. This reads as a hedge rather than a conclusion. A clearer statement is needed of what an end user can and cannot conclude (for example "do not use these χ² for generator exclusion").

## 5. Resolve or move out? Publishability and disqualifying limitations

I would prefer that the authors **resolve** the (a) items, and not move them to the note. These limitations sit at the core of what the paper claims. Moving them to a companion note would not remove them, and it would lower the visibility of limitations that a reader needs. Moving (c) items out would improve the paper.

As the manuscript stands, I do not think it is publishable in PRD as a measurement with its current limitations. No single limitation is formally disqualifying in isolation, but the following combination is, in my view:
- (1) no validated coverage;
- (2) a stopped coverage test whose failure is unexplained;
- (3) a fake-data failure at a physically reasonable truth, and a q3 tilt failure;
- (4) an unexplained 7% period-to-period data inconsistency absorbed in a post hoc block;
- (5) an estimator unstable under rounding.

Together they mean that the quoted result cannot be taken as a measurement with the stated uncertainty. The data-quality item (4) is potentially the most important physically, and the coverage item (1)–(2) the most important statistically.

I see two acceptable routes:
- **Route A (preferred).** Resolve limitations 1–5, 7 and 10 above, and re-run the main validations with the single final estimator. Then publish as a measurement, with the remaining (b) items stated.
- **Route B.** Reframe the paper as a methods and validation study. This is publishable in PRD, or perhaps better in a methods venue. The 16-cell result would be a demonstration. The claims about the cross section and the generator comparison would be removed or heavily restricted. The paper would state plainly what the unfolding is able and unable to do, and which tests the community should demand. The Discussion's list of "what must be checked" is the strongest part of the paper and could become its core. This route requires the paper to drop the language of measurement.

A paper that publishes a 7.86% uncertainty alongside the statement that its coverage was not tested (and was nearly zero in the one test of it) is, as it stands, the worst of both options.

## 6. Minor issues and presentation

1. **Register and structure.** The paper reads like a condensed analysis note: extremely dense, with a large number of qualifiers per sentence and about 40 references to "Ref. [14], Sec. x.y". A PRD reader cannot assess most of the claims without the note, which the referee does not have. Either include the key evidence (tables of block sizes, per-cell uncertainties, the correlation matrix) or reduce the claims.
2. **Overuse of the negative.** Many sentences describe what was not done or not validated ("has not been validated" appears over ten times). Collect these in one limitations section and then state the positive results with their restrictions.
3. **Abstract.** It is a single dense paragraph that lists numbers (7.86%, 105–224, 45, 1%) and then limitations. It should state the purpose, the method, the main result and the main caveat. The χ² range is given as "105–224" in the abstract, but Table III also gives 262 for Tune v1 and the text says "five predictions" (Tune v1 is the prior and not an independent prediction), so the abstract says "three external generator predictions". Make these consistent.
4. **Terminology.** The authors use "estimator", "joint-test estimator", "development variant", "two-observable estimator", "earlier purity-subtracted central value", "reported covariance", "four-block covariance" and "Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D" without a summarizing table. A table of the configurations, with which section and figure uses which, would help (see M6).
5. **Cell definition.** The choice of one edge per axis at 0.7, 4.5, 2, 0.8 and 1.8 should be motivated. "Tertiles" are mentioned for the coarse grid, but the 16 cells use different edges. Table II uses "0–100" and similar open-ended boundaries, which are confusing (100 GeV?). Mark as "∞" or "all", consistently with Fig. 4.
6. **Table II and Fig. 4.** Table II, as extracted, gives f and σ/f but not the correlation structure, and the paper asks readers to use "the full covariance". The covariance and correlation matrix should be in the paper or the Supplemental Material in machine-readable form (the data availability statement says the latter, which should be checked on submission). Fig. 4 shows data over prediction with error bars lacking correlations. Please add the shape-only or a correlation-aware display.
7. **Table III.** The ndf bookkeeping (16 and 15 for the shape-only case, 8 and 7) is clear, but give p-values. Explain why shape-only χ² barely changes for GENIE CV (109 to 109, 101 to 109) if the normalization differences are up to ±10–20% in the cells. Check whether the entries in the columns are transposed. The table gives the "with" and "without" values, and the text says the block lowers each χ², but for GENIE CV the "with" shape-only value (101) appears to be lower than the total χ² (105), and for the "without" columns the two are equal (109, 109), which is odd for a shape-only fit that frees one parameter. Please check.
8. **Fig. 1.** The bottom-left panel is not readable as extracted, and the caption describes it as "cells drawn at equal size (grey: not reported)". Quantify the "not reported" bins. The top panel should have a ratio of the unfolded result to the published result with the correct correlated uncertainty, which is not what the (overlapping) bands give.
9. **Fig. 2 and Fig. 3.** The panels have many cells with unreadable labels. In Fig. 2, the purity-based (9 experiments) and negative-weight (60 experiments) results are compared with unpaired pseudo-experiments, so a difference in scatter is not informative. Please pair them or use the same number of pseudo-experiments. In Fig. 3 the colour scale and the y-range (up to ±100%) hide the typical case. Give a median summary.
10. **Mixed statistical practice.** The "p < 0.01 or |z| > 2 in some cell" listing criterion for 12 truths × 16 cells has a nontrivial look-elsewhere rate (|z| > 2 in at least one of 16 cells occurs with probability about 0.5 for independent cells). # Physical Review D — Referee Report

**Review of:** *Simultaneous five-observable inclusive $\nu_\mu$ charged-current cross section on hydrocarbon with unbinned machine-learning unfolding* (manuscript text provided)

---

## 1. Summary of the Measurement and Claims

The manuscript reports a simultaneous cross-section measurement of inclusive $\nu_\mu$ charged-current interactions on hydrocarbon in five kinematic variables ($p_T, p_\parallel, E_\text{avail}, q_3, W$) using the MINERvA medium-energy open-data release ($1.057 \times 10^{21}$ protons on target). Detector response corrections are performed in an unbinned multi-dimensional feature space using the OmniFold machine-learning method (implemented with gradient-boosted decision trees via LightGBM regularized at five iterations) and reported as integrated cross sections across sixteen coarse kinematic cells with a full covariance matrix. The authors compare their results to five neutrino event generator configurations (GENIE CV, GENIE + Valencia MEC, MINERvA Tune v1, NuWro, and GiBUU), finding severe tension ($\chi^2 = 105\text{--}262$ for 16 d.o.f., and 45 for 8 d.o.f. in GiBUU). Crucially, the authors conduct extensive internal validation tests, reporting that the unfolding exhibits significant prior-regularization bias, fails fake-data closure tests on alternative generator truths by many orders of magnitude (e.g., $p = 5.3 \times 10^{-28}$ for a GiBUU-like truth), displays extreme sensitivity to floating-point rounding (shifts up to $2.6\sigma_\text{stat}$), exhibits unresolved 7% discrepancies across run periods, and lacks validated uncertainty interval coverage.

---

## 2. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Reject** (or, if the Editor allows, a fundamental and extensive **Major Revision** that strips the claim of an experimental cross-section measurement and reframes the manuscript entirely as a methodological case study on the failure modes and diagnostic limits of multi-dimensional OmniFold in neutrino physics).

**Reasons:** While the authors deserve genuine credit for exceptional, commendable transparency in documenting the pathologies of their analysis, a manuscript submitted to *Physical Review D* as an experimental cross-section measurement cannot be published when its central results are self-admittedly unvalidated, biased, and numerically unstable. A measurement where fake-data closure fails at $p = 5.3 \times 10^{-28}$, where coverage of the reported uncertainties is explicitly unvalidated (and where the planned coverage test was aborted due to pipeline inconsistencies), where numerical results shift by up to $2.6\sigma_\text{stat}$ under machine-precision rounding, where an ad-hoc rank-2 covariance is patched in post-hoc to mask an unexplained 7% run-period drift, and where four of the sixteen reporting cells have reconstruction stabilities below 25%, fails the standard criteria of reliability required for experimental results in PRD. These issues cannot be resolved with cosmetic text edits; they represent structural failures of the measurement that disqualify the reported numbers from being used by the nuclear and neutrino community for model tuning.

---

## 3. Major Issues

### 1. Catastrophic Failure of Fake-Data Bias Tests and Unfolding Prior Dependence
* **What is wrong/missing:** In Sec. IV H and Table I, the authors subject the 16-cell estimator to fake-data tests across 12 truth distributions. When tested against a GiBUU hybrid truth, the estimator fails catastrophically with $\chi^2 = 171.01$ for 16 degrees of freedom ($p = 5.3 \times 10^{-28}$), driven by large local deviations in cells R02 and R06 ($|z| > 2$). A synthetic $q_3$ tilt similarly fails with $p = 9.1 \times 10^{-6}$. In Sec. IV E and Fig. 3a, injecting a GiBUU-like $E_\text{avail}$ truth shape produces unfolding residuals up to 74% at high $W$.
* **Why it matters physically:** The foundational premise of cross-section unfolding in neutrino physics is model independence: the detector-corrected cross section must reflect true physical interactions rather than the simulation prior (here, MINERvA Tune v1). If the unfolding algorithm cannot recover a physically plausible alternate generator like GiBUU within quoted errors, the extracted cross section retains memory of the prior. Consequently, the huge $\chi^2$ discrepancies against external generators in Table III cannot be interpreted as physics discrepancies; they are contaminated by the estimator’s intrinsic regularization bias toward the GENIE baseline.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must either (i) re-tune the classifier regularization, loss function, or iteration stopping criterion to ensure acceptable closure ($p > 0.01$ and $|z| \le 2$) across all physical benchmark models, or (ii) construct an honest, calibrated model-bias systematic covariance that covers these residual biases, proving that the resulting inflated uncertainties restore closure.

### 2. Numerical Instability to Floating-Point Rounding
* **What is wrong/missing:** Sec. IV G documents that the joint-test estimator is unstable under perturbations of its inputs within floating-point rounding, shifting unfolded results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations ($2.6\sigma_\text{stat}$), thereby failing the authors' own data-stability threshold of $\le 0.3$ (median) and $\le 1.0$ (maximum).
* **Why it matters physically:** Scientific measurements must be reproducible. If changing machine precision or compiler math optimizations produces shifts exceeding 2.5 standard deviations, the decision trees are splitting on numerical noise in sparse multi-dimensional regions. A result that fluctuates wildly under machine epsilon cannot serve as a published physical standard.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must stabilize the training procedure. Standard remedies include ensembling across multiple training seeds, bagging, increasing the minimum number of samples per leaf (`min_child_samples`), constraining maximum tree depth, or using k-fold cross-fitting, rather than relying on a single deterministic LightGBM tree ensemble that falls into chaotic local minima.

### 3. Inconsistency Across Run Periods and Post-Hoc Ad-Hoc Covariance ($C_\text{PL,5D}$)
* **What is wrong/missing:** Sec. IV A and IV C disclose that the data across the three run-period groups disagree: one period group exhibits a ~7% step in unfolded $p_\parallel$ near 8 GeV/c relative to its own simulation. The cause is unknown. To accommodate this, the authors constructed a post-hoc rank-2 covariance matrix ($C_\text{PL,5D}$) from double ratios of the periods. The authors acknowledge that this matrix is unvalidated, contains raw bootstrap noise, and has an estimated scale whose 68% confidence interval spans an enormous range of $[0.73, 2.56]$.
* **Why it matters physically:** A 7% localized step in $p_\parallel$ is a red flag for time-dependent detector or beam mismodeling (e.g., drift in MINOS spectrometer tracking efficiency, magnetic field calibration, alignment, or horn current degradation). Injecting an unvalidated, rank-2 covariance matrix after inspecting the data masks an underlying detector/beam systematic without identifying its source, distorting the correlations and artificially lowering $\chi^2$ values (Table III shows $\chi^2$ drops by up to 40% when $C_\text{PL,5D}$ is included).
* **What the authors must do:** Perform an instrumental root-cause analysis across the MINERvA and MINOS run periods. If a time-dependent tracking or beam effect occurred, it must be simulated or corrected in the event selection. If it cannot be identified, the systematic uncertainty must be parameterized through a physically meaningful detector model rather than an ad-hoc empirical matrix derived from three noisy run groups.

### 4. Failure to Benchmark Against the Published 2D MINERvA Measurement
* **What is wrong/missing:** In Sec. IV B and Fig. 1, the authors attempt to benchmark their OmniFold implementation against the published MINERvA 2D $(p_T, p_\parallel)$ measurement (PRD 104, 092007) using the identical data set. While the total cross-section integral agrees to 1%, the covariance-weighted distance is $\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ over 205 bins ($p < 10^{-15}$), which the authors describe as an unidentified correlated shape difference spread across many eigenmodes. Furthermore, the learner was chosen *after* seeing comparisons to the published data.
* **Why it matters physically:** A prerequisite for trusting a 5D ML-unfolded cross section is demonstrating that the method reproduces the established, rigorously vetted 2D result on the exact same data. A shape difference yielding $\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ across 205 bins indicates that OmniFold introduces non-trivial shape distortions relative to traditional iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU).
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must isolate the origin of this discrepancy. They must systematically compare efficiency corrections, background subtractions, and regularization effects between IBU and OmniFold to demonstrate why their shape differs and establish whether the difference represents an ML artifact or a flaw in the published 2D extraction.

### 5. Stopped Coverage Test and Lack of Frequentist Interval Validation
* **What is wrong/missing:** The authors state throughout the paper that interval coverage has not been validated. Worse, Sec. IV H reveals that an attempted coverage test at two held-out GENIE v3 tunes was stopped at a gate before running any pseudo-experiments because "the uncertainties reproduced through its pseudo-experiments disagreed with the covariance blocks they draw, for reasons not established." Additionally, Sec. IV F shows that an earlier variant suffered from severe undercoverage (coverage dropping to 0.06 for nominal 68% intervals).
* **Why it matters physically:** In PRD, reported uncertainty bands are expected to possess well-defined frequentist coverage. Without coverage validation, quoting a "median relative uncertainty of 7.86%" is mathematically arbitrary, and the $\chi^2$ values in Table III lose all rigorous statistical meaning.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must diagnose and fix their pseudo-experiment simulation pipeline so that generated pseudo-data correctly reflect the systematic covariance blocks. They must then run full ensemble coverage tests to demonstrate that the reported 68% intervals achieve nominal coverage across realistic variations of nuisance parameters and physics models.

### 6. Unacceptable Reconstruction Stability in Reporting Cells
* **What is wrong/missing:** Sec. VI A states that cells R02, R06, R10, and R15 have reconstruction stabilities between 0.16 and 0.25; that is, only 16% to 25% of true signal events originating in these cells are reconstructed there. Noticeably, cells R02 and R06 are the precise cells that fail the fake-data bias tests in Table I ($|z| > 2$).
* **Why it matters physically:** In cross-section measurements, reporting bins dominated by 75% to 84% smearing from other kinematics violates good experimental practice. High migration rates render the unfolding matrix ill-conditioned, heavily dependent on the MC migration prior, and prone to extreme regularization bias.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors should re-bin the 16 reporting cells, merging these low-stability regions into adjacent kinematic bins to ensure stability values $\ge 0.4\text{--}0.5$, which will likely alleviate the fake-data bias in those regions.

### 7. Uncorrected Target Differences in Generator Comparisons
* **What is wrong/missing:** In Sec. II and Sec. V B, the measured cross section is on hydrocarbon (CH), but the NuWro 21.09 and GiBUU 2019 predictions are evaluated on pure Carbon ($^{12}$C) with no correction. The text explicitly notes that in GENIE, the Carbon cross section per nucleon exceeds that of CH by 8.3% for $W < 1.1$ GeV because hydrogen does not participate in quasielastic, 2p2h, or coherent CC interactions.
* **Why it matters physically:** Comparing CH data to pure $^{12}$C predictions and reporting formal $\chi^2$ values (131 for NuWro, 45 for GiBUU) without correcting for the 8.3% hydrogen dilution effect is unphysical. It conflates target composition differences with actual nuclear modeling discrepancies.
* **What the authors must do:** Re-run NuWro and GiBUU on CH target mixtures (both generators natively support composite hydrocarbon targets), or apply an explicit, model-derived target conversion factor with an appropriate systematic uncertainty before computing the $\chi^2$ metrics.

### 8. Kinematic Redundancy and Observable Definitions
* **What is wrong/missing:** Sec. II acknowledges that reconstructed $W$ is an exact algebraic function of $(p_T, p_\parallel, q_3)$ via $q_0$. Therefore, the 5 reconstructed inputs only provide 4 independent constraints.
* **Why it matters physically:** Advertising a "five-observable simultaneous cross section" is misleading when the feature space at reconstruction level has intrinsic dimension 4. Furthermore, feeding collinear, algebraically dependent variables into decision tree classifiers can cause numerical instability and erratic decision boundaries (potentially exacerbating the rounding instability in Sec. IV G).
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must either remove $W$ from the unfolding feature space (reporting it as a derived projection if desired) or provide a mathematical justification showing that the redundant feature does not degrade the stability of the density ratio estimation.

## 6. Minor Issues and Presentation

1. **Title and Abstract Precision:** The manuscript claims a simultaneous measurement in "five observables," but acknowledges in Sec. II that reconstructed $W$ is an exact algebraic function of $(p_T, p_\parallel, q_3)$. The text should specify that the reconstruction feature space is four-dimensional.
2. **Table II Range Notation:** In Table II, ranges such as `0–100` and `2–100` are used to indicate kinematically open upper bounds. Standard notation (e.g., $\ge 2$ or $[2, \infty)$) should be used instead of literal "100" values, which look like unphysical hard cuts.
3. **Table I Notation:** The second column uses dashes (`—`) in the "cells" column without defining them in the caption; explicitly state that a dash indicates that no individual cell exhibited $|z| > 2$.
4. **Figure 4 Error Bars:** Figure 4 displays ratio points with error bars showing only the diagonal uncertainties, while the text stresses that correlations completely dominate the $\chi^2$. This is visually misleading, as points that appear consistent by eye are in extreme tension under the covariance matrix. Adding a visual depiction of the correlation matrix or covariance band would improve clarity.
5. **Flux Effective Sample Size:** Sec. IV A notes that the flux covariance is estimated from 100 universes with an effective sample size of only $N_\text{eff} = 19$. Inverting a $16 \times 16$ covariance matrix with $N_\text{eff} = 19$ introduces severe Wishart-inversion bias (analogous to the Hartlap factor). The authors should discuss or correct for this finite-ensemble bias.

Merging a bin with such high background contamination requires an explanation of its effect on background subtraction systematics.
