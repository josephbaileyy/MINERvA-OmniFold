Fake-data studies find residual bias exceeding predeclared uncertainty-based thresholds at a GiBUU-like truth and a synthetic momentum-transfer tilt; an assigned fake-data bias term raises the median relative uncertainty per cell from 7.86% to about 12%.

Under the reported covariance, without the GiBUU component for the two GiBUU truths, the listed truths (p<0.01, or a cell with |z|>2) are the GiBUU hybrid truth (p=5.3e-28; cells R02, R06) and the synthetic q3 tilt, by p alone (p=9.1e-6). Without C_PL,5D, the GiBUU Eavail shape also meets the listing criterion, in cell R06 (z=2.01, against 1.76 with the block). Thus the fake-data bias exceeds the stated thresholds at the GiBUU hybrid truth and the q3 tilt under the reported covariance, and also at the GiBUU Eavail shape without C_PL,5D. The bias is the same in both; only the covariance differs, so the change is not a smaller bias or a validated uncertainty. At the other 10 truths the bias passes both criteria under the reported covariance, with no cell beyond 2 sigma and p>=0.01.

We also give a second total covariance that adds a fake-data bias term, C_FD, the sum of b b^T over one truth per generator family and the q3 tilt, chosen by family rather than by which truths fail. C_FD measures the residual bias at these truths; it is not a calibrated bound. It raises the median relative uncertainty from 7.86% to about 12% and that of cell R06 from 5.9% to 29%. Against the second total, every truth used to build C_FD passes by construction, so these fake-data tests cannot test it; the two GENIE v3 tunes, which are not in it, are the only test at a truth outside the term. C_FD and C_model share the generator directions, so the two terms are not independent.
