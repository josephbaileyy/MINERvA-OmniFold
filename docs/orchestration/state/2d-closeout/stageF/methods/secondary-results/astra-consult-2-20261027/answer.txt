**1. The paper’s core**

My recommendation is to organize the paper around the sixteen-cell measurement and move the separate calibrated-testing application to the note. The development history should be substantially reduced. **However, editing cannot resolve the measurement’s demonstrated bias or establish the coverage of its uncertainties.** Those limitations must remain prominent.

The paper must keep:

- **A fully specified, usable sixteen-cell cross section.** Keep the signal definition, exposure, target, five observable definitions, reporting domain, cell construction, central values and uncertainties. Bring a compact numerical result table into the paper; Fig. 5’s ratios are insufficient by themselves. Explain that the five observables are not independent. This is the measurement readers should be able to identify and reuse.
- **The actual estimator and its uncertainty construction.** Keep the OmniFold procedure, treatment of backgrounds and unmatched events, five-iteration regularization, relevant training choices, and construction of \(C_{\mathrm{total}}+C_{\mathrm{resp}}\). Explain what each component represents, particularly the generator-shape component and the limited recoil-response variation. A measurement needs an intelligible uncertainty model, not merely its median uncertainty.
- **Validation directly relevant to that estimator and those sixteen cells.** Keep nominal closure with backgrounds and the twelve-truth fake-data study, including its failures, selection criteria and removal of a truth’s own generator covariance component. Table IV deserves a central position. Development-estimator tests cannot substitute for this evidence.
- **A compact two-dimensional reproduction benchmark.** Keep the approximately 1% integral agreement, the unresolved correlated shape difference and the fact that learner selection followed the comparisons. This establishes continuity with the published analysis while making clear what the benchmark does—and does not—validate.
- **The sixteen-cell generator comparison and its sensitivity to correlations.** Keep Table V and Fig. 5, including rate-and-shape versus shape-only comparisons, target and domain restrictions, and the 24–48% change under weakened flux correlations. These comparisons provide the measurement’s immediate physics use, but should remain descriptive rather than become calibrated exclusion claims.
- **An accessible numerical release and concise interpretation.** The sixteen values, exact cell definitions, covariance and comparison conventions need an archival access route. The current candidate package emphasizes the separate joint tests; that does not establish availability of the headline measurement. Conclude with what this measurement adds and where its interpretation remains limited.

**3. Caveats and limitations that must stay**

Several qualifications must remain next to the measurement, even after most development studies move:

- **The 7.29% median is not demonstrated accuracy or validated coverage.** The fake-data study identifies residual bias incompatible with the tested uncertainty prescription at specified truths. Its comparison of mean estimates with covariance is not a repeated-experiment interval-coverage test.
- **The fake truths are not held out.** Removing a generator’s own covariance contribution is a useful diagnostic, but does not make those truths independent validation. Explicitly identify which Table IV entries use the reduced covariance; do not describe every failure as a test against the unchanged full covariance.
- **Prior variation is not a calibrated bias bound.** Explain that the sixteen-cell result uses a different model-uncertainty construction from the envelope study. The smaller median uncertainty does not demonstrate that the earlier precision requirement has been met.
- **The measurement’s recoil-response uncertainty has limited scope.** Keep its omissions, including neutron deposits and effects beyond the implemented scale variations. Conversely, do not transfer the joint tests’ complete omission of that band onto the sixteen-cell result: the two uncertainty treatments differ.
- **Earlier coverage and closure results have configuration-specific scope.** Miscoverage of the purity-subtracted variant does not numerically establish coverage of the final estimator; successful nominal closure does not establish recovery under generator-sized changes.
- **The reproduction is not independent validation.** Keep the shared data, post-comparison learner choice, unresolved correlated shape difference and unvalidated benchmark covariance. Its period inconsistency should remain explicit.
- **The generator comparison is conditional on the reported covariance and prediction definitions.** Preserve flux-correlation sensitivity, carbon-versus-CH mismatch, GiBUU’s restricted sample/domain, and the fact that several compared generators also contribute to the model covariance. Large descriptive \(\chi^2\) values are not automatically calibrated significances.
- **Five reported observables do not establish five independent constraints or additional discriminatory power.** Keep the reconstructed-\(W\) dependence and the absence of demonstrated gain over matched projections.
- **Reproducibility remains qualified by estimator instability and release status.** A promised package cannot replace an accessible measurement product.

If the joint-test headline moves, its entire list of calibration qualifications need not remain in the measurement paper. A neutral pointer is sufficient: “The companion note presents a separate simulation-calibrated testing application and its assumptions.” If the abstract continues to claim all ten rejections, however, the hybrid-null restriction, missing response band, unestablished fine-grid interpretation and target-correction sensitivity must accompany that claim. I recommend removing the claim from the abstract.

**4. Structure**

The current order is poorly matched to a measurement paper. The result first appears in **V F**, after an extensive account of other estimators and failed reporting definitions; its generator comparison arrives in **VII B**, followed by a substantial second inference framework. The title still advertises “Performance and limitations,” and the note’s executive summary still calls validation the main result. Those choices reveal competing organizing arguments.

A result appearing after methods and validation is normal. Burying it as the sixth subsection of a validation section, then separating it from its comparisons, is the problem.

Table I is not intrinsically inappropriate, but its present form resembles an analysis-note findings register. Most seriously, **it omits the sixteen-cell measurement, its fake-data assessment and its correlation sensitivity**, while including the separate hybrid-null tests. Remove it from the introduction. If a summary table remains useful, place a compact table of the final measurement’s validation and limitations beside that measurement.

I recommend:

1. Introduction: physics motivation, specific contribution, headline limitation and roadmap.
2. Data, simulation, signal and observable definitions.
3. Cross-section extraction: final estimator, background treatment, reporting cells and regularization choice.
4. Uncertainties and validation: final covariance; compact two-dimensional benchmark; directly relevant closure, fake-data and stability evidence.
5. Sixteen-cell cross section: numerical result, uncertainties, generator comparisons and correlation sensitivity.
6. Discussion and conclusions: physical interpretation, limitations and short pointers to the note.
7. Data availability and references.

This follows the broad progression of the published MINERvA muon-kinematic measurement—experiment, simulation, extraction, uncertainties, results and comparisons—while accommodating the extra validation needed here. [Ruterbories et al., full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.16210v4)

For the existing illustrations: retain a reduced Fig. 1, prioritize Table IV, Table V and Fig. 5, and move Table III, Fig. 4 and Table VI. Compress the background and deformation evidence from Figs. 2–3 into the validation discussion, with any retained illustration clearly tied to the final estimator.

**6. Estimated length**

I estimate **7,000–8,000 words including captions but excluding references**, after moving the separate calibrated-testing application, reducing studies of earlier configurations, and adding a compact numerical measurement table and clearer final-covariance discussion.

That is an editorial estimate, not a counted revised manuscript. The supplied extraction contains approximately 11,400 whitespace-delimited words including references and plotting text. The appropriate reduction is roughly a quarter to a third, with the retained space concentrated on the actual measurement and the evidence limiting its use.
