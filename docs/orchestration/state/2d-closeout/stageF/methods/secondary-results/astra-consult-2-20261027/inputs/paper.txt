We report a simultaneous measurement of the νµ charged-current inclusive cross section in five
observables, the muon transverse and longitudinal momenta, available hadronic energy, threemomentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass, unfolded without binning with OmniFold from
MINERvA medium-energy open data and reported in sixteen cells with a covariance whose median
per-cell uncertainty is 7.29%. Three external generator predictions compared on all sixteen cells give
χ2 of 109–272 for 16 degrees of freedom, and GiBUU, on 8 cells, 58. The coverage of the intervals
has not been validated. In a fake-data study, the reported uncertainty does not cover the estimator’s
bias at GiBUU-like truths or at a synthetic q3 tilt. The sixteen-cell χ2 values fall by 24–48% when
only the flux correlations are weakened by 10%. The same unfolding reproduces the integral of
the published muon-kinematic cross section within about 1%, with a correlated shape difference.
Pseudo-experiments measure a background-subtraction bias that negative-weight subtraction largely
removes, a partial recovery of generator-sized truth changes, and statistical undercoverage of a
deterministic purity-subtracted variant at the tested truths. As an illustration, simulation-calibrated
tests of hybrid null hypotheses for five generator predictions all reject at the 5% familywise level,
with no evidence of gain over matched coarse projections.

I.

INTRODUCTION AND SCOPE

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their interpretation depends on correcting detector response and
assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned migration
matrix [1] is widely used, but the response tensor grows
rapidly when additional observables are reported. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space
through classifier-based density-ratio estimation [2, 3].
This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding
possible without specifying a separate binned response
tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been
applied to collider data, for example eight observables in
deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [4] and twenty-four Z+jets
observables at ATLAS [5]; see Ref. [6] for a survey. In
neutrino physics it has been demonstrated on a public
simulated T2K near-detector sample [7]. Multidifferential
neutrino measurements on data reach three dimensions [8–
11].
We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy
νµ data from the MINERvA open-data release [12]. The
result is a simultaneous charged-current inclusive cross
section in five observables, the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta pT and p∥ , available hadronic energy
Eavail , three-momentum transfer q3 , and hadronic invariant mass W , reported in sixteen cells with its covariance

The validation studies assess the result through the twodimensional reproduction, background-subtraction tests,
recovery of generator-sized truth changes, a coverage test
and a fake-data study of estimator bias. They examine
whether the unfolding gives reliable multidimensional
cross sections when the simulation mismodels the truth

distinguished. The coverage of unfolded intervals is the
rate at which they contain the true cross section. The
calibration of a test is the rate at which it rejects a
true hypothesis. Calibration can be obtained without
validated intervals by simulating the whole analysis chain
at the hypothesis, so that the estimator’s bias enters the
simulated null distribution. A calibration obtained in
this way is valid to the extent that the simulated chain
reproduces the analysis of the data.
The result has three limitations, each stated with it.
The coverage of its intervals has not been validated. In
a fake-data study at 12 truths, the reported uncertainty
does not cover the estimator’s bias at the GiBUU-like
truths or at a synthetic q3 tilt (Sec. V F). The generator
χ2 depends on the flux correlations: weakening only them
by 10 % lowers the χ2 of the sixteen-cell predictions by
24–48 % (Sec. VII B).

II.

DATA, OBSERVABLES, AND DOMAINS

The analysis uses the twelve MINERvA mediumenergy, forward-horn-current run periods, corresponding
to 1.057 × 1021 protons on target and an average neutrino

energy near 6 GeV [13], from an earlier production of
the MINERvA open-data release of pre-selected analysis
ntuples (AnaTuples) [12] (see Data availability). The
detector, including the downstream MINOS near detector that serves as its muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref. [15]. The signal is a νµ charged-current interaction,
with any final state, whose true vertex lies in the tracker
fiducial volume (5980 < z < 8422 mm, inside a hexagon
of apothem 850 mm) and whose muon has θµ < 20◦ ,
1.5 < p∥ < 60 GeV/c, and pT ≤ 4.5 GeV/c. Selected simulated events outside this definition, including chargedcurrent νµ events whose muon lies outside these limits,
are background. The selection requires a reconstructed
vertex in the same volume and a reconstructed muon with
θµ < 20◦ , matched to a MINOS track of negative charge,
with a dead-time requirement, as in Ref. [13]. The flux
treatment follows Ref. [16]. After selection, the sample
contains 4.12 million selected data events. The simulation
contains 32.8 million unweighted true signal events and
20.5 million selected simulated events. The simulation is
scaled to the data exposure. The simulated events are
generated with GENIE 2.12.6 [17] and the MINERvA
Tune v1, which reweights GENIE with a non-resonant
pion reduction, a random-phase-approximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil two-particle–two-hole
(2p2h) enhancement [18]. Event selection is held fixed as
the classifier inputs and unfolded observables grow from
(pT , p∥ ) to (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ). Adding features does
not add selected events or relax the signal definition.
The unfolded observables are defined at truth level.

and p∥ = pµ cos θµ , they are the three-momentum transfer

the tests of Sec. VII do not correct. These comparisons
of the predictions with the analysis definitions are given
in Ref. [14], Secs. 8.1 and 8.3.
The fine grid is the analysis binning: the published
14 × 16 muon-momentum bins times 7 Eavail , 7 q3 , and
6 W bins. The generator tests use a coarse 35 grid in
(pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ) whose edges are the fine edges nearest the simulated tertiles. A coarse cell is reported if its
expected reconstructed signal and its truth weight at the
data exposure are both at least 1000 events. Of the coarse
cells, 109 pass. The others, which hold 0.47 % of the expected signal, stay in the total only. For GiBUU, whose
sample lacks high-energy neutrinos, the domain is the 72
supported cells with p∥ < 6 GeV/c. The construction of
the coarse grid is given in Ref. [14], Sec. 11.1.

III.

ESTIMATORS AND VALIDATION DESIGN

Each OmniFold iteration consists of two classification
steps. A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data
from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight.
The event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including
unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates

a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth
events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated
flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate
efficiency correction is applied.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction every data event in reconstructed bin b
carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1 − Bb /Db ), where
Db and Bb are the data and predicted background counts
in that bin, so the target density is D(x) wpur (b). The
unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds simulated background events with negative weight to the data,
so the target is D(x) − B(x) pointwise; a classifier refinement [22], clipped at zero, converts the signed sample
into non-negative weights before the first iteration. The
two targets share bin integrals wherever Bb < Db but
differ inside a bin wherever the background fraction varies
within it (Ref. [14], App. C).
Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose
weights it then sets, without a held-out or cross-fitted split,
early stopping, or class balancing; the training details are
in Ref. [14], Sec. 3.1.
Both estimators use five OmniFold iterations with
gradient-boosted decision-tree classifiers [23, 24]. The
iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding. The nominal settings are the scikit-learn defaults,
with LightGBM matched to their capacity. The refinement capacity of the joint-test estimator was chosen in
simulation closure studies. No hyperparameter search was
performed on data.

IV.
A.

TWO-DIMENSIONAL BENCHMARK

Comparison with the published measurement

The two-dimensional result is an end-to-end test of the
unfolding against a measurement from the same data.
Its integrated cross section is 3.069 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon,
compared with 3.039 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published analysis, a ratio of 1.010. Of the 205 reported bins,
95.1 % agree within 10 %. The standardized bin differences formed with the published uncertainties have an
RMS of 0.535. Against the full published covariance, the
descriptive covariance-weighted distance is χ2 /ndf = 2.27.
It is computed with the pseudo-inverse over the 205 bins,
which keeps all 205 eigenmodes (rank 205, condition number 1.5 × 1012 ), so that ndf is that rank. It indicates a
correlated shape difference, not a normalization difference,
spread over many eigenmodes of the published covariance.
No single pT or p∥ band carries more than 15 % of it.
The source of the shape difference has not been identified.
Figure 1 shows the principal one-dimensional projections.
We quote the LightGBM result as the central value
because its covariance uses the same estimator. The distance depends on the estimator (Ref. [14], Sec. 6.4). The
learner was selected after the LightGBM and exact-split

comparisons with the published result, so this benchmark
is a reproduction rather than an independent validation.
The purity-subtracted exact-split result differs from the
central value by a median of 1.03 % per bin (maximum
17.6 %); see Ref. [14], Sec. 3.2.

B.

Two-dimensional covariance

The covariance of our two-dimensional result is the
sum of four blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with
the central value’s estimator: systematic universes (187
universes), statistical (300 bootstrap replicas), training
seed (five seed settings, so that its own size is poorly
determined), and run period (three period groups). The
resulting relative uncertainty has a median of 6.41 % per
bin and is 4.76 % on the integrated cross section, compared with 6.85 % and 4.61 % for the publication. The
systematic-universe block dominates it bin by bin and in
the integral, and the run-period block raises the per-bin
median from 5.98 % to 6.41 %. The published flux uncertainty (median 4.01 %) is larger than ours (3.71 %), for a
reason the released inputs do not let us determine. The
covariance omits the approximation in the systematicuniverse unfoldings for events that fail reconstruction, the
learner dependence of Sec. IV A, and the dependence on
the regularization; the dependence on the prior’s generator shape is reported beside it (below). The covariance
has not been independently validated. It provides no
calibrated significance for generator comparisons. The
block sizes, the assumptions of the block sum, and the
tests of the approximation are given in Ref. [14], Secs. 6.3,
4.5, and 4.6.
The statistical block, with a median statistical uncertainty of 0.676 %, is a bootstrap of 300 replicas drawn at
the central value’s training seed, in which the background
templates are also resampled. The coverage of this block
and of the total covariance has not been tested. We make
no coverage claim on independent simulated samples or
simultaneously over all reported bins and projections.
The block supersedes two earlier statistical bands, whose
coverage studies are described in Ref. [14], Sec. 5.2.
Data from different run periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties. One period group’s
unfolded p∥ distribution, taken relative to its own simulation, differs from that of the earliest periods by a step
of about 7 % near 8 GeV/c. The cause is not known,
and these data cannot establish which period is correct.
The run-period block, built from double ratios among the
three period groups (each group’s unfolded result over
its own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the
reference), includes this inconsistency in the combined
result, 2.0 % on the integrated cross section. Because the
block is built from the observed differences among the
periods, its size is not validated and its own consistency
check cannot fail.

Normalization and closure

Of the joint-test estimator’s results, only the threeobservable marginal has been compared with the lowerdimensional result it extends: its area-weighted total
agrees with the two-dimensional result to 0.141 %, a com-

6
parison across the two estimators. The normalization
checks of the earlier purity-subtracted five-observable central value are described in Ref. [14], Sec. 8.1, and closure
checks of its estimator in three and four observables in
Ref. [14], Sec. 5.1.
Figure 2 shows that, with purity-based background
subtraction in a deterministic LightGBM estimator, endto-end pseudo-experiments at nominal truth reproduce the
input except in the highest-W cells, which are biased by
up to 4 % in simulation. In the same estimator, unbinned
negative-weight subtraction with a refinement of 100 trees
and 8 leaves reduces it to below 0.3 % in those cells (at
most 0.4 % elsewhere in the projection), leaving up to 1 %
in some joint cells. On data, the two treatments differ by
at most 1.4 %. Both kinds of closure study are described
in Ref. [14], Sec. 5.1.
In two observables, a closure test with backgrounds
(20 pseudo-experiments at nominal truth) puts the purity
and refined estimates more than one statistical standard
deviation apart in 14 of the 205 cells. The purity estimate
lies up to 5.9 statistical standard deviations from the truth
(more than one total standard deviation in one cell), and
the refined one lies within 0.44 in every cell. The twodimensional result therefore uses the refined estimator
(Ref. [14], App. C.6).

B.

C.

Statistical intervals from a deterministic LightGBM
estimator with purity-based subtraction, corrected for the
estimator’s closure bias at nominal truth, miscover at the
tested truths, most strongly when the true signal rate
is lowered by about 10 %. The end-to-end test unfolds
400 pseudo-experiments per truth. The tested change
reweights the true signal, without renormalization and
with the background unchanged, and is in effect a 10 %
reduction of the signal rate with a weak Eavail slope.
Figure 4 shows that under that change, 51 of the 153
reported cell and projection integrals have a 68% coverage
whose upper confidence bound lies below the acceptance
threshold of 0.66, with coverage as low as 0.06.
A diagnosis that fits a Gaussian bias and spread to each
quantity’s 68% and 95% coverage attributes the miscoverage to a residual bias that grows with W and survives the
nominal-truth bias correction. The source of this residual
bias has not been established. The rate change itself is
recovered, and, apart from a minor effect, the intervals
are not too narrow. Agreement in the marginal normalizations thus coexists with substantial residual bias under
the tested deformations. The interval construction and
the diagnosis are described in Ref. [14], Sec. 9.5.

Generator-sized truth changes

Figure 3 shows that the unfolding recovers only part of
a change of the Eavail shape as large as the difference between generators. When the true shape is reweighted by
the GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio, the (Eavail , W ) cells unfolded
with a development variant of the joint-test estimator using a 100-tree, 8-leaf refinement deviate from it by several
percent in the largest cells and by up to 74 % at high W ,
in the cell 0.8 ≤ Eavail < 1.5 GeV, W ≥ 3 GeV. These
deviations occur with or without background, so background subtraction alone does not explain the residual.
We interpret the residual as regularization bias because
it persists over the tested iteration numbers and classifier
capacities: in noise-free studies of that variant, thirty iterations reduce the Eavail residual by 26 %, while a larger
classifier improves the reconstruction-level fit without reducing it. For other fixed generator shapes tested with the
joint-test estimator, it is 16 % to 31 % in the most affected
(Eavail , W ) cell and lies within the quoted total uncertainty
in about half of those cells. Those uncertainties come
from an earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance that fails its specified acceptance criteria (Ref. [14],
Sec. 9.6). For the sixteen-cell result, the model term of
its reported covariance is the change in the unfolded data
when the prior is reweighted to each generator (GENIE
+ MEC, NuWro, GiBUU), unscaled and not a calibrated
bound; Sec. V F reports the fake-data tests of the estimator’s bias. The deformation studies, the iteration and
capacity tests, and the joint-test-estimator shapes are
described in Ref. [14], Secs. 9.2 and 9.3.

D.

Estimator stability

The joint-test estimator is unstable at the level of rounding: rounding-level input changes move its results by up
to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, as they move those
of its lower-capacity development variant (2.5), so it fails a
data-stability requirement of at most 0.3 (median) and 1.0
(maximum) statistical standard deviations. This spread
lies inside the data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty, but

VI.

WHAT LIMITS THE ESTIMATOR

Three results point to the unfolded result retaining part
of its prior. The first is the residual under a generatorsized change of the truth in Sec. V B, which we interpret as
regularization bias. The second is the envelope of Sec. V E,
which tracks the same bias and alone exceeds the precision
target in two of the three tested reporting definitions. The
third is the upward displacement of the external nulls by
the estimator’s pull toward its prior, which the calibration
of Sec. VII F reproduces. The miscoverage of Sec. V C is
also attributed to a residual bias, but whether that bias
has the same origin is not established.
A diagnostic in simulation, summarized in Table III,
bears on the first of these results. On the same 109 joint
cells and the fixed truth changes used to develop the
estimator, an exact binned iterative unfolding with the
same simulation and five iterations leaves about the same
median residual as the boosted-tree estimator. Of the 144
cell-and-truth-change residuals above 2 % whose diagnosis
is stable under the truth resolution, pooled over five truth
changes, the tree approximation dominates only 15. Most
of the 144 (121) lie in quantities that the binned response
constrains weakly at the analysis exposure, with Cramér–
Rao widths above 10 %, so the regularization trades a large
variance for the bias. Longer noise-free exact iteration
removes much of the residual for the GiBUU-to-GENIE
change, but not for every change. The long runs do not
converge, so this rests on their last iterates.
The diagnostic is binned, signal-only and noise-free,
with the detector response fixed at the simulation’s. It
covers only the residuals whose diagnosis is stable under
the truth resolution (38 % of the joint-cell residuals above
2 %). What unbinned information could recover, the cov-

erage failure, and the validity of any interval lie outside its
scope. Within that scope, the residual is consistent with
the regularization of the inverse problem at this binning
and prior, rather than with the tree approximation of the
classifiers (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.3).
The studies of Secs. IV and V show what must be
checked before unfolded intervals are interpreted: closure
with the backgrounds included; truth changes of generator
size, not only nominal closure; coverage under changes
of the truth, not only at nominal truth; stability under
training seeds and rounding-level input changes; and runperiod consistency. When intervals cannot be validated,
tests can still be calibrated by simulating the analysis
chain at the hypothesis, as in Sec. VII.

VII.

APPLICATION: COMPARISON WITH
GENERATOR PREDICTIONS

Unfolded results can be compared with any prediction
at truth level without simulating each generator through
the detector, which is what makes the two-dimensional
result reusable and the higher-dimensional central values
usable for qualitative comparison, and the sixteen-cell result usable for the comparison of Sec. VII B. For hypothesis tests the advantage is smaller. A test at reconstruction
level would avoid the regularization bias, but it would still
need the same response model. Each external prediction
would have to be folded through the analysis simulation’s
response, which is the hybrid construction of Sec. VII C,
with the same composition below the fine grid. The tests
of this section instead calibrate the unfolded statistic in
simulation, so that the bias enters the null distribution
rather than the decision. We have not compared their
power with that of a reconstruction-level test.

D.

Decision rule and familywise error

The Monte Carlo p value of a test is (k + 1)/(B + 1),
where k of B null pseudo-experiments reach the observed
statistic; it is never zero. Each decision uses the largest p
value over a set of shifted nulls, which add a fixed vector
to every null draw: zero, half, or all of an upper bound
D on the simulation-size difference along the null’s bias
direction (condition (iv) below), or ±2 δfine , where δfine
is the change of the noise-free unfolded result in the last
step of a refinement of the null truth’s grid (condition (i)).
The ten tests are controlled at a familywise level α =
0.05 by the Holm procedure [27], with pseudo-experiments
drawn sequentially, to at most 1999 per null. A hypothesis
is rejected only when the upper end of the two-sided 95%
Clopper–Pearson interval [28] of its exceedance fraction
lies below its Holm threshold. This rule rests on two
assumptions: each true null’s calibrated tail probability
is uniformly distributed or conservative (A1), and the
pseudo-experiments’ exceedances are independent given
the data (A2). Under them, the probability of meeting the

criterion at any number of pseudo-experiments up to 1999
is below the threshold, whatever rule determines when
sampling stops, so the decision rule controls the familywise
error rate of the ten decisions at 5% (Ref. [14], App. B.5).
The calibrated size is averaged over the nuisance priors:
at a fixed true value of the nuisance parameters it can
exceed α. The error control applies to the decisions, not
separately to the point estimates (k + 1)/(B + 1). The
calibration is valid for the simulated null to the extent
that the pseudo-experiment chain reproduces the analysis
of the data (A1). Every pseudo-experiment resamples the
same simulated events, so measuring a rejection rate for
null pseudo-data processed like the data would require an
independent simulation sample. No such rate has been
measured.
The test design was fixed before any observed statistic was computed, after descriptive per-cell residuals of
the data had been seen. The computing budget of the
calibration was extended by a bounded amount after the
observed p values were visible. Under A1 and A2, the
error control holds for any rule that decides when sampling stops, so the extension does not affect it. Of the
submitted pseudo-experiments, 224 for calibration and 53
for power did not complete within the computing time

12
limits, and the decisions use the completed ones. When
rerun, none of the lost calibration pseudo-experiments
reaches the observed statistic in any test or shifted null,
so their outcomes change no decision. A2 also requires
that the pseudo-experiments were lost independently of
their outcomes, which has not been shown. Independently
written code reproduces every p value and decision. The
design and the rerun are described in Ref. [14], Secs. 11.3
and 11.6.

E.

Conditions

The decisions rest on six conditions on the nuisance
model, numbered as in Ref. [14], Sec. 11.4.
(i) Convergence below the fine grid. Within each finegrid cell the null truth has the Tune v1 composition of the
hypothesis definition. In the L2 norm of the per-cell relative changes, the last refinement step is 0.50–0.86 of the
whole change from the coarse test grid. Convergence of
the fine grid has therefore not been shown, and the residual below it is unmeasured and potentially material. The
shifts ±2 δfine were set by assuming that the refinement
steps shrink geometrically. The measured L2 ratios also
allow slower convergence. The rejections carry over from
the simulated hybrid nulls to the generator predictions
themselves only if replacing the Tune v1’s shape below
the fine grid by G’s moves the noise-free unfolded result
by no more than ±2 δfine along δfine . Showing this would
require a demonstrated bound on the residual below the
fine grid.
(ii) Detector and hadronic response. The detector
model is the simulation’s, varied only by the drawn detector bands and a linear surrogate for the lateral bands. It
has no band for the calorimetric hadronic-energy response.
In MINERvA’s recoil-energy analyses, the uncertainty of
that response is derived from test-beam calorimetry [29]
and rises to 10 % at the highest three-momentum transfers
of the low-recoil measurement [25]. The omission matters
for the rejections. A check made after the decisions shows
that the calibrated tests restricted to the matched (pT , p∥ )
projection of Sec. VII F do not reject GENIE 2.12.10 CV
(p = 0.48 in total and 0.63 in shape), so the information
that rejects it involves the hadronic axes, on which a response band would act. The net effect of the missing band
on each p value has not been measured. The rejections
remain conditional on the simulation’s hadronic response,
and their physical interpretation would require a response
band drawn through the calibration.
In an exploratory sensitivity study, the simulation’s
reconstructed recoil energy is scaled by ±4 %, the level
of data–simulation agreement reported for test-beam protons, pions, and electrons of 0.35–2.0 GeV/c [29], and the
data are re-unfolded. In this study, every rejection stays
in place against the original null distributions, and the
observed statistics move by up to about 11 %. The study
regenerates no null distribution and tests no other form or
size of response change. Neutrons and higher-momentum

hadrons, which matter at high W , lie outside the testbeam sample. The effects the study does not test are
listed in Ref. [14], Sec. 11.8.
(iii) Interaction-model prior. The prior is the analysis’s
34-band set, with negative weights of two bands clipped.
(iv) Simulation size. The pseudo-experiments use half
of the simulation and the data all of it. The resulting shift
enters as the upper-bound shift D. It is at most about
0.07 null standard deviations, measured for the mean only,
without noise or nuisance draws. Repeating part of the
calibration with a quarter of the simulation resolved no
change of the null distributions. Under an extrapolation
to the full simulation that is conservative by design, all
ten decisions remain rejected. The two decisions closest
to their boundary exceed the value at which they would
change by 1.11 and 1.30 null standard deviations.
(v), (vi) Domain and prediction samples. GiBUU lacks
Eν > 20 GeV, and its out-of-domain truth enters the
migrations into its domain. The predictions’ own finite
samples enter the metric and the simulated residuals.

F.

Results

Table VI shows that every test is rejected at familywise
α = 0.05. NuWro and GiBUU were generated on carbon.
Correcting both predictions to the hydrocarbon target
moves both toward the data, and NuWro’s total-test rejection does not survive that correction robustly. GiBUU’s
deep-inelastic events on hydrogen do not conserve energy,
which affects its correction and possibly its carbon prediction. The decisions of Table VI concern each prediction
on the target it was generated for. The target-corrected
evaluations are descriptive and leave them unchanged. As
a sensitivity check outside the decision rule, a scan of the
multiplier of the shift along δfine finds all ten rejections determined up to a multiplier of about 5.2. The scan probes
that one direction. The calibrated null distributions differ
in location: the Tune v1 null is centred at small values,
whereas the external nulls are displaced far upward by the
estimator’s pull toward its prior, which the calibration
reproduces. The target-corrected evaluations, the scan,
and the null distributions with the observed statistics are
given in Ref. [14], Secs. 11.9 and 11.5.
The methodological contribution of these comparisons
is a calibration that puts the estimator’s bias toward its
prior and the nuisance terms into each null distribution.
The disagreements they recover were already established
by the published two-dimensional measurement on the
same data [13]. Restricted to matched 3 × 3 coarse projections of the same cells, onto (pT , p∥ ) and onto (Eavail , W ),
the same calibrated tests give p < 0.05, without a multiplicity correction, for every test in at least one of the two
projections. This study therefore provides no evidence of
additional discrimination from the joint five-observable
information. Each rejection is a global assessment of a
hybrid null. Assigning a discrepancy to a kinematic region
or nuclear mechanism would require separate tests. Power

VIII.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

The principal result is the sixteen-cell cross section of
Sec. V F, with its covariance and generator comparison;
Table I summarizes the validation findings. Section VI
relates three of them to the unfolded result retaining part
of its prior, and lists the checks they show to be needed
before unfolded intervals are interpreted. The calibrated
tests illustrate inference with a biased estimator: the
decision rule controls the familywise error rate at 5%
provided the calibration is exact.

A.

Limitations

The limitations of these results are stated with the
results they qualify, in Secs. IV–VII. The principal ones
are these. The two-dimensional covariance has not been
independently validated, and its coverage has not been
tested (Sec. IV B). The three- and four-observable central
values lack intervals of validated coverage (Sec. VII A).
The sixteen-cell result also has total uncertainties, whose
coverage is not validated; Sec. V F reports the fake-data
tests of the estimator’s bias. The generator tests reject
hybrid nulls, conditionally on the simulation’s hadronic
response. The rejections carry over to the generator
