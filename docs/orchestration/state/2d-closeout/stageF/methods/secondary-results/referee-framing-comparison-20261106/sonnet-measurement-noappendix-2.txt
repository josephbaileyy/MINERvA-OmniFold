# Referee report: Simultaneous five-observable νμ CC inclusive cross section from MINERvA open data with OmniFold

## 1. Summary of the paper

The authors apply OmniFold with gradient-boosted classifiers to the MINERvA medium-energy νμ CC inclusive open data. They unfold five observables at once (pT, p∥, Eavail, q3, W) and report the result in 16 coarse cells with a full covariance. They first reproduce the published (pT, p∥) double-differential cross section. The integrated cross section agrees to 1.0%, with a covariance-weighted χ²/ndf of 2.27 that the authors attribute to a "correlated shape difference". They then give the sixteen-cell result, whose median relative uncertainty is 7.86%. This includes a run-period block added after unexplained differences between run-period groups were seen. They compare the result with five generator predictions (χ² of 107–262 for 16 degrees of freedom, and 48 for 8 degrees of freedom for GiBUU). They also report fake-data tests in which the bias exceeds pre-set criteria at two truths: the GiBUU hybrid and a synthetic q3 tilt. The paper states openly that interval coverage is unvalidated, that a planned coverage test was stopped, and that the estimator is unstable under rounding-level input changes.

## 2. Recommendation: major revision

The measurement is interesting and the transparency is unusually high. As submitted, though, the manuscript is not a cross-section measurement that a reader can use or interpret. The central deliverable is a covariance whose size is not validated. Its largest single new component, the run-period block, was added after the problem was seen and is itself "poorly determined" (a 68% interval of 0.73–2.56 on the ratio of true to estimated size). The paper's own fake-data tests show that the estimator's bias exceeds the quoted uncertainty at a generator-sized truth change. The authors also say the estimator "retains part of its prior". The unfolding fails the authors' own data-stability requirement, by a factor of about 2.6 against a limit of 1.0 statistical standard deviation. A coverage test that would address the main concern was stopped, and the cause was not found. The χ² comparison with generators is therefore not interpretable as a physics statement. The authors disclaim this themselves, but the abstract and Sec. V B still present the numbers prominently. The paper also relies on a companion note, Ref. [14], for a large share of its substantiation. A referee cannot check that note, and many key quantities are given only as references to it. I recommend major revision. The authors should either resolve the principal deficiencies listed below or recast the paper explicitly as a methods and validation study. I do not think the current framing, a "measurement", is supported.

## 3. Major issues

**1. The uncertainties are not validated and the validation attempt failed.**
- The paper states that "no reported interval has validated coverage". The one test designed to address this (pseudo-experiments drawing flux, interaction and detector uncertainties) was stopped by a preliminary check. The uncertainties reproduced through the pseudo-experiments "disagreed with the covariance blocks they draw, for reasons not established". That is a positive indication that something in the covariance construction, or in the pseudo-experiment machinery, is wrong or inconsistent.
- It matters because the sixteen-cell result is a covariance-dependent object. Every χ², every "σ" quoted for shifts (0.19σ, 0.42σ, 0.40σ) and every statement about bias relative to uncertainty rests on it. The authors also report that an earlier statistical band undercovered in its tails, and that an earlier five-observable purity-subtracted estimator had coverage as low as 0.06 for nominal 68% intervals under a 10% rate change.
- The authors must establish why the coverage pre-check failed and fix it. They must then run the coverage test on the reported estimator and covariance, at least for the statistical block, the statistical-plus-bootstrap-based blocks, and the total at two or more truths. At minimum, a pure-statistical coverage study of the reported estimator at nominal and one shifted truth should be shown, since that needs no systematic ensemble. Without this, the quoted uncertainties cannot be called uncertainties of a measurement.

**2. The bias of the estimator is demonstrated to exceed the uncertainty, and it is not corrected or folded into the uncertainty.**
- Under the reported covariance, two truths are flagged: the GiBUU hybrid (χ² = 171, p < 10⁻²⁷, cells R02 and R06) and the q3 tilt (χ² = 52, p < 10⁻⁵). Under Ctotal alone, the GiBUU Eavail shape and the NuWro (pT, p∥, Eavail) shape are also flagged. Sec. IV E shows that for a GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail change the (Eavail, W) cells deviate "by several percent in the largest cells and by up to 74% at high W". The Discussion concedes that the unfolded result "retain[s] part of its prior".
- A measurement whose bias under generator-sized truth variations is several times its stated uncertainty is not a measurement with that uncertainty. The model block Cmodel is described as "not a calibrated bound". The authors drop the relevant generator's component when testing it, and the model term is built from shape changes in (pT, p∥) only for the 2D result. Excluding the truth's own generator from the covariance is a reasonable way to avoid circularity. It also means that the headline uncertainty visibly fails to cover the plausible truth.
- The deficiencies are: (a) the bias is a feature of an under-regularised or insufficiently iterated unfolding, since longer exact iteration removes much of the residual for the GiBUU-like change; (b) the cause is "not established" for the NuWro and q3 cases.
- The authors must do one of the following. They can identify and fix the cause, for example by studying the iteration count and classifier capacity against these truths (the paper says the iteration count was not tuned on data, but nothing prevents tuning on simulation with held-out truths). Or they can assign a calibrated bias or model uncertainty that covers the observed residuals at the held-out truths. They must show that the revised intervals then pass the fake-data tests. The correct standard is that the bias fraction at generator-sized truth changes should be small relative to the quoted total. At present it is not.

**3. The estimator fails the authors' own data-stability requirement.**
- Rounding-level input perturbations move the results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations against a requirement of at most 1.0 (maximum) and 0.3 (median), and a median of 0.23% per joint cell against a statistical uncertainty of 0.37%. This is a reproducibility defect of the estimator. The authors say this sensitivity "enters the reported statistical block by construction" (via bootstrap replicas that each carry a perturbation). They also say the share of variance was "not measured on the 16 reported cells". The training-seed block is zero because the result is bitwise independent of the seed, which is itself surprising for tree-based learners and suggests the stochastic elements are fixed rather than the estimator being stable.
- A chaotic dependence on input rounding signals that the learned weights depend on features of the training in a non-smooth way (tree split thresholds on ties and ordering, for example). It also means the bootstrap statistical block contains a component that is not statistical, so it is not what a reader would take "statistical" to mean, and its coverage as a statistical interval is unclear.
- The authors must identify the source of the instability and eliminate it, for example by averaging over an ensemble of fits, with different seeds and subsamples that actually vary the stochastic elements, and by reporting the ensemble mean. They must also measure the rounding-spread share on the 16 reported cells, which is stated as unmeasured. An estimator that is declared to fail its own acceptance requirement should not be the basis of a published central value without either passing it or a clear argument why the requirement was wrong. The cross-fitting probe was "declared" and failed, so that argument cannot rest on the probe alone.

**4. The run-period inconsistency is an unresolved data problem handled by a post-hoc covariance term.**
- The three run-period groups differ by more than bootstrap scatter (a step of about 7% near 8 GeV/c in p∥ for one group in the 2D result). The cause is unknown and "these data cannot establish which period is correct". The block has rank 2, is "not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains", and its true size is uncertain by a factor of roughly 0.7–2.6 at 68%. It was introduced after the differences were seen, and it shifts the median uncertainty by 7.8% and the most affected cell (R04) by 14.1%. It reduces each χ² by 3.6–29.3%. Its size is not independently validated.
- Adding a covariance term that scales to the observed inconsistency makes the data look more self-consistent by construction ("it contains the differences by construction"). That is an honest description, but then the check that the data pass "its own consistency check by construction" is vacuous.
- The authors must investigate the cause. Obvious candidates are the playlist-dependent flux or simulation configuration, detector calibration or alignment changes between periods, a mismatch between the simulation and data run conditions, and the "earlier production" of the release the paper says it used. A cut on run-period subsets and a comparison of per-playlist raw reconstructed distributions to simulation would help. The authors should also say what the published analysis did about this. The published analysis used the same data and presumably had the same inconsistency or a way to avoid it. If the open-data production they used differs from the published one, that alone could explain it, and the paper should state it.
- Otherwise the block should be clearly labelled as an inflationary systematic with no coverage guarantee, and results should be presented without it as the primary result with the block as an explicit alternative. At present the paper does both and takes the with-block version as "reported". The sensitivity of the conclusions to this choice is large.

**5. The 2D reproduction is described as an end-to-end test but is not one, and it shows an unexplained shape difference.**
- χ²/ndf = 2.27 against the full published covariance (pseudo-inverse over 205 bins, condition number 1.5×10¹²) shows a correlated shape difference of unidentified source. The authors state that the comparison "is therefore not an independent test of the method" because the learner implementation and the background treatment were chosen with the comparison in view. They also state that the published flux uncertainty (4.01% median) is larger than theirs (3.71%) "for a reason the released inputs do not let us determine".
- The comparison with a covariance this ill-conditioned is nearly uninterpretable. A pseudo-inverse that "keeps all 205 eigenmodes" of a matrix with condition number 10¹² gives a χ² dominated by noise in the smallest eigenvalues. I would not take the value 2.27 as indicative of anything. The authors should report the χ² restricted to a well-conditioned subspace (for example, the leading modes accounting for 99% of the variance, with the number of modes as ndf), or use a shape and norm decomposition.
- The unexplained difference could come from the choice of background subtraction, learner, the prior, unfolding regularisation, or the different data production. The authors should determine which. A tractable approach is to run a binned iterative Bayesian unfolding on the same input with the published configuration. That would show whether the shape difference comes from the data and input handling or from the ML estimator.
- The fact that the final configuration was chosen after seeing the published result (change from 2.65 to 2.27) is honestly disclosed, which I appreciate. It means that the claim in the abstract that "the unfolding reproduces the published muon-kinematic cross-section integral within about 1%" should be accompanied by the caveat that the specification was selected with the answer in view. That is a statement about the integral only, which is a weak test because it is dominated by normalization and flux.

**6. The generator comparison cannot support the physics statements it appears to make.**
- The χ² values (107–262 for 16 degrees of freedom) are described as "descriptive". With unvalidated coverage, a documented estimator bias toward the prior, a prior that is itself MINERvA Tune v1 (one of the five predictions, and the worst, with χ² = 262), and target corrections applied to NuWro and GiBUU, I do not see what a reader can learn. The target correction (adding each generator's free-proton prediction with weight 1/13) uses a GiBUU free-proton sample that "failed its sampling-closure check" with "deep-inelastic events, 46% of it, [that] do not conserve energy", and a NuWro supplement flagged for lost events at 50–100 GeV. The correction raises GiBUU's χ² by 2.3, with a sign that depends on the hydrogen sample used.
- The paper notes that the χ² is highly sensitive to correlation assumptions. Weakening the flux correlations by 10% lowers χ² by 23–38%. The model term removal changes χ² by +12.6 to +119, and removing the run-period block changes it by 3.6–29%. A statistic that moves by 25–40% under 10% changes to a correlation assumption of unvalidated accuracy is not a stable comparison. The authors say the comparison "establish[es] no exclusion of a generator", and I agree. The abstract nonetheless leads with the χ² range.
- The authors must either validate the comparison, for example with a calibrated null distribution of the sort the companion note reportedly has, or remove the χ² values from the abstract and present the comparison as a qualitative data/prediction ratio plot (Fig. 3). They should drop the pseudo-quantitative treatment, given the sensitivities listed above. The use of the covariance Cmodel built from the same generators that are then compared is circular, and the authors correctly note this, but they do not resolve it.

**7. Dependence on a companion note that the referee cannot see.**
- Nearly every substantive quantity is substantiated only by a pointer to Ref. [14] (more than 40 times). Examples are the coverage failure, the model-term properties, the stability results, the sensitivity of the Eavail definition, the run-period audit, the calibrated tests, and the sampling-closure failures of the generators. The paper cannot be assessed as self-contained. PRD papers should contain the evidence for their principal claims. Some of the necessary evidence, such as the 0.19σ and 0.42σ definitional shifts, appears only as a bare number.
- The authors must move into the paper (or a supplement that is part of the record) the evidence for each claim that determines the paper's conclusions: the coverage-test failure, the stability measurements, the run-period audit, and the justification of Cmodel. The note must be publicly available and citable. At present, the Data availability section says that a release package is "not yet deposited". That conflicts with reproducibility standards, because the paper's own quoted numbers "require the archived analysis products ... and the generator samples produced for this analysis", which are not available.

**8. Low-statistics and low-stability cells and the reporting-cell construction.**
- Cells R02, R06, R10 and R15 have a reconstruction stability of 0.16–0.25. Only 16–25% of the events reconstructed in a cell were generated there, and cells with purity below 0.1 were merged. The cells with the lowest stability (R02, R06) are exactly the cells where the largest biases are seen (both flagged in the GiBUU hybrid test). Cell definitions were chosen with one interior edge per axis (at pT 0.7, p∥ 4.5, q3 2, Eavail 0.8, W 1.8), and four boxes are left unreported. The paper does not explain the physical motivation of these cells or the selection of the edges, and says the choice is among "other reporting definitions ... studied in Ref. [14]". The sensitivity of conclusions to the edge placement is not shown. With 16 overlapping and partly "all"-range cells (for example, R01 is Eavail "all", W >1.8; R08, R09 are 'all' in q3, and 'all' in Eavail), the cell set is a collection of non-independent integrals, and it is not clear whether they partition the phase space or overlap. The text says they come from boxes and merging, but Table III lists boxes like "0–100" ranges with "1.8–100" for adjacent cells. The authors must show whether the cells are disjoint and what fraction of the total cross section they cover, given that four boxes remain unreported.
- The authors should state how the cells with stability near 0.2 can be said to carry independent information, and whether the correlation matrix shows near-degeneracy. A full 16×16 correlation matrix is given (Table V), but I cannot judge the covariance rank from the text. The run-period block has rank 2, and the statistical blocks come from 50 bootstrap replicas (reported cell level) with a 16×16 matrix. This is fine at rank 16 but sampling noise in a 50-replica covariance is substantial (roughly 20% on variances). The authors should show the effect on χ² of this finite-ensemble noise, since "no finite-ensemble correction is applied". The flux block has an effective number of universes of 19. A 16×16 covariance estimated from an effective sample of 19 is rank-deficient or badly conditioned in principle, and inverting it by Cholesky with no truncation can produce spurious χ². The authors report a bootstrap of the flux universes giving a stable χ² (within 3.8%). That is reassuring but should be demonstrated as an eigenvalue spectrum.

**9. The physical interpretability of the five-observable result.**
- The five observables are not independent: reconstructed W is an exact function of (pT, p∥, q3) (apart from a clamp), and truth W is fixed by them "only up to" the beam-axis angle and nucleon mass choice. The cross section in five observables is therefore a cross section on a lower-dimensional manifold plus smearing, and the measurement of "five simultaneous" observables does not add the information implied. In effect, the only extra information relative to (pT, p∥) is Eavail and q3 (and W through the nuclear-model assumption M). The paper acknowledges this, but the title and abstract convey a five-dimensional measurement.
- The reported object is a 16-cell integral, which is a coarse partition: five observables yielding sixteen numbers. One could ask what information the five-dimensional unfolding gives beyond a classical binned unfolding of (pT, p∥, Eavail, q3) in large bins. The motivation (avoiding the response tensor) is valid, but there is no comparison to a binned alternative at the same coarse granularity. Such a comparison is easy to make in simulation at the 16-cell level and would address whether OmniFold brings anything here.
- The definitional departures (Eavail with a 135 MeV constant, no electrons, no kaons, neutrons excluded; truth W computed with the struck-nucleon mass and q3 from generator four-vectors) are documented. The result being reported in cells that are specific to this analysis's Eavail definition makes it hard to compare with other measurements. Fine, but see item 4(d) below. I also note that Eavail, q3 and W are defined at truth level from generator four-vectors, so the quantity is generator-dependent in the sense that the unfolded observable involves the generator's FSI treatment ("after final-state interactions, where neutral pions appear undecayed"). A reader needs to know that NuWro and GiBUU were processed with the same convention, and what fraction of the Eavail uncertainty comes from the missing-neutron energy modelling. The detector response Cresp "does not cover neutron or muon deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale". For a measurement involving hadronic energy, the response systematic is therefore known to be incomplete.

## 4. Treatment of the paper's stated limitations

**Unvalidated coverage (and the stopped coverage test).** (a). This is a real deficiency. Coverage of reported intervals is the minimal requirement for a measurement. The stopped test failed on an internal consistency check, which signals a possible defect and not simply an unfinished task. Resolve by diagnosing the pre-check failure and running at least a statistical-only coverage test and a full-covariance test at held-out truths. See Major Issue 1.

**Run-period uncertainty.** (a), with a minor (b) element. The unexplained inconsistency is a deficiency in the understanding of the data. The authors should investigate its cause, by playlist-level comparisons and by checking the data production against the published version. The post-hoc block with unvalidated size can then be published as an honest, labelled conservative inflation if the cause remains unknown. The disclosure is excellent, but the block is the largest single addition to the covariance.

**Fake-data bias exceeding thresholds.** (a). This is the most serious limitation. The GiBUU hybrid has p < 10⁻²⁷ and the q3 tilt p < 10⁻⁵ under the full covariance. The estimator cannot be said to recover truth at generator-sized variations. Resolve with a regularisation and iteration study on held-out truths, or with a bias term in the covariance that is calibrated (not only the unscaled Cmodel), and a rerun of the fake-data test with an independent set of truths. The authors should also state which GiBUU-hybrid cells fail (R02, R06), and why those low-stability cells are the ones that fail.

**Stopped coverage test.** (a). See above. Note that the paper says the disagreement is "for reasons not established", which means the authors cannot rule out an error in the covariance blocks themselves.

**Low-stability cells (R02, R06, R10, R15).** (b), conditional. Reporting cells with stability 0.16–0.25 and flagging them with a dagger is acceptable if their uncertainties are honest. The data show these cells are where the bias is largest, so the uncertainties may not be honest. If the coverage and bias issues are resolved and the cells still pass, then (b). If not, these cells should be dropped or merged further. A short text on what the stability implies for the cell covariance (does the response matrix inversion amplify noise?) would help.

**Target differences (CH versus carbon, hydrogen correction, GiBUU stopping at 20 GeV).** (b) for the stated carbon-versus-CH issue, since the 8.3% effect for W < 1.1 GeV is described and the corrected comparison is shown. (a) for the specific correction applied: the GiBUU free-proton sample failed its closure check and the correction's sign depends on the sample. The GiBUU comparison on 8 cells at p∥ < 4.5 only is an acceptable restriction. The detailed hydrogen-sample tests belong in the note (c).

**Definitional departures (Eavail with 135 MeV constant, species list, truth W definition, beam-axis angle).** (b). These are acceptable if stated, and the paper states them. The first-order effect of changing the constant (0.19σ per cell, 0.42σ coherent) is small. The claim is "to first order in the unfolding, without retraining". It is acceptable, but the effect of a different species list (kaons, electrons) has not been quantified in the text. The detailed list of species and the comparison of definitions with Refs. [18–20] belong in the note (c).

**Instability under rounding-level perturbations.** (a). The estimator fails its own acceptance requirement, as discussed in Major Issue 3. This is not a limitation to be stated; it should be repaired.

**Incomplete detector response uncertainty (Cresp).** (a), partial. It covers only an energy scale on some deposit categories and does not cover neutron deposits, which are central for Eavail. The paper acknowledges this. A neutron-response variation and an energy-dependent response variation are simple additions to the existing set of universes.

**Cmodel is "not a calibrated bound".** (a). If the model uncertainty is the only protection against prior dependence, it needs some calibration against held-out truths. The G18 and AR23 tests are described as "partial evidence and are not independent". Both pass, but they share a QE model and Valencia-family MEC with the Cmodel components, as the authors say. Independent held-out truths that actually differ in the hadronic sector are needed.

**No tuning on data, but several choices made with the published result in view (learner and background subtraction).** (b). It is honestly disclosed, with the consequence that the 2D test is descriptive rather than a blind validation. The authors might note that, since the final estimator is the five-observable "joint-test" one and not the 2D one, the benchmark's relevance to the reported 16-cell result is indirect (the 2D estimator is a different configuration, with different capacity for the refinement classifier than the five-observable result). I think that point deserves a sentence and a quantitative test: unfold the 2D result with the joint-test estimator and show the distance from the published result.

**Four stated limitations about the note's calibrated tests (hybrid null; no bound on sub-grid residual; "no evidence of additional discrimination beyond matched coarse projections").** (c) mostly. The calibrated tests are not part of the paper's own claims and should either be summarised in a paragraph or left in the note. The text in item 3 of the limitations section describes tests the reader cannot see, and the abstract does not rely on them. However, the finding that "no evidence of additional discrimination beyond matched coarse projections" bears on the paper's motivation (multidimensional unfolding adds discriminating power), so it should be stated in the main text, not in a limitations list.

**Unpublished data package and non-reproducible numbers.** (a). The Data availability section says the reproduction package is "not yet deposited" and the paper's numbers require archived products and generator samples that are not released. For a PRD paper this is a real deficiency, given the paper's emphasis on reproducibility.

**Other limitations I find.**
- The input is an "earlier production" of the open-data release, so the results may not correspond to the current public release or to the published analysis's data. This could explain the shape difference and the run-period inconsistency, and must be addressed (a).
- The result uses the MINERvA Tune v1 as prior. The fake-data tests are "not held out" for most truths (the truths include the prior itself and hybrids built from generators that contribute to Cmodel). Only the two GENIE v3 tunes are held out, and they are not independent (b, but only 2 truths).
- Detector response is not varied in fake-data tests, so the tests show only the unfolding bias with a perfectly known response (b with the caveat that this is noted; see App. D closures, which are descriptive only).
- The 16 cells are chosen with the help of the data's supported region, simulated tertiles, and purity thresholds, with some cells "all" in a variable, and no check is shown that the cell definitions were frozen before looking at results (not stated; the text is silent).
- The "unbinned" advantage is partly illusory, since results are reported in 16 cells and the classifiers are trained on the same sample whose weights they set (no cross-fitting; (a), at least a test of overfitting-induced bias should be shown. The authors did a cross-fitting probe on simulated pseudo-data and report no improvement in stability, but do not say what it did to the bias).

## 5. Resolve or move out?

I would prefer that the authors resolve items (a) before publication, not move them out of the paper. Moving them into the note would leave the paper's headline claims (a cross-section measurement with a quoted covariance) unsupported while the evidence against them sits somewhere the reader does not see. The paper is already explicit that coverage is unvalidated, and I credit that. But honest disclosure of a missing validation does not make a measurement publishable as a measurement.

As a *measurement paper*, I do not think it is publishable in PRD with this set of limitations. The combination of (i) unvalidated and possibly inconsistent covariance, (ii) documented bias exceeding the uncertainty at generator-sized truth changes, (iii) an estimator failing its own stability requirement, and (iv) an unexplained data inconsistency is, taken together, disqualifying for a paper whose self-description is to report a cross section and compare it with generators. No single item is disqualifying alone, except possibly the failed coverage pre-check, whose cause is unknown and which could indicate an error in the covariance. In my view the bias-versus-uncertainty failure and the stability failure are each close to disqualifying for the measurement claim.

If the authors reframe the paper as what much of the text already is, a case study and validation of multidimensional OmniFold on public data that documents where it works and fails, and drop or heavily de-emphasise the generator χ² comparison and the "measurement" language, then the same limitations become acceptable (b) because they are the findings. In that form the paper could be published with a major revision, but it would need to be restructured and shortened. The authors should decide which paper they are writing.

## 6. Minor issues and presentation

1. The abstract is dense and lists many numbers. It lists test results (p < 0.01, |z| > 2) before the reader knows what was tested. It should be rewritten for a reader who has not seen the paper, and it should not lead with the χ² range.
2. The register is defensive and repetitive in places ("not a calibrated bound", "descriptive", "not independent" appear many dozens of times). A single, clearly structured limitations subsection, with a table of each test, its status, and its implication, would be more usable than disclaimers scattered through Secs. II–V. Sec. VI A does this partly but defers to the sections.
3. The paper reads as a condensed analysis note. There are many sentences that cannot be understood without the note: "the unbinned negative-weight treatment", "joint cells", "joint-test estimator", "hybrid truths", "support rule", "development variant". These terms should be defined where first used. "Joint-test estimator" is named for tests that are not in the paper.
4. Five estimators/variants appear (2D LightGBM, joint-test, development variant with 100/8 refinement, "earlier purity-subtracted five-observable central value", exact-split and histogram scikit-learn) and it is hard to tell which result comes from which. Fig. 2 caption says neither panel shows the reported result, and the grey band in 2b uses an earlier covariance that "fails its specified acceptance criteria". A figure that shows neither the reported estimator nor the reported covariance cannot support conclusions about the reported result. Please show the fake-data residuals for the reported estimator against the reported covariance.
5. Table I: the column layout is hard to parse in the text version, with entries such as "−GENIE + MEC" in the covariance column. The text says that Table I "does not show" Ctotal + Cresp (without the run-period block) numbers that the text then discusses (GiBUU Eavail, cell R06, z = 2.01). Please add that column.
6. Table III lists cells with "all" and overlapping ranges, for example 0–100 for Eavail and q3 in the same row as bounded ranges. The reader cannot easily see how the cells partition the space. A schematic or a statement of disjointness is needed. Is the sum of the sixteen cells equal to the total cross section, given that four boxes remain unreported?
7. The cross-section values f are integrals over boxes in units of 10⁻⁴⁰ cm²/nucleon. A cell integral in five dimensions is not a differential cross section and cannot easily be compared with any other measurement. Consider also providing a few cells' projected differential results to connect to the literature.
8. Several numbers should carry uncertainties or ranges (e.g., "0.141%" for the three-observable marginal agreement, "about 1%" in the abstract against 1.010 in the text).
9. The statement "The unfolding reproduces the published muon-kinematic cross-section integral within about 1%" is in the abstract without noting that the published and the present results use the same data, so the agreement tests the closure of the procedure and not independent systematic control.
10. Fig. 1 caption: "Here the two bands are correlated, so their overlap is not a compatibility test." That is true, but it then leaves the figure unable to display compatibility. Please provide a statistic that tests compatibility with the correlation accounted for, with the ill-conditioning problem addressed.
11. The explanation of the purity-based subtraction versus negative-weight treatment is hard to follow and could be shortened to a few sentences plus a figure of background fraction versus bin.
12. The paper says "no learner setting ... was tuned on data" but also that the refinement capacity and the iteration count were chosen "in simulation closure studies" with the same simulation that is used as the prior. State clearly how much this simulation-based tuning could still leak the prior into the result.
13. Citations in the Introduction omit related unbinned or multidimensional neutrino unfolding efforts and related ML unfolding methods beyond OmniFold (for example, other classifier-based or generative approaches, and their treatment of the prior dependence). Also, a more direct comparison with the published binned unfolding methodology for the same data would help.
14. The constant 135 MeV in the charged-pion term is nonstandard. It is stated that the definition is "used consistently by the analysis and every prediction", but a result in an unconventional definition is harder to reuse. I would ask the authors to say why they did not simply use the published definition and report the effect.
15. Length: the paper is long for the single measurement reported, and about half the text is validation narrative that points to the note. I suggest moving the secondary tests (rounding sensitivity details, the sixteen-cell versus 2D quadrant sums with correlation bounds, the Eavail constant sensitivity) to an appendix or the note.
16. The Data availability section should say clearly which numbers in the paper can be reproduced by a reader today.
17. The paper uses "σ" for cell uncertainty in several different covariances (Ctotal, Ctotal + Cresp, the full reported covariance). Please define σ each time or use distinct symbols.

## 7. Scores

SCORE: 3/10

(ii) Main contribution as I read it: a documented, unusually candid demonstration that a classifier-based unbinned unfolding of five observables on MINERvA open data can be run end to end, together with a validation record that shows where such an unfolding is biased, unstable and not yet covered by its quoted uncertainties.

(iii) The material would suit a methods-oriented venue better, for example Physical Review D in a Computational/Methods or Phys. Rev. Research style, Computer Physics Communications, or Machine Learning: Science and Technology, as a validation study of ML unfolding, with the cross-section table as a secondary product.
