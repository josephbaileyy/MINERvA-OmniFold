**Referee report: simultaneous five-observable νμ CC inclusive cross section from MINERvA open data with OmniFold**

---

## 1. Summary of the paper

The manuscript unfolds MINERvA medium-energy νμ CC inclusive open data with OmniFold in five observables (pT, p∥, Eavail, q3, W) at once. It reports the result as sixteen coarse cells with a full covariance (median relative uncertainty 7.86%). As an end-to-end test, it reproduces the published d²σ/(dpT dp∥) integral to about 1% (ratio 1.010). The reproduction has χ²/ndf = 2.27 against the published covariance, which the authors read as a correlated shape difference of unknown origin. The sixteen-cell result is compared with five generator predictions, giving χ² of 107–229 for 16 degrees of freedom (GiBUU: 48 for 8). Fake-data tests at 12 truths find bias beyond the pre-set criteria at the GiBUU-like hybrid truth and at a synthetic q3 tilt. The authors state that interval coverage has not been validated, that a coverage test was stopped by a failed preliminary check, that the estimator is unstable under rounding-level input changes, and that a run-period inconsistency of unknown cause was folded into the covariance after it was seen.

## 2. Recommendation

**Major revision.** As submitted, I would not publish this as a measurement in PRD. The paper is an unusually candid account of what was and was not validated. That candour is a real virtue, and the central message (high-dimensional unbinned unfolding on real neutrino data is possible but its uncertainties are not yet trustworthy) is useful. The paper claims to deliver a cross section "with covariance" and then compares generators with a χ² built from that covariance. Yet the authors' own findings show the covariance has no validated coverage. The estimator is also not data-stable, and the result retains a documented bias toward the prior that exceeds the authors' own thresholds at generator-sized truth changes. The run-period term, the one systematic that most affects the headline uncertainty, was tuned after seeing the discrepancy. The paper's own validation also indicates that the χ² values of 107–229 cannot be read as measures of generator disagreement. I think the work is publishable after (i) a decision about what kind of paper this is (a method demonstration on open data, or a cross-section measurement), (ii) resolving a small number of limitations by further analysis (listed below), and (iii) a substantial restructuring so the paper is self-contained instead of deferring most of its evidence to an unseen note. If the authors cannot do (ii), the paper should be recast, with a different title, abstract and conclusions, as a methods and feasibility study. It should not present generator comparisons.

## 3. Major issues

**1. The reported uncertainty has no demonstrated coverage, and the paper's central deliverable (the covariance) is therefore unvalidated.**
The paper states "No reported interval has validated coverage". The declared coverage test was stopped because "the uncertainties reproduced through its pseudo-experiments disagreed with the covariance blocks they draw, for reasons not established". The earlier statistical band undercovered in the tails. Coverage for an earlier five-observable estimator is "as low as 0.06 for 68% intervals". A covariance that cannot reproduce itself in pseudo-experiments is more than "unvalidated". It signals that either the blocks or the pseudo-experiment machinery contains an error that has not been found. This matters because every downstream quantity (the χ² table, the |z| criteria in the fake-data tests, the "median 7.86%" in the abstract) is a function of this covariance. The authors must find the cause of the disagreement and complete a coverage test for the reported estimator and reported covariance. That test should include a pointwise test and a test of the χ² statistic itself, since the χ² is what the paper uses. The test should be at nominal truth and at one or more held-out truths. If this cannot be done, the paper must not present the covariance as a total uncertainty. It should be labelled a "nominal, unvalidated error estimate", and the generator χ² should be removed.

**2. The estimator fails the authors' own stability requirement and has a documented residual bias toward the prior; neither is propagated into the result.**
Section IV F reports that rounding-level perturbations of the inputs move results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations. The pre-set requirement was ≤0.3 (median) and ≤1.0 (maximum). Two cross-fitting and regularisation variants did not fix this. The statistical block is said to absorb this scatter "by construction" through the 50 bootstrap replicas, but the paper also says this share "was not measured on the 16 reported cells" and that on the finer cells it is about 37% of the statistical variance. Separately, Sec. IV E shows that for a GiBUU-sized Eavail change the development variant recovers only part of the change, with residuals up to 74% in one (Eavail, W) cell. Exact iterative unfolding does not remove the residual for NuWro (pT, p∥, Eavail) or q3 tests, so "no single cause is established". Section VI says that the result "retain[s] part of its prior". Cmodel is "not a calibrated bound". The authors need to show that the reported covariance bounds this prior-retention bias on the sixteen cells, or add an explicit bias term. The Table I fake-data tests are the right framework, but they fail at the GiBUU hybrid truth (p < 10⁻²⁷) and the q3 tilt (p < 10⁻⁵). The failure is not marginal. An estimator whose bias at a generator-sized truth is many sigma cannot be said to give a result "within uncertainties" of the true cross section. The authors should (a) identify and, if possible, reduce the bias (more iterations, regularisation choice, cross-fitting, a flatter prior), or (b) add a bias-based systematic that makes the fake-data tests pass at held-out truths, and then re-run the tests blind. Declaring thresholds in advance and then reporting that they were exceeded, without altering the claims, does not settle the matter. Either the result is usable for generator comparison, or it is not.

**3. The generator χ² comparison (Sec. V B, Table II, abstract) is not interpretable, and the paper partly says so while still presenting it.**
The χ² values are 107–229 for 16 degrees of freedom. They were computed with a covariance of unvalidated coverage. They use an estimator with a bias toward the prior, so the test statistic is displaced for external predictions. The authors themselves say the calibration "reproduces that displacement". The χ² also depends on the run-period block (lowering χ² by 3.6–29.3%) and on the flux-correlation model (weakening the flux correlations by 10% lowers χ² by 23–38%). It depends strongly on Cmodel, whose removal raises χ² by up to +119, and whose doubling changes χ² only a little (−2 to −5), which suggests a non-monotonic or ill-conditioned dependence. Table II footnotes also disclose generator-sample defects (GiBUU's free-proton sample "failed its sampling-closure check, and its deep-inelastic events, 46% of it, do not conserve energy"; NuWro's high-energy supplement is "flagged"). The authors rightly say the comparison is "descriptive" and "establish[es] no exclusion of a generator". If so, the numbers do not belong in the abstract. If the paper wants to be useful to the generator community, the comparison must be made meaningful, for example by using a calibrated test (the note's) with the result reproduced in the paper, or by reporting shape and normalisation separately with a covariance for which the χ² null distribution is known. At minimum, the abstract and Sec. V B must state in their first sentence that the χ² has no calibrated reference distribution, and the 16-dof framing should be dropped or qualified in the abstract.

**4. The run-period inconsistency is a data-quality problem that has been absorbed into the covariance, not resolved.**
The three period groups differ "by more than their bootstrap scatter". One group has a ~7% step in p∥ near 8 GeV/c, "of unknown cause". The block CPL,5D "was added after these differences were seen", is rank 2, is "not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains", and its size has a 68% interval on the true/estimated ratio of 0.73–2.56. This raises the median uncertainty by 7.8% and R04 by 14.1%, and it lowers the generator χ² by up to 29%. A discrepancy of this size between run periods in an analysis that otherwise claims percent-level control should be investigated. Candidates the text does not discuss include period-dependent detector or flux mismodelling, a period-dependent reconstruction or MINOS efficiency, beam-intensity pile-up effects, or a bug in the open-data release being used (the paper notes an "earlier production"). If the cause cannot be found, the authors should show the per-period unfolded results in the paper and say what a collaboration-style analysis would do (for example, drop or reweight the discrepant period). They should also show the sensitivity of the headline result to excluding it. An inflation tuned after seeing the data that also happens to reduce χ² against the model under test cannot be treated as an ordinary systematic.

**5. The two-dimensional "end-to-end test" is not an independent validation, and the paper agrees, but the abstract and Introduction still rely on it.**
The abstract says the unfolding "reproduces the published muon-kinematic cross-section integral within about 1%". The text then states that the learner implementation and the background treatment were chosen "with the comparison to the published result in view", and that the final choice lowered χ²/ndf from 2.65 to 2.27 after the comparison was known. The remaining χ²/ndf = 2.27 with pseudo-inverse over 205 bins (condition number 1.5 × 10¹²) is an unreliable number in the first place. The published statistics are not available to fully reproduce, and pseudo-inverse χ² with so many near-null modes is not well-defined. The 1.010 integral ratio is a one-dimensional number. The "RMS 0.535 of standardised differences" is a weak statement given the strong correlations and the 6.41% vs 6.85% uncertainty comparison. The authors should either present the comparison honestly as a consistency check (and remove "end-to-end test" language and the abstract's implication of validation), or design a genuinely blind test (for example, hold out a feature of the published analysis and fix the configuration before looking). A mode-by-mode (eigenmode-projected) comparison with an effective-rank truncation justified by the statistical precision would be more informative than the full-rank pseudo-inverse.

**6. The paper depends on an unseen companion note for most of its evidential weight.**
Almost every key claim defers to "Ref. [14], Sec. X": the exact support rule, the calibrated joint tests, the closure tests with backgrounds, the choice of five iterations, the estimator selection (Sec. 9.2), the coverage tests, the Cmodel construction, and the effect of the definition of Eavail. A PRD referee, and a reader, cannot verify the manuscript on its own. There are around seventy references to the note in the text. The paper therefore reads as a summary of a larger document rather than a self-contained article. The authors should decide what the paper claims and include, in the paper or an appendix, all the evidence needed to support exactly those claims. The reported result's central validation (closure at nominal truth with backgrounds, for the reported estimator and reported cells) should appear in the paper with numbers, not only a figure of a development variant.

**7. The results in Fig. 2 and Sec. IV E are for estimators and covariances that are not the reported ones.**
Panel (a) is a "development variant ... not the reported estimator". Panel (b) uses a grey band from "an earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance that fails its specified acceptance criteria". Neither shows "the sixteen-cell result or its reported covariance". The text then states that, with this earlier covariance, the residual is within the band in "about half of those cells". This is a figure whose two panels do not test the object being reported. The authors should produce the equivalent of Fig. 2b for the actual reported estimator, on the reported sixteen cells, with the reported covariance. Table I partly does this, so the figure should be replaced by a figure of the Table I information (bias per cell and per truth, in units of σ).

**8. The fake-data truths are partly the same ones used to build Cmodel and the estimator, and the held-out claim is weak.**
Table I notes "The truths are not held out". Two of the 12 truths are closures, five are hybrids of the generators that also build Cmodel, and the covariance "omits that generator's component" when the truth comes from that generator. This leave-one-out construction is reasonable. But Cmodel has only three components, so removing one removes a third of the model term, and the remaining model covariance has rank at most 2. The test then asks whether a two-generator model covariance spans the third. The GENIE v3 tunes provide the closest held-out check, and these pass (χ² = 9 and 14), but the paper admits they share the quasielastic model with other truths and "are not independent". The result that a well-chosen truth near the prior passes while a truth far from it (GiBUU, q3 tilt) fails is what one expects of a regularised estimator, and it does not by itself validate the method. The authors should add at least one held-out truth with a physically different hadronic final state (for example, different pion multiplicities or a large non-Valencia 2p2h change) and report the maximum bias across a larger set of truths.

**9. Eavail and W definitions, and the target-nucleus issue, make the "cross-section" comparable to generators only with caveats that are not folded into the uncertainties.**
(a) Eavail uses a nonstandard constant (135 MeV for charged pions), omits neutrons and kaons, includes π⁰ and γ energies in full, and electrons and positrons are excluded. This is a hand-defined observable, "No single published definition of Eavail exists". The authors check it moves results by at most 0.19σ in any cell, which is fine. But it should be stated clearly in the abstract or title that these observables are not the MINERvA published Eavail, and comparisons to other MINERvA results require care.
(b) Truth W is "fixed by (pT, p∥, q3) only up to that event-by-event angle and the choice of M". This is an unusual definition (W computed with a struck-nucleon mass, or an average nucleon mass when none is recorded) and the observable is partly generator-dependent. The prediction side uses "an average nucleon mass where the generator records no single struck nucleon", and W is therefore partly generator-defined. The claim that this "changes no cell by more than 0.07 of the cell's uncertainty" addresses numerical sensitivity, but the definitional dependence on the generator's final-state bookkeeping is a model dependence in the measured quantity itself, and this should be stated.
(c) The target difference (CH vs carbon; hydrogen) affects NuWro and GiBUU. The correction by free-proton samples with weight 1/13 is documented as imperfect (the correction raises the GiBUU χ² by 2.3, and its sign depends on the noisy hydrogen sample). The paper says that the carbon-only cross section per nucleon exceeds CH by 8.3% for W < 1.1 GeV in GENIE. This is an effect comparable to the quoted uncertainties and it enters the comparison directly. Resolving this requires native CH generations for all generators, which the authors say they have for NuWro (agreement within a fixed tolerance, Σz² = 17.9 for 16 cells, largest |z| = 2.2). The result should be shown for native CH wherever it exists.

**10. Sixteen cells in five dimensions is a small fraction of what the abstract and title suggest.**
The headline says "five observables" and "without binning", but the product is sixteen coarse boxes defined by one interior edge per axis. Four of the 32 boxes remain unreported, several are merged, and four of the sixteen reported cells have stability of only 0.16–0.25. The boxes are not 5D differential cross sections. They are 16 integrals. The physics content is therefore much smaller than the method suggests, and the benefit of an unbinned method over a binned 16-bin unfolding is not demonstrated. The cell definitions also appear to have been fitted to the data and to the unfolding's behaviour (merging cells with purity below 0.1, support rule 1000 events). Please justify why the benefit of 5D unbinned unfolding exceeds a direct binned unfolding of the same 16 cells, for example by performing the binned 16-cell unfold with the same data and comparing. That comparison would also test the method.

**11. The decision rules for flagging, and the treatment of the flagged results, are internally in tension.**
The pre-declared criterion is "p < 0.01 or any cell with |z| > 2". With 12 truths × 16 cells, the chance that some cell at some truth has |z| > 2 under a correct covariance is large (about 1 − 0.954^192 ≈ 1 if cells were independent, though they are correlated). The criterion therefore has a high false-alarm rate by construction, which the paper does not discuss. Conversely, whether a result "passes" depends on which covariance variant is used: the GiBUU Eavail shape fails under Ctotal and Ctotal + Cresp but passes (z=1.76) with CPL,5D, whose size has a large and unvalidated uncertainty. The pass/fail therefore depends on a block that was tuned after the fact. The authors should (a) report the per-truth, per-cell z-values in a table or supplement, (b) give the expected false-alarm rate of the criterion under the null, and (c) not use the run-period block to rescue a failure.

**12. Interpretation of the correlation structure and the bin-merging procedure leaves some residual information content unclear.**
The correlation matrix contains many large entries (up to 0.92) and a few negative ones. The run-period block has rank 2 and the model block rank ≤3. The covariance used in χ² is therefore built from several low-rank pieces on top of the full-rank statistical and flux blocks. χ² values will be dominated by the few directions in which the low-rank blocks are absent. The authors should show the eigen-spectrum of the reported covariance and the decomposition of χ² into eigenmodes, so readers see which modes drive the χ² and whether these are modes where the covariance is well determined.

## 4. The paper's stated limitations: classification

For each, (a) = real deficiency to resolve before publication; (b) = acceptable honestly-stated limitation; (c) = belongs in the companion note.

1. **Unvalidated coverage / stopped coverage test.** **(a).** The coverage test failed a preliminary check because the uncertainties reproduced by the pseudo-experiments disagreed with the covariance blocks they draw. This is a symptom of a possible defect in the uncertainty machinery. It is not a case of "coverage not tested". Resolving it requires finding the cause of the mismatch, then running the declared test (pointwise and χ²-level) for the reported estimator at nominal and at least one held-out truth. A simultaneous (global) coverage test is a stronger requirement. I would accept pointwise coverage with an explicit statement that simultaneous coverage is not established, provided the χ² statistic itself has been shown to follow its nominal distribution under pseudo-experiments that draw all the systematics.

2. **Run-period uncertainty.** **(a).** The cause of the inconsistency is unknown, the block is rank 2, is not noise-corrected, has a 0.73–2.56 size range, and was added post hoc. The resolving analysis is to investigate the origin (per-period detector/flux/efficiency diagnostics, per-period fake-data closure, checks against the published analysis's per-period consistency) and show the result with and without the discrepant group. If the cause stays unknown, the inflation can stay as an honest conservative treatment (b), but the abstract and Sec. V B must state the dependence. As it stands the covariance used in the main comparison is partly tuned to the data.

3. **Fake-data bias exceeding thresholds.** **(a).** Failing the author's own pre-registered criterion at a GiBUU-like truth (p < 10⁻²⁷) is disqualifying for the claim that the covariance covers the unfolding bias. Further analysis is needed: reduce the bias, or add a bias term, and re-test at held-out truths. The paper's alternative of describing the bias as "retaining part of its prior" and leaving the covariance unchanged is not adequate for a cross-section measurement, but would be adequate for a method paper that explicitly says the covariance excludes the prior-dependence bias.

4. **Low-stability cells (R02, R06, R10, R15; stability 0.16–0.25).** **(b), with a caveat.** Low stability is an inherent feature of five-dimensional reconstructions with limited resolution, and it is clearly flagged in Table III. It is acceptable provided that these cells are not over-interpreted. It becomes (a) because the fake-data failures are located in exactly these cells (R02, R06; R07 for the Ctotal-only case) and the prior-retention bias is strongest there. The authors should say so and either merge these cells or state that these four cells are prior-dominated.

5. **Target differences (CH vs carbon, hydrogen correction).** **(a) partly, (b) partly.** The effect is of order 8% in a W < 1.1 region and is comparable to the uncertainties. The GiBUU and NuWro samples used have disclosed defects (hydrogen samples that fail checks). Native CH generations should replace the free-proton correction wherever practical; the paper reports one for NuWro that agrees within a pre-set tolerance. Where native CH is not available, an explicit target-correction uncertainty should be added to Vg, because the current χ² uses only finite-sample statistical error for the prediction. The remaining text-level discussion of the details belongs in (c).

6. **Definitional departures (Eavail with 135 MeV, W with struck-nucleon mass, muon angle from the beam axis).** **(b).** Each is stated, quantified (≤0.19σ per cell, 0.42σ coherent for Eavail; ≤0.07σ for the W/angle choices), and applied consistently to the prediction. This is acceptable for a measurement of a defined observable. The abstract should say these are analysis-defined observables, and the paper should avoid language suggesting direct comparability to other MINERvA Eavail results. The details of the cross-definition comparison with Refs. [18–20] belong in (c).

7. **Unvalidated coverage of Cmodel and of the model uncertainty at held-out truths.** **(a).** Cmodel is a three-component, rank-≤3 envelope that is "not a calibrated bound". For a measurement whose main systematic is the dependence on the prior, an unvalidated model term is the key uncertainty. An analysis that builds Cmodel from generator-shape reweightings of the prior, varies the held-out set (for example, GENIE v3 tunes beyond the two used, or additional MEC/FSI variations), and reports whether the bias at each held-out truth is covered, would resolve this.

8. **Instability under floating-point rounding (Sec. IV F).** **(a).** A requirement set in advance (≤0.3 median, ≤1.0 max statistical sigma) is exceeded by a factor of 2.6 at the maximum. An estimator that moves by 2.6 statistical standard deviations under a rounding-level perturbation of its input is not numerically well-conditioned. The authors tried cross-fitting and two tree regularisations without success. Further analysis: determine where the instability arises (for example, histogram-bin ties in the gradient-boosted trees, float32 inputs, or near-degenerate splits), try double-precision inputs and fixed histograms (`max_bin`) or random-forest/NN classifiers, and show that the reported result is stable. Alternatively, report the ensemble-averaged result over perturbations, with the ensemble scatter as an explicit uncertainty. The paper says the scatter is in the statistical block by construction through the bootstrap replicas, but also says the share on the 16 reported cells was not measured; that measurement should be done.

9. **Shape difference of unknown origin with the published 2D result (χ²/ndf = 2.27).** **(b)**, provided that the language in the abstract and Sec. IV B is toned down (see issue 5). A reproduction at 1% in the integral with a 6.4% median uncertainty and a modest shape difference is a reasonable outcome for an independent method on open data, and honestly stated. The choices of learner and background handling made with the comparison in view are disclosed. Per-bin or eigenmode diagnostics would be a useful addition (c or a, depending on whether it reveals a trend).

10. **Prior retention / regularisation dependence (residual up to 74% in a high-W cell in the development variant).** **(a).** The authors say that "no single cause is established". This is the central methodological question of the paper (does the unfolding recover generator-sized changes?). The comparison with an exact binned iterative unfolding is informative (partially regularisation, not for NuWro or q3), but leaves the cause open. The additional analysis is to separate (i) finite iterations, (ii) classifier capacity/regularisation, (iii) the training on the same sample ("no cross-fitting"), and (iv) the information content of the features (e.g., W being a function of the other reconstructed variables), for example by running an oracle test with the true reco/truth pairs. A statement of which of these dominates for the reported estimator would convert an open-ended caveat into a result.

11. **No cross-fitting, classifiers trained on the sample whose weights they set.** **(a), minor.** A classifier fitted on the full sample it reweights can overfit and absorb statistical fluctuations, biasing the weights, and this is a candidate contributor to the instability and the prior-retention problems above. The authors state a cross-fitting probe was run on simulated pseudo-data and "found no variant that met the data-stability requirement". The probe is described only by reference, and the result for bias and coverage under cross-fitting (as opposed to stability) is not given. I would want it shown.

12. **Selection and normalization details that the paper defers: flux treatment (flux uncertainty median 3.71% vs published 4.01%, "for a reason the released inputs do not let us determine"), the 1.40% normalisation, and the "approximation in the systematic-universe unfoldings for events that fail reconstruction".** **(b)/(c).** These are honest and modest. The flux discrepancy with the published analysis should be mentioned, but need not block publication. The approximation for systematic universes on unmatched truth events should be summarised with its size in the paper.

13. **Ordering of the design choices relative to results (iteration count chosen without data; capacity "scikit-learn defaults"; estimator choice partly made while the comparison was in view).** **(b).** The paper is very explicit about this. I appreciate it and recommend the authors keep this level of disclosure. The detail of which choices were made when belongs in a short table (or in the note), not in the main text.

14. **Hybrid nulls and calibrated tests in the note "reject hybrid nulls conditionally on the simulation's hadronic response; no bound on the sub-fine-grid residual".** **(c) for the details, with (b) for the statement in the paper.** The text already says the tests are not physical exclusions. It is fine for the paper to say that, and drop the claims.

15. **GiBUU sample stops at Eν ≈ 20 GeV; GiBUU compared on 8 cells.** **(b).** A clear and reasonable restriction. The abstract describes it adequately.

16. **Generator-sample defects (NuWro 50–100 GeV supplement with aborts; GiBUU free-proton sample with 46% non-conserving DIS events).** **(a) for any comparison that is quoted; otherwise (b).** If the defective samples enter Table II, the χ² for these predictions is not a measurement of the generator. Either regenerate, or state that the NuWro and GiBUU rows are provisional and omit them from the abstract.

17. **Absence of the muon deposits, neutrons and resolution effects in Cresp.** **(a), minor.** The Cresp term is a single-scale variation of the recoil energy response. The paper's own descriptive closures show shifts of up to 0.97σ when the whole recoil energy is scaled by ±4%, which is not small. Neutron response is among the largest known recoil-energy systematics for MINERvA Eavail, and the omission should either be repaired (a neutron-response variation exists in the GEANT4 neutron reweights under "Detector") or its effect quantified.

## 5. Resolve or move out? Is the paper publishable, and is anything disqualifying?

I would prefer the authors resolve items 1, 2 (investigate), 3, 8, 10 and 16 in the classification above. They should move the remaining detail, notably most of the content on definitions, selection choices and calibrated-test minutiae, to the companion note, while keeping a self-contained validation in the paper. The paper cannot do both things it presently tries to do, which are to be a measurement paper and to disclaim every property that makes a measurement citable.

As a "measurement" paper, I do not think it is publishable in PRD with the current limitations. The combination of (i) uncovered coverage with a failed test of unknown cause, (ii) a data-instability failure of the estimator against the authors' own requirement, (iii) bias beyond pre-registered thresholds at generator-sized truths, and (iv) a post hoc tuned run-period term with unknown cause, together mean that the quoted covariance cannot be used by anyone for the purpose for which a cross-section covariance exists, which is inference. No single item, taken alone, is disqualifying for a method study. Item (i), the uncoverable covariance with an unexplained internal disagreement, is the one I would treat as disqualifying for a measurement, because it implies the uncertainty budget may contain an error.

As a "method demonstration on open data" paper, a restructured version could be publishable in PRD (or better elsewhere), provided that it (1) drops the generator-comparison claims or presents them as illustrative, (2) states in the abstract that the covariance is unvalidated and that the unfolding retains prior dependence, and (3) presents the validation failures as findings, with a quantified bias, instead of as limitations. That is the paper the evidence supports, and it would be a contribution: it documents the failure modes of high-dimensional OmniFold on real data, which are not yet in the literature.

## 6. Minor issues and presentation

1. **Register.** The text is dense, with long compound sentences heavy with parenthetical qualifications (for example, the paragraph on the Cmodel dependence of the GiBUU χ² in Sec. V B). Many sentences are accurate but hard to parse. I recommend short declarative sentences, with the qualification given once and then referred to.
2. **Abstract.** The abstract is 9 sentences and carries about 15 numbers. It states a median uncertainty of 7.86% while saying that coverage has not been validated, which a reader will find contradictory. It should state the main finding (what was learned about the method), the headline number, and the two or three most important caveats, and remove the χ² ranges (see issue 3).
3. **Length and note dependence.** The roughly seventy "Ref. [14], Sec. X" pointers make the text hard to follow. Group them, and summarise the content of the note where the paper relies on it.
4. **Section ordering.** Sec. IV is called "Uncertainties and validation" but the validation of the actual result occupies only Sec. IV G. The reported covariance is defined first (IV A) using terms (Cmodel, Cresp, CPL,5D) introduced before their motivation. A short roadmap table of the covariance blocks (name, what it covers, how it was built, validation status) would help.
5. **Appendix A and Table IV.** Table IV lists groups, but readers will want the group definitions (a short table of the 33 + 4 + 5 systematic bands) and how each is propagated (full retraining or reweighting of the prior-reweighted result). The statement "the approximation ... for events that fail reconstruction" should be made precise.
6. **Table I.** The table is hard to read as typeset (columns are split in the text extraction I received). It should give per-truth bias in units of σ for each of the 16 cells in a supplementary table, and the two covariance variants side by side. The p-values of order 10⁻²⁷ and 10⁻³⁰ are, in practice, meaningless given the unvalidated covariance and should be expressed as χ² per degree of freedom or as the maximum |z|.
7. **Figure 1.** The caption says the bands are correlated "so their overlap is not a compatibility test". It would help to add a ratio panel with the published uncertainty band and an eigenmode-based residual. The inset text is too small.
8. **Figure 2.** See issue 7. The grey step labelled "injected change of the cell truth" and the standard-error bars with 20 pseudo-experiments are small-sample statements. State how many seeds or pseudo-experiments were used in each panel in the caption. The caption already does this, but the figure axis (-40 to +100%) hides the typical residuals of several percent.
9. **Figure 3.** Error bars are the diagonal only and the paper itself says the correlations matter. A second panel showing the leading eigenmode residuals would be more informative. The x-axis key listing cell boxes should be a table (Table III already has it).
10. **Abstract and text terminology.** "Fake-data studies at a GiBUU-like truth" (abstract) is not consistent with the more precise "GiBUU hybrid truth" (text). "Joint-test estimator", "joint cells", "development variant", "refined negative-weight treatment" and "hybrid truth" are all jargon defined only in the text or the note. A glossary or earlier definitions would help.
11. **Introduction.** The statement that Iterative Bayesian unfolding "response tensor grows rapidly" is correct but omits that the sixteen-cell result needs no response tensor either, so the example does not demonstrate the advantage claimed. See issue 10.
12. **Definition of the cross section.** Eq. (1) is garbled in the typeset text: the sums over π±, π⁰ and γ are hard to parse. Please check that the typeset equation is complete and unambiguous. The phrase "(Eπ − 135 MeV)" is described as "a defined constant of 135 MeV, rather than the charged-pion mass", but is written as subtracting 135 MeV from the total energy, which is not a kinetic energy. State clearly that the observable is not the pion kinetic energy.
13. **Notation.** "χ² ... 8 degrees of freedom" is used for the eight-cell GiBUU comparison but the first (16-cell) value of ndf for shape-only is 15 and for GiBUU it is 7. Present all in one table with columns for ndf.
14. **Terminology on "unbinned".** The final output is histogrammed in the reporting cells. The unbinned feature is in the classifiers; the title/abstract phrase "without binning" should be qualified.
15. **Statistical significance claims.** "p < 10⁻²⁷" is reported with no caveat in the text about its dependence on the unvalidated covariance in the tails of the distribution (the covariance is estimated from 50 and 100 universes with an effective number 19 for flux). The χ² of a 16-dimensional vector with an estimated covariance follows a Hotelling-type distribution, not χ². Mention this, since 50 bootstrap replicas for a 16×16 statistical block (and 19 effective flux universes) will inflate χ² noticeably. The statement "No finite-ensemble correction is applied" is a concern for the χ² values in Table II.
16. **Data availability.** The release package is "not yet deposited". The numbers in the paper need the "archived analysis products" and "generator samples produced for this analysis" which are not available. For a paper whose main purpose is to show what open data can do, this is a substantive limitation. Deposit the release before publication.
17. **Unexplained flux difference.** Median 3.71% vs 4.01% for the published flux uncertainty, "for a reason the released inputs do not let us determine". A sentence about the dominant contributions would be helpful.
18. **Citations.** Check that the neutrino multidimensional cross-section reviews and the previous MINERvA q3/Eavail measurements are cited; the introduction cites only [8–11] and the MINERvA double-differential result [13].
19. **The title (not supplied) and abstract** should say "feasibility" or "method study" if the paper is recast.

## 7. Scores

**(i)** SCORE: 3/10

**(ii)** The paper's main contribution, as I read it, is a documented, candid first application of unbinned five-observable OmniFold unfolding to real MINERvA open data, which shows that the method reproduces a published integrated cross section to about 1%. It also shows that its uncertainty estimate, stability and prior dependence cannot yet be validated at the level a cross-section measurement requires.

**(iii)** Because its strongest and most original content is methodological, an honest feasibility and failure-mode study of a machine-learning unfolding method on open data, I think it would suit a journal such as Machine Learning: Science and Technology, or Computer Physics Communications / the Journal of Instrumentation as a methods paper, better than PRD in its current form. Alternatively, a restructured version stating that it is a method study could be considered by PRD.
