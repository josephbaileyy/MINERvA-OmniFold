===== Section IV (paper_body.tex lines 72-116) =====
\section{Uncertainties and validation}
\label{sec:validation}

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together, and the coverage tested here is pointwise, for each reported quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them. The closure, deformation, and coverage studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed and true distributions together and keeps the detector response at fixed truth. Throughout, the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune~v1. The envelope of Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status} instead reweights the prior used to unfold the data. Every study uses the analysis simulation's detector response.

\subsection{The reported covariance}
\label{sec:cov16}

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is $C_{\mathrm{total}}+C_{\mathrm{resp}}+C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$. Unlike the envelope of Sec.~\ref{sec:uncertainty-status}, its model term is a covariance built from three generator shifts. The term $C_{\mathrm{total}}$ sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization uncertainties of the joint-test estimator and $C_{\mathrm{model}}$. The model term $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ is built from the change in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn. It represents unscaled generator-shape dependence, not a calibrated bound, and its coverage at truths held out of its construction has not been validated, and the first two tests of an earlier route to a calibrated model term with validated coverage did not pass (\nsec{sec:fived16-cov}). The response term $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ is the response of the result to a one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil energy in the simulation. The scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and other deposits. $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$ does not cover neutron or muon deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale. It enters only this result, its fake-data tests and its comparison in Sec.~\ref{sec:gen16}. The run-period block $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ accounts for differences among the three run-period groups, whose unfolded results, each relative to its own simulation, differ by more than their bootstrap scatter. The cause is not known. As for the two-dimensional result (Sec.~\ref{sec:uq2d}), the block is built from the double ratios among the groups, with no reference period; it has rank \fvval{pl-rank}, is not corrected for the bootstrap noise it contains, and with three groups its size is poorly determined: the 68\,\% interval on the ratio of its true to its estimated size is \fvval{pl-sigratio-lo}--\fvval{pl-sigratio-hi}. It was added after these differences were seen, following an audit whose stopping criterion was fixed in advance. Passing that audit does not validate the block: its size and coverage have not been independently validated. The median per-cell relative uncertainty is \fvval{sigmed-reported}\%, \fvval{sigmed-reported-nopl}\% without $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$, and \fvval{sigmed-C_total}\% without $C_{\mathrm{PL,5D}}$ or $C_{\mathrm{resp}}$. The block raises the median by about \fvval{pl-medrise}\,\%, and the uncertainty of the most affected cell, \fvval{pl-cellrise-maxcell}, by \fvval{pl-cellrise-max}\,\%. The fake-data listing (Sec.~\ref{sec:fd16}) and the generator comparison (Sec.~\ref{sec:gen16}) are given both with and without it; the note gives the per-cell uncertainties both ways too, with the block's construction and audit (\nsec{sec:fived16-pl}).

Every $\chi^2$ of this result inverts the full covariance of the cells compared, $16\times16$ or $8\times8$, by Cholesky decomposition and without truncation, so its number of degrees of freedom is the number of cells (one fewer for the shape-only $\chi^2$). Of the blocks, the statistical block is the unbiased sample covariance of \fvval{s1-stat-replicas} bootstrap replicas, and the flux block a covariance of \fvval{fb-universes} weighted universes (effective number \fvval{s1-flux-neff}), normalized by the sum of the weights. The others are a 2p2h band, two-point shifts, chosen models, the normalization, the run-period block, built from three exposure-weighted periods, and the training-seed block, which is zero; each comparison with a prediction adds the prediction's diagonal finite-sample term $V_g$. No finite-ensemble correction is applied. The note gives each block's ensemble and normalization, the cells, the central values, the per-group uncertainties and the correlation matrix of the reported covariance (\nsec{sec:fived16-cov}).

\subsection{Two-dimensional benchmark}
\label{sec:repro2d}
\label{sec:repro2d-pub}

The two-dimensional result is an end-to-end test of the unfolding against a measurement from the same data. Its integrated cross section is \SI{\sigTwoD}{cm^2/nucleon}, compared with \SI{\sigTwoDpaper}{cm^2/nucleon} in the published analysis, a ratio of $\ratioTot$. The standardized bin differences formed with the published uncertainties have an RMS of $\pullRMS$. Against the full published covariance, the descriptive covariance-weighted distance is $\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}=\chiPaper$. It is computed with the pseudo-inverse over the 205 bins, which keeps all 205 eigenmodes (rank 205, condition number $1.5\times10^{12}$), and ndf is that rank. It indicates a correlated shape difference, not a normalization difference, spread over many eigenmodes of the published covariance. The source of the shape difference has not been identified. Figure~\ref{fig:validation} shows the principal one-dimensional projections.

We quote the LightGBM result as the central value because its covariance uses the same estimator. The distance depends on the estimator (\nsec{sec:results-tension}). The learner was selected after the LightGBM and exact-split comparisons with the published result, so the benchmark is a reproduction rather than an independent validation. The purity-subtracted exact-split result differs from the central value by a median of \SI{1.03}{\percent} per bin (maximum \SI{17.6}{\percent}), as detailed in \nsec{sec:method-config}.

\subsection{Two-dimensional covariance}
\label{sec:uq2d}

The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central value's estimator: systematic universes (187 universes, including the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap replicas), run period (three period groups), and training seed (five seed settings, so that its own size is poorly determined). The resulting relative uncertainty has a median of \SI{\uqMedian}{\percent} per bin and is \SI{\uqIntegral}{\percent} on the integrated cross section, compared with \SI{\uqPaper}{\percent} and \SI{\uqPaperIntegral}{\percent} for the publication. The published flux uncertainty (median \SI{4.01}{\percent}) is larger than ours (\SI{\fluxBand}{\percent}), for a reason the released inputs do not let us determine. The covariance omits the approximation in the systematic-universe unfoldings for events that fail reconstruction, the learner dependence of Sec.~\ref{sec:repro2d-pub}, and the dependence on the regularization, while the dependence on the prior's generator shape is reported beside it (below). The covariance has not been independently validated and provides no calibrated significance for generator comparisons. The block sizes, the assumptions of the block sum, and the tests of the approximation are given in Ref.~\cite{AnalysisNote2026}, Secs.~\ref*{N-sec:results-uq}, \ref*{N-sec:uq-combine}, and~\ref*{N-sec:uq-2d-scope}.

The statistical block, with a median statistical uncertainty of \SI{0.676}{\percent}, is a bootstrap of 300 replicas drawn at the central value's training seed, in which the background templates are also resampled. The coverage of this block and of the total covariance has not been tested. We make no coverage claim on independent simulated samples or simultaneously over all reported bins and projections.

Data from different run periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties. One period group's unfolded $\pPar$ distribution, taken relative to its own simulation, differs from that of the earliest periods by a step of about \SI{7}{\percent} near \SI{8}{GeV/c}. The cause is not known, and these data cannot establish which period is correct. The run-period block is built from double ratios among the three period groups, each group's unfolded result over its own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the reference. It includes this inconsistency in the combined result and amounts to \SI{2.0}{\percent} on the integrated cross section. Because the block is built from the observed differences among the periods, its size is not validated and its own consistency check cannot fail.

We report the dependence on the prior's generator shape as a separate covariance beside the four-block covariance, $C_{\mathrm{model}}=\sum_k s_k s_k^{\mathsf{T}}$. Here $s_k$ is the per-bin relative change of the result when the prior truth is reweighted by the ratio of the $(\pT,\pPar)$ shape of generator~$k$ (GENIE with meson-exchange currents, NuWro, or GiBUU) to that of GENIE, with its normalization kept. Its median is \SI{\cmodelMedian}{\percent} per bin. It is a measure of generator-shape model dependence, not a calibrated bound on the unfolding bias. Added to the four-block covariance, it gives a total with a median of \SI{\qrCmodelMedian}{\percent} per bin. The comparison with the published uncertainty uses the covariance without $C_{\mathrm{model}}$, because neither has a component for the residual dependence on the prior's generator shape. For the published analysis, this is because it chose its ten iterations in a fake-data study and assigns no uncertainty to the remaining dependence~\cite{MINERvA:2021owq}. The iteration counts differ (ten there, five here), so we make no statement about the size of the published analysis's residual.


===== Limitations subsection =====
\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Apart from the low stability of four of the sixteen cells (below), the limitations of these results are stated with the results they qualify, in Secs.~\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:result16}, and those of the calibrated tests in the companion note. The principal ones are these.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The two-dimensional covariance has not been independently validated, and its coverage has not been tested (Sec.~\ref{sec:uq2d}); the three- and four-observable central values lack intervals of validated coverage (Sec.~\ref{sec:central}); and the coverage of the sixteen-cell total uncertainties is not validated (the fake-data tests of Sec.~\ref{sec:fd16} assess the estimator's bias).
\item Four of the sixteen cells, \fvval{h2-lowstab-cells}, have a reconstruction stability of \fvval{h2-lowstab-min}--\fvval{h2-lowstab-max}: of the simulated signal events generated in such a cell and reconstructed, only that fraction is reconstructed in the same cell.
\item The note's calibrated tests reject hybrid nulls, conditionally on the simulation's hadronic response. However, a rejection extends to the generator prediction itself only if the residual below the fine grid, where each null takes the simulation's event composition, is bounded, and no such bound has been shown. Their calibration is valid to the extent that the simulated chain reproduces the analysis of the data (\nsec{sec:joint5d-conditions}). They are not physical exclusions of the generators, and they show no evidence of additional discrimination beyond matched coarse projections.
\end{enumerate}
