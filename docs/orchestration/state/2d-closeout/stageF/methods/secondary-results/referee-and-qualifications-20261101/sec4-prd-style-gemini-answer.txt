### 1. Handling of Untested Coverage in PRD

In published PRD neutrino cross-section measurements (MINERvA, MicroBooNE, T2K, NOvA), **frequentist coverage of unfolded covariance matrices is virtually never evaluated or claimed.** Regularized unfolding inherently trades bias for variance; nominal $1\sigma$ (68.3%) coverage across bins is mathematically non-trivial and rarely achieved without over-inflation. 

Instead, the community standard is:
* Report uncertainties as standard error propagation (multisim/bootstrap covariance).
* Assess estimator bias and robustness via **closure tests and fake-data/stress tests** with alternate generators.
* If a bias is within uncertainties, no extra coverage tuning is done; if significant, an ad-hoc systematic or model term is added.

**Recommendations on current text:**
* **Cut entirely (or keep only in the analysis note):** Historical dead-ends such as *"the first two tests of an earlier route ... did not pass"*. PRD reports the adopted method and its properties, not abandoned development paths.
* **Consolidate into Limitations:** The broad disclaimer that uncertainties represent propagated statistical and systematic variations rather than guaranteed frequentist coverage. 
* **Keep in Section IV:** The factual definitions of what $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{PL}}$ capture (e.g., sensitivity to generator shape; empirical run-period spread), retaining the fact that they are empirical envelopes rather than calibrated bounds.

### Community Practice Flags
* **Prior-shape covariance ($C_{\mathrm{model}}$):** Practices diverge across collaborations. While some include alternate generators in the covariance, others (notably recent MicroBooNE and MINERvA analyses) avoid adding model spreads directly to the covariance matrix to prevent double-counting or masking discrepancies in generator comparisons, presenting them instead as separate comparisons. Presenting $C_{\mathrm{model}}$ both with and without the total is fully acceptable and transparent.
