For a later paper, I would require the following procedure:

1. **Define the scientific resolution worth preserving.** Specify allowed merges, protected boundaries, minimum information requirements, and a deterministic selection rule. “Keep merging until everything passes” is insufficient.
2. **Treat these results as development evidence.** Freeze the selected binning before evaluating genuinely reserved truth deformations and independent samples. Fresh random seeds alone do not make familiar truth shapes held out.
3. **Freeze the whole acceptance procedure:** threshold, floor, truth domain, noise treatment, treatment of unresolved cells, and the consequence of any failure. Include an explicit terminal outcome of “no acceptable binning.”
4. **Check cancellation.** Summed signed biases can cancel when bins merge; truth departures can cancel too. A coarse-bin pass establishes performance for that coarse integral, not recovery of the lost structure.
5. **Validate the resulting product separately.** Propagate covariance consistently and test bias, stability and coverage for the intended claims. For pure merging with an unchanged estimator, assess whether existing products permit exact aggregation, including \(C_{\rm new}=ACA^\mathsf{T}\), before assuming retraining is necessary. Changed estimators or observables require broader reassessment.

These are my proposed safeguards, not claims that such a procedure has already been authorized or shown feasible.
