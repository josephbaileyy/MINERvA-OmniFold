This Supplement gives the definitions that the paper’s results depend on: the event selection,
the observables, the reporting cells and their machine-readable files, the estimators, the reported
covariances, the joint tests and the comparisons with binned unfolding, the designs of the coverage
and fake-data tests, the illustrative output on data with its descriptive comparisons with generator
predictions, and two per-cell validity rules. Several sections reproduce or adapt material from the
companion analysis note [1]. Other studies are documented in this Supplement and the accompanying
paper. References to the note identify the sections containing further detail.

S1

Signal definition and event selection

The signal definition and phase space are exactly those of Ref. [2]. Truth-level signal events are
charged-current νµ interactions in the central-tracker fiducial volume with muon phase space
θµ < 20◦ ,

1.5 < p∥ < 60 GeV/c,

pT < 4.5 GeV/c.

The fiducial volume spans 5980 < z < 8422 mm within an 850 mm apothem and contains 3.2353×1030
nucleons, the fixed tracker-geometry constant.
Selected reconstructed events have a vertex in the same fiducial volume and apothem, a muon
track with θµ < 20◦ matched to a track in the MINOS near detector with a successful MINOS track
fit, no dead-time overlap, and a neutrino (rather than antineutrino) candidate.
Genuine backgrounds are reconstructed events whose true interaction fails the signal definition:
charged-current events of other flavors or wrong sign, neutral-current interactions, and out-of-fiducialvertex events. Their predicted rate is 0.35 % of selected events, evaluated on playlist 1A. This is the
same sub-percent scale as the 8655 (0.2 %) predicted background events of Ref. [2], which counts only
the wrong-flavor, wrong-sign and neutral-current components; exact agreement is not expected given
the differences in definition and selection, and no uncertainty is printed beside either quoted fraction.
Out-of-phase-space signal, reconstructed inside the phase space with its true muon outside it, is
treated separately. Both are POT-scaled and subtracted from the reconstructed-data target before
unfolding (Ref. [1], Sec. 3). That figure is the predicted genuine-background fraction on playlist 1A.
It is not stated for the full selected sample. The overall multiplicative correction of Sec. S4 is the
total of genuine background and out-of-phase-space signal. This paragraph does not split that total,
and it does not give the kinematic distribution of either piece. The sub-percent genuine-background
scale refers to the genuine-background fraction alone.
Only playlist 1A predates the current release. Within the selection, its events and kinematics
are the same in both copies; its selected yield per proton on target differs by −0.9 × 10−5 in the
data and −1.8 × 10−5 in the simulation, of order 10−6 on the combined sample. This comparison

concerns selected samples; it does not compare unfolded results or these inputs with those of the
published analysis.

S4

Algorithm, background treatment and estimator configurations

OmniFold [6, 7] unfolds per-event observables by iterated reweighting with machine-learned likelihood
ratios, without fixed histogram bins. Each iteration has two steps:
1. Step 1 (detector space). A classifier separates measured data from reconstructed simulation
weighted by the current push-weights. Its calibrated output p gives the likelihood-ratio reweight
w = p/(1 − p) for each reconstructed simulated event. Each simulated event is a (truth, reco)
pair, allowing the weight to be pulled back directly to its truth record. Truth events without
a reconstructed partner (“misses”) receive step-1 weights from a regressor trained on the
reconstructed subsample, w(truth features). OmniFold thus accounts for acceptance without
a separate efficiency correction.
2. Step 2 (truth space). A second classifier separates the original truth-level simulation from
the same sample carrying the pulled-back step-1 weights. Its smooth truth-space reweight
becomes the next iteration’s push-weight.
After n iterations, the push-weights define an efficiency-corrected, reweighted truth-level simulation
sample. Histogramming this sample in any subsequently chosen binning gives the unfolded spectrum.
The iteration count and learner capacity regularize the unfolding.
Training details. Each classifier is trained on the full sample to which it then assigns weights,
with no held-out or cross-fitted split and no early stopping. The two classes keep their physical
weights, with no class balancing and no renormalization between iterations, so the learned ratio
p/(1 − p), with p restricted to [10−6 , 1 − 10−6 ], carries the normalization. Truth events without a
reconstructed partner receive weights predicted by an unweighted regression of the step-1 weight on
the truth observables. In the refined configurations, a classifier separating positive from negative
weights, trained with |w|, gives each event the weight |w| max(0, 2g − 1). The effective sample size
of the reported result’s final weights is given at the end of this section.
Background subtraction. In the binned purity subtraction, all selected data events enter step 1;
none is discarded and no negative-weight events are injected. Each receives a reco-bin purity weight.
The POT-scaled background prediction includes genuine backgrounds and out-of-phase-space signal
(Sec. S1) and is binned on the reconstructed analysis grid. A data event in reco bin b receives

• (e), the five-observable estimator (the joint-test estimator of Ref. [1]): a deterministic LightGBM

Production uses n = 5 iterations. Huang et al. [8] select 45 iterations for OmniFold-family
methods (20 for their binned benchmark) using a binned χ2 against known mock-data truth, a
criterion they note is infeasible on real data. We do not adopt that recommendation. Their mock

The reported five-observable covariance

The reported covariance is Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D , with Ctotal = Ce + Cmodel ; the run-period block
CPL,5D is described in Sec. S5. Ce sums
• the statistical bootstrap of configuration (e), 50 replicas, each of which refits the whole unfolding
with its own rounding-level perturbation of the inputs,
• the flux universes of the Package to Predict the Flux (PPFX), weighted by the neutrino–electron
and inverse-muon-decay constraint,
• the interaction-model and detector bands, which reweight simulated events,
• the lateral bands, which shift reconstructed kinematics (Ref. [1], Sec. 4),

• and the training-seed block.
With full-sample binning and deterministic training, the unfolds at the other seeds are bitwise equal
to the central one, so the measured training-seed block is zero in every cell. Table S4 gives each
group’s per-cell relative σ.
For Cmodel , the prior is reweighted in turn by the ratio of GENIE + MEC, NuWro or GiBUU
to GENIE CV, and the data are unfolded again. The NuWro and GiBUU ratios are built from
those generators’ carbon samples, without a correction to CH, so their shifts include the difference
between the targets as well as between the generators; the reported Cmodel keeps them on carbon.
Each generator’s shift is the relative change of the data result, and Cmodel is the unscaled sum of
their outer products, a fractional covariance; in cross-section units it is diag(x) Cmodel diag(x), with
x the cell-integrated cross sections. It represents dependence on the generator shapes. It is not a
calibrated bound, and its coverage at truths held out of its construction has not been validated. The
GiBUU reweighting is applied only below p∥ = 10 GeV/c; above it the prior is unchanged. Between
p∥ = 6 and 10 GeV/c, the GiBUU sample lacks the contribution from Eν > 20 GeV, which is at
least 4.9–5.3% of the cross section there in the GENIE CV and NuWro samples (which stop at
Eν = 50 GeV). The GiBUU shift contributes 0.890 of the trace of Cmodel , the NuWro shift 0.095 and
the GENIE + MEC shift 0.014. In every cell, 77–100% of the prior weight lies next to a discontinuity
larger than 0.1 in the reweighting ratio, created by the ratio’s statistical gate or by a cut; the shifts
are used unmodified.
For Cresp , each of four deposit categories in the simulation, protons, mesons, electromagnetic
deposits and other deposits, is scaled by its own one-standard-deviation factor, applied to that
category’s reconstructed energy. Reconstructed Eavail , q3 and W move with that scale; the muon
kinematics and the data do not. Each category’s contribution is the outer product of the halfdifference of the two unfolded shifts of configuration (e)’s data result, and Cresp is their sum. The
per-cell relative standard deviations of the four contributions are the last four columns of Table S4.
Cresp does not cover neutron or muon deposits, the effects of particle species, energy dependence and
resolution beyond a scale, the null distributions of the joint tests, the configuration-(a) products or
the two-, three- and four-dimensional results. The median per-cell relative σ of Cresp is 2.14%, and
adding Cresp to Ctotal raises the per-cell σ by a factor of 1.011–1.220 (median 1.048). The cross-talk
deposit is varied by the same two-endpoint construction. That term is reported beside Cresp and is
not summed into it, because the adopted block keeps the deposit as a separate variant. Its median
per-cell relative σ is 0.36%. Of the trace of (Ctotal + Cresp )/f f T , Ctotal contributes 0.9262 and the

constructions. Two are sample covariances of an ensemble: the statistical block, from 50 bootstrap

No finite-ensemble correction is applied to any block. The standard correction is derived for a single
unweighted sample covariance; none of these sums has that form. The flux bootstrap below measures
the scatter of the χ2 due to estimation of the flux block, without correcting it.

The block was chosen after the period differences had been seen. Before it was added, an audit

from the projection of the two-dimensional period offsets, although their sizes agree, and (iv) the
stability of its rank-two construction. Before any of its numbers were read, the audit stated that the
block would not be added if one existing component, within two standard deviations of its own size,
explained at least half of both period-group patterns, if the replica spread of its median per-cell width

within noise and part of the other two. Built from the observed differences, the block contains them

uncertainties, the fake-data tests and the generator comparison are given here with and without it.

S6

The two-dimensional covariance

The nominal covariance is the block sum C = Csyst + Cstat + CML + CPL , which assumes the
blocks are independent and the unfolding responds linearly to simultaneous perturbations, so that
per-band covariances add. The unified throw tests linearity directly (Ref. [1], App. A.2.4; Ref. [1],
Sec. 9.6) by perturbing all reweightable bands and the flux universes simultaneously in each event
and re-unfolding jointly. The procedure interpolates asymmetrically between actual −1σ and +1σ
event-weight endpoints, fixes the estimator seed for every throw, and centers the joint covariance
on the throw mean. That mean’s displacement from CV is stored separately as a per-bin vector
and its norm; per-band endpoint displacements are not stored. The matched block-sum comparator
re-unfolds both endpoints of each band using MAT’s universe-mean centering and biased 1/N
normalization. A single-throw superposition probe is insufficient to test nonlinear response. No
scalar run-to-run jitter floor is subtracted. The configuration-(a) 5D result uses the strict joint
ensemble and matched-endpoint comparator. No two-dimensional comparison of a joint throw with
the block sum is reported, and the stored mean-shift norm is not quoted.
The run-period block CPL assigns an uncertainty for the observed inconsistency between run
periods, which exceeds the other uncertainties. Its cause is unknown, so the block does not represent
a measured systematic uncertainty from an identified source. After unfolding, the double ratio (data
over simulated truth) of the data-taking periods (playlists) 1G–1M to the earliest periods, 1A–1F,
has a p∥ step of (+6.89 ± 0.46)% at 8 GeV/c. Diluted into the combined result, the raw shift of
those playlists is small in each bin: every marginal z is below 0.7. Its p∥ χ2 against the earlier 2D
budget is nevertheless 34.4, where noise would give 2.0. These data cannot establish which period, if
any, is correct.
The block is built from separate seed-42 unfoldings of three run periods, the playlist groups
1A–1F, 1G–1M and 1N–1P, each unfolded through its own simulated detector response. The block
uses each period’s unfolded result over that period’s own simulated truth. Each period is compared
with the exposure-weighted mean of the three, so no reference period is needed; the spread, applied
to the central value, gives the block. It is added after the other blocks and has rank two. With only
three period groups, its size is poorly determined. Its per-bin median fractional standard deviation
is 2.09 % (84th percentile 3.34 %, maximum 6.48 %); it is 1.17 % to 3.36 % on the p∥ projections
and 1.97 % on the integrated cross section. Added to the other blocks, it raises the per-bin σ by
a median of 5.8 % (Ref. [1], Table 4). Taking 1A–1F as the reference would give a smaller block
(per-bin median 1.38 %, integral 1.09 %).
The block’s interpretation is limited by the small number of period groups, by possible shrinkage
from the regularization, and by the statistical and training noise it retains. The block contains the
observed differences among periods by construction, so it carries no information about its coverage,
that is, about whether its size is right. For the same reason, the double-ratio χ2 against any total
that contains the block is bounded whatever the data, so it does not test the block. The raw 1G–1M
p∥ χ2 is 34.8 against the other blocks alone and 2.59 with the block added; these values indicate
sensitivity only.
In the eigenbasis of the published covariance, the largest standardized component is 4.8 and the
next nine 3.4–3.8, and the ten smallest-eigenvalue modes carry 9.6 % of the χ2 . Of the six leading
modes, three are poorly determined directions (λ/λmax ≲ 4 × 10−9 ) in the highest-p∥ bins, and three

are moderate-λ modes (4 × 10−6 to 6 × 10−5 ) below them (the paper’s figure of per-bin contributions;
Ref. [1], Sec. 6.4).
The published flux uncertainty (median 4.01 %) is larger than ours (3.71 %), for a reason that
cannot be determined from the released inputs.
The statistical block, with a median statistical uncertainty of 0.676 %, is a bootstrap of 300
replicas drawn at the central value’s training seed, in which the background templates are also
resampled.
In the 132 poorly determined directions, the combination of the other two run-period groups is
farther from the published result than the three-group combination, by 0.69 per direction, a fraction
0.31 of the three-group value, beyond noise estimated from 10 replicas. Of the six leading modes
of the χ2 , all outside the best-determined directions, three are independent of the period group at
this resolution, two are unresolved, and one differs between the 1A–1F and 1G–1M groups beyond
noise, among 18 tests at 3σ. Each group’s p∥ high-to-low ratio matches the published one, whose
own uncertainty (4.1–4.6 %) is wider than the differences between groups. Each σ comes from 10
bootstrap draws and one seed draw per group and is therefore uncertain by about ±24 %. The noise
estimate excludes the background template and systematic universes and is therefore a lower bound.
Adding the model-dependence block to the four-block covariance gives a total with a median of
7.90 % per bin. The comparison with the published uncertainty uses the covariance without Cmodel ,
because neither has a component for the residual dependence on the prior’s generator shape. For
the published analysis, this is because it chose its ten iterations in a fake-data study and assigns no
uncertainty to the remaining dependence [2]. The iteration counts differ (ten there, five here), so no
conclusion is drawn about the size of the published analysis’s residual.

S7

Joint tests: grid, support rule, statistics, calibration and conditions

The joint tests of the generator predictions, with their grid, support rule, statistics, calibration,
generative model and conditions, are given in Ref. [1], Secs. 11.1, 11.2 and 11.4; in each calibration
pseudo-experiment, the flux universe and the band universes are drawn independently, and their
weight ratios multiply.
The note’s calibrated tests reject hybrid nulls, conditional on the simulation’s hadronic response
and on the simulated chain reproducing the analysis of the data. A rejection extends to a generator
prediction only if the residual below the fine grid is bounded, and no such bound has been shown.
The rejections are not physical exclusions, and show no evidence of additional discrimination beyond
matched coarse projections. The null distributions of the note’s calibrated joint tests reproduce the
estimator’s pull toward its prior (Ref. [1], Sec. 11.5).

S9.2

The fake-data bias tests

The fake-data tests compare the estimator’s bias at truths other than the simulation with the
reported covariance. Pseudo-data are generated at 12 truths and unfolded with configuration (e).
The mean unfolded result is compared with each truth on the 16 cells, through χ2 with 16 degrees
of freedom and the per-cell pull z. The truths are
• the simulation’s prior,
• the five hybrid truths of the generator tests (Ref. [1], Sec. 11), each a prediction on the fine
grid,
• the historical shapes W1, W2 and W3 of Ref. [1], Sec. 9.4,
• a GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail shape,
• a noise-free closure at the NuWro shape,
• and a synthetic tilt of the q3 distribution at fixed (Eavail , W ).
A truth from a Cmodel generator is compared with the covariance without that generator’s shift. A
truth is flagged when p < 0.01 or when any cell has |z| > 2; these criteria were fixed before any
of the tests was graded against them. These are not held-out truths, and the detector response is
not probed. Ref. [1], Table 19 gives each truth under the reported covariance, without CPL,5D , and
under Ctotal alone. The bias of each truth is the same in all three; only the covariance differs.
The truths are evaluated in three separate groups: (i) the 8 truths from the Cmodel generators,
(ii) the other truths (the GENIE CV hybrid and the q3 tilt), and (iii) the prior truths (the simulation
prior and the Tune v1 hybrid). Under the reported covariance, the Cmodel -generator truths have 2
of 128 cells with |z| > 2 (Gaussian reference 5.82) and 1 of 8 truths with p < 0.01 (reference 0.08).
The other truths have 0 of 32 and 1 of 2, and the prior truths 0 of 32 and 0 of 2. The Gaussian
references provide context only, because the bias is deterministic.
Two truths are held out of the analysis: the GENIE v3 tunes G18_10a_02_11b and
AR23_20i_00_000, generated for this test. Each tune enters as its normalized shape ratio to
GENIE CV on the fine grid, constructed in the same way as the reweighting ratios of the Cmodel
shifts are. Where the tune’s sample fails the ratio’s statistical gate, the ratio is pooled over a coarser
cell or set to one. The ratio is applied to the simulated truth, under the simulation’s detector
response. At each tune, 20 Poisson pseudo-experiments on half the simulation and a noise-free
closure are tested against the full reported covariance, as truths with no shift in Cmodel , in a fourth
group, graded separately. The bias meets both criteria for the pseudo-experiment mean and the
closure at each tune, giving four records. The mean of the pseudo-experiments gives χ2 = 9.26
(p = 0.90, largest |z| 0.61 in R11) at G18_10a_02_11b and 14.06 (p = 0.59, 0.61 in R07) at
AR23_20i_00_000. The closures give 9.14 (p = 0.91, 0.60 in R11) and 13.43 (p = 0.64, 0.57 in
R07).

In every cell of the four records, the bias is below 0.62 of the cell’s reported σ; in cell R11 at
G18_10a_02_11b, the bias lies below the reported uncertainty only with Cmodel included. This

The prior-retention study

The prior-retention study’s thresholds. In the prior-retention study of the paper’s section
on what moves the retained prior, at the two flagged truths, each setting is classified according to
whether any tested level reduces or increases ρ by more than a threshold fixed in advance, whichever
side of the reported setting that level lies on. This threshold is the larger of 0.05 and 9.03 times the
standard deviation of the paired change of ρ over five splits of the simulation. The factor 9.03 is the
two-sided Student-t quantile on four degrees of freedom at a familywise rate of 0.01 over the study’s
twelve primary comparisons. The IBU at five iterations is classified according to whether its ρ is
lower or higher than the five-observable estimator’s (the classes “IBU retains less” and “IBU retains
more”), using the same rule with its own threshold. The retained fraction is undefined at a truth
where fewer than two cells depart from the prior by at least 1 %.

The covariance is fixed at each tune. It is not rebuilt for each pseudo-experiment, so the tests

from each pseudo-dataset.
The nominal content of each per-cell interval |ec | ≤ σc is P0 = 0.6827 for a Gaussian error.

Because the band draws are two-point or discrete, each test replaces P0 with a draw-matched null
content. The null error is the sum of one draw from each drawn band’s centred discrete law at
the tune (its universes’ shifts with their draw probabilities) and one Gaussian with the remaining
blocks’ summed covariance, including Cmodel . The components must reproduce CT to 10−12 of
its scale. From 2 × 106 simulated null errors, the null gives each cell’s content pc , the probability
+
that |ec | ≤ σc , and the joint content q, the probability that χ2 = e⊤ CT+ e ≤ Fχ−1
2 (P0 ), with CT the
n
inverse on CT ’s non-null subspace and n its dimension. The joint region is one-sided: it has an
upper boundary only. Each content is lowered by three Monte Carlo standard errors before use. A
cell under-covers when its count of the 300 errors inside the interval is at most the largest k with
P (Bin(300, pc ) ≤ k) < 0.025/Ncells , and the joint region under-covers when its count is at most the
largest k with P (Bin(300, q) ≤ k) < 0.025. The rule is one-sided, so over-coverage is reported and is
not a failure. The test size per tune (and, in the five-observable test, per reading) is at most 0.05. A
cell whose clipping narrows the drawn spread below 0.9 of its variance reads “passes, weakened by
clipping” when it is not flagged.
Neither test draws the run-period block, and the five-observable test also leaves out Cresp . Both
are positive semi-definite, so adding them can only lower each |ec |/σc and each χ2 : a cell that covers
under CT also covers under the full covariance at the tune, and an error inside the joint region stays
inside it when both matrices have the same n. Whether an under-coverage under CT persists under
the full covariance is left open, and neither test relates CT to the covariance made at the data.
Where the two-dimensional test’s χ2 sits. A descriptive decomposition of the test’s pseudoexperiments, made after its result, splits the mean χ2 at each tune as ē⊤ CT+ ē + tr(CT+ S), with ē the
mean error over the 300 pseudo-experiments and S their covariance (divisor 300). The mean-error
part is 82 % of the mean at AR23_20i_00_000 and 87 % at G18_10a_02_11b (13.6 and 21.0 per
degree of freedom); the spread part is 2.94 and 3.21 per degree of freedom. In CT ’s eigenbasis, ordered
by decreasing eigenvalue, the 32 largest directions carry under 1 % of the mean-error part and the
directions beyond the 73 largest carry 77 % and 87 %. The spread’s mean square per component is
1.6 and 1.5 over the 73 largest directions, 3.0 and 3.6 over the next ones to rank 139, and 4.4 and 4.7
beyond, where 1 is the value if CT describes the spread. Truncated to the 73 or 139 largest directions,
the joint test finds none of the pseudo-experiments in its region at either tune, against its truncated
null’s thresholds of 193 and 190 at AR23_20i_00_000 and 193 and 191 at G18_10a_02_11b. Two
eigensolvers agree on every component to at most 1.5 × 10−6 relative, no rank is unresolved, and the
truncated counts agree. The decomposition locates the χ2 and leaves open why the test under-covers.
It changes no verdict and says nothing about coverage at the data’s point.

S9.5

The stopped first five-observable coverage test

A first coverage test of the five-observable covariance at the two tunes, specified in advance, gave
no coverage result: one of its preliminary checks, which compared pseudo-experiments that move
only the detector response at fixed truth with blocks built from the prior and the response together,
stopped it before any coverage pseudo-experiment ran, and a staged redesign stopped at its first
stage under stopping rules fixed in advance. The diagnostic study of that check gave the following
results. No set of three or fewer of the 38 drawn bands brings every cell inside the window; in R08,
the cell with the largest ratio, the interaction bands MaRES, MaCCQE and MvRES carry most of
the excess. Estimator noise is rejected as the explanation for every failing cell.
For the interaction group at the nominal truth, the pseudo-experiments reproduce the block of
the response alone moved in the simulation, within 0.986–1.015 in every cell, whereas the reported
construction’s full universe at the same truth gives a block that differs from it by a factor of median

2.72 (0.49–6.51); the full block is not that response block plus a cell-mean truth-reweight block, whose
sum misses it by about the response block’s own size. A like-for-like control, whose pseudo-data
carry each interaction universe’s full event weights, reproduces the full-universe interaction block
within 0.9–1.1 in all 16 cells at the nominal truth.
The corresponding tests of the flux and detector groups draw each band alone. In all three drawn
groups, the pseudo-experiments reproduce, within 10 % in σ in every resolved cell, the block that the
same weight factors give on the simulation side. For the flux band, the departure from the window
mostly reflects a comparison of different quantities: the pseudo-experiments’ factors and the reported
construction’s whole universe at the same truth. This accounts for 7 of its 9 failing cells; two remain
unresolved. For the detector group, in cells R03, R05 and R06 the dominant factor is the full universe
at the nominal truth against the reported blocks, made at the data. Full-universe pseudo-data
reproduce the reported construction’s block at the nominal truth for the flux, GEANT4 pion and
MINOS matching-efficiency bands. For the GEANT4 neutron band, the pseudo-experiments and
the simulation side give blocks more than 10 % apart in σ in cells R00, R02, R08, R09, R10 and
R11, and for the GEANT4 proton band in R01, R05, R07 and R08, for an unknown reason. The
blocks’ non-additivity remains unexplained. Their truth dependence, between the nominal truth
and the data used to construct the reported block, lies outside the window in 15 of the 16 cells.

S10.2

Descriptive comparison with generator predictions

Table S14 compares the sixteen-cell output with five predictions through a χ2 against the reported
covariance plus each prediction’s finite-sample (diagonal) covariance Vg , with a shape-only χ2 beside
it. The comparison is descriptive: its χ2 values establish no exclusion of a generator, and they
depend on the covariance (below). For GENIE + MEC and the Tune v1, the shape-only χ2 is within
one unit of the total, although Fig. S2 shows both predictions below the data in most cells: freeing
the normalization lowers χ2 only by the residual’s component along the result’s own cell values
in the metric of the reported covariance, and for these two predictions that component is small.
GiBUU’s sample stops at Eν = 20 GeV, so GiBUU is compared on the 8 cells with p∥ < 4.5 GeV/c
only. MINERvA Tune v1 is the simulation’s prior. The size of Cmodel is not calibrated; Table S15
gives how the total χ2 changes with it.

With the run-period block, each total χ2 is lower by 3.6–30.6 % of its value without it, and each
shape-only χ2 by 7.4–39.7 %.
Weakening only the flux correlations by 10 % lowers the χ2 of the GENIE CV, GENIE + MEC
and Tune v1 predictions by 23–38 %, and the same change to the detector or lateral correlations by
at most 18 %; with the correlations of the interaction, flux, detector and lateral groups removed, each
of the three keeps χ2 ≥ 41 for 16 degrees of freedom. Bootstrapping the 100 flux universes, with the
flux variances kept, gives 16th–84th percentile bands for each of their χ2 with edges within 3.8 %
of the reported value and a median at most 0.4 % above it (Ref. [1], Sec. 10.7). These sensitivity
studies calibrate neither set of values, and no exclusion of a generator follows.

S11

The usage-recommendation and prior-spread rules

Two of the three per-cell validity rules are defined here, the usage-recommendation rule (the first)
and the prior-spread rule (the second); the third, the bias-half rule, is graded in the paper’s section
on per-cell validity and in Table S10 (test 6). The results of the prior-spread rule are withheld.
The usage-recommendation rule applies per cell the thresholds of the usage recommendations
of Ref. [14]: (i) at least 5000 effective simulated events, here those of the simulation’s weights, a
deviation from that definition, (ii) a data statistical uncertainty below 15 %, and (iii) |z| ≤ 2 at each
of the 12 graded truths under the reported covariance. Conditions (i) and (ii) are met in every cell
(the smallest effective number is 331 614, in R07, and the largest statistical uncertainty 0.368 %, in
R05); the effective number of the final weights (Sec. S4) is at least 325 119 in every cell (the smallest,
in R07), so condition (i) also holds under the definition of Ref. [14]. The rule therefore rests on
condition (iii), and its recommended test of correlation assumptions on pseudo-data is not met here.
Fourteen cells are valid under it; R02 and R06 fail, each from the GiBUU hybrid truth alone, at
z = +4.19 and +4.53.
The second rule is ours, not taken from Ref. [14]: it compares the spread of each cell’s fake-data
bias over the graded truths with the spread of the generator predictions about the analysis prior.
We withhold its results, because it uses NuWro and GiBUU predictions corrected from carbon to
CH with free-proton samples whose validation problems are unresolved (Sec. S10.1). The usagerecommendation rule grades one truth’s closure pull. It validates no interval, since the fake-data
tests grade bias only, and it excludes no cell from the output.
