The earlier no-reading precedent expressly depended on **nothing having been read that could inform a choice**, which distinguishes it from this STOP ([LL5D-AMENDMENT-A1A1.md:7–10](LL5D-AMENDMENT-A1A1.md:7)).

**Q2. Changing the designation now matters, even for an adverse finding.**

The November 9 rule explicitly assigns **Descriptive “whatever its readings”** after a repair following a STOP ([DECISION-20261109-joseph-coverage-case-wording.md:38–46](DECISION-20261109-joseph-coverage-case-wording.md:38)). The relaunch authorization did not rescind that reporting rule ([DECISION-20261110-joseph-ll5d-rerun-16-cells.md:19–24](DECISION-20261110-joseph-ll5d-rerun-16-cells.md:19)).

Giving an adverse result greater inferential weight can strengthen a methods paper’s central criticism. Therefore, the direction of the result does not make a later upgrade neutral. A referee retaining access to these records would distinguish:

- Keeping the designation: faithful application of an outcome-independent reporting convention.
- Clarifying its reason: useful editorial precision.
- Removing its inferential qualification: a substantive departure chosen after the outcome, requiring explicit disclosure and justification.

This is **not an immutable statistical law**. The wording remained open to review ([November 9 decision:52–53](DECISION-20261109-joseph-coverage-case-wording.md:52)). But openness to revision does not erase the timing of a revision.
