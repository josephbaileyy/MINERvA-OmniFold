This Supplement gives the definitions and checks behind the paper’s results. Sections S1–S3
define the event selection, the observables, and the reporting cells with their machine-readable files
and joint tests. Section S4 describes the estimators: the algorithm, the reported configurations
and their history. Sections S5–S8 give the reported covariances, the comparisons with binned
unfolding, and the designs and results of the coverage and fake-data tests. Section S9 gives the
illustrative output on data with its descriptive comparisons with generator predictions, and Sec. S10
the usage-recommendation rule, a per-cell bias-screening rule. Several sections reproduce or adapt
material from the companion analysis note [1]. Other studies are documented in this Supplement
and the accompanying paper. References to the note identify the sections containing further detail.

S1

Signal definition and event selection

The signal definition and phase space are exactly those of Ref. [2]. Truth-level signal events are
charged-current νµ interactions in the central-tracker fiducial volume with muon phase space
θµ < 20◦ ,

1.5 < p∥ < 60 GeV/c,

pT < 4.5 GeV/c.

The fiducial volume spans 5980 < z < 8422 mm within an 850 mm apothem and contains 3.2353×1030
nucleons, the fixed tracker-geometry constant.
Selected reconstructed events have a vertex in the same fiducial volume and apothem, a muon
track with θµ < 20◦ matched to a track in the MINOS near detector with a successful MINOS track
fit, no dead-time overlap, and a neutrino (rather than antineutrino) candidate.
Genuine backgrounds are selected events whose true interaction fails the signal definition: chargedcurrent events of other flavors or wrong sign, neutral-current interactions, and out-of-fiducial-vertex
events. Out-of-phase-space signal, reconstructed inside the phase space with its true muon outside
it, is routed into the background template with them, and both are POT-scaled and subtracted
from the reconstructed-data target before unfolding (Ref. [1], Sec. 3). Their sum, Nbkg (b) in each
reconstructed bin, enters the purity weight wpur (b) of Sec. S4.1.
Only playlist 1A predates the current release. Within the selection, its events and kinematics
are the same in both copies. Its selected yield per proton on target differs by −0.9 × 10−5 in the
data and −1.8 × 10−5 in the simulation, of order 10−6 on the combined sample. This comparison
concerns selected samples. It does not compare unfolded results or these inputs with those of the
published analysis.

The reported product has neither underflow nor overflow, by construction rather than by

it is excluded from the measured sample under Ref. [1], Sec. 3.3’s masking convention, and its
truth event enters the unfolding as one without a reconstructed partner. The treatment of truth
events without a reconstructed partner recovers its in-phase-space truth contribution, as for any
unreconstructed event.

criterion. Table S3 gives each cell’s box, its cell-integrated cross section f and its relative uncertainty.

The note’s calibrated tests reject hybrid nulls, conditional on the simulation’s hadronic response
and on the simulated chain reproducing the analysis of the data. A rejection extends to a generator
prediction only if the residual below the fine grid is bounded, and no such bound has been shown.
The rejections are not physical exclusions, and show no evidence of additional discrimination beyond
matched coarse projections. The null distributions of the note’s calibrated joint tests reproduce the
estimator’s pull toward its prior (Ref. [1], Sec. 11.5).

lower and upper edge of each of the sixteen cells R00–R15 on each axis. xsec16_cross_section.csv

S4

Algorithm, background treatment and estimator configurations

This section gives the algorithm and the reported estimator (Sec. S4.1), the background treatment
(Sec. S4.2), the learner settings and the iteration count (Sec. S4.3), diagnostics of the estimators
(Sec. S4.4), and the superseded configurations and how the reported ones were chosen (Sec. S4.5).

S4.1

The algorithm and the reported estimator

OmniFold [6, 7] unfolds per-event observables by iterated reweighting with machine-learned likelihood
ratios, without fixed histogram bins. Each iteration has two steps:
1. Step 1 (detector space). A classifier separates measured data from reconstructed simulation
weighted by the current push-weights. Its calibrated output p gives the likelihood-ratio reweight
w = p/(1 − p) for each reconstructed simulated event. Each simulated event is a (truth, reco)
pair, allowing the weight to be pulled back directly to its truth record. Truth events without
a reconstructed partner (“misses”) receive step-1 weights from a regressor trained on the
reconstructed subsample, w(truth features). OmniFold thus accounts for acceptance without
a separate efficiency correction.
2. Step 2 (truth space). A second classifier separates the original truth-level simulation from
the same sample carrying the pulled-back step-1 weights. Its smooth truth-space reweight
becomes the next iteration’s push-weight.
After n iterations, the push-weights define an efficiency-corrected, reweighted truth-level simulation
sample. Histogramming this sample in any subsequently chosen binning gives the unfolded spectrum.
The iteration count and learner capacity regularize the unfolding.
Training details. Each classifier is trained on the full sample to which it then assigns weights,
with no held-out or cross-fitted split and no early stopping. The two classes keep their physical
weights, with no class balancing and no renormalization between iterations. The learned ratio
p/(1 − p), with p restricted to [10−6 , 1 − 10−6 ], therefore carries the normalization. Truth events
without a reconstructed partner receive weights predicted by an unweighted regression of the step-1
weight on the truth observables. In the refined configurations, a classifier separating positive from
negative weights, trained with |w|, gives each event the weight |w| max(0, 2g − 1). The effective
sample size of the reported result’s final weights is given in Sec. S4.4.
The estimator configurations. The analysis uses six estimator configurations, labeled (a)–(f)
and listed in Table S4. All use five OmniFold iterations and gradient-boosted trees of nominal
capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps. “Deterministic” means fixed seeds and
reproducible training. Results from different configurations are not combined, and each result
identifies its configuration. Two give the reported results.
Configuration (e), the five-observable estimator (the joint-test estimator of Ref. [1]), is a deterministic LightGBM variant with the unbinned negative-weight background treatment (Sec. S4.2),
whose refinement classifier has 400 trees and 31 leaves. The negative-weight treatment reduces the
purity method’s nominal-truth bias in the highest-W column by about an order of magnitude (from
3.5–4.1 % to 0.11–0.24 %, the level of the no-background closure). The larger refinement classifier
removes most of the background-related bias that remains (Ref. [1], Sec. 9.2–Ref. [1], Sec. 9.3). A
refined five-dimensional data unfolding with it gives the sixteen-cell result, whose reported covariance
on the sixteen cells is described in Sec. S5. The 3D and 4D central values have been regenerated with
it, and no 3D or 4D covariance is quoted. Configuration (f), the two-observable estimator, is the
nominal LightGBM configuration above with refined negative-weight subtraction and the refinement
classifier of (e), seeded at the estimator seed plus three. It gives the 2D central value (seed 42) and
its uncertainty ensembles (Ref. [1], Sec. 6). The other four give none of this paper’s reported results.
Section S4.5 describes them.

The reported estimator, step by step. Configuration (e) runs as follows. Table S5 gives the
code that implements each step.
1. Refinement of the measured side. The signed sample is every measured event in the analysis
window at weight +1 and every background-simulation event in the window at −wbkg , its
exposure-scaled weight. A LightGBM classifier g (400 trees, 31 leaves, deterministic, seed plus
three) separates the positive- from the negative-weight rows, each weighted by |w|, and every
row receives ν = |w| max(0, 2g − 1), with g restricted to [10−6 , 1 − 10−6 ].
2. Iterations (five). The push weights start at ωi = 1 on the truth-selected simulated events. In
each iteration:
(a) Step 1: a LightGBM classifier (100 trees, 8 leaves, learning rate 0.1, deterministic)
separates the reconstructed simulated events that have a reconstructed partner, weighted
ωi wireco , from the refined measured rows, weighted ν, and each such event receives
ui = p/(1 − p) at its reconstructed values, with p restricted as above.
(b) Events without a reconstructed partner receive ui from a LightGBM regressor of the
same capacity, fitted with equal event weights to u on the truth coordinates of the events
that have one. The regressed ui therefore estimates the mean weight at fixed truth over
simulated events, each counted equally.
(c) The pull weight is πi = ωi ui .
(d) Step 2: a second classifier separates the truth coordinates weighted witruth from the same
coordinates weighted πi witruth , and ωi = p/(1 − p) at each event’s truth coordinates.
3. Cross section. The truth coordinates of the truth-selected events are histogrammed on the fine
grid with weight ωi witruth and divided by the completeness, the flux, the exposure, the number
of nucleons and the cell volume.
4. Reported cells. Each reporting cell’s integral is the volume-weighted sum of the fine cells it
contains.

S4.2

Background treatment

Background subtraction. In the binned purity subtraction, all selected data events enter step
1. None is discarded and no negative-weight events are injected. Each receives a reco-bin purity
weight. The POT-scaled background template comprises all selected simulated events other than
signal inside the phase space (Sec. S1), that is, genuine backgrounds and out-of-phase-space signal,
and it is binned on the reconstructed analysis grid. A data event in reco bin b receives

wpur (b) = max 0, Ndata (b) − Nbkg (b) Ndata (b),
the predicted signal purity, floored at zero in the rare bins where the background template exceeds
the data. The step-1 classifier retains the full unbinned kinematics. Before final histogramming,
only this multiplicative correction uses binning, an ∼3 % effect overall, largest where backgrounds
concentrate.
In the purity-based configurations (a)–(c), binned purity subtraction, defined above, is the only
step that uses analysis binning before final histogramming. Each data event in reconstructed bin b
receives the purity weight wpur (b) defined above. Injecting background simulation on the measured
side with negative weight gives a fully unbinned correction with the same bin-integrated estimand.
Its agreement with the binned correction is quantified on the 2D measurement below. This is the
continuous version of the purity weight recommended for nontrivial backgrounds in Ref. [8], and the
natural treatment for the larger backgrounds of the extended-fiducial analysis.
OmniFold step 1 trains a classifier to separate simulated reconstruction (class 0, weighted density
S) from the measured sample (class 1, weighted density ϕ1 ), then reweights simulation by the

The floor. Where D − B < 0, binned purity floors the correction at zero through max(0, ·). The
unbinned classifier’s bounded output p ∈ (0, 1) forces r = p/(1 − p) ≥ 0. Non-negativity is structural,
while the value at the floor follows from that bound and ordinary regularization, rather than from
a minimizer of the fit (Ref. [1], Sec. C.2). Locally negative class-1 mass makes the fit ill-posed
and requires a positive target. We apply the “Stay Positive” refinement of Ref. [9], then explicitly
zero-clip the learned refinement factor before step-1 training. Agreement between plain injection
and clipped refinement depends on the classifier.
Refined negative-weight subtraction. The refinement used the unfolding’s deterministic GBDT
configuration and independent halves of the background simulation for injection and subtraction.
Each bootstrap replica resampled every pseudo-event and refitted the refinement. The refinement
preserves the signed total to 3 × 10−6 and clips 0.01 % to 0.05 % of events. Corner bias falls from
1.66 % to 0.24 %, but residuals in some joint cells fail the nominal closure criterion. The criterion
allows none of the 153 reported quantities a mean residual beyond 3.6 standard errors of the mean
(|t| > 3.6, a fixed threshold at about the two-sided 5% level for a normal reference, Bonferronicorrected over the 153), or beyond half its bootstrap standard deviation with |t| > 3. The difference
on data has the same sign pattern as the simulated purity bias but is an order of magnitude smaller.
It measures sensitivity to the method, rather than bias. With either treatment, the estimator does
not follow generator-sized Eavail shape changes cell by cell (for the reported estimator, Sec. S4.4).
This study of the refinement ran no coverage test. The bias reduction alone does not validate
an uncertainty or complete the reassessment of the background treatment of the configuration-(a)
five-dimensional central value (Ref. [1], Sec. C.6).

S4.3

Learner settings and iteration count

Hyperparameter choice. The learner settings were fixed a priori. No hyperparameter search was
performed on data, which avoids tuning on the result. They are the scikit-learn GradientBoosting
defaults: 100 trees, learning rate 0.1, and depth-3 capacity. The LightGBM learner of the central
value and its ensembles uses matched capacity (num_leaves = 8 = 23 ), giving every learner
the same deliberately modest capacity, one of OmniFold’s regularizers (Sec. S4.1). The twoobservable estimator, configuration (f), and the superseded configuration (a) do not set the histogram
construction. Their bin edges therefore come from the library’s default row sample, and the training
seed reaches the result through that sample. The configurations this section calls deterministic set
those edges from every training row and enable the library’s deterministic training.
The Keras cross-check of Ref. [1], Sec. 7.5 uses the same multilayer perceptron for the two
classifiers and the missed-event regressor: three hidden layers of 64, 128 and 64 units, a GELU
activation, Adam, at most 50 epochs with early stopping, a per-fit standard scaling of the inputs,
and a fixed shuffle. It does not vary those choices. Its agreement does not address the float32 tie
sensitivity of Ref. [1], Sec. 9.3. The checks that follow report performance on those diagnostics. They
do not validate the quoted estimator or its intervals. They are the closure and classifier-calibration
tests (Ref. [1], Sec. 5, Ref. [1], Sec. 7.5), the insensitivity to doubling the iteration count, measured
for the exact-split cross-check (below), the backend agreement in the method-dependence studies
(Ref. [1], Sec. 6.4), and the training-seed variation band for residual ML stochasticity (Ref. [1],
Sec. 4).
Production uses n = 5 iterations. Huang et al. [10] select 45 iterations for OmniFold-family
methods (20 for their binned benchmark) using a binned χ2 against known mock-data truth, a
criterion they note is infeasible on real data. We do not adopt that recommendation. Their mock
data inject a large prior–data shape discrepancy through an RPA-modified quasielastic model,

requiring many iterations. The simulation prior differs from the measured distributions mainly in
normalization: the data exceed it by about 12%, and the data/MC ratio rises with pT from 0.88 to
about 1.2 (Ref. [1], Sec. 2.5). Cross-experiment production also typically uses ∼5 iterations [8]. The
checks support n = 5: for the exact-split cross-check, the total cross section agrees to within 0.026 %
between five and ten iterations (Ref. [1], Sec. 5), and the unfolded result reproduces the integrated
cross section of an independently published binned measurement of the same data (Ref. [1], Sec. 6).

S4.5

Superseded configurations and selection history

None of these four configurations gives one of this paper’s reported results. Configuration (a), the
nominal LightGBM learner with purity-based subtraction (seed 42 in its two-dimensional runs), gave
the companion note’s five-observable central value and covariance before the bias of the purity-based
subtraction (Sec. S4.2) was found. Configuration (b), scikit-learn’s exact splits with purity-based
subtraction, was the quoted two-dimensional central value in an earlier version of the analysis.
Studies labeled exact-split use it and have not been recomputed. Configuration (c) is a deterministic
LightGBM variant with purity-based subtraction, and (d) adds the negative-weight treatment with a
100-tree, 8-leaf refinement. Configuration (d) is the lower-capacity variant of Fig. S1. Their history
is in Ref. [1], Sec. 3.2.
The two reported configurations were chosen as follows. Configuration (e) was selected before the
joint-test design was fixed (Ref. [1], Sec. 11). The 2D central value and its covariance were rebuilt
with configuration (f) after a two-dimensional closure test with backgrounds found the purity bias
(Ref. [1], App. C.6). The closure test determined the change before the distance between the rebuilt
and published results was computed. The rebuilt result was adopted after that distance was known.
Simulation closure studies determined the refinement capacity shared by the two estimators
and the five-observable estimator’s five iterations (below). The two-observable estimator uses five
iterations following common practice. This choice was checked on data, against ten iterations with
purity-based subtraction and against the published result, without a search over the iteration count
(Ref. [1], Sec. 3.2).
The five-observable estimator keeps five iterations because 10, 15, or 20 iterations did not reduce
its model dependence, measured as the largest residual proxy over the truth changes used in its
development. In the noise-free study, this measure on the 27 coarser joint cells is 0.314 at five
iterations and at least 0.384 at 10, 15 or 20. A classifier of 400 trees and 31 leaves lowers it by 8 %,
short of the 20 % the rule required for a change (Ref. [1], Sec. 9.4).
LightGBM was chosen over the scikit-learn exact-split estimator at the same settings after both
had been compared with the published result, along with a third implementation, the scikit-learn
histogram estimator. With purity-based subtraction and with ndf the 205 reported bins, their χ2 /ndf
values were 3.66 for exact-split, 2.65 for LightGBM and 2.70 for the histogram estimator (a mean
over ten seeds), and the distance depends on the estimator (Ref. [1], Sec. 6.4).
The refined negative-weight background treatment was chosen on a closure test in simulation.
With the simulation’s own truth as the target, the 20-seed mean residual of the purity-based estimate
reached a maximum over the 205 bins of 5.9, in units of the data result’s per-bin statistical uncertainty.
The corresponding maximum for the refined estimate was 0.44. In the closure, the two estimates
differed by more than one such unit in 14 of the 205 bins. With purity-based subtraction, using the
published configuration’s ten iterations instead of five changes the LightGBM χ2 /ndf from 2.65 to
2.66 (Ref. [1], App. A.5). The purity-subtracted exact-split result differs from the central value by a
median of 1.03 % per bin (maximum 17.6 %; Ref. [1], Sec. 3.2).

S5

The reported five-observable covariance

The reported covariance is Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D , with Ctotal = Ce + Cmodel . The run-period block
CPL,5D is described in Sec. S5. Ce sums

• the statistical bootstrap of configuration (e), 50 replicas, each of which refits the whole unfolding
with its own rounding-level perturbation of the inputs,
• the flux universes of the Package to Predict the Flux (PPFX), weighted by the neutrino–electron
and inverse-muon-decay constraint,
• the interaction-model and detector bands, which reweight simulated events,
• the lateral bands, which shift reconstructed kinematics (Ref. [1], Sec. 4),
• the normalization,
• and the training-seed block.
With full-sample binning and deterministic training, the unfolds at the other seeds are bitwise equal
to the central one. The measured training-seed block is therefore zero in every cell. Table S7 gives
each group’s per-cell relative σ.
For Cmodel , the prior is reweighted in turn by the ratio of GENIE + MEC, NuWro or GiBUU
to GENIE CV, and the data are unfolded again. The NuWro and GiBUU ratios are built from
those generators’ carbon samples, without a correction to CH. Their shifts therefore include the
difference between the targets as well as between the generators, and the reported Cmodel keeps
them on carbon. Each generator’s shift is the relative change of the data result, and Cmodel is the
unscaled sum of their outer products, a fractional covariance. In cross-section units, the form in
which it is summed with the other blocks (as in Ctotal = Ce + Cmodel ), it is diag(x) Cmodel diag(x),
with x the cell-integrated cross sections. It represents dependence on the generator shapes, as an
implementation-specific shape sensitivity, and its GiBUU shift also carries the construction effects
below. It is not a calibrated bound, and its coverage at truths held out of its construction has not
been validated.
The GiBUU reweighting is applied only below p∥ = 10 GeV/c, and above it the prior is unchanged.
Between p∥ = 6 and 10 GeV/c, the GiBUU sample lacks the contribution from Eν > 20 GeV, which
is at least 4.9–5.3% of the cross section there in the GENIE CV and NuWro samples (which stop at
Eν = 50 GeV). The GiBUU shift contributes 0.890 of the trace of Cmodel , the NuWro shift 0.095 and
the GENIE + MEC shift 0.014. In every cell, 77–100% of the prior weight lies next to a discontinuity
larger than 0.1 in the reweighting ratio, created by the ratio’s statistical gate or by a cut. The shifts
are used unmodified.
For Cresp , each of four deposit categories in the simulation, protons, mesons, electromagnetic
deposits and other deposits, is scaled by its own one-standard-deviation factor, 3.5 % for protons,
5 % for mesons, 3 % for electromagnetic deposits and 20 % for other deposits, applied to that
category’s reconstructed energy. Reconstructed Eavail , q3 and W move with that scale, while the
muon kinematics and the data do not. Each category’s contribution is the outer product of the
half-difference of the two unfolded shifts of configuration (e)’s data result, and Cresp is their sum. The
per-cell relative standard deviations of the four contributions are the last four columns of Table S7.
Cresp does not cover neutron or muon deposits, the effects of particle species, energy dependence
and resolution beyond a scale, the null distributions of the joint tests, the configuration-(a) products
or the two-, three- and four-dimensional results. The median per-cell relative σ of Cresp is 2.14%, and
adding Cresp to Ctotal raises the per-cell σ by a factor of 1.011–1.220 (median 1.048). The cross-talk
deposit is varied by the same two-endpoint construction. That term is reported beside Cresp and is
not summed into it, because the adopted block keeps the deposit as a separate variant. Its median
per-cell relative σ is 0.36%. Of the trace of (Ctotal + Cresp )/f f T , Ctotal contributes 0.9262 and the
four deposit categories 0.0168, 0.0436, 0.0071 and 0.0062.

Every χ2 in this section uses the inverse of the full covariance of the cells compared, 16 × 16
or 8 × 8 for GiBUU’s cells, obtained by Cholesky decomposition without truncation. The inverted
dimension p equals the number of cells and is fixed in advance. The shape-only χ2 inverts the same
matrix with 100 f f T added and has one degree of freedom fewer.
The covariance blocks have different constructions. Two are sample covariances of an ensemble:
the statistical block, from 50 bootstrap replicas with the unbiased 1/(N − 1) normalization, and the
flux block, from 100 PPFX universes with constraint weights, normalized by the sum of the weights
(the weighted analogue of 1/N , with an effective number of universes of 19). The 2p2h band has

three universes. The other bands and the lateral shifts are two-point shifts, the normalization is
one outer product, Cmodel sums three chosen generator shifts, Cresp four ±1σ shifts, and CPL,5D is
built from three exposure-weighted periods. The training-seed block is zero. Each comparison with
a prediction adds Vg , its diagonal sum of squared event weights, and each fake-data comparison the
variance of the mean over its pseudo-experiments.
No finite-ensemble correction is applied to any block. The standard correction is derived for a
single unweighted sample covariance, and none of these sums has that form. The flux bootstrap
below measures the scatter of the χ2 due to estimation of the flux block, without correcting it.
Table S9 summarizes each block’s construction.
By the Sherman–Morrison identity, the shape-only statistic is χ2 − (f ⊤ C −1 ∆)2 /(λ−1 + f ⊤ C −1 f )
with λ = 100, where ∆ is the difference compared. As λ → ∞ it is the χ2 with the normalization
along f profiled out. At λ = 100 it differs from that limit only through the term λ−1 beside f ⊤ C −1 f .
Under the reported covariance f ⊤ C −1 f is 584 on the sixteen cells and 439 on GiBUU’s eight. The
term λ−1 is therefore below 3 × 10−5 of it.

The block was chosen after the period differences had been seen. Before it was added, an audit
examined (i) its overlap with the existing blocks, especially the flux and detector-response ones, (ii)
its noise treatment, with bootstrap replicas of each period, (iii) why its cell pattern differs from the
projection of the two-dimensional period offsets, although their sizes agree, and (iv) the stability of
its rank-two construction. Before any of its numbers were read, the audit stated that the block would
not be added if one existing component, within two standard deviations of its own size, explained at
least half of both period-group patterns, if the replica spread of its median per-cell width exceeded
half that width, or if less than half of a replica block’s variance lay in the observed block’s plane.
None of those results occurred. The addition therefore followed the data.
Passing the audit establishes neither the block’s size nor the absence of overlap: descriptively,
one detector-response pair, a pion-interaction band with the meson response shift, reproduces one
period’s offset pattern within noise and part of the other two. Built from the observed differences,
the block contains them by construction, and its size and coverage have not been independently
validated. The per-cell uncertainties, the fake-data tests and the generator comparison are given
here with and without it.

S6

The two-dimensional covariance

The nominal covariance is the block sum C = Csyst + Cstat + CML + CPL , which assumes that the
blocks are independent and that the unfolding responds linearly to simultaneous perturbations.
Under these assumptions the per-band covariances add. The unified throw tests linearity directly
(Ref. [1], App. A.2.4 and Sec. 9.6) by perturbing all reweightable bands and the flux universes
simultaneously in each event and re-unfolding jointly. The procedure interpolates asymmetrically
between actual −1σ and +1σ event-weight endpoints, fixes the estimator seed for every throw,
and centers the joint covariance on the throw mean. That mean’s displacement from CV is stored
separately as a per-bin vector and its norm. Per-band endpoint displacements are not stored.
The matched block-sum comparator re-unfolds both endpoints of each band using MAT’s universemean centering and biased 1/N normalization. A single-throw superposition probe is insufficient

to test nonlinear response. No scalar run-to-run jitter floor is subtracted. The configuration-(a)
5D result uses the strict joint ensemble and matched-endpoint comparator. No two-dimensional
comparison of a joint throw with the block sum is reported, and the stored mean-shift norm is not
quoted.
The run-period block CPL assigns an uncertainty for the observed inconsistency between run
periods, which exceeds the other uncertainties. Its cause is unknown. The block therefore does not
represent a measured systematic uncertainty from an identified source. After unfolding, the double
ratio (data over simulated truth) of the data-taking periods (playlists) 1G–1M to the earliest periods,
1A–1F, has a p∥ step of (+6.89 ± 0.46)% at 8 GeV/c. Diluted into the combined result, the raw shift
of those playlists is small in each bin: every marginal z is below 0.7. Its p∥ χ2 against the earlier 2D
budget is nevertheless 34.4, where noise would give 2.0. These data cannot establish which period, if
any, is correct.
The block is built from separate seed-42 unfoldings of three run periods, the playlist groups
1A–1F, 1G–1M and 1N–1P, each unfolded through its own simulated detector response. The block
uses each period’s unfolded result over that period’s own simulated truth. Each period is compared
with the exposure-weighted mean of the three. No reference period is therefore needed. The spread,
applied to the central value, gives the block. It is added after the other blocks and has rank two.
With only three period groups, its size is poorly determined. Its per-bin median fractional standard
deviation is 2.09 % (84th percentile 3.34 %, maximum 6.48 %). Its fractional standard deviation is
1.17 % to 3.36 % on the p∥ projections and 1.97 % on the integrated cross section. Added to the
other blocks, it raises the per-bin σ by a median of 5.8 % (Ref. [1], Table 4). Taking 1A–1F as the
reference would give a smaller block (per-bin median 1.38 %, integral 1.09 %).
The block’s interpretation is limited by the small number of period groups, by possible shrinkage
from the regularization, and by the statistical and training noise it retains. The block contains the
observed differences among periods by construction. It therefore carries no information about its
coverage, that is, about whether its size is right. For the same reason, the double-ratio χ2 against
any total that contains the block is bounded whatever the data and therefore does not test the block.
The raw 1G–1M p∥ χ2 is 34.8 against the other blocks alone and 2.59 with the block added. These
values indicate sensitivity only.
In the eigenbasis of the published covariance, the largest standardized component is 4.8 and the
next nine 3.4–3.8, and the ten smallest-eigenvalue modes carry 9.6 % of the χ2 . Of the six leading
modes, three are poorly determined directions (λ/λmax ≲ 4 × 10−9 ) in the highest-p∥ bins, and three
are moderate-λ modes (4 × 10−6 to 6 × 10−5 ) below them (Fig. S2; Ref. [1], Sec. 6.4).
On the 15 bins that contribute most to the published-covariance χ2 of Fig. S2, together 51 % of
it, the background template is 3.08 % of the selected events, against 2.91 % over all 205 bins. Over
the 205 bins, its fraction has a rank correlation of −0.09 with the contribution.
The published flux uncertainty (median 4.01 %) is larger than ours (3.71 %), for a reason that
cannot be determined from the released inputs.
The statistical block, with a median statistical uncertainty of 0.676 %, is a bootstrap of 300
replicas drawn at the central value’s training seed, in which the background templates are also
resampled.

S7

Comparisons with binned iterative Bayesian unfolding

The statistic, null and interpretation of each of the paper’s three comparisons with binned iterative
Bayesian unfolding (IBU) were specified before the comparisons were run. IBU is a cross-check in all
three: it enters neither the reported result nor its covariance.
The sixteen-cell comparison. The IBU uses D’Agostini’s method, with the background template
included as a known additive term in reconstructed space. It uses the same data, simulation,
background template and weight-only systematic universes as the five-observable estimator. Its
reconstructed bins are the fine five-observable cells inside the reconstruction window, its truth grid
is the coarse grid whose unions are the cells R00–R15, its prior is the simulation’s own truth, and
it runs for five iterations (iterations 1–4, 10 and 20 are reported but do not enter any decision).
Each of its 50 bootstrap replicas and each systematic universe is paired with the five-observable
estimator’s product made from the same draw or the same weights.

C∆,norm : the sample covariance of the paired replica differences, the reported covariance’s estimators
applied to the paired universe differences, and the 1.4 % normalization applied to ∆. The model,

The two-dimensional comparisons. The second comparison runs a D’Agostini IBU (RooUnfold’s
implementation) on the two-dimensional result’s own inputs and grid, at the published analysis’s
ten iterations, with five iterations reported beside it. The third runs the five-observable estimator on
the same inputs: the two-dimensional pipeline with LightGBM’s four determinism settings added to
every learner it builds, refinement included, and nothing else changed.
In both, each replica of the difference δ between the two results is formed on the same bootstrap
draws, Cδ is the replicas’ sample covariance, and the statistic is Tδ = δ ⊤ Cδ+ δ, with Cδ+ the pseudoinverse truncated to the ⌊N/4⌋ leading eigenmodes, because N replicas cannot support all bins.
The null is exact agreement. Its distribution is estimated empirically by leaving out each of the N
replicas in turn and computing its T about the mean of the others, with Cδ+ from the others at the
same rank. The thresholds are that null’s 95th percentile and its maximum. A reading of “differ”
(above the maximum) therefore means that a difference is detectable at this noise level, without
implying that it is large.
For the IBU comparison the descriptive ratio κ is the IBU result’s χ2 /ndf against the published
result under the published covariance divided by that of the two-dimensional result, computed on
each paired replica as well as on the data. The interval is the central 90 % range of the same ratio
evaluated on each of the 300 paired bootstrap replicas: on replica b, κb = DIBU,b /D2D,b , where both
unfoldings use the same resampled data and each distance D is the χ2 /ndf against the published
result under the published covariance. It describes the spread of the ratio over resampled datasets
and was not constructed as a confidence interval for the ratio on the data. The reading specified in
advance uses it only to decide whether the ratio lies within one class. Why the ratio on the data lies
below this range was not tested, and only the range’s end points were retained. The paper does not
use this classification.
In that comparison, 121 of the 144 residuals stable under the truth resolution lie in quantities
that the binned response constrains weakly at the analysis exposure, with Cramér–Rao widths above
10 % (median 31 %), and the tree approximation dominates 15, of which 2 lie in identified quantities.
This holds for a binned, signal-only, noise-free comparison at the simulation’s detector response.

The noise-free comparison on the joint cells. In a noise-free comparison in simulation on the
109 joint cells, an exact binned iterative unfolding of the same truth changes leaves about the same
median residual at five iterations as the boosted trees do. Its medians are 8.3 %, 5.4 %, 4.9 % and
5.3 % for the GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail change, a NuWro Eavail shape, a NuWro (pT , p∥ , Eavail ) shape
and a q3 stress test, against 9.5 %, 5.4 %, 5.6 % and 4.3 % for the boosted trees.
Longer exact iteration removes much of the residual of the GiBUU-to-GENIE change (its median
falls to 3.3 % at 200 iterations) and part of the NuWro Eavail one, but not that of the other two
(4.4 % and 6.6 % at 200), and the long runs do not converge. The residual is therefore consistent
with finite-iteration regularization for the GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail change, but not for every change
tested, and no single cause is established (Ref. [1], Sec. 9.3). This binned comparison uses its own
truth grid and its own truth changes. It therefore bounds neither the event-level problem nor the
study below.

The fake-data bias tests

The fake-data tests compare the estimator’s bias at truths other than the simulation with the
reported covariance. Pseudo-data are generated at 12 truths and unfolded with configuration (e).
The mean unfolded result is compared with each truth on the 16 cells, through χ2 with 16 degrees
of freedom and the per-cell pull z. The truths are
• the simulation’s prior,
• the five hybrid truths of the generator tests (Ref. [1], Sec. 11), each a prediction on the fine
grid,
• the historical shapes W1, W2 and W3 of Ref. [1], Sec. 9.4,
• a GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail shape,
• a noise-free closure at the NuWro shape,
• and a synthetic tilt of the q3 distribution at fixed (Eavail , W ).
A truth from a Cmodel generator is compared with the covariance without that generator’s shift. A
truth is flagged when p < 0.01 or when any cell has |z| > 2. These criteria were fixed before any of
the tests was graded against them. None of these truths is held out, and the detector response is
not probed. Ref. [1], Table 19 gives each truth under the reported covariance, without CPL,5D , and
under Ctotal alone. The bias of each truth is the same in all three, and only the covariance differs.
The truths are evaluated in three separate groups: (i) the 8 truths from the Cmodel generators,
(ii) the other truths (the GENIE CV hybrid and the q3 tilt), and (iii) the prior truths (the simulation

prior and the Tune v1 hybrid). Under the reported covariance, the Cmodel -generator truths have 2 of

S8.4

The stopped first five-observable coverage test

A first coverage test of the five-observable covariance at the two tunes, specified in advance, gave
no coverage result: one of its preliminary checks, which compared pseudo-experiments that move
only the detector response at fixed truth with blocks built from the prior and the response together,
stopped it before any coverage pseudo-experiment ran, and a staged redesign stopped at its first
stage under stopping rules fixed in advance. The diagnostic study of that check gave the following
results. No set of three or fewer of the 38 drawn bands brings every cell inside the window. In R08,
the cell with the largest ratio, the interaction bands MaRES, MaCCQE and MvRES carry most of
the excess. Estimator noise is rejected as the explanation for every failing cell.
For the interaction group at the nominal truth, the pseudo-experiments reproduce the block of
the response alone moved in the simulation, within 0.986–1.015 in every cell, whereas the reported
construction’s full universe at the same truth gives a block that differs from it by a factor of median
2.72 (0.49–6.51). The full block is not that response block plus a cell-mean truth-reweight block,
whose sum misses it by about the response block’s own size. A like-for-like control, whose pseudo-data
carry each interaction universe’s full event weights, reproduces the full-universe interaction block
within 0.9–1.1 in all 16 cells at the nominal truth.
The corresponding tests of the flux and detector groups draw each band alone. In all three
drawn groups, the pseudo-experiments reproduce, within 10 % in σ in every resolved cell, the block
that the same weight factors give on the simulation side. For the flux band, the departure from the
window mostly reflects a comparison of different quantities: the pseudo-experiments’ factors and the
reported construction’s whole universe at the same truth. This accounts for 7 of its 9 failing cells,
and two remain unresolved.
For the detector group, in cells R03, R05 and R06 the dominant factor is the full universe
at the nominal truth against the reported blocks, made at the data. Full-universe pseudo-data
reproduce the reported construction’s block at the nominal truth for the flux, GEANT4 pion and
MINOS matching-efficiency bands. For the GEANT4 neutron band, the pseudo-experiments and
the simulation side give blocks more than 10 % apart in σ in cells R00, R02, R08, R09, R10 and
R11, and for the GEANT4 proton band in R01, R05, R07 and R08, for an unknown reason. The
blocks’ non-additivity remains unexplained. Their truth dependence, between the nominal truth
and the data used to construct the reported block, lies outside the window in 15 of the 16 cells.

Relation to the second test. The second test builds each drawn group’s pseudo-data as its
covariance block is built: the composed truth and reconstruction weights, the truth’s denominator
composed and clipped row by row, and the simulation resampled with each pseudo-experiment
(Table S15). The first test’s preliminary check instead compared pseudo-experiments that move only
the detector response at fixed truth with blocks built from the prior and the response together. For
the GEANT4 neutron and proton bands, the two constructions match in each of these operations.
The truth weights change the σ ratio between the two simulation-side blocks by at most 2 × 10−7 .
The flux and denominator are unchanged for these bands. Simulation resampling is not part of
either compared object, and clipping is not exercised.
The pseudo-data and the simulation side carry the same factor on the same reconstructed rows
inside the window, for every detector universe. What separates the two sides lies inside the estimator
and is unclassified, and the second test’s primary reading excludes the two bands.

S10

The usage-recommendation rule

The paper grades two per-cell bias-screening rules. This section defines the first, the usagerecommendation rule. The second, the bias-half rule, is graded in the paper’s section on the
fake-data tests of the sixteen-cell result and in Table S12 (test 6).
The usage-recommendation rule applies per cell the thresholds of the usage recommendations
of Ref. [14]: (i) at least 5000 effective simulated events, here those of the simulation’s weights, a
deviation from that definition, (ii) a data statistical uncertainty below 15 %, and (iii) |z| ≤ 2 at each
of the 12 graded truths under the reported covariance.
Conditions (i) and (ii) are met in every cell (the smallest effective number is 331 614, in R07,
and the largest statistical uncertainty 0.368 %, in R05). The effective number of the final weights
(Sec. S4.4) is at least 325 119 in every cell (the smallest, in R07). Condition (i) therefore also holds
under the definition of Ref. [14]. The rule therefore rests on condition (iii), and its recommended
test of correlation assumptions on pseudo-data is not met here. Fourteen cells pass it; R02 and R06
fail, each from the GiBUU hybrid truth alone, at z = +4.19 and +4.53.
A further rule of our own, the prior-spread rule, compares the spread of each cell’s fake-data bias
with the spread of the generator predictions, and is defined in Ref. [1], Sec. 10.5. We withhold its
results, because it uses NuWro and GiBUU predictions corrected from carbon to CH with free-proton
samples whose validation problems are unresolved (Sec. S9.1). The usage-recommendation rule tests
each graded truth’s closure pull separately. It validates no interval, since the fake-data tests grade
