We test OmniFold on MINERvA open data, unfolding five observables simultaneously, without
binning. As a descriptive benchmark, a two-observable unfolding reproduces the published muonkinematic cross-section integral within about 1%, but differs from the published shape more than a
binned unfolding of the same inputs does. Under controlled truth changes, the result retains part of its
prior, and some cells move opposite to them. Fake-data studies at a GiBUU-like truth and a synthetic
momentum-transfer tilt find residual bias beyond uncertainty-based criteria fixed before grading.
On noise-free pseudo-data at both truths, binned iterative unfolding overshoots the truth along
the prior-to-truth direction. The five-observable estimator’s results are sensitive to rounding-level
changes of its inputs, beyond a requirement fixed in advance. The procedure’s two-dimensional
covariance, rebuilt at each of two held-out generator tunes, under-covers at both. The reported
five-observable intervals have no validated coverage. [PENDING: the second five-observable
coverage test] On data, the sixteen reported cells depend on the unfolding algorithm: binned
iterative unfolding differs from them. We therefore present the five-observable output on data only
as an illustration.
1.

INTRODUCTION

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their
interpretation requires corrections for detector response
and an assessment of the bias and uncertainty of the
unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on
a binned migration matrix [1] is widely used, but its
response tensor grows rapidly as observables are added.
Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach
three dimensions [2–5]. OmniFold learns event weights
in an unbinned feature space through classifier-based
density-ratio estimation [6, 7]. This allows simultaneous
higher-dimensional unfolding without a separate binned
response tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding
has been applied to collider data, for example to eight
observables in deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [8] and
to twenty-four Z+jets observables at ATLAS [9] (see
ref. [10] for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been
demonstrated on a public simulated T2K near-detector
sample [11].
This paper is a benchmark and diagnostic study of
a simultaneous unbinned unfolding in five observables
on neutrino data. We apply OmniFold to MINERvA
medium-energy νµ data from the open-data release [12],
in the transverse and longitudinal muon momenta pT
and p∥ , the available hadronic energy Eavail , the threemomentum transfer q3 , and the hadronic invariant mass
W . Because reconstructed W is fixed by the reconstructed

(pT , p∥ , q3 ), the five observables are unfolded simultaneously but are not independent. The event weights allow
the same unfolded sample to be histogrammed in projections or cells chosen afterwards. An uncertainty for a new
choice must be constructed and checked for that choice,
and a practical gain over binned unfolding remains to be
shown.
The findings apply to one OmniFold implementation on
one data set, for the observables, configurations and deformations tested. The sixteen-cell cross section that the
estimator gives on the data illustrates the method and is
not a measurement. The supplementary material gives it
with its covariance and descriptive comparisons with generator predictions (supplementary material, section S9).
We report three findings and the tests supporting them,
including tests that failed (table 4). First, a benchmark
against the published double-differential cross section
d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) from the same data [13] shows agreement
in normalization and a correlated shape difference that
accompanies the unfolding method on fixed inputs and
whose mechanism has not been identified (section 4).
Second, controlled changes of the simulated truth show
that the five-observable result retains part of its prior on
the sixteen reporting cells, while individual (Eavail , W )
cells can move opposite to a generator-sized change or
overshoot it (section 5). Third, the estimator is reproducible but numerically unstable, and the calibration of
its uncertainties has not been validated: these are distinct
properties (section 6).
Section 2 describes the data and observables, and section 3 describes the unfolding, covariances and test design.
Sections 4–6 report the three findings, and section 7 discusses their implications. The supplementary material
provides additional definitions, test designs and results.

The companion analysis note [14] documents the analysis’s development and provides further technical detail in
the sections cited here.

3.

UNFOLDING METHOD AND TEST DESIGN
3.1.

Unfolding

The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees [18, 19] of
nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold
steps, and negative-weight background subtraction with
a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves. The two
estimators differ in the observables their classifiers see,
two or five, and in LightGBM’s training mode, seeded or
deterministic (supplementary material, section S4). Results from the two estimators are not combined: the twodimensional benchmark uses one, and the five-observable
results use the other (table 2).
The two-observable estimator, LightGBM [19] at a fixed
seed, gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance (section 4.1). We quote its result as the central
value because its covariance uses the same estimator: its
systematic-universe ensemble is built around that central
value. The five-observable results use the five-observable
estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with the same background treatment and refinement capacity. The note’s
calibrated joint tests of generator predictions also use
it (supplementary material, section S3). Its seeds are
fixed, and its training is reproducible. The five-observable
estimator is the baseline this study characterizes (configuration (e) of section S4): the stability probe and the
ensemble average of section 6.1 were tested against it, and
neither replaced it.
Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps.
A reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from
simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight.
The pairing of reconstructed and true events transfers
that weight to truth, including unmatched events, and a
truth-level classifier propagates a smooth reweighting to
the next iteration. Because events that fail reconstruction
enter as truth events without a reconstructed partner,
no separate efficiency correction is applied. Their step-1
weight comes from a regressor of that weight on the truth
coordinates, fitted with equal event weights to the events
that have a partner: it estimates the mean weight at
fixed truth over simulated events, not over their physical

weights. These events carry 15.7 % to 68.1 % of a cell’s
final weight (section S4). The cross section in a bin of
the observables x1 , . . . , xn is

Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction, every data event in reconstructed
bin b carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1 − Bb /Db ),
where Db and Bb are the data and predicted background
counts in that bin. The classifier’s target density is then
D(x) wpur (b). The unbinned negative-weight treatment
instead adds simulated background events with negative
weight to the data. The target is then D(x) − B(x)
pointwise. A classifier refinement [20], clipped at zero,
converts the signed sample to non-negative weights before
the first iteration. The two targets share bin integrals
wherever Bb < Db but differ inside a bin wherever the
background fraction varies within it (supplementary material, section S4). With the refinement classifier, the
negative-weight treatment removes most of the bias of
purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highestW cells (ref. [14], section 9.2).
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize
the unfolding. No learner setting was tuned on data,
including capacity, learning rate, refinement capacity and
iteration count. The five-observable estimator uses five
iterations because 10, 15, or 20 iterations did not reduce
its model dependence for the truth changes used in its
development (supplementary material, section S4). Each
classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it
then sets, without a held-out or cross-fitted split or early
stopping. The two classes retain their physical weights
(supplementary material, section S4). Two choices of
method for the two-dimensional result are described with
its benchmark (section 4.1).
3.2.

The reported covariances

Two covariances are reported: the sixteen-cell result’s,
described first, and the two-dimensional result’s four-block
sum, described after it. The sixteen-cell covariance is
Crep = Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D ,

Ctotal = Ce + Cmodel .
(3.2)
In eq. (3.2), Ce is the sum of the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization uncertainties of

the five-observable estimator and its training-seed block,
and Cmodel is the model term. The lateral uncertainties
shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle
rather than reweighting events (supplementary material,
section S9). Cresp is a recoil-response term and CPL,5D a
run-period term, each described below.
Each bootstrap replica of the statistical block refits the
whole unfolding with its own rounding-level perturbation
of the inputs. The block therefore includes the estimator’s sensitivity to such perturbations (section 6.1). The
perturbation moves each input by at most half a float32
unit in the last place and holds values on an edge of
the analysis grid (section 3.3) fixed. The block therefore
estimates the spread of a randomized procedure, data
resampling with a fresh rounding perturbation in each
replica. For the deterministic reported output, it adds a
numerical-variability term to the sampling variance. If the
two variances add, the perturbation makes up a median
26 % (15–67 %) of each sixteen-cell bootstrap variance.
Without it, the reported uncertainty of any cell would be
at most 0.05 % smaller. These are the diagonal elements
only.
In the weight universes, every simulated event’s truth
and reconstructed weights, and the truth denominator,
take the universe’s values. The events without a reconstructed partner therefore carry their universe weights
too (supplementary material, section S5).
The model term Cmodel is constructed from the change
in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted to
GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn, with GENIE
CV as the common reference. It measures the result’s
unscaled dependence on the generator shape and is not
calibrated as a bound. It is an implementation-specific
shape sensitivity: its GiBUU shift, which carries 0.890 of
its trace, also contains the target difference of a carbon
sample, that sample’s energy truncation, a cut in p∥ and
the steps of the ratio’s statistical gate (supplementary
material, section S5). The response term Cresp is the
response of the result to a one-standard-deviation scale
of the reconstructed recoil energy in the simulation. The
scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and
other deposits. Cresp does not cover neutron or muon
deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution
effects beyond a scale.
The unfolded results of the three run-period groups,
each relative to its own simulation, differ by more than
their bootstrap scatter. The cause is unknown, and these
data cannot establish which period is correct. The runperiod block CPL,5D includes this inconsistency in the
reported covariance. It was added after the differences
were observed and is constructed from the double ratios
among the groups: each group’s unfolded result divided by
its own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the reference. It therefore contains the differences by construction.
It has rank 2 and is not corrected for the bootstrap noise
it contains. It is an empirical sensitivity allowance, not an
independently established uncertainty. With three groups
its size is poorly determined. supplementary material,

section S5 gives an illustrative interval on its size under a
stated model, and that interval describes no uncertainty
in the block’s orientation.
The median per-cell relative uncertainty is 7.86%, 7.29%
without CPL,5D , and 6.74% without CPL,5D or Cresp . The
fake-data tests are reported with and without this block
(section 5.2 and ref. [14], section 10.4).
The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four
blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central
value’s estimator: systematic universes (185 universes in
43 bands, plus the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap replicas), run period (three period groups), and
training seed (five seed settings, too few to determine its
size well). The resulting relative uncertainty has a median
of 6.41 % per bin and is 4.76 % on the integrated cross section, compared with 6.85 % and 4.61 % for the publication.
The covariance omits the approximation in the systematicuniverse unfoldings for events that fail reconstruction, the
learner dependence of section 4.1, and the dependence on
the regularization (supplementary material, section S6).
The dependence on the prior’s generator shape is reported separately as a covariance Cmodel , constructed from
the change in the result when the prior is reweighted to
each generator’s (pT , p∥ ) shape. Its median is 3.71 % per
bin. It measures model dependence and is not calibrated
as a bound on the unfolding bias.
For every χ2 of the five-observable result, we invert the
full covariance of the cells compared, 16 × 16, or 8 × 8 for
GiBUU’s eight cells with p∥ < 4.5 GeV/c, by Cholesky
decomposition and without truncation. The number of
degrees of freedom of each χ2 is then the number of
cells. Each block is defined in supplementary material,
section S5.

3.3.

Test design

A closure test measures how well the unfolding recovers a specified deformation of the known simulated truth
while holding the prior (the initial truth distribution)
fixed. Coverage is the frequency with which intervals
contain their target over repeated experiments. It tests
the estimator and the interval construction together. The
fake-data tests grade bias alone, since at most the event
counts fluctuate in them, with the systematic parameters
fixed. Coverage is graded only by the coverage tests of section 6.2. The closure, deformation, and coverage studies
reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as
pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed and true
distributions together and keeps the detector response at
fixed truth. The unfolding prior remains Tune v1 throughout. Every study uses the analysis simulation’s detector
response.
The fine grid is the analysis binning: the published
14 × 16 muon-momentum bins, of which the publication
reports 205, crossed with 7 Eavail , 7 q3 , and 6 W bins.

the fine edges nearest the simulated tertiles. The same

The bias is graded on Poisson pseudo-experiments,
whose mean is compared with the truth, or on noisefree pseudo-data, in which every Poisson draw is replaced
by its mean. The comparison of the ensemble average
with the baseline (section 6.1) uses noise-free pseudo-data
at all 17 distinct truths, graded and descriptive, since the
Tune v1 hybrid equals the prior.
The grading rules and their timing are stated with the
individual tests; comparisons without a grading rule are
descriptive. Criteria, thresholds and readings were fixed

before the results they grade were read, unless a test says
otherwise. A truth is flagged when the bias’s χ2 over the
16 cells has a reference tail area p < 0.01 under χ216 , or
when a cell has |z| > 2, where z is the cell’s bias divided
by its uncertainty under the same covariance. Because
the bias is deterministic, p is a reference score and carries
no calibrated probability.
An estimator meets the stability requirement when
rounding-level changes of its inputs move its results by
at most 0.3 statistical standard deviations in the median
and 1.0 at maximum. A coverage test grades the fraction
of pseudo-experiments whose result lies within its interval,
per cell and jointly, against a threshold set from the test’s
own null distribution. The two-dimensional benchmark
(section 4) concerns the two-observable estimator. The
truth-recovery and stability findings (sections 5 and 6.1)
concern the five-observable estimator, whose rounding
sensitivity is carried in its statistical uncertainty (section 6.1).

4.1.

Normalization, per-bin agreement and the
covariance-weighted distance

We benchmark the two-dimensional unfolding against
a measurement from the same data. The comparison
is descriptive and is not a blind validation. No learner
setting was tuned on data (section 3.1), but two choices
of method for the two-dimensional result, the learner
implementation and the refined background treatment,
were made with this comparison in view. The learner was
chosen after three had been compared with the published
result. The background treatment, chosen from a closure
test in simulation, was adopted only after that comparison
was known (supplementary material, section S4). The
comparison is therefore conditional on both choices.
The two-dimensional result’s integrated cross section
is 3.069 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon, compared with 3.039 ×
10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published analysis, a ratio of
1.010: a normalization benchmark, which leaves open
whether the unfolding reproduces the published analysis
in shape. The standardized bin differences formed with
the published uncertainties have an RMS of 0.535 (figure 1). Because the two bands in figure 1 are correlated,
their overlap is not a compatibility test. The two results
share the data and the flux, detector and generator systematics, and their cross-covariance is unavailable, so the
significance of their difference cannot be calibrated.

4.3.

Run periods

In the two-dimensional result, data from different run
periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties (figure 2). Relative to its own simulation, the 1G–
1M group’s p∥ distribution differs from that of 1A–1F by a
step near 8 GeV/c, of unknown cause. The step is already
in the reconstructed data, at +5.99 %. In a per-period
unfolding with the refined background of the reported
configuration, the double ratio of each period’s result to
its own simulated truth gives +(6.89 ± 0.46) %, where ± is
the bootstrap noise from ten paired draws. The step therefore survives unfolding at about its reconstruction-level
size. The 1N–1P group shows a smaller step, +1.26 %
and +(1.85 ± 0.64) %. The effect of the 1G–1M step on
the distance to the published result is given with that
distance (section 4.1).

4.4.

The five-observable quadrant sums

When summed over each of the four (pT , p∥ ) quadrants,
the sixteen-cell result agrees with the two-dimensional
result: the sums differ by −1.5 to +0.7 per cent, and the
four differences, using the covariance between the two
results evaluated from their shared systematic variations,
give χ2 ≤ 1.25 for four degrees of freedom (p ≥ 0.87),
with no quadrant beyond one standard deviation. The
statistical correlation, and that of four systematic terms
that could not be evaluated directly, are bounded within a
one-parameter family of valid covariances, with each such
cross term taken as a common fraction, from zero to one, of
its fully correlated value. The agreement holds throughout
this family. The five-observable result therefore agrees
with the two-dimensional normalization in each muonkinematic quadrant. The benchmark does not test its
distribution in the three hadronic observables.

Reproducibility and numerical stability

Reproducibility and rounding stability are distinct properties. The five-observable estimator is reproducible under
a change of training seed, in the sense stated below, but
it is not stable under rounding-level changes of its inputs.
The studies of this instability use different quantities and
ensembles, listed in table 7. On the sixteen reported cells,
the spread over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the
data is a median 0.51 of each cell’s statistical uncertainty
(0.39–0.82).
The five-observable estimator is unstable under floatingpoint rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, the reported quantities scatter
by a median of 0.23 % per joint cell, against a median
data-bootstrap statistical uncertainty of 0.37 % over 50
replicas, as in the reported block, and each bootstrap
replica of the data shows a scatter of the same size. Over
153 quantities (the joint cells, the (Eavail , W ) cells and
two totals), rounding-level changes of its inputs move its
results by a median of 0.8 and up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and those of the lower-capacity variant
by up to 2.5. The estimator therefore fails a data-stability
requirement of at most 0.3 (median) and 1.0 (maximum)
statistical standard deviations (figure 5).
The five-observable covariance has its own training-seed
block, separate from the two-dimensional covariance’s
block of five seed settings. The training-seed block of

6.2.

Calibration of the uncertainties

Each coverage test in this section asks whether a covariance, rebuilt at a held-out truth by the reported procedure,
covers the errors of pseudo-experiments drawn about that
truth. The target is the nominal content of each interval:
per cell, the fraction of errors ec with |ec | ≤ σc , which is
0.6827 for a Gaussian error, and jointly the fraction with
χ2 below its null distribution’s quantile at that content.
Because the drawn uncertainties are two-point or discrete,
each test replaces the Gaussian content with one matched
to its draws, and flags under-coverage when a count of
the pseudo-experiments falls to a binomial threshold of
that content. Over-coverage is reported and is not a fail-

b. The second five-observable test. A second test carries the design of the two-dimensional test to the sixteen
cells. At each of the same two tunes it draws 300 pseudoexperiments in each variant, with the same random draws
in both: each drawn uncertainty group’s pseudo-data are
built as its covariance block is built, the lateral uncertainties are carried by a surrogate, and the model, response
and run-period terms are not drawn. The covariance is
rebuilt once per tune by the reported procedure. The
graded covariance includes the model term, whose role is
to cover each tune’s departure from the simulation, and

leaves out the response and run-period terms. The test
has two variants, correlated by construction: a primary
one without the GEANT4 neutron and proton bands,
and one with them. The primary variant leaves out the
two bands whose pseudo-experiment and simulation-side
blocks disagreed in the first test’s checks, from its pseudodata and from its covariance alike. The two constructions
match in each operation checked, and what separates
them lies inside the estimator and is unclassified (supplementary material, section S8.4). Excluding them defines
a restricted test, which cannot validate their treatment.
A run continued or repaired after a check was seen is
descriptive.
The test uses the reported estimator. The tunes are
held out of the analysis but not independent of it: they
share a quasielastic model, and G18_10a_02_11b shares
its 2p2h model family with one of the generators of Cmodel .
The construction of the pseudo-data, and the checks made
on it before the main run, are given in supplementary
material, section S8.
Each tune and each variant is graded separately, per
cell and jointly, against thresholds set from the test’s null
distribution. A pass means that no under-coverage is
detected at these thresholds, whose resolution is about
0.10 in coverage per cell and 0.08 jointly. In one variant at
one tune, the cell tests raise a false flag with probability
at most 0.025, and so does the joint test. Because the
response and run-period terms are left out of the graded
covariance, a cell that passes at a tune also passes under
the procedure’s full covariance there, and the joint reading,
the fraction with χ2 below a cut fixed in advance, carries
over only if both matrices have 16 degrees of freedom.

7.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

This implementation performs simultaneous unbinned
unfolding in five observables, without a separate binned
response tensor for each projection. The five-observable
result agrees with the two-dimensional result in each
muon-kinematic quadrant. The reported cells depend
on the unfolding algorithm. [PENDING: the second
five-observable coverage test] Until the coverage, the
prior dependence, the numerical stability, and the runperiod effect are resolved, the sixteen-cell output is an
illustration of the method. The output’s limitations are

listed in section 7.1.
The studies of sections 3–6 and of the companion note
give five checks to make before the intervals of an unbinned
unfolding are interpreted. The checks are general. The
findings stated with them apply to this implementation.
1. Closure should include the backgrounds (section 5.1).
2. Truth changes of generator size should be recovered,
and what is not recovered measured, as the retained
fraction of the prior. Here part of the prior is retained, and binned iterative unfolding overshoots
the truth along the prior-to-truth direction at the
two flagged truths (sections 5.1 and 5.3).
3. Coverage should be tested at changed truths, jointly
as well as per cell, and the joint statistic read in the
covariance’s eigenbasis. Here the two-dimensional
test flags 2 and 1 of the 205 cells while its joint test
fails at both tunes, its χ2 dominated by mean bias
in the poorly determined directions (section 6.2).
4. Robustness should be tested against rounding-level
input changes as well as training seeds. Here the result is bitwise independent of the seed but not stable
under rounding, which on the pseudo-experiment
studied acts through ties among equal float32 inputs
(section 6.1).
5. Run periods should be compared before and after unfolding. Here a step between period groups
is present before unfolding and survives it (section 4.3).
The benchmark shows agreement in normalization and
a shape difference in two dimensions, much reduced when
only the unfolding method is changed, whose mechanism
is unidentified. The benchmark does not validate the fiveobservable distribution. The bias-screening rules grade
the estimator’s bias under simulated truths. The rules
do not identify where the detector model is inadequate.
Whether the two-dimensional shape difference arises in
the hadronic observables is open. Testing it needs contrasts within fixed muon-kinematic regions, with their
joint uncertainty, and a check that plausible changes of
the unfolding and detector response cannot produce the
pattern. That test is a next step.

7.1.

Limitations

1. Estimator bias. Recovery within the reported uncertainty, as graded on the sixteen reporting cells, is
not achieved for every generator-sized truth change
tested. At the four changes of figure 3, the median
unfolded (Eavail , W ) cell moves by 56 % to 88 % of
the injected change and up to 12 of 39 supported
cells with an injected change of magnitude above
2 % move opposite to it. The unfolded result retains
part of its prior (section 5, table 4).
2. Four of the sixteen cells, R02, R06, R10 and R15,
have a diagonal fraction (section 2) of only 0.16–
0.25.
3. The step-1 weight of truth events without a reconstructed partner comes from a regression fitted with

equal event weights (section 3.1). Its effect on the
result was not tested.
4. Numerical behaviour. The estimator fails its datastability requirement under rounding-level changes
of its inputs. An ensemble-averaged variant that
meets it on the pseudo-data used to test stability
is worse at one truth and is not adopted. The
instability of the data result itself is therefore not
mitigated (section 6.1).
5. Uncertainty calibration. No reported interval has
validated coverage. The fake-data tests assess only
bias. The first coverage test of the five-observable
covariance did not run to a result. The second
test of section 6.2 grades the procedure’s covariance
at two truths. The procedure’s two-dimensional
covariance under-covers at both held-out tunes (section 6.2). Neither the coverage of Cmodel at truths
held out of its construction nor the size of either
run-period block is independently validated (supplementary material, section S5, and section S6).
The per-cell coverage readings cited are pointwise,
for each reported quantity separately. Simultaneous
coverage of all of them is not graded. The joint
readings grade the fraction of pseudo-experiments
whose χ2 lies in a region fixed in advance. The covariances are estimated from finite ensembles, and
their sampling precision is not quantified.
6. The systematic-universe unfoldings of the twodimensional covariance approximate the weighting
of events that fail reconstruction. The approximation is not corrected. Tested in two universes, its
effect is excluded only above twice the spread of an
earlier training-seed block (section 3.2 and ref. [14],
section 4.6).
7. Data and response. The data show a run-period
inconsistency, present before unfolding, of unknown
cause (section 4.3).
8. The simulated detector response is not varied in
the closure, deformation and fake-data tests (sections 3.3 and 5.2), beyond the descriptive closures
with a shifted recoil response of section 5.2. The
coverage tests vary it only through the detectorsystematic uncertainties they draw (section 6.2).
Because the closure, deformation, fake-data and
coverage tests all use simulated pseudo-data, none
tests the detector model against data.
