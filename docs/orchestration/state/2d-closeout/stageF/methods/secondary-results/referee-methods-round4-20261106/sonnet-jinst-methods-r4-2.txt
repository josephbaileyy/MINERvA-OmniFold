**Referee report for the Journal of Instrumentation**

**Manuscript:** Simultaneous five-observable OmniFold unfolding of MINERvA medium-energy open data: a methods and validation study

---

## 1. Summary of the paper

The authors apply the OmniFold method to the MINERvA medium-energy νμ open data. They unfold five observables at once: pT, p∥, Eavail, q3 and W. They use a LightGBM implementation, a negative-weight background treatment with a refinement classifier, and five iterations. The two-dimensional (pT, p∥) result is benchmarked against the published MINERvA cross section. The integral ratio is 1.010, but the shape differs, with χ²/ndf = 2.27 over 205 bins, and the authors do not identify why. A comparison with binned iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU) shows the methods differ, and it does not localise the shape difference.

The five-observable estimator is validated through a battery of tests. These are closure at the simulation's own truth, fake-data tests at 12 truths, input-rounding stability, a coverage test, and a run-period consistency check. Several fail or are stopped, and the authors report all of them. The tests that fail are the fake-data bias at two truths, the rounding-stability requirement, the run-period consistency, and the coverage test, which was stopped before any pseudo-experiment ran. The sixteen-cell data result is therefore presented only as "illustrative output", in an appendix, and not as a measurement.

## 2. Recommendation

**Major revision.**

The paper is unusually candid, and the full reporting of failed pre-declared tests is valuable. Reporting negative results of this kind is rare and welcome. However, as written it is not a coherent JINST methods paper. It is a status report on an unfinished validation programme that leans on a companion note ([14]) for most of the evidence. In that note I count at least 25 section or appendix citations. The paper's principal positive claims are a 1% integral agreement and the existence of a feasible five-observable workflow. The remaining findings are negative or unresolved: the shape difference is unexplained, the estimator is numerically unstable, the bias exceeds its own criteria, coverage is unvalidated, and the data are internally inconsistent across run periods.

None of these is, in itself, a reason to reject a methods paper. Together they leave the reader without a usable conclusion about what OmniFold does on neutrino data. Several of the failures also look like fixable analysis defects and not intrinsic method limitations. The most important is the rounding-level numerical instability. Another is the interaction-group coverage gate, which was constructed such that it compared unlike objects. The authors know how to resolve some of the open items and have not done so. The paper also needs restructuring: it is dense and hard to follow, and it mixes the findings with the machinery of the note. I would be glad to see a revised version.

## 3. Major issues

**1. The central deliverable is not a measurement, and the paper does not make clear what the reader should take from it.**
The abstract and Sec. V say the five-observable data result is "only an illustration". The conclusions say the output is "a demonstration of the method". Yet a method demonstration needs validated performance, and the paper itself shows that the five-observable result fails its pre-declared bias, stability and coverage criteria. The paper therefore ends up demonstrating that the method as implemented does not yet meet its own acceptance criteria. That is a legitimate finding, but the framing is inconsistent. Titles, abstract and Sec. I describe a "methods and validation study", while the conclusions describe a successful "simultaneous unbinned unfolding... whose normalization agrees with the two-dimensional result".

What the authors must do: state one clear thesis. The candidates are (a) a feasibility and negative-results study of high-dimensional OmniFold on real neutrino data, or (b) a validated estimator. The text supports only (a). The paper should also state what a reader planning a similar analysis should do differently.

**2. The estimator is numerically unstable, and this looks like a fixable implementation defect.**
Rounding-level perturbations of the inputs shift the results by a median of 0.8σ_stat and up to 2.6σ_stat. The pre-declared requirement was 0.3 and 1.0. The same estimator is bitwise independent of its training seed and row order. This combination points to a chaotic dependence in a deterministic pipeline. A plausible mechanism is tree-split ties, histogram binning in LightGBM, or threshold discretisation. The paper does not investigate the mechanism.

The consequences matter. The statistical bootstrap block absorbs this scatter "by construction", so the reported statistical uncertainty is partly numerical noise. At the same time, the training-seed block is set to zero. The claim that the seed uncertainty is zero is true only in a trivial sense. The paper's own result shows that the actual sensitivity to numerical noise is not zero. A reader should not conclude that the estimator is reproducible or stable.

What the authors must do: identify the mechanism. Useful tests are float64 versus float32 inputs, a different histogram bin count (`max_bin`), an exact-split learner, and a comparison of the split tables between perturbed runs. Alternatively, average over an ensemble of perturbations and treat the ensemble mean as the estimator. This would reduce the scatter by a known factor, and the ensemble spread would be an honest numerical uncertainty. One probe was run (cross-fitting and two tree regularisations). It was run only on simulated pseudo-data and reached no mechanism. It is not enough.

**3. The coverage test was stopped on a gate that the authors acknowledge was ill-posed. They should repair the gate and run the test.**
The gate compared the uncertainty reproduced through fixed-truth pseudo-experiments, which move only the response, with the reported blocks, which are built from the prior and the response. The authors state these "would be predicted to fail by construction". They then built a like-for-like control that passes at nominal truth in all 16 cells. This is the right resolution, but it is described as a diagnosis and not used as the repaired gate.

The paper also lists three things "still open". The flux and detector groups have no split. The non-additivity of the blocks is unexplained. The truth dependence lies outside the window in 15 of 16 cells. These are the substance of the coverage question, and they are left open.

Without validated coverage, the reported uncertainties (the paper's main output) have an unknown meaning. The 7.9% median uncertainty may be too small or too large. The paper concedes "no interval has validated coverage". Ref. [14] reports that earlier statistical intervals undercovered "as low as 0.06" for 68% intervals. This is a large failure of the earlier estimator, and nothing in the paper shows that the current one is better.

What the authors must do: run the coverage test with the like-for-like construction on at least the nominal truth and the two held-out tunes, for the statistical, flux, interaction and detector groups. Report coverage with binomial intervals. If they decline, state clearly in the abstract that the uncertainties are unvalidated, as they do now, and remove the claim of "total uncertainties" as a deliverable.

**4. The estimator retains substantial prior dependence, and this is a physics-level limitation.**
The fake-data tests flag the GiBUU-like hybrid truth (χ² = 171 for 16 dof, p < 10⁻²⁷) and the synthetic q3 tilt (χ² = 52, p < 10⁻⁵). Under Ctotal alone, two further truths are flagged. The Eavail residual under the GiBUU-to-GENIE change reaches 74% in one cell (using a development variant, not the reported estimator). Fig. 2 shows residual patterns that grow with the size of the injected change.

This is the central result of the paper, and it is a critical limitation for a measurement whose purpose is to compare generators. It means the estimator is biased toward the prior (Tune v1) by an amount that depends on how far data sit from the prior. For a generator-comparison measurement, the dominant bias is therefore in the direction that makes generators look more like the prior.

Two points weaken the interpretation. First, the residual is "consistent with finite-iteration regularization for the GiBUU-like change, but not for every change tested, and no single cause is established". Second, 121 of the 144 stable residuals lie in directions the binned response constrains weakly (Cramér–Rao widths above 10%). That suggests many of these cells are not measurable at this exposure whatever the estimator. If so, the error is in the cell design, since a reporting cell that cannot be measured should not be reported.

What the authors must do: (i) identify which cells are weakly constrained and remove or merge them in a prespecified way, then rerun the fake-data grading on the remaining cells; (ii) report the bias as a systematic uncertainty or a bias-corrected result, which standard OmniFold practice allows, and not only as a failed test; (iii) show the effect of iteration count and classifier capacity on the flagged truths, with the criteria fixed in advance.

**5. The unexplained shape disagreement with the published result undermines the benchmark.**
The integral agrees at 1.010, but χ²/ndf = 2.27 over 205 bins (shape part 465, normalisation part 0.04). Three of the six leading eigenmodes are in poorly determined directions (λ/λmax ≲ 4×10⁻⁹) at the highest p∥. The pseudo-inverse over 205 modes with a covariance condition number of 1.5×10¹² is numerically delicate. The χ²/ndf quoted with "ndf = 205 bins" is not a proper χ² per degree of freedom when the covariance has this many near-null directions. The authors state this and quote no p-value, which is correct.

Several choices were also made with the comparison in view: the learner implementation and the background-subtraction treatment. The authors state that "the comparison is therefore not an independent test of the method". The improvement from 2.65 to 2.27 is described honestly, but it means the comparison is tuned.

The binned IBU comparison does not localise the difference. The ratio ρ = 0.452 has a 90% interval of 0.701–1.128 and "reads unresolved". The paper thus cannot say whether the difference comes from the inputs (ntuple production, selection, flux, background template) or the algorithm.

What the authors must do: the most direct resolution is to run a standard binned unfolding on the published inputs, if released, and test whether the open-data AnaTuples reproduce the published reconstructed distributions. The paper cites an earlier production of the open-data release, with only playlist 1A compared. It has no comparison of the full reconstructed-level distributions with the published analysis's detector-level distributions. Without this, the 2D shape difference cannot be assigned to the method, and the benchmark is uninformative about OmniFold. A truncated-eigenmode χ² should be shown, with an explicit regularised treatment, not only a pseudo-inverse.

**6. Run-period inconsistency of unknown cause, handled by a covariance block added after the fact.**
The three run-period groups differ beyond bootstrap scatter, in 2D by a step of 6.1% at reconstruction level and (6.94 ± 0.41)% after unfolding. The step already exists in the reconstructed data, so it is not caused by OmniFold. It is, however, a data-quality or simulation-modelling problem of order 7%, comparable to the entire quoted uncertainty. The paper includes a rank-2 covariance block built from the group differences "after these differences were seen", and this block is "poorly determined" (68% interval on its size: 0.73–2.56).

It is reasonable to flag this. But it is an unresolved problem in the input data, not the unfolding. A methods paper needs data that are consistent enough to test the method, and a 7% unexplained step makes every data-level comparison ambiguous. In particular, it contaminates the 2D published comparison. The step is in the p∥ distribution near 8 GeV/c, and the shape disagreement is concentrated at the highest p∥. The authors do not test whether the two are connected, and I suspect they may be.

What the authors must do: (i) repeat the 2D benchmark using only the playlist group that is consistent with the published analysis, or per group; (ii) investigate the cause in the reconstructed data (flux, POT normalisation, detector configuration, simulation tune by period); (iii) quantify the effect of the run-period issue on the χ² to the published result.

**7. The paper relies too heavily on the companion note, and it is not self-contained.**
Nearly every key number carries a "(Ref. [14], Sec. X)" citation. This includes the closure test, the support rule, the covariance construction, the held-out tests, and the diagnoses of the coverage gate. A referee and a reader have no way to verify these from the paper alone. Table I states results for tests whose definition is spread across Secs. III, IV and the note. The 2.6σ stability result cannot be fully understood from the paper, because the "development variant", "joint-test estimator", "two-observable estimator" and the "earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance" are all different objects, used in different places.

What the authors must do: make the paper standalone for its principal claims, or reduce the claims to those it can support. Define each estimator once, in a table. Say clearly which estimator is behind each number. Currently there are at least five (the two-observable LightGBM, the joint-test estimator, the development variant at 100 trees and 8 leaves, the exact-split and histogram variants, and the early purity-based five-observable estimator).

**8. Definitional departures make cross-experiment comparison difficult.**
The paper defines Eavail with a constant of 135 MeV in the charged-pion term, omits kaons, electrons and positrons, and gives neutrons no contribution. It states that "No single published definition of Eavail exists". It also computes W from truth with the struck-nucleon mass and the muon angle from a fixed beam axis, "so truth W is fixed by (pT, p∥, q3) only up to that event-by-event angle". The reconstructed Eavail is 1.17 times a calorimetric energy. The effect on the cross section is bounded at 0.19σ per cell for the pion constant, but the other omissions are not bounded in the paper.

The concern is that the observables are defined in a way that makes the measurement not directly comparable with any published or generator-level calculation unless the exact definitions are reproduced. This is acceptable for a methods study, but it should not be given as a cross section.

What the authors must do: provide the generator-level definitions as an explicit, unambiguous list with code or pseudo-code, and state the size of the effect of the kaon and neutron conventions. The prediction comparisons use these definitions for GENIE, NuWro and GiBUU, which is good, but the analysis of target differences (carbon versus CH) is not carried through (see Sec. 4 below).

**9. The effective information content of five observables is overstated.**
The paper acknowledges that reconstructed W is an exact function of the reconstructed (pT, p∥, q3) apart from a clamp, so the five observables carry four independent reconstructed quantities. At truth level W is nearly but not exactly determined. Reporting results in five observables is therefore not as informative as it sounds. The claim in the abstract of "five observables" should be tempered, and the paper should show what the fifth observable adds. A natural test: unfold in four observables without W, and show how the (Eavail, q3) cells differ. Only the three-observable marginal versus the two-dimensional result is described, and only as a 0.141% total.

The reporting cells also come from only 32 boxes defined by one interior edge per axis. This is a very coarse reporting, and the 16 cells are not "multidifferential" in any useful sense. The comparison with the published three-dimensional measurements ([8–11]) is not carried out. Because the reporting is so coarse, I do not see how the method's advantage in "avoiding a response tensor for every projection" is exercised: with 16 cells, a binned unfolding would be straightforward. This weakens the motivation, and the IBU on those cells (T = 194.4 against bars of 28 and 39) shows disagreement but not a reason to prefer either.

What the authors must do: either show the value of the unbinned approach where binned methods cannot go (finer, genuinely multidimensional binning with a documented advantage in precision or bias), or reduce the claim.

**10. Multiple selections and post hoc elements weaken the "pre-declared" framing.**
The authors emphasise criteria "fixed before grading". This is good practice. But the paper also states that: the truths in the fake-data test "are not held out"; the closure at nominal truth is "not held out from its development"; the refinement capacity was chosen on simulation closures; the run-period block was added after seeing differences; and the learner and background treatment were chosen with the comparison to the published result in view. The paper also includes several descriptive tests with no pass criterion (the recoil shift closures, the 8-cell subset χ² = 132.4). The held-out GENIE v3 tunes pass, but the paper notes they "share a quasielastic cross-section model" and are "not independent".

The overall effect is that the paper has a mix of pre-registered and post hoc analyses. Some are labelled clearly. Others require careful reading. A reader cannot easily tell the number of tests run, the number that fail, and the family-wise effect of the thresholds. There are several tests with p-values near 0.01 to 0.05 (GiBUU Eavail p = 0.078, NuWro Eavail p = 0.093, NuWro (pT,p∥,Eavail) p = 0.0027 under Ctotal alone), and no discussion of multiplicity.

What the authors must do: tabulate all tests with their pre-declaration status (pre-declared and graded; pre-declared and descriptive; post hoc) in a single table, and discuss the look-elsewhere effect for the flagged truths.

## 5. Resolve or move out?

I would prefer the authors resolve the limitations I have marked (a), at least the numerical instability, the coverage test, the low-stability cells and the run-period effect on the benchmark. These are tractable with the tools the authors already have. The remaining limitations can be kept, since they are properly stated.

Two options are open to the authors:

- **Option A.** Resolve the (a) items, and then present a paper whose conclusion is that OmniFold in five observables works, with documented bias and quantified uncertainties.
- **Option B.** Retitle and reframe the paper as an explicit negative-results and lessons-learned study. In that case the data result and Appendix A should be removed from the paper and left in the note. The paper would state a checklist of what must be tested, which Sec. VI already starts to do, and present each failure with its diagnosis.

As currently submitted, the paper is not publishable in JINST as a methods paper, because its claims are split between the two options. Its stated aim is "a methods and validation study". The study is real, and the validation itself is of high quality. The method's performance is, however, not established. I do not see any single limitation as disqualifying, because all are honestly reported and each has a route to resolution. The unvalidated coverage and the numerical instability are the two that I would treat as most serious. Together with the unidentified shape difference, they prevent the paper from supporting its stated conclusion that the method "provides a simultaneous unbinned unfolding... whose normalization agrees with the two-dimensional result" as a usable tool. A paper presenting itself as a negative-results study could be published once the confusion over framing is removed.

## 6. Minor issues and presentation

1. **Register and length.** The paper is dense and written in a note-like register, with sentences such as "reads unresolved", "Five kept", "Same size" and "Recovered in part" in Table I. The text is about 11,000 words, with heavy use of internal terms ("joint-test estimator", "support rule", "development variant"). Most readers will not follow the abstract on first reading. Simplify the abstract, which is a single dense paragraph of nine claims.
2. **Table I is overloaded.** It mixes test results, status and statistics in cells that cannot be parsed in text form. Present it as a one-page table with columns: test, pre-declared (yes/no), criterion, result, pass/fail.
3. **Inconsistent counts.** "12 truths" in the Table II text, "17 truths it was graded on" in Sec. IV G, "6 further truths, given in the note". Reconcile. Also "40 pseudo-experiments" and "50 bootstrap replicas" versus "300 replicas" for different results.
4. **Estimator nomenclature.** The "two-observable estimator", "joint-test estimator", "development variant" and "earlier purity-subtracted five-observable estimator" should be defined once in a table, including which results use which. "Joint-test estimator" is named for tests of the note that the reader has not seen.
5. **The abstract's "p < 0.01, or any cell with |z| > 2".** The "any cell |z| > 2" criterion over 16 cells has a substantial false-positive rate under the null (about 1 − 0.9545¹⁶ ≈ 52% for independent cells, lower under correlation). State the null false-flag rate of the combined criterion. The authors say the criteria are fixed in advance, but a criterion with a high false-positive rate cannot discriminate well. It is therefore an uninformative flag for the borderline cases.
6. **Fig. 1.** The text says the band is "the four-block covariance of Sec. IV D, without Cmodel". Fig. 1's panel labels (the extracted text is garbled) should be checked for legibility. The comparison panel should show the shape residual on the eigenmode basis, since that is what the text discusses.
7. **Fig. 2.** The 74% outlier is in a development variant, not the reported estimator, and the grey band is from "an earlier purity-subtracted covariance, not the reported one". A figure that shows neither the reported estimator nor the reported covariance is hard to connect to the paper's conclusions. Show the reported estimator.
8. **Fig. 3.** It uses the pseudo-inverse and shows negative contributions. A plot against eigenmode index would be more informative than the bin grid.
9. **Fig. 4 and Table VI.** The comparison with generators is called illustrative, yet χ² values of 107 to 262 are reported for 16 dof. Readers will take these as exclusions despite the disclaimers. Either drop the table from the paper, or say what the comparison would look like with a validated covariance. Table VII shows that doubling Cmodel changes χ² by amounts that range from −5.6 to +0.2, and removing it changes it by +12.6 to +119.0. This shows that the generator comparison is strongly covariance-dependent.
10. **Data release.** The package that regenerates the figures is "not yet deposited". The quoted numbers need "archived analysis products", "generator samples produced for this analysis" and an "earlier production" of the open data. This falls well short of reproducibility for a paper whose main point is a reproducibility-type validation. Deposit the code and the processed products before publication, and state which parts of the open-data release the analysis used and whether current releases reproduce them.
11. **Use of an earlier production of the open-data release.** The paper says only playlist 1A predates the current release and that selected yields differ by a negligible amount. This is reasonable, but the 2D result would be more useful on the current release.
12. **Terminology.** "Closure" is used for tests at the simulation's own truth, but also for deformed-truth "closure" tests. "Closure" at a deformed truth is normally called a fake-data or bias test. Use one term.
13. **The Introduction** lists high-dimensional unfolding at H1, ATLAS and T2K but does not cite or discuss the neutrino-specific issues that make the problem hard (flux dependence, nuclear-model dependence of efficiency corrections, the use of the generator in the prior). It is missing a clear statement of why OmniFold is expected to help for MINERvA specifically.
14. **Statistical comparison of OmniFold and IBU.** The "T statistic" with calibrated bars is explained only in the note. A paragraph in the paper defining T and the null would help.
15. **Hartlap-type correction.** See Sec. 4, last row.
16. **The Conclusions** state "The method provides..." and then list what it does not yet provide. Rephrase to match what the evidence shows.
17. **Units.** The 1/13 weight per nucleon for the hydrogen correction should be justified in one line (CH has 12 + 1 nucleons per hydrogen atom).
18. **Typographical and layout issues.** The extracted text suggests tables and figures are broken across the text. Check the layout.

## 7. Scores

**(i) Publishability in JINST as submitted**

SCORE: 4/10

**(ii) Main contribution as I read it.** The paper's main contribution is a transparent record, with pre-declared criteria, of how a five-observable unbinned OmniFold unfolding of real MINERvA data fails to meet its own bias, stability, coverage and run-period criteria, together with a 1% integral agreement with the published two-dimensional result and a checklist of what must be validated before such unfolded intervals can be interpreted.

**(iii) Better-suited journal.** After the (a) items are resolved, JINST is appropriate; as a negative-results and validation study with the data appendix removed, a journal such as Computer Physics Communications, or Machine Learning: Science and Technology, or an experimental-methods section of Physical Review D, would also be a reasonable home.
