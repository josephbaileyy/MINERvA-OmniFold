# Referee Report for Physical Review D

**Manuscript:** Inclusive Muon-Neutrino Charged-Current Cross Section in Five Observables with OmniFold on MINERvA Open Data  
**Reviewer Focus:** Neutrino cross sections, multidimensional unfolding (OmniFold), and systematic uncertainty treatment  

---

### 1. Summary of Claims and Measurements

The manuscript presents a simultaneous measurement of the inclusive $\nu_\mu$ charged-current cross section on hydrocarbon (CH) across five kinematic observables: muon transverse momentum ($p_T$), longitudinal momentum ($p_\parallel$), available hadronic energy ($E_\text{avail}$), three-momentum transfer ($q_3$), and hadronic invariant mass ($W$). Using $1.057 \times 10^{21}$ protons on target from the MINERvA medium-energy open-data release, the authors implement the machine-learning-based OmniFold method to perform unbinned density-ratio estimation and report cell-integrated cross sections and an accompanying covariance matrix across sixteen coarse kinematic cells ($R00$–$R15$). The reported median per-cell relative uncertainty is 7.86%, which includes an empirical, post-hoc covariance contribution ($C_\text{PL,5D}$) introduced to account for observed run-period inconsistencies. The authors compare these sixteen-cell results against five model predictions from GENIE, NuWro, and GiBUU (yielding $\chi^2$ values of 107–229 for 16 degrees of freedom, and 48 for 8 degrees of freedom for GiBUU), while benchmarking the method in two dimensions against MINERvA's published $d^2\sigma/(dp_T dp_\parallel)$ cross section. Crucially, the authors document extensive stress tests and openly state that the uncertainty intervals lack validated coverage, exhibit significant prior-retention bias exceeding predefined thresholds under generator-sized truth shifts, and suffer from numerical instabilities.

---

### 2. Recommendation and Summary of Reasons

**Recommendation: Major Revision (with mandatory re-scoping from a physics measurement to a methodology/benchmarking paper, or rejection as a cross-section measurement).**

**Reasons:**  
This manuscript presents an unusual and challenging dilemma. On one hand, the authors are commended for an extraordinary, commendable level of scientific honesty: they thoroughly document and transparently report technical failure modes that are too often swept under the rug in machine-learning applications to experimental data. On the other hand, evaluated strictly as a *measurement* of a neutrino cross section intended for publication in *Physical Review D*, the result fails several foundational criteria of experimental physics. Most critically:
- The estimator exhibits severe regularization bias that fails fake-data closure tests by many orders of magnitude ($p = 5.3 \times 10^{-28}$ for a GiBUU-like truth) and retains up to 74% residual bias in hadronic energy and invariant mass;
- Frequentist interval coverage is explicitly unvalidated, with the pre-declared coverage test having been aborted due to unexplained internal discrepancies between pseudo-experiments and the covariance model;
- The algorithm is numerically unstable under floating-point precision, shifting by up to $2.6\sigma$ under input rounding;
- The method fails to reproduce MINERvA's published 2D benchmark on the exact same data, displaying an unexplained correlated shape difference ($\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ over 205 bins); and
- An unexplained 7% time-dependent step in the data across run periods is absorbed via a post-hoc, rank-2 covariance matrix of poorly determined magnitude.

Consequently, the cross-section values reported in Table II cannot be recommended for physics interpretation, nuclear-model discrimination, or neutrino-oscillation generator tuning. However, the diagnostic rigor, the forensic autopsy of high-dimensional OmniFold, and the candid identification of these failure modes are of exceptional value to the neutrino physics and machine-learning communities. Therefore, the manuscript requires a **Major Revision** to fundamentally reframe the paper: the authors must either resolve these major technical hurdles (which would require substantial re-analysis) or withdraw the claim of a definitive cross-section measurement, re-titling and restructuring the paper as a critical methodological investigation into the performance, validation requirements, and limitations of high-dimensional unbinned unfolding in neutrino scattering.

---

### 3. Major Issues

#### 1. Severe Regularization Bias and Failure of Fake-Data Closure Tests
* **What is wrong:** Under alternative truth distributions, the unfolding fails to recover the truth by margins far exceeding the claimed uncertainties. In the 16-cell fake-data tests (Table I), pseudo-data deformed to a GiBUU hybrid truth fails with $\chi^2 = 171.01$ ($p = 5.3 \times 10^{-28}$, with cells $R02$ and $R06$ displaying $|z| > 2$), and a synthetic $q_3$ tilt yields $\chi^2 = 52.48$ ($p = 9.1 \times 10^{-6}$). In the continuous $(E_\text{avail}, W)$ space (Sec. IV E and Fig. 2), the estimator deviates from an injected GiBUU truth change by up to 74% at high $W$. The authors explicitly attribute this to "regularization bias, because it persists over the tested iteration numbers and classifier capacities."
* **Why it matters physically:** The primary purpose of unfolding in neutrino cross-section physics is to remove detector effects in a model-independent manner so that the resulting data can be used to test arbitrary, unseen nuclear models. If the estimator retains memory of the simulation prior (GENIE Tune v1) and cannot adapt to a realistic alternative physics model like GiBUU, the extracted cross section is heavily contaminated by the prior. The severe model comparisons in Sec. V B (where GiBUU yields $\chi^2 = 48$ on 8 cells) cannot be interpreted as a physical rejection of GiBUU when the estimator itself is demonstrably incapable of unfolding a GiBUU-like universe.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must either:
  1. Re-engineer the OmniFold architecture (e.g., investigating classifier capacity, network architectures beyond default shallow BDTs, alternative loss formulations, regularization schemes, or data-driven stopping criteria) to achieve acceptable closure ($p > 0.01$ and $|z| < 2$) across all generator-sized truth changes; or
  2. Derive and incorporate an explicit, validated systematic uncertainty envelope that covers this regularization bias across the entire phase space, demonstrating closure with the inflated uncertainty; or
  3. Formally reframe the manuscript as a methodological demonstration of the failure modes of BDT-based OmniFold in sparse, high-dimensional neutrino phase space, retracting the cross-section values as physical benchmarks.

#### 2. Unvalidated Frequentist Coverage and Aborted Coverage Machinery
* **What is wrong:** The manuscript repeatedly acknowledges: *"The coverage of the reported uncertainty intervals has not been validated"* (Abstract, Sec. I, Sec. IV, Sec. VI A). Furthermore, Sec. VI A reveals that a formal coverage test *“was stopped at one of its gates before any coverage pseudo-experiment ran: the uncertainties reproduced through its pseudo-experiments disagreed with the covariance blocks they draw, for reasons not established.”* Earlier statistical intervals undercovered dramatically, reaching coverage as low as 0.06 for nominal 68% intervals (Sec. IV E).
* **Why it matters physically:** In PRD, reported uncertainties must correspond to well-defined statistical confidence intervals. If coverage is unvalidated and known to drop to 6% in related estimators, the statement that the median uncertainty is 7.86% is physically uninterpretable. Without validated coverage, error bars cannot be used in global oscillation fits or generator parameter extractions, and the $\chi^2$ values in Table III have no well-defined probabilistic interpretation.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must debug their pseudo-experiment generation pipeline to resolve why simulated pseudo-experiments fail to reproduce their input covariance blocks. Once the pipeline is mathematically sound, they must execute an ensemble coverage study across both Poisson fluctuations and systematic nuisance variations at multiple truth hypotheses to establish whether the reported intervals achieve nominal 68% coverage, adjusting uncertainty estimates where undercoverage occurs.

#### 3. Unresolved Discrepancy with Published 2D Benchmark ($\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$)
* **What is wrong:** In the 2D benchmark ($p_T, p_\parallel$) against MINERvA's published result [13] on the identical dataset, the authors observe a descriptive covariance-weighted distance of $\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ across 205 bins (Sec. IV B). The text admits: *"It indicates a correlated shape difference, not a normalization difference, spread over many eigenmodes of the published covariance. The source of the shape difference has not been identified."* Moreover, the choice of learner (LightGBM vs. exact split) was made post-hoc after inspecting the comparison to the published data.
* **Why it matters physically:** Before trusting an unbinned machine-learning method in five dimensions, it must demonstrate consistency with established, binned D'Agostini unfolding on the identical dataset in two dimensions. A correlated shape discrepancy of $\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ across 205 degrees of freedom corresponds to an astronomically small $p$-value ($p \sim 10^{-20}$), proving that the two analyses extract fundamentally different physical shapes from the same raw detector events. Lacking an explanation, one cannot determine whether the published result or this new OmniFold extraction contains an uncorrected experimental bias.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must perform a rigorous differential comparison between the two pipelines. They must test whether this shape difference stems from the negative-weight background refinement, efficiency handling for unmatched events, beam angle corrections, or the iteration count. If the discrepancy is algorithmic, it must be resolved; if it represents an inherent feature of unbinned vs. binned response matrices, its origin must be pinpointed and physically justified.

#### 4. Numerical Instability Under Floating-Point Rounding
* **What is wrong:** Sec. IV F states that the joint-test estimator is unstable under floating-point rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations, the results scatter by a median of 0.23% per joint cell (comparable to the median statistical uncertainty of 0.37%), with a single perturbation moving results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations. The authors concede that the estimator fails their pre-declared data-stability requirement ($\le 0.3$ median, $\le 1.0$ maximum statistical $\sigma$).
* **Why it matters physically:** Scientific measurements must be reproducible and numerically stable. If a shift in the least significant bits of floating-point representations alters physical cross sections by up to $2.6\sigma$, the decision-tree splitting logic is hyper-sensitive to numerical noise. This indicates severe local variance, tree instability, or an ill-conditioned optimization landscape.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must implement adequate tree regularization. Increasing minimum leaf sample weights (`min_child_weight` / `min_child_samples`), reducing learning rates, introducing feature subsampling/bagging, or ensembling predictions across multiple random seeds and floating-point representations must be used to ensure that numerical rounding perturbations produce negligible shifts ($\ll 0.1\sigma$).

#### 5. Ad-Hoc Post-Hoc Treatment of Unexplained Run-Period Inconsistency
* **What is wrong:** Unfolded data from different MINERvA run periods exhibit statistically significant discrepancies relative to their respective simulations, notably an unexplained ~7% step in the unfolded $p_\parallel$ distribution near 8 GeV/c (Sec. IV A, IV C). To absorb this, the authors constructed a post-hoc, rank-2 covariance block ($C_\text{PL,5D}$) from double ratios across three period groups. Its magnitude is poorly constrained, with a 68% confidence interval on its scale spanning 0.73 to 2.56.
* **Why it matters physically:** An unexplained 7% kinematic discontinuity across data-taking periods points to an unmodeled time-dependent detector or beam systematic (e.g., drift in MINOS spectrometer calibration, magnetic field stability, tracking efficiency, or beam alignment). Absorbing an unmodeled physical defect into an ad-hoc covariance block after observing the data is statistically hazardous. Because the block is built from only three period groups, it has rank 2, injecting an artificial, rigid two-dimensional correlation structure into a sixteen-dimensional space without physical justification.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must investigate the experimental origin of the 8 GeV/c feature in the MINERvA and MINOS sub-detectors. If the feature represents localized detector degradation or unmodeled dead time in specific playlists, those periods should either be recalibrated or excised. If the error must remain a systematic uncertainty, it must be modeled via continuous physical nuisance parameters rather than an uncalibrated, rank-deficient post-hoc matrix.

#### 6. Low Reconstruction Stability in Multiple Reporting Cells
* **What is wrong:** Four of the sixteen reporting cells ($R02$, $R06$, $R10$, and $R15$) exhibit reconstruction stabilities between 0.16 and 0.25 (Sec. VI A.2). That is, only 16% to 25% of the signal events generated in these cells and reconstructed are reconstructed within the same cell.
* **Why it matters physically:** In cross-section measurements, a reconstruction stability below ~50% is traditionally considered unacceptable because the reported cross section is dominated by migrations from neighboring phase space (75% to 84% background from event migration). This makes these specific cells acutely vulnerable to mismodeling of detector resolution and classifier bias. Tellingly, cells $R02$ and $R06$ are the exact cells that drive the catastrophic failure of the GiBUU fake-data test ($|z| > 2$).
* **What the authors must do:** The reporting cell boundaries must be redesigned. The authors should merge these low-stability boxes with adjacent kinematic cells to form reporting regions that achieve acceptable reconstruction stability (e.g., $\ge 50\%$), thereby mitigating extreme migration susceptibility.

#### 7. Methodological Circularity and target mismatch in $C_\text{model}$
* **What is wrong:** The model covariance term $C_\text{model}$ is constructed from unscaled differences when the prior is reweighted to GENIE+MEC, NuWro, and GiBUU on carbon (Sec. IV A). In Sec. V B, these exact same generator predictions are compared against the unfolded data using the covariance that incorporates them. The authors admit: *"The comparisons there with GENIE+MEC, NuWro and GiBUU therefore use covariance contributions derived from the same generators. It is a measure of unscaled generator-shape dependence, not a calibrated bound... size of $C_\text{model}$ is not calibrated... removing it [changes $\chi^2$ by up to +119]."
* **Why it matters physically:** Including the predictions being tested within the measurement covariance matrix introduces circularity and artificially suppresses the $\chi^2$ test statistic. When $C_\text{model}$ is removed, GiBUU's $\chi^2$ jumps by 119 units. Furthermore, building $C_\text{model}$ from carbon simulations while evaluating CH predictions conflates nuclear modeling with chemical composition discrepancies (carbon cross section per nucleon exceeds CH by 8.3% at low $W$).
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must separate model-bias validation from hypothesis testing. If $C_\text{model}$ represents an unfolding systematic, it must be evaluated using independent, held-out model variations (or parameterized synthetic deformations) and evaluated on the correct target (CH). When comparing data to external generators in Table III, the models under test must not appear in the definition of the measurement covariance matrix.

### 4. Evaluation of Stated Limitations and Qualifications

The manuscript candidly lists numerous limitations. Below is the systematic evaluation of each, categorized as:
* **(a)** A real deficiency of the measurement that must be resolved with further analysis prior to publication;
* **(b)** An acceptable, honestly stated limitation that a PRD measurement may publish with as is;
* **(c)** Detail that belongs in the companion analysis note (Ref. [14]), not the main paper.

---

#### 1. Unvalidated Coverage
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reason:** In an experimental measurement submitted to PRD, uncertainties must represent valid confidence intervals. Publishing cross sections whose coverage is known to be untested (and shown in earlier iterations to undercover down to 6%) undermines any quantitative use of the data.
* **Resolution:** Execute a complete ensemble pseudo-experiment pipeline incorporating Poisson fluctuations and systematic universes across alternative truths to evaluate and tune coverage.

#### 2. Run-Period Inconsistency and $C_\text{PL,5D}$
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reason:** A 7% step in kinematics between run periods indicates unmodeled detector/beam physics. Patching this post-hoc with a rank-2 matrix whose magnitude has a 68% uncertainty spanning a factor of 3.5 ($0.73$–$2.56$) is a major experimental defect.
* **Resolution:** Conduct an experimental diagnostic of MINOS/MINERvA calibrations across the 12 playlists to isolate the hardware/software source; parameterize it as a physical detector variation systematic or exclude unrecoverable run periods.

#### 3. Fake-Data Bias Exceeding Predeclared Thresholds ($p \sim 10^{-28}$)
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reason:** A failure of this magnitude ($p = 5.3 \times 10^{-28}$, $|z| > 2$) demonstrates that the estimator cannot be considered an unbiased or reliable cross-section extractor when nature deviates from the base simulation.
* **Resolution:** Re-tune the BDT/neural network architecture, tree depth, and iteration stopping to achieve closure on GiBUU and synthetic tilts, or construct a validated bias systematic covering the discrepancy.

#### 4. The Stopped Coverage Test
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reason:** An aborted validation pipeline due to an unexplained mathematical/sampling contradiction between pseudo-experiments and covariance blocks indicates that the analysis software infrastructure is incomplete or contains unresolved bugs.
* **Resolution:** Identify and resolve the bug in the pseudo-experiment generation code, verify consistency with input covariance matrices, and execute the test to completion.

#### 5. Low-Stability Cells ($R02, R06, R10, R15$ at 16–25%)
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reason:** Presenting cross sections where 75–84% of reconstructed events migrated from other kinematic regions renders those bins fragile and hyper-dependent on the simulation's migration model.
* **Resolution:** Merge these four problematic cells with adjacent kinematic bins to ensure stability values meet standard experimental thresholds ($\ge 50\%$).

#### 6. Target Differences and Defective Comparator Samples
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reason:** Evaluating carbon-only predictions against CH data via an ad-hoc 1/13 proton addition is physically problematic. Worse, Table III footnotes reveal severe generator defects: GiBUU's free-proton sample failed sampling closure and 46% of its DIS events violate energy conservation, while NuWro has unquantified generator aborts at 50–100 GeV. A PRD paper cannot benchmark data against model samples that fail basic four-momentum conservation.
* **Resolution:** Re-generate clean, native CH samples for all comparator generators without generator aborts, and ensure strict energy-momentum conservation across all interaction channels.

#### 7. Definitional Departures in $E_\text{avail}$ and Constrained Kinematics of Reconstructed $W$
* **Classification:** **(b) Acceptable, honestly stated limitation.**
* **Reason:** Equation (1) provides an exact, transparent, and reproducible definition of true $E_\text{avail}$. The authors rigorously evaluate the effect of replacing the 135 MeV offset with the charged-pion mass, finding shifts bounded by $0.19\sigma$ (and $0.42\sigma$ coherently). Furthermore, clearly stating that reconstructed $W$ is constrained by $(p_T, p_\parallel, q_3)$ through the calorimetric response is an honest and helpful clarification of feature dimensionality. As long as the truth definition is applied identically to generators, this is standard and acceptable practice.

#### 8. Sensitivity to Input Floating-Point Rounding
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reason:** Numerical shifts of up to $2.6\sigma$ resulting purely from floating-point rounding perturbations violate basic algorithmic stability and reproducibility standards.
* **Resolution:** Apply tree-level regularization (e.g., minimum leaf instances, tree shrinkage, sub-sampling) or ensemble bagging across multiple initializations/seeds to ensure numerical stability.

#### 9. Unidentified Correlated Shape Discrepancy in 2D Benchmark ($\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$)
* **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**
* **Reason:** An unexplained failure to reproduce MINERvA's own published cross section on the same data in 2D undermines confidence in the 5D extraction.
* **Resolution:** Conduct an end-to-end ablation study comparing the binned and unbinned pipelines to isolate the source of the shape discrepancy.

#### 10. Low Effective Degrees of Freedom in Flux Covariance ($N_\text{eff} = 19$)
* **Classification:** **(b) Acceptable limitation / (c) Detail for companion note.**
* **Reason:** Using 100 PPFX flux universes resulting in 19 effective universes is an intrinsic limitation of the available open-data release. The authors performed bootstrap tests showing the resulting $\chi^2$ values are stable to within 3.8%. This is an acceptable, honestly stated experimental constraint; the detailed bootstrap distribution formulas belong in Ref. [14].

#### 11. Calibrated Tests Rejecting Hybrid Nulls Without Sub-Grid Bounds
* **Classification:** **(c) Detail that belongs in the companion note.**
* **Reason:** The nuance of whether hybrid null rejections generalize to native generators without sub-grid composition bounds pertains to auxiliary tests developed in Ref. [14]. A brief qualifying sentence in the paper suffices; extensive mathematical discussion belongs in the companion note.

### 5. Referee Assessment: Publishability, Resolution vs. Removal, and Disqualifying Factors

* **Should the authors resolve these limitations or move them out of the paper?**  
  The authors should **under no circumstances move these limitations out of the paper** to make the result appear more acceptable. Sweeping known failures of coverage, bias, and stability into a companion document while presenting an apparently clean cross section in PRD would be scientifically misleading. The authors' transparency is their greatest asset.

* **Is the measurement publishable in PRD with its current set of limitations?**  
  **No.** Evaluated as a physical cross-section measurement, it is not publishable. A measurement whose coverage is unvalidated, whose bias fails closure tests at $p \sim 10^{-28}$, whose numerical implementation shifts by $2.6\sigma$ under floating-point rounding, and whose comparison to existing data disagrees at $\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ cannot be used by the scientific community to draw physical conclusions about neutrino-nucleus interactions.

* **Is any limitation disqualifying?**  
  Yes. The combination of **Issue 1 (catastrophic failure of fake-data closure at GiBUU truth)**, **Issue 2 (aborted coverage validation)**, and **Issue 4 (floating-point instability)** is completely disqualifying for a primary experimental cross section.

* **The Path Forward:**  
  The manuscript *can* be published in PRD if the authors execute a **major pivot in scope**. If reframed from a *cross-section measurement* to a *methodological study and diagnostic stress test of high-dimensional OmniFold on real neutrino detector data*, this work would represent a landmark contribution. The paper would serve as a vital warning and roadmap for the field regarding regularization bias, prior retention, and validation pitfalls in machine-learning unfolding. In such a revision, the sixteen-cell results in Table II would be presented not as definitive physical cross sections, but as an empirical case study illustrating the practical behavior of the algorithm.

### 6. Minor Issues and Presentation

1. **Disjointed Register and Narrative Dissonance:**  
   The paper suffers from an internal contradiction in tone. The Abstract and Introduction read like a standard, triumphant cross-section paper (*"We measure the inclusive muon-neutrino charged-current cross section simultaneously in five observables..."*), whereas Sections IV, V, and VI read like an autopsy of why the measurement cannot be trusted (*"coverage has not been validated"*, *"bias exceeds thresholds"*, *"stopped at one of its gates"*). The narrative must be harmonized to reflect a consistent, objective methodological evaluation.

2. **Heavy Reliance on Ref. [14]:**  
   Ref. [14] is cited over 25 times, often for essential details (e.g., the construction of $C_\text{PL,5D}$, the failure of the coverage gate, the definition of the joint-test estimator, and generator defects). While a companion analysis note is helpful, PRD requires that the paper and its Supplemental Material be self-contained. Key definitions and critical failure descriptions must be summarized directly in the text.

3. **Upper Kinematic Bounds in Table II:**  
   In Table II, kinematic ranges for several cells list upper bounds of "100" (e.g., $E_\text{avail} \in [0, 100]$, $W \in [0, 100]$). While the text notes this represents the outer analysis grid edge, listing "100" without units in a physical cross-section table is unconventional. These should be formally written as $[0, \infty)$ or specified with their actual physical kinematic limits (e.g., $E_\nu < 60$ GeV implies strict kinematic bounds on $W$ and $E_\text{avail}$).

4. **Flawed Generator Predictions in Table III:**  
   Footnote (b) of Table III states that GiBUU's free-proton sample *"failed its sampling-closure check, and its deep-inelastic events, 46% of it, do not conserve energy."* Publishing quantitative $\chi^2$ values against a model sample where nearly half the events violate four-momentum conservation is unacceptable for PRD. These events must be re-simulated correctly or removed from the comparison.

5. **Clarity of Figure 1 (2D Benchmark):**  
   In Figure 1, the lower-left plot shows the standardized residuals $({\text{OmniFold}} - \text{published})/\sigma_\text{published}$ across 205 bins. Because the cells are drawn at equal size rather than physical kinematic area, the plot is difficult to interpret physically. A physical $(p_T, p_\parallel)$ phase-space heat map would make the nature of the correlated shape discrepancy immediately apparent.

6. **Terminology: "Support Rule":**  
   In Sec. II, the authors define the "support rule" (requiring expected reconstructed signal and truth weight $\ge 1000$ events). This is simply an occupancy or fiducial phase-space cut. The authors should use standard HEP terminology (e.g., "phase-space occupancy criterion").

7. **Equation (1) Motivation:**  
   In Equation (1), the subtraction of a defined constant of 135 MeV for charged pions instead of the physical charged-pion mass (139.57 MeV) is noted as departing from prior literature by 4.57 MeV. The text should explicitly explain the physical or technical motivation for this specific choice.
