You are advising the authors of a JINST methods paper (a diagnostic study of OmniFold on MINERvA open data) on one decision. Below are (1) the paper's text on its two-dimensional covariance and its coverage test, (2) the referee's request, and (3) three options with their compute costs. Compute is cheap relative to the authors' allocation (about 1500 node-hours available); the costs matter mostly as time and complexity.

B. Given the paper's own decomposition of the joint chi-square (mean-error part versus spread part), how likely is it that correcting the approximation changes the qualitative coverage verdict (severe under-coverage at both tunes)? Reason from the numbers given; do not invent others.

D. Anything the authors should state in the paper whichever option they choose.

Calibration of the uncertainties

Each coverage test in this section asks whether a covariance, rebuilt at a held-out truth by the reported procedure,
covers the errors of pseudo-experiments drawn about that
truth. The target is the nominal content of each interval:
per cell, the fraction of errors ec with |ec | ≤ σc , which is
0.6827 for a Gaussian error, and jointly the fraction with
χ2 below its null distribution’s quantile at that content.
Because the drawn uncertainties are two-point or discrete,
each test replaces the Gaussian content with one matched
to its draws, and flags under-coverage when a count of
the pseudo-experiments falls to a binomial threshold of
that content; over-coverage is reported and is not a failure (supplementary material, section S9). Each such test
grades the procedure’s covariance as rebuilt at its truths;
none grades the reported intervals at the data. These are
conditional tests of covariance adequacy: the systematic
parameters are drawn from their bands’ discrete or Gaussian laws, the covariance is rebuilt once at each tune and
then held fixed, the lateral uncertainties are represented
by a surrogate, the nominal Gaussian content is replaced
by a content matched to the draws, some terms are included in the covariance without being drawn and others
are left out, and the truths are gated, normalized shape
deformations from two tunes (supplementary material,
section S9).

===== (2) THE REFEREE'S REQUEST (major issue 3) =====
3. **The two-dimensional systematic-universe approximation must be corrected or bounded against the relevant uncertainty.**

   The covariance omits an approximation affecting events that fail reconstruction. Its effect was examined in only two universes and bounded relative to an earlier training-seed spread. That is not a physically persuasive bound on its contribution to the final covariance, particularly when the coverage test finds spread contributions of approximately three per degree of freedom and still fails after substantial eigenmode truncation.

   The authors should rebuild the affected universe propagation consistently, or demonstrate a sufficiently tight bound across representative and potentially adverse bands. The relevant comparison is with the final covariance and the directions driving under-coverage, rather than with a superseded seed variation.

   This need not make the coverage failure disappear. It would establish that the published diagnostic concerns the intended covariance procedure rather than an avoidable propagation approximation. Agreement between eigensolvers, already documented, addresses numerical inversion accuracy but does not resolve this issue.

===== (3) THE OPTIONS =====
The approximation: in the 185 systematic-universe unfoldings of the 2D covariance, events that fail reconstruction are not given their universe's own truth weight.
(a) Consistent rebuild: rerun the 185 weight universes with each missed event at its universe truth weight, rebuild the systematic block, and compare it with the reported 2D covariance along the failing eigen-directions of the coverage test. About 3.3 node-hours expected (cap 8.75), 1-2 h wall. It does not regrade coverage.
(a+) Option (a), plus rebuilding the coverage test's covariance C_T at both tunes and regrading the 2D coverage test (the 300 pseudo-experiments per tune). About 6.7 node-hours more expected (cap 17.5 more); if the 300 errors per tune are not stored, a rerun of the test.
(b) Bound only: the 25 universes carrying the most weight in the failing directions, two seeds, both treatments; compare. About 1 node-hour expected (cap 2.5).
