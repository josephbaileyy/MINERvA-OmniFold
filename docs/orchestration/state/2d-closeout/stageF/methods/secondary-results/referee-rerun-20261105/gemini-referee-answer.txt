# Referee Report on Manuscript Submitted to Physical Review D

**Title / Subject:** Five-dimensional unbinned cross-section measurement of $\nu_\mu$ charged-current interactions on hydrocarbon using MINERvA open data and OmniFold  
**Reviewer Expertise:** Neutrino cross-section measurements, unbinned and machine-learning unfolding (OmniFold / density ratio estimation), systematic uncertainty quantification, and detector response modeling.

---

### 1. Summary of the Measurement and Claims

The manuscript presents a simultaneous measurement of the inclusive $\nu_\mu$ charged-current cross section on a hydrocarbon (CH) tracker target using MINERvA medium-energy NuMI open data ($1.057 \times 10^{21}$ protons on target). The measurement is performed across five kinematic observables—muon transverse momentum ($p_T$), muon longitudinal momentum ($p_\parallel$), available hadronic energy ($E_\text{avail}$), three-momentum transfer ($q_3$), and hadronic invariant mass ($W$)—unfolded simultaneously without binning using the machine-learning-based OmniFold method (gradient-boosted decision trees) and reported in sixteen coarse kinematic cells with a full covariance matrix. As an end-to-end benchmark, the authors apply the method to the two-dimensional $(p_T, p_\parallel)$ phase space, reproducing the published cross-section integral to within 1.0% but observing a significant correlated shape difference ($\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ across 205 bins). The sixteen-cell result is compared to predictions from several neutrino event generators (GENIE, NuWro, GiBUU), yielding large $\chi^2$ values ranging from 48 (8 degrees of freedom) to 262 (16 degrees of freedom). Crucially, the authors report extensive self-diagnostics: the reported uncertainties have unvalidated coverage; a scheduled coverage test was aborted; the estimator exhibits sensitivity to floating-point rounding of its inputs up to $2.6\sigma$; four of the sixteen reporting cells have low reconstruction stability ($0.16$–$0.25$); and fake-data tests at alternative generator truths display large residual biases that fail the authors' pre-declared acceptance criteria ($p < 10^{-27}$, $|z| > 2$).

---

### 2. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Major Revision** (with an explicit caveat that the manuscript cannot be accepted as a physics cross-section measurement unless the fundamental validation failures and numerical instabilities are resolved, or the paper is fundamentally rescaled and reframed as a methodological case study).

**Reasons:**  
The authors deserve commendation for an extraordinary level of scientific candor: rather than concealing the failure modes and discrepancies that frequently plague high-dimensional machine-learning unfoldings, they have documented them with meticulous honesty. However, Physical Review D is a journal of record for experimental physics measurements. A primary experimental cross-section measurement cannot be published when its uncertainty intervals have unvalidated coverage, its planned coverage test was aborted due to unexplained internal inconsistencies, its central values exhibit numerical instability under floating-point rounding exceeding 2.5 statistical standard deviations, and its estimator fails pre-declared fake-data closure tests on realistic physics truths with astronomical rejection significances ($p < 10^{-27}$). Under these conditions, the reported sixteen-cell cross sections and their corresponding generator $\chi^2$ values cannot be reliably used by the nuclear and particle physics communities for model discrimination, generator tuning, or neutrino oscillation systematics. The authors must either complete the necessary analyses to resolve these deficiencies or transparently reframe the paper from a primary physics measurement to a methodological study detailing the challenges, regularization requirements, and diagnostic limits of multidimensional unbinned unfolding in neutrino scattering.

---

### 3. Major Issues

#### 1. Catastrophic Fake-Data Bias and Failure of Pre-Declared Validation Criteria
* **What is wrong/missing:** The authors pre-declared objective bias criteria ($p \ge 0.01$ and no cell with $|z| > 2$) to judge whether the unfolding reliably recovers alternative truths. As reported in Sec. IV G and Table I, the joint-test estimator fails these criteria catastrophically: at the GiBUU hybrid truth, the fit yields $\chi^2 = 171$ for 16 degrees of freedom ($p < 10^{-27}$), with cells R02 and R06 exceeding $2\sigma$ bias; the synthetic $q_3$ tilt yields $\chi^2 = 52$ ($p < 10^{-5}$). Without the post-hoc run-period block, the GiBUU $E_\text{avail}$ shape also fails ($z = 2.01$). In Sec. IV E, the authors note that the unfolding recovers "only part of a change of the $E_\text{avail}$ shape as large as the difference between generators," leaving residuals up to 31% in joint cells and 74% in developmental tests.
* **Why it matters physically:** The core premise of unfolding is to deliver a measurement that is independent of the simulation prior within stated uncertainties. These fake-data failures demonstrate that the OmniFold estimator retains a strong memory of the MINERvA Tune v1 prior when confronted with physically plausible variations in hadronic energy and momentum transfer. Consequently, the large $\chi^2$ values observed when comparing external generators to data (Table III) cannot be interpreted as physical rejections of those generators; they are heavily confounded by the unfolding's algorithmic inability to track true physical departures from the prior.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must determine whether this bias is driven by insufficient iterations (regularization), inadequate tree capacity, lack of feature depth, or the refinement reweighting scheme. They must either:
  1. Retrain or regularize the estimator so that it successfully passes the pre-declared bias tests across all benchmark truths; or
  2. Derive and validate an explicit model-dependence systematic uncertainty (or bias correction) that restores certified coverage and lowers $|z| \le 2$ and $p \ge 0.01$ across all fake-data tests; or
  3. Formally withdraw the sixteen-cell result as an unbinned physical cross-section measurement and present these findings as a cautionary demonstration of prior bias in multidimensional ML unfolding.

#### 2. Unvalidated Uncertainty Coverage and Aborted Verification Pipeline
* **What is wrong/missing:** The paper explicitly acknowledges in Sec. I, Sec. IV, and Sec. VI A.1 that *"No reported interval has validated coverage."* More critically, a coverage test across pseudo-experiments that sampled flux, interaction, and detector variations *"was stopped by one of its preliminary checks before any coverage pseudo-experiment ran: the uncertainties reproduced through its pseudo-experiments disagreed with the covariance blocks they draw, for reasons not established"* (Sec. VI A.1). Furthermore, the authors note that an earlier five-observable purity-subtracted estimator exhibited severe undercoverage, with coverage dropping as low as 0.06 for 68% intervals (Sec. IV E).
* **Why it matters physically:** In PRD, reported experimental uncertainties must represent valid confidence intervals. If the coverage of the reported intervals is unknown—and has been observed to collapse to 6% in related configurations—the reported covariance matrix cannot be used for quantitative hypothesis testing or parameter fitting. Furthermore, an aborted coverage test whose preliminary diagnostics failed for "unestablished reasons" strongly hints at an underlying software or mathematical defect in the pseudo-experiment generation pipeline.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must identify why the reproduced pseudo-experiment uncertainties disagreed with the covariance blocks, fix the inconsistency in the simulation pipeline, and complete the full coverage study. They must show that the reported intervals achieve near-nominal frequentist coverage (pointwise and joint) under realistic variations of the truth and detector response.

#### 3. Numerical Instability Under Floating-Point Rounding
* **What is wrong/missing:** As stated in Sec. IV F, the joint-test estimator is unstable under floating-point rounding of its inputs: 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data cause the unfolded results to scatter by a median of 0.23% per joint cell (approaching the median statistical uncertainty of 0.37%), with individual cell shifts reaching up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations ($2.6\sigma$). The estimator explicitly fails the authors' internal stability requirement ($\le 0.3$ median and $\le 1.0$ maximum statistical standard deviations).
* **Why it matters physically:** Physical observables must not depend on numerical representation noise. An estimator that shifts by more than $2.5\sigma$ upon sub-precision bit-level perturbations of the inputs is operating in a numerically chaotic, ill-conditioned, or severely overfitted regime. This undermines the reproducibility of the central values and indicates that individual decision-tree split thresholds are latching onto noise spikes.
* **What the authors must do:** The training algorithm must be stabilized. In tree-based models like LightGBM, this behavior typically indicates that leaves have too few minimum events per leaf, that the learning rate is too aggressive without early stopping, or that feature/row subsampling is omitted. The authors must introduce appropriate tree-level regularization (e.g., minimum child weight/samples, bagging/subsampling, or averaging across an ensemble of fixed seeds) to ensure the output is demonstrably invariant to floating-point rounding.

#### 4. Correlated Discrepancy with Published 2D Measurement
* **What is wrong/missing:** In the two-dimensional $(p_T, p_\parallel)$ benchmark (Sec. IV B), the unfolding reproduces the integrated cross section to within 1% but yields a descriptive distance of $\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ across the 205 reported bins against the published covariance. The manuscript states plainly: *"The source of the shape difference has not been identified."* Furthermore, the choice of the LightGBM implementation and the refined negative-weight background subtraction was adopted *after* comparing these variations against the published data, which reduced $\chi^2/\text{ndf}$ post hoc from 3.66/2.65 to 2.27.
* **Why it matters physically:** The 2D analysis was designed as the primary end-to-end ground-truth validation against MINERvA's published PRD measurement [13] on the identical data set. A $\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ for 205 degrees of freedom corresponds to a negligible $p$-value ($p \sim 10^{-16}$), proving that the two measurements are statistically incompatible under the published covariance. Without identifying the physical or algorithmic mechanism driving this shape difference, one cannot assess whether the 5D result is affected by the same distortion.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must isolate the origin of this discrepancy. They should perform a differential comparison between standard iterative Bayesian unfolding and OmniFold on this exact sample, isolating the role of: (a) unbinned vs. binned background subtraction; (b) the efficiency correction for events that fail reconstruction; (c) phase-space boundary migration; and (d) iteration stopping. If the discrepancy arises from genuine differences in how acceptance/efficiency or migration are handled, this must be quantified and explained.

#### 5. Ad-Hoc Post-Hoc Run-Period Covariance Block ($C_\text{PL,5D}$)
* **What is wrong/missing:** In Sec. IV A and IV C, the authors note that the unfolded results across the three run-period groups disagree beyond their bootstrap scatter, including an unexplained 7% step in $p_\parallel$ near 8 GeV/c. Because the cause is unknown, the authors added an ad-hoc covariance block, $C_\text{PL,5D}$, built from empirical double ratios across the three period groups. This block is rank 2, is uncorrected for bootstrap noise, has a poorly determined magnitude (68% confidence interval on the ratio of true to estimated size is 0.73–2.56), and was introduced *after* inspecting the data discrepancies.
* **Why it matters physically:** An unexplained 7% discontinuity in muon momentum distributions across run periods strongly points to an uncorrected detector or beam calibration issue (e.g., MINOS spectrometer magnetic field drift, alignment changes, target degradation, or light-yield attenuation in the tracker). Inflating uncertainties by adding an empirical rank-2 covariance matrix does not resolve the physical problem; it simply dilutes the statistical significance of discrepancies (lowering generator $\chi^2$ values by up to 40%, as shown in Table III).
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must examine whether known instrumental variations across the twelve playlists (such as MINOS tracking efficiency or muon energy scales) explain the run-period shifts. If an empirical uncertainty must be retained, its mathematical properties must be cleaned up: its bootstrap noise must be regularized, and its rank-deficient structure must be shown not to distort the inversion of the full $16 \times 16$ covariance matrix.

#### 6. Circularity and Construction of the Model Uncertainty $C_\text{model}$
* **What is wrong/missing:** The model uncertainty matrix $C_\text{model}$ is constructed from the differences in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted to GENIE+MEC, NuWro, and GiBUU. However, in Sec. V B and Table III, the resulting measurement is compared directly against GENIE+MEC, NuWro, and GiBUU using this very covariance matrix. Furthermore, $C_\text{model}$ was constructed from NuWro and GiBUU generated on pure carbon, while the measurement target is hydrocarbon.
* **Why it matters physically:** Using the specific models under test to construct the experimental covariance against which those same models are evaluated introduces logical circularity. As the authors show in Sec. V B, the generator $\chi^2$ values swing wildly depending on the presence and definition of $C_\text{model}$ (e.g., removing $C_\text{model}$ increases the GiBUU $\chi^2$ by +119.0). This prevents any rigorous, unbiased model testing.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors should disentangle the measurement covariance from the external generator predictions. Model-dependence systematic uncertainties should be based on parameterized nuclear and interaction uncertainties (e.g., continuous variations of RPA, 2p2h strength, axial masses, and FSI parameters within a unified generator) or a validated, model-independent bias envelope, rather than discrete two-point differences from the exact generators being evaluated.

#### 7. Flawed External Generator Samples and Target Corrections
* **What is wrong/missing:** NuWro and GiBUU were simulated on carbon and corrected to CH by adding free-proton predictions weighted by 1/13 per nucleon. However, Table III footnotes reveal severe issues with these generator samples:
  1. GiBUU's free-proton sample failed its sampling-closure check, and 46% of its sample (deep-inelastic scattering) *does not conserve energy*.
  2. NuWro's free-proton sample contains an unquantified generator abort rate between 50 and 100 GeV.
  3. Normalizing per nucleon does not account for the absence of QE, 2p2h, and coherent channels on hydrogen, which the authors note causes an 8.3% target difference in GENIE for $W < 1.1$ GeV.
* **Why it matters physically:** Comparing experimental data to generator predictions that violate four-momentum conservation or suffer from unvetted generator crashes is unphysical. Publishing $\chi^2$ values against unphysical theoretical curves undermines the standards of PRD.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must discard the compromised free-proton samples. They must obtain clean, physical generator predictions generated directly on CH with energy-conserving events and proper handling of neutrino interactions on bound and free nucleons.

#### 8. Unacceptable Reconstruction Stability in Reporting Cells
* **What is wrong/missing:** As stated in Sec. VI A.2 and Table II, four of the sixteen reporting cells (R02, R06, R10, R15) exhibit reconstruction stability between 0.16 and 0.25. That is, between 75% and 84% of the signal events reconstructed in these cells were generated in a different cell.
* **Why it matters physically:** When reconstruction stability drops to 16–25%, the measurement in that cell is overwhelmingly dominated by smearing and migration from other regions of phase space. In an unbinned ML unfolding exhibiting prior bias, low-stability cells are precisely where the estimator leans most heavily on the simulation prior. Indeed, it is not a coincidence that cells R02 and R06 are the exact cells that fail the fake-data bias tests with $|z| > 2$.
* **What the authors must do:** The authors must redefine their reporting grid. In standard experimental practice, cell boundaries should be chosen such that stability and purity exceed at least 40–50%. The authors must merge adjacent low-stability boxes to ensure that all reported cells represent physically robust, well-measured kinematic regions.

### 4. Classification of Stated Limitations and Qualifications

Below is the classification of each limitation and qualification stated in the manuscript into:
* **(a) Real deficiency:** Must be resolved with further analysis prior to publication.
* **(b) Acceptable limitation:** Honestly stated limitation that may be published as is in PRD.
* **(c) Detail belonging in companion note:** Belongs in the companion note, not the main paper.

---

1. **Unvalidated coverage of reported uncertainty intervals (Sec. I, IV G, VI A.1)**  
   * **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**  
   * **Reason & Required Analysis:** A scientific measurement without validated uncertainty coverage cannot be interpreted statistically. The authors must execute an ensemble of pseudo-experiments spanning statistical and systematic variations and demonstrate that the reported intervals contain the true value with the claimed frequentist confidence level.

2. **Run-period uncertainty and observed 7% step in $p_\parallel$ (Sec. IV A, IV C, VI A.1)**  
   * **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**  
   * **Reason & Required Analysis:** An unexplained 7% shift across run periods indicates an unmodeled instrumental effect. The authors must analyze beam, detector, and alignment stability across playlists to identify the physical cause and correct it, or provide a regularized, physically justified systematic treatment.

3. **Fake-data bias exceeding pre-declared thresholds ($p < 10^{-27}$, $|z| > 2$) (Sec. I, IV G, Table I)**  
   * **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency (Disqualifying for a physics measurement).**  
   * **Reason & Required Analysis:** Failing pre-declared closure criteria on realistic alternatives demonstrates unacceptable prior bias. The authors must retrain/regularize the estimator or incorporate an appropriate bias uncertainty that restores passing status across all benchmark truths.

4. **The stopped/aborted coverage test (Sec. VI A.1)**  
   * **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**  
   * **Reason & Required Analysis:** Aborting a validation test because the pseudo-experiment uncertainties disagree with the covariance blocks indicates an internal pipeline bug. The authors must debug the pseudo-experiment code, establish why the discrepancy occurred, and complete the test.

5. **Low-stability reporting cells (stability 0.16–0.25 in R02, R06, R10, R15) (Sec. VI A.2, Table II)**  
   * **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**  
   * **Reason & Required Analysis:** Reporting physical cross sections in bins dominated by 75–84% migration violates standard unfolding standards. The authors must merge or re-bin these cells to achieve acceptable stability ($\ge 0.40$–$0.50$).

6. **Target differences and compromised generator predictions (Sec. II, V B, Table III footnotes)**  
   * **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**  
   * **Reason & Required Analysis:** Publishing comparisons against generator samples where 46% of events violate energy conservation or have unquantified aborts is unacceptable. The authors must re-generate NuWro and GiBUU on CH with verified physical integrity.

7. **Definitional departures in $E_\text{avail}$ (135 MeV constant subtraction; kaon/electron omissions) and $W$ (Sec. II, Eq. 1)**  
   * **Classification:** **(b) Acceptable, honestly stated limitation.**  
   * **Reason:** In neutrino physics, truth-level definitions of available energy vary across experiments. The authors provide a precise mathematical formula (Eq. 1), show that substituting the charged pion mass shifts the result by at most $0.19\sigma$ in any cell, and apply the definition consistently to data and models. This is completely acceptable provided Rivet routines or code snippets are provided for model builders.

8. **Instability to floating-point rounding (shifts up to $2.6\sigma$) (Sec. IV F)**  
   * **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**  
   * **Reason & Required Analysis:** Measurements cannot be sensitive to machine-precision floating-point noise. The authors must apply regularization (tree depth, leaf size, subsampling) or ensemble averaging to make the estimator stable within a small fraction of statistical error.

9. **Correlated shape difference in the 2D benchmark ($\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$) (Sec. IV B)**  
   * **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**  
   * **Reason & Required Analysis:** As the primary validation of the method against published data, an unexplained $\chi^2/\text{ndf} = 2.27$ undermines confidence in the 5D results. The authors must conduct a differential audit to identify whether this is caused by efficiency modeling, background subtraction, or iteration limits.

10. **Approximations in 2D covariance (omission of unmatched-event variations and learner dependence) (Sec. IV C)**  
    * **Classification:** **(c) Detail belonging in companion note.**  
    * **Reason:** Because the 2D result serves as an auxiliary validation check rather than the primary reported physical measurement, the detailed approximations of its systematic universe variations are appropriate for documentation in the companion note, provided the main text mentions their omission.

11. **Absence of sample splitting / cross-fitting during classifier training (Sec. III)**  
    * **Classification:** **(a) Real deficiency.**  
    * **Reason & Required Analysis:** Training classifiers on the entire data set and evaluating them on the same events without early stopping or $k$-fold cross-fitting is known in machine learning to cause overfitting and variance underestimation. The authors should implement standard out-of-fold cross-fitting.

12. **Calibrated test limitations (rejection of hybrid nulls conditional on simulation hadronic response) (Sec. VI, VI A.3)**  
    * **Classification:** **(c) Detail belonging in companion note.**  
    * **Reason:** The calibrated tests and their hybrid null definitions are auxiliary hypothesis tests described in detail in Ref. [14]. Stating that they do not constitute physical model exclusions is sufficient for the paper; the technical derivation belongs in the note.

### 5. Referee's Synthesis on Limitations and Publishability

**Should the authors resolve these limitations or move them out of the paper?**  
The authors must **under no circumstances move these limitations out of the paper**. Sweeping known failure modes, unvalidated coverage, and fake-data biases into an inaccessible companion note (Ref. [14]) would be a severe breach of scientific transparency. The authors' honesty is the strongest asset of this manuscript. The correct scientific approach is to **resolve the limitations through further analysis**, not to conceal them.

**Is the measurement publishable in PRD with its current set of limitations?**  
**No.** In its present form, the paper is not publishable as an experimental measurement. Physical Review D publishes measurements that serve as lasting benchmarks for the field. A cross section whose uncertainty intervals have unvalidated coverage, whose estimator fails fake-data closures at $p < 10^{-27}$, and whose numerical values fluctuate by $2.6\sigma$ upon input rounding cannot be used by the community.

**Is any limitation disqualifying?**  
Yes. The following limitations are individually and collectively disqualifying for a physics measurement:
1. **The catastrophic bias in fake-data tests ($p < 10^{-27}$ on GiBUU truth)** proves that the measurement is strongly biased toward the GENIE Tune v1 prior and cannot faithfully measure alternative hadronic dynamics.
2. **The unvalidated coverage and aborted coverage pipeline** mean that the reported uncertainties have no proven statistical meaning.
3. **The floating-point instability ($2.6\sigma$)** indicates an unstable, overfitted machine-learning estimator.

*Alternative path to publication:* If the authors cannot resolve these issues due to fundamental limitations in the open-data sample or OmniFold in five dimensions, the manuscript could be restructured as an **experimental/methodological techniques paper**. In that framing, the paper would not claim to report a new benchmark cross section, but would instead serve as a valuable case study documenting the perils, diagnostic requirements, and regularization challenges of applying unbinned machine learning to high-dimensional neutrino interactions.

### 6. Minor Issues and Presentation

1. **Accessibility and Over-reliance on Ref. [14]:**  
   The paper cites the companion note Ref. [14] more than twenty times for critical information (e.g., three- and four-variable results, coarse grid construction, covariance ensembles, and calibration tests). Ref. [14] is not available to the referee or the reader. The manuscript submitted to PRD must be self-contained in its essential physics, methodology, and definitions.

2. **Choice of Reported Quantity in Table II ($f$ vs. Differential Density):**  
   Table II reports the cell-integrated cross section $f$ (in $10^{-40}\text{ cm}^2/\text{nucleon}$) rather than the multi-differential cross-section density. Because the sixteen cells have vastly different phase-space volumes (some span $W$ from 0 to 1.8 GeV, others from 1.8 to 100 GeV), the raw numbers $f$ cannot be compared physically across cells. The authors should either divide by the multi-dimensional bin volume or explicitly provide the bin widths and volumes in the table.

3. **Figure 1 Presentation:**  
   In Fig. 1 (bottom left), the standardized residual grid is difficult to read. The grey boxes are labeled "not reported" without explaining in the caption why they are excluded. The color/step scale needs a clear legend, and font sizes should be increased for print readability.

4. **Figure 2 Presentation and Obsolete Covariance:**  
   Figure 2 displays recovery residuals, but the caption notes that panel (a) shows a development variant and panel (b) uses a grey band from *"an earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance that fails its specified acceptance criteria."* It is confusing and unhelpful to plot residuals against the error band of an obsolete, failed covariance. The authors should plot residuals against the actual reported covariance.

5. **Figure 3 and Table III Generator Comparisons:**  
   In Fig. 3, error bars show only diagonal uncertainties, whereas the text repeatedly emphasizes that the comparisons are dominated by strong correlations. This creates a misleading visual impression: data and predictions appear to overlap in several cells where the actual $\chi^2$ indicates total incompatibility. A statement should be added to the caption warning the reader that visual inspection of the points does not reflect the correlated $\chi^2$.

6. **Terminology and Clarity:**  
   * The "support rule" ($\ge 1000$ events expected signal and truth weight) is introduced abruptly in Sec. II without explaining its physical motivation (e.g., ensuring sufficient classifier training statistics).
   * In Sec. III, the negative-weight refinement procedure should be summarized with a clear formula rather than only citing Ref. [24], as this step is critical to the background subtraction.

7. **Condition Number and Covariance Inversion:**  
   In Sec. IV B, the authors note that the published 2D covariance has a condition number of $1.5 \times 10^{12}$. What is the condition number of the reported $16 \times 16$ matrix in Table V? Given the presence of a rank-2 run-period block and high correlations (e.g., 0.92 between R10 and R12), the authors should report the condition number and verify that matrix inversion is stable against numerical precision.

8. **Data Availability / Reproducibility:**  
   The Data Availability section mentions that the candidate release package of 16 MB is *"not yet deposited."* For publication in PRD, all supplemental code, covariance matrices, and release packages needed to reproduce the results and plot the figures must be deposited in a permanent public repository (such as Zenodo) with a valid DOI.
