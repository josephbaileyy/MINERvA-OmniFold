We measure the inclusive muon-neutrino charged-current cross section simultaneously in five
observables: muon transverse and longitudinal momenta, available hadronic energy, three-momentum
transfer and hadronic invariant mass. MINERvA medium-energy open data are unfolded with
OmniFold without binning and reported in sixteen cells. The median relative uncertainty per cell is
7.86%, including a run-period contribution estimated after differences between periods were observed.
As a descriptive comparison, three external generator predictions give χ2 values of 107–229 for 16
degrees of freedom; GiBUU gives 48 for 8 degrees of freedom on the eight cells covered by its sample.
The unfolding reproduces the published muon-kinematic cross-section integral within about 1%,
with a correlated shape difference. Fake-data studies at a GiBUU-like truth and at a synthetic
momentum-transfer tilt find residual bias exceeding uncertainty-based criteria (p < 0.01, or any cell
with |z| > 2) that were fixed before the tests were graded. The coverage of the reported uncertainty
intervals has not been validated.
I.

INTRODUCTION

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their
interpretation depends on correcting detector response
and on assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned
migration matrix [1] is widely used, but its response
tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space
through classifier-based density-ratio estimation [2, 3].
This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding
possible without specifying a separate binned response
tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been
applied to collider data, for example to eight observables
in deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [4] and to twenty-four
Z+jets observables at ATLAS [5] (see Ref. [6] for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on
a public simulated T2K near-detector sample [7]. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions [8–11].
We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy
νµ data from the open-data release [12]. The result is a
simultaneous charged-current inclusive cross section in
five observables: the transverse and longitudinal muon
momenta pT and p∥ , the available hadronic energy Eavail ,
the three-momentum transfer q3 , and the hadronic invariant mass W . It is reported in sixteen cells with its
covariance (Sec. V) and compared with five generator
predictions (Sec. V B). We first reproduce the published
double-differential cross section d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) [13] as an

end-to-end test of the unfolding. The three- and fourobservable central values are given in the companion
note [14]. Reconstructed W is fixed by the reconstructed
(pT , p∥ , q3 ), so the five observables are simultaneous but
not independent.
The validation studies assess the result through the
two-dimensional reproduction, recovery of generator-sized
truth changes, a coverage test, and a fake-data study
of estimator bias. They examine whether the unfolding gives reliable multidimensional cross sections when
the simulation mismodels the truth by generator-sized
amounts.
Interval coverage has not been validated. The bias tests,
the fake-data study, and the dependence of the generator
χ2 on the flux correlations are reported for the sixteencell result in Secs. IV G and V B. The findings are those
of one OmniFold implementation on one data set, for
the observables, configurations, and deformations tested,
rather than of multidimensional OmniFold in general.
Section II describes the data, the simulation and the
observables, and Sec. III the unfolding and the reporting
cells. Section IV gives the uncertainties and the validation studies, from the reproduction of the published
two-dimensional measurement to the fake-data tests of
the sixteen-cell result, and Sec. V gives the result and
its comparison with generator predictions. Section VI
discusses what limits the estimator, and Sec. VI A summarizes the principal limitations. The companion analysis
note [14], cited below by section, gives the full validation
record, the construction of the uncertainties, and the tests
with their assumptions.

II.

DATA, SIMULATION, AND OBSERVABLES

The data come from the twelve MINERvA mediumenergy, forward-horn-current run periods (playlists 1A–1G
and 1L–1P), corresponding to 1.057 × 1021 protons on target, with an average neutrino energy near 6 GeV [13].
The input is an earlier production of the MINERvA
open-data release (see Data availability), in the form
of pre-selected analysis ntuples (AnaTuples) [12]. The
detector, including the downstream MINOS near detector that serves as a muon spectrometer, is described in
Ref. [15]. The signal is a νµ charged-current interaction, with any final state, whose true vertex lies in the
tracker fiducial volume (5980 < z < 8422 mm, inside
a hexagon of apothem 850 mm). The signal muon has
θµ < 20◦ , 1.5 < p∥ < 60 GeV/c, and pT ≤ 4.5 GeV/c. Selected simulated events outside this definition, including
νµ charged-current events whose muon lies outside these
limits, are background. The selection requires a reconstructed vertex in the same volume and a reconstructed
muon with θµ < 20◦ matched to a MINOS track of negative charge, together with a dead-time requirement, as
in Ref. [13]. The flux treatment follows Ref. [16]. For
the results in more than two observables, the integrated
flux is Φ = 6.243 × 10−8 cm−2 per proton on target, from
0 to 100 GeV, after the neutrino–electron-scattering constraint and averaged over the twelve playlists by exposure
(Ref. [14], Sec. 3.4). The analysis uses 4.12 million selected
data events. The simulation contains 32.8 million unweighted true signal events and 20.5 million selected simulated events, and it is scaled to the data exposure. The
simulated events are generated with GENIE 2.12.6 [17]
and reweighted with the MINERvA Tune v1, which applies a non-resonant pion reduction, a random-phaseapproximation correction, and an empirical low-recoil
two-particle–two-hole (2p2h) enhancement [18]. Event
selection is held fixed as the classifier inputs and unfolded
observables grow from (pT , p∥ ) to (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ).
Adding features does not add selected events or relax the
signal definition.

14 × 16 muon-momentum bins, crossed with 7 Eavail , 7 q3 ,
and 6 W bins. A coarse 35 grid in (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W )
has its edges at the fine edges nearest the simulated tertiles.

which hold 0.47 % of the expected signal, stay in the

Ref. [14], Sec. 11.1.
III.

CROSS-SECTION EXTRACTION

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The
event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including
unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates
a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth
events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated
flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate
efficiency correction is applied.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin b
carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1−Bb /Db ), where Db
and Bb are the data and predicted background counts in
that bin, so the classifier’s target density is D(x) wpur (b).
The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds
simulated background events with negative weight to the
data, so that the target is D(x) − B(x) pointwise. A
classifier refinement [24], clipped at zero, converts the
signed sample to non-negative weights before the first
iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever
Bb < Db but differ inside a bin wherever the background
fraction varies within it (Ref. [14], App. C).
The results use two estimators. Both use five OmniFold iterations, gradient-boosted decision trees [25, 26]
of nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps, and negative-weight background subtraction
with a refinement classifier at 400 trees and 31 leaves.
The two-observable estimator, LightGBM [26] at a fixed
seed, gives the two-dimensional central value and its covariance (Sec. IV B). This reproducible central value is
that of its own systematic-universe ensemble, so its covariance is built with the same estimator. The three-

and four-observable central values and the sixteen-cell
five-observable result (Sec. V) use the joint-test estimator,
a deterministic LightGBM with the same background
treatment and refinement capacity, named for the note’s
calibrated joint tests of generator predictions, which use
it (Ref. [14], Sec. 11). Its seeds are fixed, so its training is
reproducible, and it also gives the held-out shape tests of
Fig. 2b. This estimator removes most of the bias of puritybased subtraction at nominal truth in the highest-W cells
(Ref. [14], Sec. 9.2). Results from the two estimators are
not combined. The other configurations used below, and
their results, are described in Ref. [14], Sec. 3.2.
The iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the
unfolding. No learner setting, whether capacity, learning
rate, refinement capacity or iteration count, was tuned on
data. The nominal settings are the scikit-learn defaults,
with LightGBM matched to their capacity. The refinement capacity, which the two estimators share, was chosen
in simulation closure studies, and so were the joint-test
estimator’s five iterations (below). The two-observable
estimator uses five iterations following common practice;
this choice was checked on data, against ten iterations
with purity-based subtraction and against the published
result, without a search over the iteration count (Ref. [14],
Sec. 3.2). Two other choices for the two-dimensional result
were made with the comparison to the published result
in view: the learner implementation and the backgroundsubtraction treatment (Sec. IV B). That comparison is
therefore not an independent test of the method. Each
classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it
then sets, so no held-out or cross-fitted split is used; it is
trained without early stopping, and the two classes keep
their physical weights (Ref. [14], Sec. 3.1).
The joint-test estimator keeps five iterations because 10,
15, or 20 iterations did not reduce its model dependence,
measured as the largest residual proxy over the truth
changes used in its development. The comparison and its
values are given in Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4.
A.

Reporting cells

A five-observable cross section with total uncertainties
is reported as the joint-test estimator’s data result on
16 cells, R00–R15. Other reporting definitions of a fiveobservable measurement, with their precision, are studied
in Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4. They come from the 32 boxes formed
by one interior edge per axis, at pT 0.7, p∥ 4.5 and q3
2 GeV/c, and at Eavail 0.8 and W 1.8 GeV. In forming
the cells, each supported box (one that meets the support
rule) with purity, the fraction of the simulated signal
reconstructed in it that was generated in it, below 0.1 is
merged into a neighbour, 4 boxes remain unreported, and
every reported cell passes the support rule. The result’s
covariance is given in Sec. IV A.

IV.

UNCERTAINTIES AND VALIDATION

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the
prior, the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how often intervals contain their target over repeated
experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction together. The closure, deformation, and coverage
studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events
used as pseudo-data, which changes their reconstructed
and true distributions together and keeps the detector
response at fixed truth. Throughout, the prior used to unfold them stays at Tune v1. Every study uses the analysis
simulation’s detector response.
A.

The reported covariance

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is
Ctotal + Cresp + CPL,5D . The term Ctotal sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization
uncertainties of the joint-test estimator and Cmodel ; the
lateral uncertainties shift the reconstructed muon kinematics and beam angle rather than reweighting events
(Appendix A). The model term Cmodel is built from the
change in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted
to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU in turn. Its contributions come from those generators’ samples in their
own definitions, with NuWro and GiBUU on carbon and
GENIE CV as the common reference, not from the predictions compared in Sec. V B. The comparisons there with
GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU therefore use covariance contributions derived from the same generators. It
is a measure of unscaled generator-shape dependence, not
a calibrated bound (Ref. [14], Sec. 10.3). The response
term Cresp is the response of the result to a one-standarddeviation scale of the reconstructed recoil energy in the
simulation. The scale is applied to proton, meson, electromagnetic and other deposits. Cresp does not cover neutron
or muon deposits, nor species, energy-dependence or resolution effects beyond a scale. It enters only this result,
its fake-data tests and its comparison in Sec. V B. The
unfolded results of the three run-period groups, each relative to its own simulation, differ by more than their
bootstrap scatter. The offsets of the twelve playlists differ
between the three period groups by more than expected
from their playlist-to-playlist scatter (Ref. [14], Sec. 10.4).
The cause is not known, and these data cannot establish
which period is correct. The run-period block CPL,5D
includes this inconsistency in the reported covariance. It
is built from the double ratios among the groups, each
group’s unfolded result over its own simulated truth, with
no group chosen as the reference, so it contains the differences by construction. It was added after these differences
were seen, following an audit of its overlap with the other
blocks and of its noise treatment, whose stopping condition was fixed before any of its numbers were read. It
has rank 2 and is not corrected for the bootstrap noise

it contains, and with three groups its size is poorly determined: the 68 % interval on the ratio of its true to its
estimated size is 0.73–2.56. The median per-cell relative
uncertainty is 7.86%, 7.29% without CPL,5D , and 6.74%
without CPL,5D or Cresp . The block raises the median
by about 7.8 %, and the uncertainty of the most affected
cell, R04, by 14.1 %. The fake-data tests (Sec. IV G) and
the generator comparison (Sec. V B) are given both with
and without it; the note gives the per-cell uncertainties
both ways too, with the block’s construction and audit
(Ref. [14], Sec. 10.4).
Every χ2 of this result inverts the full covariance of the
cells compared, 16×16 or 8×8, by Cholesky decomposition
and without truncation, so its number of degrees of freedom is the number of cells (one fewer for the shape-only
χ2 ). Of the blocks, the statistical block is the unbiased
sample covariance of 50 bootstrap replicas, and the flux
block a covariance of 100 weighted universes (effective
number 19), normalized by the sum of the weights. The
others are a 2p2h band, two-point shifts, chosen models,
the normalization, the run-period block, built from three
exposure-weighted periods, and the training-seed block,
which is zero; each comparison with a prediction adds
Vg , the diagonal covariance from the finite size of the
prediction’s own simulated sample. No finite-ensemble
correction is applied. Appendix A lists the systematic
groups and gives the per-group uncertainties and the
correlation matrix of the reported covariance; the note
gives each block’s ensemble and normalization (Ref. [14],
Sec. 10.3).
B.

Two-dimensional benchmark

unfolding against a measurement from the same data. Its
integrated cross section is 3.069×10−38 cm2 /nucleon, compared with 3.039 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published

D.

Normalization and closure

Summed over each of the four (pT , p∥ ) quadrants, the
sixteen-cell result agrees with the two-dimensional result:
the sums differ by −1.5 to +0.7 per cent, and with the
covariance between the two results evaluated from the
systematic variations they share, the four differences give
χ2 ≤ 1.25 for four degrees of freedom (p ≥ 0.87), with
no quadrant beyond one standard deviation. The statistical correlation, and that of four systematic terms
that could not be evaluated directly, are bounded within
a one-parameter family of valid covariances, each such
cross term taken as a common fraction, from zero to one,
of its fully correlated value, over which the agreement
holds. Of the joint-test estimator’s other results, only
the three-observable marginal has been compared with
the two-dimensional result, agreeing with it to 0.141 % in

area-weighted total over all 205 reported cells (Ref. [14],
Sec. 10.2). The normalization checks of the earlier puritysubtracted five-observable central value are described in
Ref. [14], Sec. 8.1, and the closure checks of its estimator
in three and four observables in Ref. [14], Sec. 5.1.
Closure tests with backgrounds included, comparing
purity-based with unbinned negative-weight subtraction,
are given in Ref. [14], Sec. 9.2 and Ref. [14], App. C.6.
E.

Generator-sized truth changes

Figure 2 shows that the unfolding recovers only part of
a change of the Eavail shape as large as the difference between generators. When the true shape is reweighted by
the GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio, the (Eavail , W ) cells unfolded
with a development variant, used while the joint-test estimator was developed, with a 100-tree, 8-leaf refinement,
not the reported estimator, deviate from it by several
percent in the largest cells and by up to 74 % at high W ,
in the cell 0.8 ≤ Eavail < 1.5 GeV, W ≥ 3 GeV. These
deviations occur with or without background, so background subtraction alone does not explain the residual.
In a noise-free comparison in simulation, an exact binned
iterative unfolding of the same development truth changes
leaves about the same median residual at five iterations;
longer exact iteration removes much of the residual of the
GiBUU-to-GENIE change (its median falls from 8.3 %
at five iterations to 3.3 % at 200) but not of a NuWro
(pT , p∥ , Eavail ) shape or a q3 stress test. The residual is
therefore consistent with finite-iteration regularization for
the GiBUU-like change, but not for every change tested,
and no single cause is established (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.3). For
other fixed generator shapes tested with the joint-test estimator, it is 16 % to 31 % in the most affected (Eavail , W )
cell and lies within the quoted total uncertainty in about
half of those cells. Those uncertainties come from an
earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance that
fails its specified acceptance criteria (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.6).
For the sixteen-cell result, Sec. IV G reports the fake-data
tests of the estimator’s bias. The deformation studies, the
iteration and capacity tests, the exact binned comparison
with its scope, and the joint-test-estimator shapes with
the seed sets of their pseudo-experiments are given in
Ref. [14], Secs. 9.2 and 9.3.
Statistical intervals of an earlier five-observable estimator with purity-based subtraction, corrected for its
closure bias at nominal truth, miscover at the tested
truths, most strongly when the true signal rate is lowered by about 10 %, with coverage as low as 0.06 for 68%
intervals (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.5).
F.

Sensitivity to input rounding

The joint-test estimator is unstable under floating-point
rounding of its inputs: over 20 rounding-level perturbations of the data, the reported quantities scatter by a

median of 0.23 % per joint cell, against a median databootstrap statistical uncertainty of 0.37 %, and each bootstrap replica carries this scatter at the same size. It limits
how exactly the central values can be reproduced, and
a single perturbation moves the most affected quantity
further. Rounding-level changes of its inputs move its
results by up to 2.6 statistical standard deviations, and
those of the lower-capacity development variant by up
to 2.5, so the estimator fails a data-stability requirement
of at most 0.3 (median) and 1.0 (maximum) statistical
standard deviations.

A.

Limitations

Apart from the validation of the uncertainties and
the low stability of four of the sixteen cells (below), the
limitations of these results are stated with the results they
qualify, in Secs. III–V, and those of the calibrated tests
in the companion note. The principal ones are these.
1. No reported interval has validated coverage. The
fake-data tests of Sec. IV G assess bias, not coverage:
only event counts fluctuate, with systematic parameters fixed, so interval widths cannot be tested. A
coverage test at two of their truths, declared in
advance for the covariance without the run-period
block, with pseudo-experiments that also draw the
flux, interaction, and detector uncertainties, was
stopped by one of its preliminary checks before
any coverage pseudo-experiment ran: the uncertainties reproduced through its pseudo-experiments
disagreed with the covariance blocks they draw, for
reasons not established (Ref. [14], Sec. 10.5). Neither the coverage of Cmodel at truths held out of its
construction nor the size of either run-period block
is independently validated (Ref. [14], Secs. 10.3,

10.4 and 4.5). The two-dimensional covariance has
not been independently validated and gives no calibrated significance for generator comparisons; an
earlier statistical band of that result undercovered
in its tails in a coverage test at fixed truth (Ref. [14],
Sec. 5.2), and the coverage of the current statistical block and of the total covariance has not been
tested. The coverage tests cited are pointwise, for
each reported quantity separately, not the stronger
simultaneous coverage of all of them. We make
no coverage claim on independent simulated samples or simultaneously over all reported bins and
projections.
2. Four of the sixteen cells, R02, R06, R10 and R15,
have a reconstruction stability of 0.16–0.25: of the
simulated signal events generated in such a cell and
reconstructed, only that fraction is reconstructed in
the same cell.
3. The note’s calibrated tests reject hybrid nulls, conditionally on the simulation’s hadronic response.
However, a rejection extends to the generator prediction itself only if the residual below the fine grid,
where each null takes the simulation’s event composition, is bounded, and no such bound has been
shown. Their calibration is valid to the extent that
the simulated chain reproduces the analysis of the
data (Ref. [14], Sec. 11.4). They are not physical
exclusions of the generators, and they show no evidence of additional discrimination beyond matched
coarse projections.

Appendix A: Uncertainty tables

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result
(Sec. IV A) is a sum of blocks, grouped as follows.
• Statistical : the bootstrap of the data, 50 replicas.
• Flux : 100 hadron-production (PPFX) flux universes,
weighted by the neutrino–electron and inverse-muondecay constraint (effective number 19).
• Interaction: the 33 interaction-model bands of the
MINERvA systematic set, which reweight simulated
events.
• Detector : the 4 detector-model bands, which
reweight simulated events: the MINOS muonmatching efficiency and the GEANT4 neutron, pion
and proton reweights.
• Lateral : the 5 bands that shift the reconstructed
kinematics of each simulated event rather than
reweighting it: the beam angle in two directions, the
muon momentum resolution, and the muon energy
scales in MINERvA and in MINOS.

• Normalization: 1.40 % in every cell.
• Cmodel , Cresp and CPL,5D , as defined in Sec. IV A.
The statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral and normalization groups, with a training-seed block that is zero
in every cell, form Ce , and Ctotal = Ce + Cmodel . Table IV
gives each group’s relative uncertainty in each cell, and
Table V the correlation matrix of the reported covariance.
With the cross sections and the marginal uncertainties
of Table II, the correlation matrix gives the reported covariance to the printed precision; the covariance is also
provided in the Supplemental Material.
