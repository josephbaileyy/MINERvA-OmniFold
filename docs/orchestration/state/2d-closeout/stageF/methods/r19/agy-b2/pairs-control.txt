Q6.
BEFORE: A diagnostic in simulation, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:binned}, bears on the first of these results. On the same \jtNcells{} joint cells and the fixed truth changes used to develop the estimator, an exact binned iterative unfolding with the same simulation and five iterations leaves about the same median residual as the boosted-tree estimator. Of the 144 cell-and-truth-change residuals above \SI{2}{\percent} whose diagnosis is stable under the truth resolution, pooled over five truth changes, the tree approximation dominates only 15. Most of the 144 (121) lie in quantities that the binned response constrains weakly at the analysis exposure, with Cram\'er--Rao widths above \SI{10}{\percent}, so the regularization trades a large variance for the bias. Longer noise-free exact iteration removes much of the residual for the GiBUU-to-GENIE change, but not for every change. The long runs do not converge, so this rests on their last iterates. The diagnostic is binned, signal-only and noise-free, with the detector response fixed at the simulation's. It covers only the residuals whose diagnosis is stable under the truth resolution (\SI{38}{\percent} of the joint-cell residuals above \SI{2}{\percent}). What unbinned information could recover, the coverage failure, and the validity of any interval lie outside its scope. Within that scope, the residual is consistent with the regularization of the inverse problem at this binning and prior, rather than with the tree approximation of the classifiers (\nsec{sec:val-departure}).

Q7.
BEFORE: Statistical intervals from a deterministic LightGBM estimator with purity-based subtraction, corrected for the estimator's closure bias at nominal truth, miscover at the tested truths, most strongly when the true signal rate is lowered by about \SI{10}{\percent}. The end-to-end test unfolds 400 pseudo-experiments per truth. The tested change reweights the true signal, without renormalization and with the background unchanged, and is in effect a \SI{10}{\percent} reduction of the signal rate with a weak $\Eavail$ slope. Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} shows that under that change, 51 of the 153 reported cell and projection integrals have a 68\% coverage whose upper confidence bound lies below the acceptance threshold of 0.66, with coverage as low as 0.06. A diagnosis that fits a Gaussian bias and spread to each quantity's 68\% and 95\% coverage attributes the miscoverage to a residual bias that grows with $W$ and survives the nominal-truth bias correction. The source of this residual bias has not been established. The rate change itself is recovered, and, apart from a minor effect, the intervals are not too narrow. Agreement in the marginal normalizations thus coexists with substantial residual bias under the tested deformations. The interval construction and the diagnosis are described in \nsec{sec:coverage-5d}.
