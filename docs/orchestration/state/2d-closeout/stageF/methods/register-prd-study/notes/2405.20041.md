- **Where limitations live.**
  - **Uncertainties paragraph.** The method's limitations appear as named uncertainty components there.
  - **Validation paragraph.** The validation results are given as p values, with the worst case named.
  - **Supplement.** Restrictions on use live in the supplement's "Usage recommendations", written as imperative guidance to users.
  - **Abstract and conclusion.** Neither contains a limitation.
  - **Introduction.** The weaknesses of traditional unfolding are listed there as the motivation: Q[2405.20041]: "This analysis presents developments that address four features of traditional unfolding techniques that potentially limit the future utility of the published data." (l.235, intro)
- **Model dependence, regularization or unfolding bias.** Two named uncertainty components, "unfolding prior" and "hidden variable", each defined operationally. The Letter reports that these dominate for some observables and does not hedge them. The iteration count is a one-line design fact.
  - Q[2405.20041]: "Imperfections inherent to the choice of Monte Carlo simulation contribute to two sources of systematic uncertainty: the sensitivity to differences in the underlying truth distribution of the measured observables, and the sensitivity to mismodelling of detector effects by the Monte Carlo generator that are not captured by the chosen input variables." (l.369, uncertainties)
  - Q[2405.20041]: "The unfolding uncertainty from the unfolding prior and hidden variables tends to be the dominant contributor for many observables, in particular for the ten jet substructure variables (see Figure N(d))." (l.382, uncertainties)
  - Q[2405.20041]: "The number of iterations was fixed to five following a dedicated study that found unfolding performance plateaued around that number." (l.333, analysis)

- **Patterns worth copying.**
  1. Turn a limitation into a user rule with a threshold, placed where users act ("Each bin should contain a minimum of 5,000 effective events", l.657), not into a caveat on each result.
  2. Report a marginal validation as a number inside the summary sentence ("except for …, with a p-value of 0.038", l.475), with no hedge words.
  3. Name the model-dependence terms as uncertainty components and say plainly where they dominate (l.382).
