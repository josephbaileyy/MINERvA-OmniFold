BEFORE:
This section describes the joint five-dimensional tests of generator predictions. The tests evaluate hybrid null hypotheses about five predictions (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-design}). They are not a measured cross section, add no reportable uncertainty, and do not validate the five-axis covariance used elsewhere in this note (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-adopted}), which they do not use. The section gives the design, the six conditions of the declared model in the words of the test specification (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}), the decisions, the recovery of the lost pseudo-experiments and the later sensitivity studies with their numbers. Reference~\cite{JointTestRecord2026} gives the specification and the decisions, and each later study has its own reference, cited where it is described.

=== PASSAGE JT-2 (sec_joint.tex) ===
BEFORE:
For each of five predictions $G$ (the MINERvA Tune~v1, GENIE~2.12.10 without and with MEC, NuWro~21.09 and GiBUU~2019; \S\ref{sec:eavailw-band}), the null $H_{0}(G)$ is that the true cross section equals $G$ on every cell of a fine five-dimensional grid. Below that grid, the null truth has the MINERvA Tune~v1 simulation's event composition, varied in each pseudo-experiment by the drawn interaction-model universes with every fine-cell integral held fixed, and the background is the Tune~v1 simulation's, with its uncertainty bands. This \emph{hybrid null} is the hypothesis the calibration simulates; for the Tune~v1, the analysis simulation itself, no external prediction is combined with the Tune~v1 composition. Three requirements are distinct. The hybrid null is defined exactly on the finite grid, and no convergence is needed to define it. Whether it represents the physical generator prediction depends on the composition below the fine grid and on convergence of that grid (condition~(i), \S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}). Whether the calibration reproduces the analysis of the data is calibration fidelity (conditions~(ii)--(iv)). Each prediction is tested in total (rate and shape together) and in shape alone: \jtNtests{} tests. The fine grid is the analysis binning of \S\ref{sec:method-binning}, $14\times16\times7\times7\times6$ bins in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$; the test cells below are its integrals.

The tests are evaluated on a coarse $3^{5}$ grid in $(\pT,\pPar,\Eavail,q_{3},W)$ with edges $\pT\in[0,0.55,0.85,4.5]\,\si{GeV/c}$, $\pPar\in[1.5,3.5,6,60]\,\si{GeV/c}$, $\Eavail\in[0,0.4,1.5,100]\,\si{GeV}$, $q_{3}\in[0,1.2,2,100]\,\si{GeV}$ and $W\in[0,1.4,2.2,100]\,\si{GeV}$. Each inner edge is the fine-grid edge nearest a tertile of the weighted simulated truth distribution of the signal sample in that coordinate, taken from the development simulation, so the cells keep physical bin widths. The tests are restricted to the \jtNcells{} supported cells (J); for GiBUU, whose sample lacks high-energy neutrinos, the domain is the \jtNcellsGiBUU{} cells with $\pPar<\SI{6}{GeV/c}$. The grid specifies a finite hybrid hypothesis and a reporting statistic; it does not turn OmniFold's event-level training into binned unfolding.

Two statistics are formed from the unfolded cell values $F$ and the prediction $\mu_{G}$: a total (rate-and-shape) statistic $T_{\mathrm{tot}}=r^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}r$ with $r=F-\mu_{G}$, and a shape statistic $T_{\mathrm{sh}}$ of the same form on the normalized cell fractions, with the normalization Jacobian applied to $\Sigma$ and one cell dropped. The metric $\Sigma=V+\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ adds the prediction's own finite-sample variance to $V$, a Ledoit--Wolf-shrunk covariance~\cite{LedoitWolf:2004} of \jtVn{} pilot pseudo-experiments at the Tune~v1 null. The metric defines how hypotheses are compared and so affects power; the null calibration below, not the metric, sets each test's size.

The prediction variance is diagonal because each simulated event of a prediction falls in one cell. The pilot's seeds are disjoint from those of the calibration, but it reuses the same simulated events and omitted the MINOS-efficiency band. The diagonal of $V$ matches the calibration's null variances (median ratio 1.00), but the shrinkage (intensity 0.015) floors about 80 of the 109 nearly degenerate correlation directions, so at the Tune~v1 null the statistic has a whitened trace of about 47 rather than 109. Since $V$ was fixed before the calibration, this affects power only (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-power}); the power at other shrinkage intensities has not been measured.

BEFORE:
The null distribution of each statistic is obtained from pseudo-experiments that run the full analysis chain at the null truth, using the joint-test estimator (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}: deterministic LightGBM OmniFold, five iterations, negative-weight background treatment with a 400-tree, 31-leaf refinement classifier). Each pseudo-experiment \begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt] \item draws Poisson pseudo-data from one half of the simulation together with an independent background source, refits the background-refinement classifier, and unfolds with the estimator trained on the other half; \item draws one of \jtFluxUniverses{} flux universes and every one of the \jtModelBands{} interaction-model bands at one of its universes; \item draws the weight-based detector bands (MINOS muon-matching efficiency and the GEANT4 neutron, pion and proton interaction reweights) and the five muon and beam-angle lateral bands through a linear surrogate; \item draws a \SI{1.4}{\percent} normalization, the data's rounding noise (taken from 20 real-data re-unfolds with rounding-level input changes), and the prediction's finite-sample residual. \end{itemize} The data are unfolded once, with the full simulation. The Monte Carlo $p$ value of a test is $(k+1)/(B+1)$, where $k$ of $B$ null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic; it is never zero. The \emph{claim} $p$ value is the largest over declared variants that move the null by an upper bound on the simulation-size difference between the pseudo-experiments and the data unfold (condition~(iv) below) and by $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ (condition~(i)). The external nulls are strongly non-central, with non-centralities $\lambda$ of \jtLambdaMin{}--\jtLambdaMax{} in these units, because the calibration reproduces the estimator's pull toward its prior (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}); each observed statistic is compared with that displaced distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:joint5d-nulls}).

\paragraph{Generative model of one pseudo-experiment.} Each calibration pseudo-experiment splits the simulation at random into two halves (Table~\ref{tab:joint5d-genmodel}) and refits the background refinement. Its truth equals $G$ on every fine cell that the source half populates; cells it leaves empty lower a coarse cell by at most \SI{0.2}{\percent}, or about \SI{4}{\percent} of the null standard deviation (the per-experiment spread is below \SI{1.5}{\percent} of it). On the signal, each interaction-model band is renormalized within each fine truth cell, so it moves only the composition within the cell, and with it the efficiency and the migrations. The flux universe and the band universes are drawn independently, and their weight ratios multiply. The muon-energy, muon-resolution, and beam-angle lateral bands enter as $\sum_{b}z_{b}\Delta_{b}$, with $\Delta_{b}$ from endpoint unfoldings of the data; the detector weight bands include the GEANT4 hadron-interaction reweights. Because the $\Delta_{b}$ come from the observed data, the null distributions are conditional on these data-derived inputs, which would differ in a repeated experiment; that conditioning has not been validated.

BEFORE:
The ten tests are carried out with the Holm procedure~\cite{Holm:1979} at familywise level $\alpha=\jtAlpha$. A determinacy requirement also applies: a step rejects only when the 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval~\cite{Clopper:1934} of $k/B$ lies entirely below its Holm threshold. Calibration proceeded in batches of \jtBatch{} pseudo-experiments per null, to at most \jtBcap{}, under a sequential rule that stopped a null once both of its decisions were determined. A null stopped when a 99.5\% Clopper--Pearson interval of $k/B$ lay entirely on one side of each of its thresholds and met a fixed precision requirement. The decision rule uses the upper end of the two-sided 95\% interval, a one-sided 97.5\% upper bound. If each true null's calibrated tail probability is uniformly distributed or conservative (A1), and the pseudo-experiments' exceedances are independent given the data (A2), the probability that this upper end falls below the threshold at any number of pseudo-experiments up to \jtBcap{} is below the threshold, whatever rule determines when sampling stops, including the budget extensions; the decision rule therefore controls the familywise error rate of the ten decisions, the probability of falsely rejecting at least one true hypothesis among them, at 5\% under these assumptions, with the bound $\mathrm{FWER}\le0.8902\,\alpha<0.9\,\alpha$ derived in \S\ref{sec:joint5d-fwer}. A2 requires the lost pseudo-experiments to have been lost independently of their outcomes, which has not been shown, although none of them reaches the observed statistic when rerun (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-lost}). The calibrated size is averaged over the nuisance priors: at a fixed true value of the nuisance parameters it can exceed $\alpha$.

The joint-test estimator (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-calibration}) was chosen before the design was fixed. The hypotheses, statistics, variants, multiplicity rule, stopping rule and every input were fixed before any production pseudo-experiment or observed statistic, with two data-informed elements. The design's reviewers had seen descriptive per-cell residuals of the data before it was fixed. During production, the calibration's monitoring output showed observed claim $p$ values, and a bounded extension of the production budget was decided while they were visible, so that the sequential rule, rather than the budget, could set the stop. No statistic, variant, threshold or stopping rule was changed, and the familywise bound above holds whatever rule determined when sampling stopped. An independent recomputation of every $p$ value and decision from the stored products, with separately written code, agrees with the production evaluation on all \jtRecompRows{} compared rows; it verifies the calculation, not the adequacy of the calibration. The calibration is valid for the simulated null to the extent that the pseudo-experiment chain reproduces the analysis applied to the data; no rejection rate has been measured for null pseudo-data processed like the data, which would require an independent simulation sample.

Three studies followed the decisions and are for reporting only (outside the decision rule); each was specified before it ran: the scan of the shift multiplier (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-kappa}), the rerun of the lost pseudo-experiments (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-lost}), and the matched coarse-projection comparison (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-projections}), which was specified after the five-dimensional outcomes were known.

BEFORE:
\paragraph{Numerical convergence.} (i) Below the fine grid the null truth has the Tune~v1 composition of the hypothesis definition (varied by the drawn interaction-model universes, with every fine-cell integral fixed), and the fine-grid convergence check has not shown convergence. In the $L_{2}$ norm of the per-cell relative changes, the last refinement, from a twofold-merged grid to the fine grid, is \jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax{} of the whole change from the coarse test grid to the fine grid, so in that norm the steps are not shown to shrink and the residual below the fine grid is unmeasured and potentially material. The design takes the step, in a proxy of the test metric, to shrink by a factor of \jtMoneWnormMin--\jtMoneWnormMax{} per halving; this factor comes from the review of the design, and its derivation is not given. If it kept shrinking geometrically at that rate, the residual would be about one to three times the last step, $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ (the change of the noise-free unfolded result when the null truth is refined from the twofold-merged grid to the fine grid). This sets the claim variants at $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$, the central extrapolated estimate, and the robustness check, for reporting only, at $\pm\jtMoneKappaRobust\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. The extrapolation assumes geometric convergence; the measured $L_{2}$ ratios also allow slower rates and larger residuals. Measured before any observed statistic, the claim variants move the external nulls by \jtMoneClaimSdMin--\jtMoneClaimSdMax{} null standard deviations and the robustness variants by \jtMoneRobSdMin--\jtMoneRobSdMax{}. For the hybrid null as simulated the residual plays no role; the variants carry the rejections over to $G$ itself only if replacing the Tune~v1's shape below the fine grid by $G$'s moves the noise-free unfolded result by no more than $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ along $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. (vi) The predictions' own finite samples enter the metric as $\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ and the simulated residuals as a drawn term.

\paragraph{Calibration validity.} The calibration's validity, and what the independent recomputation verifies, are stated in \S\ref{sec:joint5d-frozen}. Two known departures are bounded. (iv) The pseudo-experiments unfold with half of the simulation and the data with all of it; the resulting shift, at most about 0.07 null standard deviations but not always consistent with zero, enters as the bias-aligned upper-bound variant. That shift was measured for the mean only, in a process without noise or nuisance draws. A separate check reran the first 200 calibration pseudo-experiments of the NuWro, GENIE~2.12.10, and GENIE~2.12.10 + MEC nulls with a quarter of the simulation. It resolved no change of the null distributions' location, width, or tails between half and quarter simulation (medians within 0.074 null standard deviations, widths within 0.975--1.010 of the half-simulation values), only a mean paired shift of 0.006--0.034 null standard deviations. Under an assumed extrapolation from half to full simulation that is conservative by design, all three shape decisions, and so all ten, remain rejected. The check concerns the simulation size only; it is not a test of the data process. Every pseudo-experiment resamples the same simulated events, so the null distributions are conditional on that simulation. The decisions closest to their boundary are the shape tests of NuWro and GENIE~2.12.10: their observed statistics exceed the value at which the decision would change by 1.11 and 1.30 null standard deviations, with the Holm order held fixed (1.01 and 0.67 with the robustness variants at $\pm\jtMoneKappaRobust\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$); the other eight tests lie 2.7 to 203 null standard deviations from it (2.0 to 203 with the robustness variants). Crossing that value would make a test undetermined, its Clopper--Pearson interval straddling the threshold, rather than not rejected. The data unfold's rounding-level nondeterminism, measured over the 20 re-unfolds, leaves every observed $p$ value unchanged except NuWro's shape test (2/1752 to 3/1752).

\paragraph{Physical-model adequacy.} (ii) The detector model is the simulation's, varied only by the drawn detector bands and the linear surrogate for the lateral bands; the surrogate has been checked only for the symmetry of its endpoints. The analysis has no band for the calorimetric recoil-energy response; \S\ref{sec:joint5d-response} gives the consequences. (iii) The interaction-model prior is the analysis's \jtModelBands-band set, every band drawn in every pseudo-experiment; negative weights in two of them (\texttt{LowQ2\_1}, \texttt{HighQ2\_1}) are clipped per universe, at most 0.35\% of a band's shift. (v) GiBUU lacks $E_{\nu}>\SI{20}{GeV}$, and its out-of-domain truth enters the migrations into its domain; for all predictions $E_{\nu}>\SI{100}{GeV}$ is absent, which is negligible. The use of the Tune~v1 composition below the fine grid in (i) is also a modelling choice of this kind.
