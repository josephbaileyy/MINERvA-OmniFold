\subsection{Closure: the one injected $\Eavail$ deformation is recovered} \label{sec:3d-closure} A reweighting closure tests the 3D unfolding on one injected deformation. It tests configuration~(a) only. The configuration-(a) unfolding recovers a known $+\SI{30}{\percent}$ Gaussian bump injected into pseudo-data in \emph{truth} $\Eavail$ (center \SI{0.3}{GeV}, width \SI{0.15}{GeV}). The recovered step-2 mean weight is $1.048$ against the injected $1.0485$. Residuals against reweighted truth on the $\Eavail$-marginal have median $0.9999$ and standard deviation \textbf{\num{0.0006}} (\SI{0.06}{\percent}); the per-3D-bin residual standard deviation is \SI{3.2}{\percent} from MC statistics in sparse high-recoil corners. The closure's marginal scatter is far below the $\SI{3.7}{\percent}$ data-versus-2D scatter, but the two belong to different configurations (the closure to (a), the data comparison to (e) against (f)), so their ratio is not quoted. Total normalization matches to better than \SI{1}{\percent}. In the metric of \S\ref{sec:validation} ($\Eavail$ projection) the residual is \SI{1.84}{\percent} vs \SI{18.1}{\percent} injected (ratio $0.102$). No acceptance threshold fixed before the test is on record, so this residual is not graded.

The test covers one shape, this Gaussian, injected into the pseudo-data and unfolded \emph{with the same detector response that the unfolding uses}. On this injection the configuration-(a) estimator neither manufactures nor destroys the feature. That does not extend to configuration~(e), and the data-versus-2D scatter compares (e) with (f), so the scatter is not read here as data\,$\leftrightarrow$\,MC structure resolved by the available-energy axis. The test does \emph{not} exclude estimator bias for other deformations and does not test response mismatch, and the hidden-variable closure exceeds its threshold at the largest amplitude (\S\ref{sec:validation}, App.~\ref{app:response-mismatch}).

\paragraph{The tested pion-FSI variations do not account for it.} Reweighting 2M central-value GENIE events with GENIE's \texttt{grwght1p} changes $d\sigma/d\Eavail$ by less than a percent for both tested pion-FSI dials. \texttt{FrInel\_pi} shifts the spectrum by $\le\SI{0.74}{\percent}$ (peak in $0.10$--$\SI{0.20}{GeV}$) and \texttt{FrAbs\_pi} by $\le\SI{0.82}{\percent}$ (peak in the lowest bin). Both are ratios within the unrepaired GENIE sample (\S\ref{sec:3d-models}), in which the flux defect cancels to first order, and neither was recomputed. The integrated rate moves only $\pm\SI{0.02}{}$--$\SI{0.03}{\percent}$ at $\pm1\sigma$. \footnote{\texttt{FrInel\_pi} is disabled in the standard MAT-MINERvA set of systematics. This is MINERvA's current practice (private communication from the collaboration, 2026), and the reason is a degeneracy (of overlapping pion-FSI dials, one must be dropped), not a defect of that dial.} A dial that moves the spectrum by less than \SI{1}{\percent} cannot account for an excess of \SIrange{8}{36}{\percent} per bin over the GENIE central value below $\Eavail=\SI{0.4}{GeV}$, so the tested variations do not account for the excess. This conclusion is limited to these dials and their range; other transport models or proton-FSI effects are not excluded. Recent MINERvA quasielastic-like measurements point toward overestimated proton and charged-pion FSI in their model comparisons~\cite{MINERvA:2026qeleme}. Their different signal definition prevents directly identifying that effect with our inclusive discrepancy.

\paragraph{Comparison with a missing 2p2h component.} The base GENIE central value uses the default event-generator list and contains \emph{no} meson-exchange-current (2p2h/MEC) events (none of the $1.48\times10^{6}$ CC events of the generated sample is a MEC event; the sample's unweighted modes, before the flux reweight of \S\ref{sec:3d-models}, are QE \SI{11}{\percent}, RES \SI{22}{\percent}, DIS \SI{66}{\percent}, COH \SI{0.9}{\percent}; the 50--\SI{100}{GeV} supplement is MEC-free as well). 2p2h must therefore be \emph{added}; reweighting cannot supply it. The mode decomposition of central-value $d\sigma/d\Eavail$ (per-bin mode count-fractions $\times$ bin value), overlaid with data, locates the largest positive gaps in the quasielastic--$\Delta$ dip, $[0.10,0.20)$ and $[0.20,0.40)$, while central values are much closer in the QE-dominated $[0,0.10)$ and $[0.40,1.50)$ regions. At central value, \SI{69}{\percent} of the positive deficit lies at $\Eavail\le\SI{0.4}{GeV}$, where 2p2h events populate. Closing the integrated deficit requires 2p2h at $\approx\SI{45}{\percent}$ of the QE rate (\SIrange{68}{71}{\percent} in the two dip bins), the standard magnitude of the empirical and Valencia MEC models, compared with sub-percent FSI shifts (Fig.~\ref{fig:modedecomp}).

\paragraph{GENIE with Valencia 2p2h enabled.} We regenerated the GENIE central value with Valencia 2p2h enabled (\texttt{--event-generator-list Default+CCMEC}, 2M events). The Nieves--Simo--Vacas MEC channel is present in the splines but absent from the total-CC cross-section graph written by GENIE's \texttt{gspl2root}, so we normalize by the non-MEC CC count: QE$+$RES$+$DIS$+$COH is fixed to the known total, and MEC is added through $1/(1-f_{\mathrm{MEC}})$ with $f_{\mathrm{MEC}}=\SI{2.87}{\percent}$. 2p2h falls in the dip and brings all three low-$\Eavail$ bins toward data (Fig.~\ref{fig:mec}), closing, in central values, \SI{52}{\percent} of the data$-$CV gap at $\Eavail\le\SI{0.4}{GeV}$ and \SI{63}{\percent} of the integrated deficit (CV $-\SI{7.2}{\percent}\to-\SI{2.7}{\percent}$). The prediction still falls short in the dip, especially $[0.10,0.20)$, but its integral over the reported $\Eavail$ range is closer to data than Tune~v1 ($-\SI{2.7}{\percent}$ against $-\SI{9.5}{\percent}$). Tune v1 layers MINERvA's empirical low-recoil 2p2h \emph{enhancement} and RPA on top of the stock Valencia MEC. The central values are consistent with the known need for that enhancement, and with NuWro and GiBUU (native, un-enhanced 2p2h) sitting low in the dip as well, but they do not establish it.

\paragraph{Bin-identical comparison to the published low-recoil measurement.} Supplemental data from Ref.~\cite{MINERvA:2022incl} (44-cell $d^{2}\sigma/(d\Eavail\,dq_{3})$ with its full covariance, distributed inside the public arXiv source package) permit a direct quantitative cross-check. Merging both measurements onto their maximal common grid --- edges present in \emph{both} binnings --- and restricting our $(\pT,\pPar)$ marginalization to $\pPar<\SI{20}{GeV/c}$ to mirror the Ascencio muon selection ($\theta_\mu<20^{\circ}$, $1.5<p_\mu<\SI{20}{GeV}$) gives two super-cells, $\Eavail<\SI{0.4}{GeV}$ in $q_{3}\in[0.4,0.6)$ and $[0.6,1.2)\,\si{GeV}$: the low-recoil 2p2h region. Our central result lies \SI{9.2}{\percent} and \SI{6.0}{\percent} above Ascencio. No pulls or full-covariance $\chi^{2}$ are reported (no corrected 4D covariance, above; Fig.~\ref{fig:ascencio}). Both share MINERvA flux/detector systematics, treated here as independent. The $p_\mu$ and $\pPar$ cuts differ by $\lesssim\SI{6}{\percent}$ kinematic smearing at the \SI{20}{GeV} boundary. The two analyses also define true $\Eavail$ differently (\S\ref{sec:eavail-definition}): in this analysis's simulation, Ascencio's broader definition moves \SI{11.0}{\percent} of the population of our lowest true-$\Eavail$ bin ($\Eavail<\SI{0.1}{GeV}$) out of it. That shift has the sign and location of the residual. The two common cells, however, span our three lowest bins ($\Eavail<\SI{0.4}{GeV}$), whose aggregate shift has not been computed, so the definition difference remains an alternative explanation that is not excluded. At the \SI{10}{\percent} scale of the residual, the comparison neither refutes nor validates the result. A finer comparison on all 44 cells remains future work; it requires re-unfolding on the Ascencio edges, which is straightforward for the unbinned method, \emph{and} a systematic sweep on that binning.
