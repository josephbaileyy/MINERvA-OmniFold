BEFORE:
\paragraph{Ruterbories 2021, Section V.} Three uncertainty categories --- flux, detector response, neutrino-interaction model --- evaluated by ``re-extracting the cross section using modified simulations'', i.e.\ the universe method, but with \emph{no covariance formula given}. Specifics quoted: flux below $4\%$ across most of phase space thanks to the $\nu$-$e$-scattering constraint (Valencia, arXiv:1906.00111); $1.4\%$ target-nucleon normalization; in-situ test-beam hadron response with per-particle GEANT inelastic variations; muon energy scale $1\%$ constrained by the low-recoil fit of Bashyal (JINST 16 P08068), with the explicit statement that \emph{``the fit causes the flux and muon energy scale uncertainties to be correlated and that correlation is propagated to the final result''} --- the only non-block-diagonal piece the paper admits. The only covariance in its equations is $V$ in the model-test $\chi^{2}$ (Eq.~1, ``the measurement covariance matrix''), with $205$ degrees of freedom (Table I), plus a log-normal $\chi^{2}$ variant motivated by the highly-correlated flux normalization (Peelle's Pertinent Puzzle). \emph{What the paper does not say:} no per-band covariance construction, no $\pm1\sigma$ pair formula, no band-independence/block-sum statement; nothing about rank, condition number, or regularization of $V$; no bootstrap or coverage validation (the paper uses D'Agostini iterative unfolding). All of this is delegated to MAT or simply unstated.

\paragraph{MAT per band and in total.} \texttt{MnvVertErrorBand::CalcCovMx} implements Eq.~\eqref{eq:matcov}, referenced to the universe mean $\langle x\rangle_{u}$ rather than $X_{\CV}$, normalized by biased $1/N$ rather than Bessel's $1/(N-1)$ (a $1\%$ offset for PPFX, $N=100$), with no special case for $N=2$. The folk ``MINERvA-101 $\pm1\sigma$ pair'' formula $\tfrac12(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{+}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{+}\T + \boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-}\T)$ agrees only for CV-symmetric shifts. The quoted universe block, filled into a \texttt{PlotUtils::MnvH1D} with MAT's universe weights on Flux, agrees with \texttt{MnvH1D::GetTotalErrorMatrix} to $1.3\times10^{-16}$ of its largest element; the quoted Flux band carries MAT's $\nu$--$e$ and inverse-muon-decay constraint weights. An earlier check compared \texttt{MnvH1D::GetTotalErrorMatrix} with our band sum without the normalization band (\texttt{-{}-add-norm 0}), over $44$ bands / $187$ universes, and gave a maximum difference of $5.5\times10^{-17}$ of the largest element (machine $\varepsilon$) and $\sqrt{\Tr\mathbf{C}}$ matching to seven digits. It filled the \texttt{MnvH1D} without MAT's per-universe weights (\texttt{SetUnivWgts}), so both sides were unweighted, and it ran on an older sweep from before the flux fix, not on a quoted product, so it could not detect that the Flux band omitted those constraint weights.

\paragraph{External statistical floor of the \emph{quoted} combined $\chi^{2}$.} The quoted $\chiCombined$ inverts the \emph{sum} of the paper's covariance and ours, not our blocks alone. That sum contains the paper's \texttt{StatOnlyCov}, which has rank $205$ and is near-diagonal (\S\ref{sec:rank}), so it places a variance floor under \emph{every} eigendirection of the combined matrix. It is the larger of the two statistical contributions: our statistical block is $0.407$ of it in \emph{square-root} trace, and the square of that factor in \emph{trace} (operands in \S\ref{sec:rank}). The power has to be named because the two ratios describe the same comparison and differ by more than a factor of two. An external, published block, rather than any ensemble of ours, therefore sets the small-eigenvalue directions on which a finite-$N$ debiasing factor would act. This bounds the quoted number's exposure to declaration~(v) of \S\ref{sec:nd-declarations}.
