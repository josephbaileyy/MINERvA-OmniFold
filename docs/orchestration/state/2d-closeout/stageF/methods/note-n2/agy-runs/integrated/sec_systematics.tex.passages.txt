\subsection{Systematic universes} \label{sec:uq-universes} Detector and model systematics use the July universe sweep: MAT-MINERvA's 44 error bands and 187 universes, identical to the published analysis's \texttt{GetStandardSystematics} set. Each universe is re-unfolded with LightGBM at a fixed seed (seed~42), against a reference central-value unfold with the same estimator and seed, which is the quoted 2D central value. The five lateral bands are taken from re-unfolds of re-selected universes (event loops that re-apply the selection to each universe's shifted kinematics; \S\ref{sec:uq-higherdim}) at the same seed. The band sum drops Rvp1pi, which encodes the same shift as Rvn1pi (\S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}), and holds 43 bands plus the flat normalization. Per-band covariances follow MAT's universe-mean centering and biased $1/N$. For a $\pm1\sigma$ pair, the covariance is the half-difference outer product, excluding any common endpoint displacement from CV. The 100 PPFX universes use the same mean-centered $1/N$ formula.

\subsection{ML/optimization uncertainty: training-seed variation} \label{sec:uq-ml} The ML block is the sample covariance, over five training-seed settings, of the mean of 20 bootstrap replicas at each setting; each setting is a triplet $(s,s{+}1,s{+}2)$ of seeds for the step-1 classifier, step-2 classifier and miss regressor, with $s\in\{42,45,48,51,54\}$, and the block has rank~4 (\S\ref{sec:seedscan}). It is the smallest block, with median \SI{0.048}{\percent} per bin. The block measures LightGBM seed variation, with the bootstrap\,$\times$\,seed interaction at its $1/20$ share. With four degrees of freedom, its variance has a Gaussian relative standard error of about \SI{71}{\percent}. Its per-bin $\sigma$ is a median 0.27 of that of the earlier ten-seed block, because the bootstrap\,$\times$\,seed interaction, which dominated the earlier scan, is already inside the fixed-seed statistical block; this block carries it only at its $1/20$ share, a median 0.763 of its diagonal. The interaction itself (a median 0.072 of the statistical variance) and the universe\,$\times$\,retraining term (\S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}) are reported and not added.

The dependence on the learner is in no block. The central value and the exact-split cross-check, which differ in learner and in background treatment, differ by a per-bin median of \SI{1.03}{\percent} (maximum \SI{17.6}{\percent}; \S\ref{sec:method-config}), and the mean of the earlier ten-seed LightGBM scan (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, superseded values: retired material) differs from the cross-check by a median of \SI{0.99}{\percent} per bin. One reseeded exact-split run differs from the unseeded one by a per-bin median of \SI{0.18}{\percent} (maximum \SI{1.6}{\percent}); the spread of the exact-split estimator over seeds is unmeasured (\S\ref{sec:method-config}).

\subsection{Further checks of the 2D covariance} \label{sec:uq-2d-construction} The products of the July sweep, which the quoted universe block uses, do not record a checksum of the driver code that produced them, and that driver version was not committed. Seven of its universes, four of them also checked in an earlier pilot, are reproduced to $\le1.4\times10^{-11}$ by the committed driver; the others are accepted by inference, because the same code produced them.

The block does not identify which period is correct. Its interpretation is limited by the small number of period groups, by possible shrinkage from the regularization, and by the statistical and training noise it retains. Because the block is built from the observed differences among the periods, it contains them automatically; this says nothing about its coverage, that is, about whether its size is right. For the same reason, the block's own double-ratio $\chi^{2}$ against any total that contains it is bounded whatever the data, so it is not a test. The raw 1G--1M $\pPar$ $\chi^{2}$, 34.8 against the other blocks alone and 2.59 with the block added, is a sensitivity reading only.

One systematic category provided in the input products, \texttt{NormDISCC}, is identically zero; we carry it as zero, which we have not been able to confirm is intended. No separate uncertainty is assigned to the counting of protons on target: the NuMI flux prediction carries a \SI{2}{\percent} counting term by convention, so a separate band would risk counting it twice. Two links in that argument, that the flux universes carry the term and that the neutrino--electron constraint absorbs it, are inferred, not verified. The twelve playlists are combined with one global exposure ratio rather than per-period ratios, and the resulting bias is not corrected. At reconstruction level the pooled result is high, by \SI{0.18}{\percent} in total and by at most \SI{0.32}{\percent} in a marginal bin. After unfolding, the pooled result is \SI{0.076}{\percent} above the exposure-weighted combination of the period results on the integral (the same sign as at reconstruction level), and differs from it by at most \SI{0.39}{\percent} in a marginal bin (the highest $\pPar$ bin; at most \SI{0.33}{\percent} in $\pT$); in single cells the combination differs from the pooled result by \SIrange{-1.83}{3.78}{\percent}, each with $|z|$ at most 0.31.

\subsection{What changes for the higher-dimensional products} \label{sec:uq-higherdim} Two event-loop modes supply the lateral bands. The single-pass event loop, which writes every universe in one pass, stores each lateral universe's shifted observables only for events that pass the selection at the central value, so it omits events that the shift moves into the selection. Re-selected event loops re-run the selection with one lateral universe active at a time, so they include these migrations. For the five kinematic bands (BeamAngleX/Y, MuonResolution, and Muon\_Energy\_MINERvA/MINOS), the re-selected event loops supply the lateral-band block of the five-axis covariance used in this note; higher-dimensional products built from the single-pass shifted observables are not used for any quoted uncertainty. Replacing the single-pass lateral-band block by the re-selected one moves that block's square-root trace by \SI{\latTraceMove}{\percent}. That is a change in a square-root trace and is \emph{not} a bound on any individual bin: across the \latBins\ reported bins the per-bin ratio of re-selected to single-pass standard deviation runs \latRatioMin\ to \latRatioMax, and within the top \SI{1}{\percent} of bins by single-pass variance --- which carry \SI{\latTopVarShare}{\percent} of the total --- it runs \latRatioTopMin\ to \latRatioTopMax. A small share of the aggregate variance does not make a bin irrelevant to a quoted result.
