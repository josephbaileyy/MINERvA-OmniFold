BEFORE:
\paragraph{Prior OmniFold applications.} Over the last few years OmniFold has moved from a method paper to production measurements. H1 published the first real-data applications in deep-inelastic scattering: lepton--jet correlations unfolded simultaneously in eight dimensions~\cite{H1:2021wkz}, followed by jet substructure at high $Q^{2}$~\cite{H1:2023fzk}. LHCb unfolded identified-hadron distributions in $Z$-tagged jets~\cite{LHCb:2022rky}. ATLAS performed a simultaneous unbinned measurement of twenty-four $Z+$jets observables~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets}. Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} collects these and other applications and distills cross-experiment practice; three of the practices it audits appear in our uncertainty budget: the ensemble-mean cross-check of the central value, the bootstrap statistical block, and the unified-throw covariance check of \S\ref{sec:uq-combine}.

H1 cut backgrounds to a negligible level; LHCb corrected per track before unfolding. ATLAS left its less-than-\SI{0.25}{\percent} background in place and assigned it as an uncertainty, while identifying negatively-weighted simulated events as the natural in-method subtraction~\cite{ATLAS:2024jets,Andreassen:2021zzk}; Ref.~\cite{Canelli:2025guide} recommends that route for nontrivial backgrounds. Recent methodological work explicitly treats backgrounds, fakes, acceptance, and inefficiency in OmniFold-HI~\cite{Falcao:2025backgrounds} and generative unfolding~\cite{Butter:2025genfold}. These constructions address related problems; their subtraction prescriptions can differ.

Closest to this analysis is the study of Huang \emph{et al.}~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold}, the first application of OmniFold to a neutrino cross section: a mock-data unfolding of muon kinematics on the public T2K near-detector simulated dataset, benchmarked against traditional binned approaches. Two of its findings bear on neutrino applications: its iteration count, which we do not adopt (\S\ref{sec:method-config}), and an ensemble of reweighting trials with different train/test splits that controls the ML stochastic component, which we budget explicitly as the training-seed variation band (\S\ref{sec:systematics}). The present work differs in unfolding \emph{real} MINERvA data to an absolutely normalized cross section, with a 2D uncertainty budget whose blocks are all built with the central value's own learner (\S\ref{sec:method-config}), in using GBDT learners suited to a low-dimensional tabular feature set, and in validating the method directly against a published binned measurement of the same data (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Scope.} The results hold for one implementation of unbinned OmniFold, applied to one data set, and for the configurations, observables and deformations tested. The two-dimensional result has an uncertainty budget whose coverage has not been tested (\S\ref{sec:uq-boot}, \S\ref{sec:uq-2d-scope}, \S\ref{sec:coverage}); above two dimensions the results are central values without intervals of validated coverage (\S\S\ref{sec:3d}--\ref{sec:5d}). The generator tests of \S\ref{sec:joint5d} evaluate hybrid null hypotheses and add no reportable uncertainty. Point-cloud (PET) unfolding of the full event is future work, under development and documented separately (App.~\ref{sec:pet}); no result in this note depends on it.
