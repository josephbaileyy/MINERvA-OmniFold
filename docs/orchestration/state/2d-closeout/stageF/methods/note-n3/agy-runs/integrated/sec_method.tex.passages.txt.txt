BEFORE:
The unfolding code supports interchangeable learners (component terminology and code paths in App.~\ref{app:codebase}). The quoted 2D central value and all of its uncertainty ensembles (the systematic-universe sweep, the Poisson bootstrap, the training-seed block and the run-period unfolds) use LightGBM with $100$ trees, $8$ leaves and learning rate $0.1$, with the same settings for the step-1 classifier, the step-2 classifier and the miss regressor. They use refined negative-weight background subtraction (App.~\ref{app:negweight}), whose refinement classifier has 400 trees and 31 leaves and takes the estimator seed plus three as its seed. This is configuration~(f) below. The central value is the universe sweep's own central value at seed~42, so the systematic block shares its estimator and seed (\S\ref{sec:uq-universes}). The statistical bootstrap also uses seed~42, and the training-seed block uses five seed settings that include it (\S\ref{sec:uq-boot}, \S\ref{sec:uq-ml}). The bootstrap replica mean differs from the central value by a per-bin median of 4.37 standard errors of the mean. A central unfold at seed~45 differs from the seed-42 one by at most \SI{1.4}{\percent} in a cell. With the purity-based subtraction (configuration~(a)), the seed-42 central value differs from the seed-1 one by a per-bin median of \SI{0.18}{\percent} (maximum \SI{1.79}{\percent}). That is 0.30 (maximum 1.28) of the per-bin standard deviation of the earlier statistical band, which was built at seed~1. Both of those central values are regenerated to $\lesssim2\times10^{-11}$ by runs with one driver and one input that differ only in the seed. The difference is therefore attributable to the seed, as inferred from the two regenerations. The quoted central value records its run configuration; it repeats to $9\times10^{-12}$ and equals the refined 2D data unfold of App.~\ref{sec:negweight-footing} to $7\times10^{-12}$. The purity-subtracted seed-42 central value has no recorded run configuration. An independent rerun reproduces it to $1.4\times10^{-11}$, and so does a regeneration whose run configuration is recorded (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, \recentry{2d-central-value}). Against the ten purity-subtracted LightGBM central values at seeds 1--10 it is mildly over-dispersed (RMS standardized difference 1.28, maximum 4.25). Seed~42 was chosen on computing cost: of the two costed ways to give the central value and the statistical band a common seed, building the statistical band at seed~42 was the cheaper. The quoted statistical block is a seed-42 band drawn in three independent streams (data, signal and background simulation; \S\ref{sec:uq-boot}).

\paragraph{The estimator configurations.} Six estimator configurations, labeled (a)--(f) as in the article (Table~\ref{tab:estimators-matrix}), are used, and each result names the one it uses. All use five OmniFold iterations and gradient-boosted trees of nominal capacity (100 trees, 8 leaves) in both OmniFold steps; ``deterministic'' means fixed seeds and reproducible training. Results from different configurations are not combined. Four are described in this section: \begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt] \item (f), the nominal LightGBM configuration above with refined negative-weight subtraction and the refinement classifier of (e), seeded at the estimator seed plus three: the 2D central value (seed~42) and its uncertainty ensembles (\S\ref{sec:results}); \item (a), the same learner with purity-based background subtraction (\S\ref{sec:method-algo}): the five-dimensional central value (\S\ref{sec:eavailw}), the 3D and 4D injected-bump closures and Figs.~\ref{fig:eavail-marg} and~\ref{fig:q3excess} (\S\ref{sec:3d}), and the purity-subtracted 2D comparisons of this section and \S\ref{sec:results-tension}; \item (e), the joint-test estimator: a deterministic LightGBM variant with the unbinned negative-weight background treatment (App.~\ref{app:negweight}), whose refinement classifier has 400 trees and 31 leaves. The negative-weight treatment reduces the purity method's nominal-truth bias in the highest-$W$ column at least fifteen-fold (from \SIrange{3.5}{4.1}{\percent} to \SIrange{0.11}{0.24}{\percent}, the level of the no-background closure), and the larger refinement classifier removes most of the background-related bias that remains (\S\S\ref{sec:val-negweight}--\ref{sec:val-departure}). It gives the 3D and 4D central values (\S\ref{sec:3d}). It is used for the generator tests (\S\ref{sec:joint5d}), the study of generator shapes held out of its development (\S\ref{sec:val-departure}) and the precision study (\S\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}). It was selected before the joint-test design was fixed (\S\ref{sec:joint5d}); \item (b), the exact-split cross-check above. \end{itemize} Configuration (c), the deterministic variant with purity-based subtraction, and (d), the same variant with negative weights and a nominal-capacity refinement, are used in the background-bias, generator-sized-change and coverage studies listed in the table. In five dimensions, the central value and its covariance use purity-based subtraction: they were produced before the purity bias was found and the refinement was developed. A refined five-dimensional data unfold exists (configuration~(e)), but it is not the quoted central value, and no covariance has been built for it. The 2D central value and its covariance were rebuilt with configuration~(f) after a two-dimensional closure test with backgrounds found the purity bias (App.~\ref{sec:negweight-footing}). Studies in this note labeled \emph{exact-split} use the exact-split central value, which was the quoted 2D central value until 2026-10-11; they have not been recomputed. The 3D and 4D central values have been regenerated with configuration~(e); no 3D or 4D covariance is quoted.

=== PASSAGE ME-3 (sec_method.tex) ===
BEFORE:
\paragraph{Hyperparameter choice.} The learner settings were fixed \emph{a priori}; no hyperparameter search was performed on data, which avoids tuning on the result. They are the scikit-learn \texttt{GradientBoosting} defaults: 100 trees, learning rate $0.1$, and depth-3 capacity. The LightGBM learner of the central value and its ensembles uses matched capacity ($\texttt{num\_leaves}=8=2^{3}$), giving every learner the same deliberately modest capacity, one of OmniFold's regularizers (\S\ref{sec:method-algo}). The configuration is instead validated after the fact by closure and classifier-calibration tests (\S\ref{sec:validation}, \S\ref{sec:3d-classifier}), insensitivity to doubling the iteration count (below), backend agreement in method-dependence studies (\S\ref{sec:results-tension}), and an explicit training-seed variation band for residual ML stochasticity (\S\ref{sec:systematics}).

Production uses $n=5$ iterations. Huang \emph{et al.}~\cite{Huang:2025omnifold} select 45 iterations for OmniFold-family methods (20 for their binned benchmark) using a binned $\chi^{2}$ against known mock-data truth, a criterion they note is infeasible on real data. We do not adopt that recommendation. Their mock data injects a large prior--data \emph{shape} discrepancy through an RPA-modified quasielastic model, requiring many iterations. Our prior already describes the data shapes well (\S\ref{sec:controlplots}); the data pull is dominantly a normalization. Cross-experiment production also typically uses ${\sim}5$ iterations~\cite{Canelli:2025guide}. Our $n=5$ is instead justified empirically: doubling it to $n=10$ moves the total cross section by \SI{0.026}{\percent} (\S\ref{sec:validation}), and the unfold reproduces an independently published binned measurement of the same data (\S\ref{sec:results}).
