BEFORE:
This section describes the joint five-dimensional tests of generator predictions. The tests evaluate hybrid null hypotheses about five predictions (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-design}). They are not a measured cross section, and they add no reportable uncertainty. They do not use, and do not validate, the five-axis covariance used elsewhere in this note (\S\ref{sec:eavailw-adopted}). The section gives the design and the six conditions of the declared model in the words of the test specification (\S\ref{sec:joint5d-conditions}). It then gives the decisions, the recovery of the lost pseudo-experiments and the later sensitivity studies, with their numbers. Reference~\cite{JointTestRecord2026} gives the specification and the decisions. Each later study has its own reference, cited where it is described.

The hypotheses, statistics, variants, multiplicity rule, stopping rule and every input were fixed before any production pseudo-experiment or observed statistic, with two data-informed elements. The design's reviewers had seen descriptive per-cell residuals of the data before it was fixed. During production, the calibration's monitoring output showed observed claim $p$ values. A bounded extension of the production budget was decided while they were visible, so that the sequential rule, rather than the budget, could set the stop. No statistic, variant, threshold or stopping rule was changed, and the familywise bound above holds whatever rule determined when sampling stopped. An independent recomputation, with separately written code, of every $p$ value and decision from the stored products agrees with the production evaluation on all \jtRecompRows{} compared rows. It verifies the calculation, not the adequacy of the calibration. The calibration is valid for the simulated null to the extent that the pseudo-experiment chain reproduces the analysis applied to the data. No rejection rate has been measured for null pseudo-data processed like the data, which would require an independent simulation sample.

\paragraph{Numerical convergence.} (i) Below the fine grid the null truth has the Tune~v1 composition of the hypothesis definition, varied by the drawn interaction-model universes with every fine-cell integral fixed. The fine-grid convergence check has not shown convergence. In the $L_{2}$ norm of the per-cell relative changes, the last refinement, from a twofold-merged grid to the fine grid, is \jtMoneMin--\jtMoneMax{} of the whole change from the coarse test grid to the fine grid. In that norm the steps are therefore not shown to shrink, and the residual below the fine grid is unmeasured and potentially material. The design takes the step, in a proxy of the test metric, to shrink by a factor of \jtMoneWnormMin--\jtMoneWnormMax{} per halving. This factor comes from the review of the design, and its derivation is not given. If it kept shrinking geometrically at that rate, the residual would be about one to three times the last step, $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. Here $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ is the change of the noise-free unfolded result when the null truth is refined from the twofold-merged grid to the fine grid. That estimate sets the claim variants at $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$, the central extrapolated estimate. It also sets the robustness check, for reporting only, at $\pm\jtMoneKappaRobust\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. The extrapolation assumes geometric convergence. The measured $L_{2}$ ratios also allow slower rates and larger residuals. Measured before any observed statistic, the claim variants move the external nulls by \jtMoneClaimSdMin--\jtMoneClaimSdMax{} null standard deviations and the robustness variants by \jtMoneRobSdMin--\jtMoneRobSdMax{}. For the hybrid null as simulated, the residual plays no role. The variants carry the rejections over to $G$ itself only if replacing the Tune~v1's shape below the fine grid by $G$'s moves the noise-free unfolded result by no more than $\pm\jtMoneKappa\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ along $\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. (vi) The predictions' own finite samples enter the metric as $\mathrm{diag}\,\mathrm{Var}(\mu_{G})$ and the simulated residuals as a drawn term.

\paragraph{Calibration validity.} The calibration's validity, and what the independent recomputation verifies, are stated in \S\ref{sec:joint5d-frozen}. Two known departures are bounded. (iv) The pseudo-experiments unfold with half of the simulation and the data with all of it. The resulting shift is at most about 0.07 null standard deviations, but it is not always consistent with zero. It enters as the bias-aligned upper-bound variant. That shift was measured for the mean only, in a process without noise or nuisance draws. A separate check reran the first 200 calibration pseudo-experiments of the NuWro, GENIE~2.12.10, and GENIE~2.12.10 + MEC nulls with a quarter of the simulation. Between half and quarter simulation, it resolved no change of the null distributions' location, width, or tails: the medians agree within 0.074 null standard deviations, and the widths lie within 0.975--1.010 of the half-simulation values. The only change it resolved is a mean paired shift of 0.006--0.034 null standard deviations. Under an assumed extrapolation from half to full simulation that is conservative by design, all three shape decisions, and so all ten, remain rejected. The check concerns the simulation size only. It is not a test of the data process. Every pseudo-experiment resamples the same simulated events, so the null distributions are conditional on that simulation. The decisions closest to their boundary are the shape tests of NuWro and GENIE~2.12.10. With the Holm order held fixed, their observed statistics exceed the value at which the decision would change by 1.11 and 1.30 null standard deviations, and by 1.01 and 0.67 with the robustness variants at $\pm\jtMoneKappaRobust\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$. The other eight tests lie 2.7 to 203 null standard deviations from that value, and 2.0 to 203 with the robustness variants. Crossing that value would make a test undetermined, its Clopper--Pearson interval straddling the threshold, rather than not rejected. The data unfold's rounding-level nondeterminism, measured over the 20 re-unfolds, leaves every observed $p$ value unchanged except NuWro's shape test (2/1752 to 3/1752).

The lost seeds were rerun, for reporting only, under a procedure that was fixed, and reviewed independently, before the rerun~\cite{JointTestRecovery2026}. A determinism check fixed in advance (16 of 16 completed seeds rerun bitwise identically) shows that each recovered draw is the lost draw, so every submitted draw has been observed. No recovered draw reaches the observed statistic in any test, claim variant or robustness variant, so no assignment of the lost draws changes any decision. The closest margin is 4.6 units of $T$ below the observed value of 868.6 (GENIE~2.12.10 + MEC shape, $\pm\jtMoneKappaRobust\,\delta_{\mathrm{fine}}$ variant). The next is 49.8 (GENIE~2.12.10 shape, same variant). With complete batches the predeclared rule would have stopped the Tune~v1, GENIE~2.12.10, GENIE~2.12.10 + MEC and GiBUU nulls one batch earlier ($B=1200$), with the same decisions.

The scope of the recovery's checks is narrower than that of the main recomputation: \begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt] \item its independent cross-check (856 of 856 quantities) covers only the reading with the variant shifts of the primary evaluation; \item the reading in which the recovered draws are added to the calibration, with the shift recomputed on all draws, was reproduced only by a standalone rerun of the same code, a consistency check rather than an independent recomputation; \item the earlier-stop decisions rest on the calibration run's own re-application of the sequential rule, with no independent check. \end{itemize} The recovery is for reporting only: the primary decisions are those of the evaluation before recovery, at the $B$ at which its rule stopped. With every submitted draw observed and none reaching the observed statistic, the ten rejections hold, with the closest margin and the earlier stops given above. The finding of the sensitivity analysis, that every decision could change under some assignment of the lost draws, is superseded by their observed outcomes.

The projected tests do not enter the Holm family and change no decision of Table~\ref{tab:joint5d}. The comparison and its criterion were specified after the five-dimensional outcomes were known but before the comparison was run. No test meets it, and the values are the same in both readings. Table~\ref{tab:joint5d-projections} gives every projected claim $p$ value. Hadronic information therefore participates in that rejection. This does not establish added power of the joint test over all matched projections, or accept GENIE as true. The powers of the joint and projected tests have not been compared.

=== PASSAGE JT-7 (sec_joint.tex) ===
BEFORE:
\paragraph{Generation checks.} The GiBUU re-evaluation is reported under the label ``B3 (sampling closure) failed on hydrogen; a post-hoc bias check of its premise passed at $\le0.1\,\sigma_{c}$''. The sampling-closure check failed on the hydrogen sample. A post hoc bias check of that check's premise, declared before it was computed, met its criterion of at most $0.1\,\sigma_{c}$ in every cell, moving the CH prediction by at most $5.1\times10^{-4}\,\sigma_{c}$. Here $\sigma_{c}$ is the standard deviation of the cell's result over NuWro's null distribution used for the decisions ($B=1751$). The GiBUU re-evaluation has a further limitation: GiBUU's deep-inelastic events on hydrogen, 46\% of its hydrogen sample, do not conserve energy. They lose a median of \SI{45}{MeV}, with a median transverse-momentum imbalance of \SI{31}{MeV}, while its other hydrogen events balance exactly. The cause has not been identified. The available energy of those events may be low. It may also affect GiBUU's carbon prediction, which cannot be checked in this way. In NuWro's hydrogen generation, four chunks aborted and were rerun on alternate seeds. The fraction of events this excludes is estimated as $1.8\times10^{-5}$ in the main sample and $3.8\times10^{-5}$ in the 50--\SI{100}{GeV} supplement, or $1.9\times10^{-5}$ and $6.2\times10^{-5}$ including the first attempt. These estimates rest on four to six events. The supplement's 95\% upper bound of $1.8\times10^{-4}$ exceeds the $10^{-4}$ level set for it, so the supplement is flagged rather than assumed negligible.
