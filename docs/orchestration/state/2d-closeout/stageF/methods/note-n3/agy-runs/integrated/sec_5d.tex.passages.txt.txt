BEFORE:
\paragraph{Comparison with exact binned unfolding.} A diagnostic in simulation compares the boosted-tree residual with that of an exact binned iterative unfolding. It uses the 109 J cells, noise-free signal-only pseudo-data, and the fixed historical truth deformations D1, W1, W3 and the $q_3$ stress (labels as in \S\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence}), pooled with W2. W2's boosted-tree comparator includes background, and the outcome is unchanged without it. The binned unfolding unfolds the same simulated events, with their exact efficiency and the response binned in the 10,499 occupied five-dimensional reconstructed cells, starting from the nominal prior. At five iterations the J-cell medians of the absolute residual agree: \SI{9.5}{\percent}, \SI{5.4}{\percent}, \SI{5.6}{\percent} and \SI{4.3}{\percent} for the boosted trees, against \SI{8.3}{\percent}, \SI{5.4}{\percent}, \SI{4.9}{\percent} and \SI{5.3}{\percent} for the exact binned unfolding, for D1, W1, W3 and the $q_3$ stress. Of the 144 eligible residuals, pooled over the deformations, 15 are dominated by the tree approximation, 2 of them in identified quantities, a share of 0.014. Most of the 144 (121) lie in quantities that the binned response constrains weakly at the analysis exposure. Their Cram\'er--Rao widths exceed \SI{10}{\percent} at both truth resolutions tested (median \SI{31}{\percent} at the coarser, the J cells themselves), so the regularization trades a large variance for the bias. Longer noise-free exact iteration removes much of the residual for D1 (J median \SI{3.3}{\percent} after 200 iterations and \SI{0.8}{\percent} after $10^{5}$) and part of it for W1 (\SI{3.3}{\percent} and \SI{2.8}{\percent}). It does not do so for W3 and the $q_3$ stress (\SI{4.4}{\percent} and \SI{6.6}{\percent} after 200 iterations, \SI{8.9}{\percent} and \SI{6.7}{\percent} after $10^{5}$), whose within-cell binning bias grows with the iteration number. The long runs do not converge (only 9--34 of the 109 J quantities converge for each traced deformation), so these values are last iterates. The 144 eligible residuals are those whose diagnosis is stable under the truth resolution: of 376 J-cell and deformation combinations with residuals above \SI{2}{\percent}, 232 changed classification with the truth resolution or the response weighting and were excluded, leaving the 144 (\SI{38}{\percent}). The coarser muon projection retained only three cases and is uninformative. Within this comparison, the residual is consistent with the regularization of the inverse problem at this binning and prior, and the tree approximation is not supported as its main cause. The comparison is binned, signal-only and noise-free; it fixes the detector response at the simulation's, uses the development truths, and covers these 144 residuals only. What unbinned or biased estimators could recover, the coverage failure of \S\ref{sec:coverage-5d} and the validity of any interval lie outside the diagnostic's scope. The diagnostic establishes no impossibility, and the precision thresholds it proposes are not acceptance criteria for any result in this note. The exclusions, definitions and numerical verification are in the binned response diagnostic record (Ref.~\cite{NoteRecords2026}, \recentry{response-diagnostic}).

The refinement classifier underfits, which causes the residual background bias (App.~\ref{app:negweight}). A candidate with raised refinement capacity passes the nominal background-inclusive closure on 40 fresh pseudo-experiments (largest deviation 2.7 standard errors) and a pooled calibration test of its statistical intervals (pull width 1.02). The pooled test does not establish calibration for every functional. The candidate fails the data-stability test, whose criterion was set before it was run: input perturbations within floating-point rounding move functionals by a median of 0.8 and at most 2.6 statistical standard deviations. The baseline estimator's shifts, 0.8 and 2.5, place this instability in the estimator family, and it explains the difference on data between the driver and the development pipeline, at most \SI{1.2}{\percent}. The data bootstrap, which refits everything per replica, already contains this spread: each replica carries the same spread (median \SI{0.23}{\percent} per joint cell), about \SI{37}{\percent} of the bootstrap variance and below a tenth of any total uncertainty. Central values are reproducible only to this size. The paired perturbation study of \S\ref{sec:gbdt-model-dependence} tests this containment for this candidate only.
