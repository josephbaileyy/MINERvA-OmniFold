We evaluate this implementation as a case study of multidimensional unfolding
and of inference with a biased estimator.  The question is whether it gives
reliable multidimensional cross sections when the simulation mismodels the
truth by generator-sized amounts, and what validation is needed before its
outputs can be interpreted.  Two properties must be distinguished.  The
coverage of unfolded intervals is the rate at which they contain the true cross
section.  The calibration of a test is the rate at which it rejects a true
hypothesis.  Calibration can be obtained without validated intervals, by
simulating the whole analysis chain at the hypothesis so that the estimator's
bias enters the simulated null distribution, to the extent that the simulated
chain reproduces the analysis of the data.

and their intervals can be validated.  These validation studies are the paper's

\subsection{Statistical uncertainty: Poisson bootstrap}
\label{sec:uq-boot}
The statistical block jointly bootstraps data and simulation with 300 per-event
Poisson(1) weight replicas, each fully re-unfolded with the LightGBM estimator at the
central value's training seed (seed~42), and is their sample covariance about the
replica mean (Fig.~\ref{fig:bootstrap}). The central value and its statistical block
are therefore matched in estimator and seed. Each replica draws its weights in three
independent streams: data, signal simulation and background simulation. Statistical
fluctuations of the background simulation are thus drawn independently of those of the
signal simulation; possible shared events between the two were not assessed. A
separable background-only term (a median 0.26 of the statistical $\sigma$) is
contained in the statistical block and reported separately. MC reco and
truth weights share a \emph{single} event
