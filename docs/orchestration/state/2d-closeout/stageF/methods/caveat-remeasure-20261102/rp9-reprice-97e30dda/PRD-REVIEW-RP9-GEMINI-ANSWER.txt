### 1. Caveat Density vs. Scientific Substance

As a PRD editor and referee, I do not count "caveats per thousand words," and no referee report will ever cite that metric. What referees react to is tone and focus: does the manuscript read like a confident physics measurement, or does it read like a defensive quality-assurance audit? 

A density of 9–10 is not objectionable because of the arithmetic; it is objectionable because it signals an exhausting, defensive posture that obscures the physics. What matters is the **content**:
* Referees care deeply if coverage is unvalidated, if closure bias exceeds uncertainties at plausible truths, or if run periods disagree. 
* Referees do not care about internal project-management history, aborted validation scripts, or minor generator quirks.

The target of $\le 6$ is an arbitrary, counterproductive proxy (Goodhart’s Law). As the study demonstrated, moving sections barely shifts the ratio because words and caveats leave together. Chasing $\le 6$ is actively harming the manuscript by encouraging you to cut essential physics benchmarks while preserving defensive disclaimers.
