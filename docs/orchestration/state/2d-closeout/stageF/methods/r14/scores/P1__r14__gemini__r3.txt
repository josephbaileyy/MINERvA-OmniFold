RATING: 2.5
JUSTIFICATION: While grammatically articulate and technically precise, the manuscript frequently departs from PRD register by adopting the defensive, record-keeping tone of an internal collaboration analysis note—overburdened by developmental history, internal audit trails, and preemptive disclaimers that overshadow direct scientific exposition.

1. QUOTE: "On 100 closure pseudo-experiments at a fixed truth, a coverage test specified in advance found an earlier statistical band, distinct from the quoted block, undercovering in its tails: pooled over experiments and bins, 91.2 % of standardized residuals fell within two standard deviations, against 95.4 % nominal (Ref. [14], Sec. 5.2). The band rebuilt after that test, also distinct from the quoted block, reuses in its simulated part the resampling mechanism of those pseudo-experiments, so a post hoc rescoring of them against it is not an independent coverage test; that rescoring removes the tail undercoverage: 97.7 % fall within two standard deviations and 78.1 % within one."
   REWRITE: In closure tests with pseudo-data at nominal truth, evaluating coverage across all bins yields 97.7% of standardized residuals within two standard deviations (compared to the nominal 95.4%) and 78.1% within one standard deviation (nominal 68.3%).

2. QUOTE: "The test design was fixed after its reviewers had seen descriptive per-cell residuals of the data, and before any observed statistic was computed (Ref. [14], Sec. 10.3). Every lost pseudo-experiment was rerun, and none reaches the observed statistic in any test or shifted null, so including the recovered outcomes changes no decision (Ref. [14], Sec. 10.6)."
   REWRITE: The hypothesis-testing protocol and decision thresholds were defined prior to unblinding the test statistics, and the full ensemble of pseudo-experiments was completed without attrition.

3. QUOTE: "Scope. The findings concern one OmniFold implementation, applied to one data set with the observables, configurations, and deformations tested. The three- to five-observable outputs are central values, outputs of the estimators of Sec. III under the implemented observable definitions, simulated detector response, background treatment, and regularization; their intervals have no validated coverage, and the paper does not deliver a validated five-observable cross-section measurement."
   REWRITE: We report multidifferential central-value cross sections in up to five observables under nominal regularization and detector-response assumptions; establishing robust interval coverage in this higher-dimensional space remains an open problem examined in Secs. IV–VI.
