Validation of the two-dimensional measurement

In a simulated experiment, truth is the known target distribution, the prior is the starting simulated
truth distribution, and the response describes reconstruction and selection conditional on truth. A
closure test compares the unfolded estimate with its known target under a specified deformation.
Bias is the mean estimate minus that target over repeated experiments; a single closure residual also
contains sampling fluctuations. Coverage is the fraction of repeated intervals containing the specified
truth, and tests the estimator and interval procedure together. Table 4 separates these questions

Requirements for validated total uncertainties. The follow-up assessment [42] distinguishes
a proposed split-sample width check from repeated-sampling coverage. With the MC sample,
binning maps and background subtraction held fixed, such a check could establish only the precision
conditional on them, at its tested exposure. Nonlinear unfolding does not guarantee a simple
scaling from half to full exposure. Nor can the check supply the variation across independent MC
populations. With the current inputs, validated total uncertainties for all 205 bins, the integral
and the projections therefore cannot be obtained. They would require independent populations
or a validated generative law, a continuous nuisance generator for tests of total intervals, and an
untouched generator-truth domain for bias checks (§4.6). This is a limit of the available inputs, not
a general impossibility result for 2D unfolding.

5.1

Closure tests across dimensions

Only the MINERvA simulation supplies a detector response, the conditional distribution of reconstructed events given truth. Other generators (GiBUU, NuWro, GENIE variants) supply truth
only. Every closure test here therefore keeps the simulated MINERvA response and changes the
truth distribution, by reweighting the simulated events, or changes the analysis chain around it.
Reweighting to another generator’s shape is a valid known-truth test of the unfolding; it is not a
test of the detector model. An ordinary closure, whose pseudo-data are the simulation itself, is an
identity check that a constant estimator would also pass. Table 5 lists, for each test, what it changes
relative to the unfolding’s simulation and what its result can detect.

5.2

Fixed-truth coverage of the 2D statistical band

This test asks whether a 2D statistical band covers a fixed truth at its nominal rate. The test scored
the completeness-divided band, the first-built statistical band, quoted when the test ran, whose
replicas divide by a bootstrapped completeness. The undivided band is the rebuilt band, without
the division, since superseded. The quoted statistical block (§4.2) is a separate ensemble of 300
replicas at the central value’s training seed. The design, criteria and positive control of the test were
specified before any toy was generated (Ref. [37], R1.1).
Each toy draws pseudo-data event by event as Poisson counts with means equal to the simulated
reconstructed weights. It resamples the simulation with the per-event Poisson(1) bootstrap of the
statistical band and unfolds with that band’s estimator, LightGBM with five iterations at a fixed
training seed, the learner of the quoted central value at a different seed (§3.2). The reference T is
the unfluctuated simulated truth, identical in every toy. The no-fluctuation configuration reproduces
both the production closure output and T exactly.
In reported bin b of toy t, ztb = (Utb − Tb )/(rb Tb ), where rb is the production band’s relative
spread. Coverage Ck is the fraction of all (toy, bin) pairs with |z| ≤ k, with intervals from resampling
whole toys. The band counts as nominal if its ratio to the true scatter lies in [0.9, 1.1], which gives
the windows C1 ∈ [0.632, 0.729] and C2 ∈ [0.928, 0.972]. A failure at the interim analysis of 100 toys,
with 99.5% intervals, stops the study.
At the interim analysis (Ntoy = 100, 205 bins) the completeness-divided band undercovers in its
tails. Its one-standard-deviation coverage C1 = 0.679 [0.669, 0.690] is nominal (Gaussian 0.683), but
its two-standard-deviation coverage C2 = 0.912 [0.907, 0.918] lies entirely below its window (Gaussian
0.954). The pull RMS is 1.60, and the mean pull is consistent with zero. By the criteria above
this is a failure by undercoverage, and the study stopped at the interim analysis. An independent
reimplementation of the scoring reproduces the pooled coverages, their intervals, the controls and
the secondary analysis below.
The positive control rescales the band on the same toys. At ×0.7 and ×1.3, C2 moves to 0.809
and 0.955, and the test flags both directions.
The failure is one of width, not bias. The per-bin pattern, which is descriptive and post hoc, is
uneven in both directions. The band is too wide in 82 bins (pull RMS below 0.9) and too narrow in
71 (above 1.1). The pooled C1 is nominal partly through this cancellation; the median bin’s pull
RMS is 0.97. 35 bins have a per-bin C2 below 0.85. Of these, 21 form a block at p∥ ≥ 15 GeV/c
and pT < 0.55 GeV/c. Six more lie in the highest-pT row (2.5 GeV/c to 4.5 GeV/c) at p∥ 6 GeV/c
to 20 GeV/c. The 14 bins with pull RMS above 2 carry 72 % of the C2 deficit.


Unlike the band’s replicas, the central value has completeness exactly 1. A secondary analysis,
added after the pilot, applies the band’s division to the toys. Scored against the same band, the
divided toys overcover: C1 = 0.833, with its interval above the window. The attribution rule was
fixed before the full run, but its clause for the undercoverage outcome was written after the pilot
pulls had been seen. Under this rule the primary undercoverage is attributed to the completeness
term of the band. The attribution is partial: in the replica form, the p∥ 40 GeV/c to 60 GeV/c
column still undercovers.
The band was then rebuilt without this term. The resulting undivided band was rescored only
post hoc (below), and the quoted statistical block has not been tested.
Post hoc rescoring against the undivided band. Scoring the same 100 toys against
the undivided band removes the tail undercoverage: C2 = 0.977 [0.974, 0.980] and C1 = 0.781
[0.771, 0.790], both above their nominal windows, so the undivided band is wider than the toy scatter
(pull RMS 0.84, mean pull consistent with zero). The rescoring is post hoc and partly circular,
because the band’s MC part reuses the toys’ resampling mechanism.
The excess width lies in the data-statistics component. The per-bin pull RMS falls as that
component’s share of the band’s variance rises, which corresponds to a data-statistics part about
1.6× wider in σ than the toys produce. Background purity does not explain it, and its cause is not
established. A post hoc diagnostic bootstraps a single toy; its decision rule was recorded before it
ran. Its result is consistent with the closure toys scattering too little, rather than with the data
bootstrap scattering too much. The toys would scatter too little because their pseudo-data are built
on the training MC events. The diagnostic is neither an independent-population coverage test nor a
calibration on real data. A re-test on held-out simulation would separate the two explanations; it
has not been run.
Scope. The test concerns the statistical band alone and changes no central value or quoted
uncertainty. It tests the LightGBM statistical estimator at the band’s training seed (seed 1), not
at the quoted central value’s seed 42, and does not address systematic, total or five-dimensional
coverage. Its simulation-side scatter reuses the resampling that built the band, so only the data-side
fluctuation is tested by an independent mechanism. Because T equals the prior, the test cannot
detect regularization bias toward the prior.

5.3

Other validation diagnostics

Unbinned diagnostic (C2ST). In the classifier two-sample test (C2ST), a classifier is trained
to separate the data from the (reweighted) simulation in the full unbinned feature space, and its
held-out area under the ROC curve (AUC) or accuracy is compared with the chance value 0.5. The
AUC drops from ≈ 0.535 before unfolding to ≈ 0.501 after, indicating removal of the reconstructed
data ↔ MC mismatch. This binning-free check is descriptive, and no calibrated p-value is quoted.
As run, the C2ST trains and evaluates on the same sample without cross-fitting; a valid null would
require re-running the full cross-fitted OmniFold pipeline per pseudoexperiment. Calibrated unbinned
goodness-of-fit remains open [18].
Same-ensemble pull diagnostic (not a coverage test). An earlier ensemble of Ntoy = 200 2D
closure unfolds (Ref. [37], R1.2) uses weighted MC reco pseudo-data and stores the truth marginals
in truth histograms. Each toy has its own bootstrap seed k and per-event Poisson(1) draws, as in
production, with seeds chosen not to collide with the production replicas. Toy seeds are independent;
bins within a toy are correlated. The MC bootstrap multiplies the truth weight, so it fluctuates
both the unfolded result and the stored truth. The stored truth varies in all 205 reported bins, and


its all-toy mean X̄ truth is therefore not an independent fixed truth for a split-sample coverage claim;
it only centers a same-ensemble diagnostic.
