Abstract
We report a simultaneous measurement of the νµ charged-current inclusive cross section
in five observables: the muon transverse and longitudinal momenta, available hadronic energy,
three-momentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass. The cross section is unfolded without
binning with OmniFold from MINERvA medium-energy open data. It is reported in sixteen
cells, with a covariance whose median per-cell uncertainty is 7.29%. Three external generator
predictions, compared on all sixteen cells, give χ2 between 109 and 272 for 16 degrees of freedom.
GiBUU, compared only on the 8 cells its sample covers, gives χ2 = 58 on those cells. The
coverage of the result’s intervals has not been validated; a bias test at two held-out GENIE v3
truths finds the estimator’s bias below the reported uncertainty in every cell but does not test
interval widths. In a fake-data study, the reported uncertainty does not cover the estimator’s
bias at GiBUU-like truths or at a synthetic q3 tilt.

Executive summary
We apply unbinned machine-learning unfolding (OmniFold [1, 2]) to MINERvA’s medium-energy
neutrino data. The principal result is the sixteen-cell cross section of §10 in the five observables pT ,
p∥ , Eavail , q3 and W . Its covariance gives a median per-cell uncertainty of 7.29%. Three external
generator predictions, compared on all sixteen cells, give χ2 between 109 and 272 for 16 degrees
of freedom. GiBUU, compared only on the 8 cells its sample covers, gives χ2 = 58 on those cells
(§10.4).
The unbinned result for d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) reproduces the published integrated cross section [3]
within about one percent, with a correlated shape difference (§6). Whether it is ready for publication
as a measurement has not been established. LightGBM was chosen over the exact-split estimator,
which is kept as a cross-check, after both comparisons with the publication were known (§3.2).

The generator comparisons of the central values are descriptive. All four truth-level generators,
GENIE [4], MINERvA Tune [5], NuWro [6] and GiBUU [7], under-predict the unfolded central
value in the quasielastic–∆ dip (0.1 ≤ Eavail < 0.4 GeV), the region of the established MINERvA
low-recoil discrepancy [5, 8] (§7.6, §7.7). In cell-integrated terms, most of the positive difference
from the Tune v1 prior lies at Eavail ≥ 0.8 GeV, much of it at W ≥ 1.8 GeV (§8.4). The high-Eavail ,
high-W region carries the largest regularization bias measured in §9. The four external generators
also fall below the data in this region, each by about its overall shortfall once GiBUU’s sample limit
is allowed for (§8.5).
The generator tests are an application that illustrates calibrated inference with a biased estimator.
Calibrated joint five-dimensional tests of hybrid null hypotheses for five predictions, in total (rate
and shape) and in shape, reject all ten under six stated conditions (§11.4). The rejections are not
physical exclusions, and matched coarse projections reject as well (§11.7). The pseudo-experiments
lost to time limits have been rerun, for reporting only, so every submitted draw has been observed.
No recovered draw changes a decision (§11.6). Correcting the carbon-only NuWro and GiBUU
predictions to the CH target moves both toward the data. The ten decisions, made with the
carbon-only predictions, are unchanged. In the descriptive re-evaluation with corrected predictions,
NuWro’s total-test rejection is not robust to the correction (§11.9).
A methodological finding concerns the standard covariance, which sums single-source systematic
matrices, each with all other sources nominal, and so omits nonlinear cross-source response terms.
A corrected unified-throw test, which re-unfolds with all reweightable sources perturbed together,
stores the joint mean shift separately (§4.5).
A preliminary extended-fiducial study removes the truth-muon cuts and keeps the fiducial volume
and MINOS-based selection. It puts about 6 % of the scalar 2D rate in newly acceptance-supported
cells (part of tier 1, the interpreted measurement) and about 28 % in low- or zero-efficiency cells
(tier 2, extrapolated from the prior; §12).

Introduction and motivation

MINERvA (Main Injector Experiment for ν-A) is a dedicated neutrino–nucleus scattering experiment
in Fermilab’s NuMI beamline, immediately upstream of the MINOS near detector [9]. During
roughly a decade of operation ending in 2019, it recorded high-statistics samples in low-energy and
medium-energy (ME) beam configurations [10] and in neutrino-dominated (forward horn current,
FHC) and antineutrino-dominated modes. MINERvA measures neutrino–nucleus cross sections

on a range of nuclear targets in the few-GeV region most relevant to accelerator-based oscillation
experiments. These cross sections and their nuclear effects are a leading systematic uncertainty
for T2K [11], NOvA [12], Hyper-Kamiokande [13], and DUNE [14]. The few-GeV regime combines
quasielastic, resonant, and deep-inelastic processes that no single model describes well. This analysis
uses the public ME FHC CC-inclusive data product (detector details in §2).
The MINERvA ME FHC charged-current (CC) inclusive double-differential cross section in muon
transverse and longitudinal momentum, d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ), was published by Ruterbories et al. [3] using
D’Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU) [15] with a binned response matrix. We reproduce it
with unbinned OmniFold [1, 2], which iteratively reweights events using machine-learned likelihood
ratios on per-event observables rather than on a fixed histogram binning.
Machine-learning advances have enabled unbinned unfolding (overviews in Refs. [16, 17]; crossexperiment guidance in Ref. [18]). One broad family iteratively reweights a starting simulation;
OmniFold [1, 2, 19] is its most widely adopted method. The other generates new samples directly
with a neural network [20–23]. Both lift the binned-response limitations of widely-used classical
methods [24] such as IBU, the SVD method [25], and TUnfold [26].
Prior OmniFold applications. In the last few years OmniFold has progressed from a method
proposal to published measurements. H1 published the first real-data applications in deep-inelastic
scattering: lepton–jet correlations unfolded simultaneously in eight dimensions [27], followed by jet
substructure at high Q2 [28]. LHCb unfolded identified-hadron distributions in Z-tagged jets [29].
ATLAS performed a simultaneous unbinned measurement of twenty-four Z+jets observables [30].
Ref. [18] collects these and other applications and distills cross-experiment practice. Three of the
practices it audits appear in our uncertainty budget: the ensemble-mean cross-check of the central
value, the bootstrap statistical covariance, and the joint-perturbation (unified-throw) covariance
check of §4.5.
H1 cut backgrounds to a negligible level; LHCb corrected per track before unfolding. ATLAS left
its less-than-0.25 % background in place and assigned it as an uncertainty, while identifying negativelyweighted simulated events as the natural in-method subtraction [2, 30]. Ref. [18] recommends this
in-method subtraction for nontrivial backgrounds. Recent methodological work explicitly treats
backgrounds, fakes, acceptance, and inefficiency in OmniFold-HI [31] and generative unfolding [32].
These constructions address related problems; their subtraction prescriptions can differ.
Closest to this analysis is the study of Huang et al. [33], the first application of OmniFold to a
neutrino cross section: a mock-data unfolding of muon kinematics on the public T2K near-detector
simulated dataset, benchmarked against traditional binned approaches. Two of its findings bear on
neutrino applications. The first is its iteration count, which we do not adopt (§3.2). The second
is an ensemble of reweighting trials with different train/test splits that controls the ML stochastic
component; we include that component explicitly, as the training-seed variation band (§4). The
present work differs from that study in three ways. It unfolds real MINERvA data to an absolutely
normalized cross section, with a 2D uncertainty budget whose covariance components are all built
with the central value’s own learner (§3.2). Its gradient-boosted decision-tree (GBDT) learners suit
a low-dimensional tabular feature set. It validates the method directly against a published binned
measurement of the same data (§6).
Motivation for unbinned unfolding. First, OmniFold learns event weights in the full observable
space and histograms only the final result, so reported bins can be chosen or refined after unfolding
without rerunning it. Background subtraction is unbinned too in the 2D result and its covariance,
in the 3D and 4D central values and in the sixteen-cell five-dimensional result (§10), which use

refined negative-weight subtraction (§3.1). The configuration-(a) five-dimensional central value and
its five-axis covariance, and the extended-fiducial value, use the binned purity weight, a per-reco-bin
(D − B)/D weight fixed before training. Second, additional observables enter as input features,
without multiplying response-matrix bins. The available-energy extension (§7) is built this way; a
binned method such as IBU would need a correspondingly larger multidimensional response matrix
to include it. Third, OmniFold regularizes through model capacity and through an iteration count,
which it shares with IBU. Comparing the two methods on the same data probes the result’s method
dependence (§6).
Analysis strategy. The published 2D cross section provides a quantitative validation of unbinned
OmniFold on MINERvA data and a size comparison for its uncertainty budget (§§2–6). The same
pipeline then adds Eavail , q3 (§7) and W (§8) as classifier inputs, tested by marginalization to each
lower-dimensional result, closure, coverage and classifier-family comparisons. The validation and
uncertainty of the five-dimensional estimator (§9), the sixteen-cell result (§10) and the calibrated
generator tests built on the estimator (§11) follow these central values. A scalar study with relaxed
truth-muon cuts (§12) probes an alternative signal definition. The companion journal article is a
shortened account, and this note gives the detail it omits.
Scope. The results hold for one implementation of unbinned OmniFold, applied to one data set,
and for the configurations, observables and deformations tested. The principal limitations are listed
in the executive summary (p. 2). The generator tests of §11 evaluate hybrid null hypotheses and add
no reportable uncertainty. Full-event unfolding with point-cloud transformer (PET) representations
is in progress and documented separately (App. F), and no result in this note depends on it.
