We report a simultaneous measurement of the νµ charged-current inclusive cross section in five
observables, the muon transverse and longitudinal momenta, available hadronic energy, threemomentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass, unfolded without binning with OmniFold from
MINERvA medium-energy open data and reported in sixteen cells with a covariance whose median
per-cell uncertainty is 7.29%. Three external generator predictions, compared on all sixteen cells,
give χ2 between 109 and 272 for 16 degrees of freedom. GiBUU, compared only on the 8 cells its
sample covers, gives χ2 = 58 on those cells. In a fake-data study, the reported uncertainty does
not cover the estimator’s bias at GiBUU-like truths or at a synthetic q3 tilt. The same unfolding
reproduces the integral of the published muon-kinematic cross section within about 1%, with a
correlated shape difference.

I.

INTRODUCTION

Multidifferential neutrino cross sections test interaction
models across muon and hadronic observables. Their
interpretation depends on correcting detector response
and on assessing the bias and uncertainty of the unfolding procedure. Iterative Bayesian unfolding on a binned
migration matrix [1] is widely used, but its response
tensor grows rapidly as observables are added. OmniFold learns event weights in an unbinned feature space
through classifier-based density-ratio estimation [2, 3].
This makes simultaneous higher-dimensional unfolding
possible without specifying a separate binned response
tensor for every projection. Unbinned unfolding has been
applied to collider data, for example to eight observables
in deep-inelastic scattering at H1 [4] and to twenty-four
Z+jets observables at ATLAS [5] (see Ref. [6] for a survey). In neutrino physics, it has been demonstrated on
a public simulated T2K near-detector sample [7]. Multidifferential neutrino measurements on data reach three
dimensions [8–11].
We apply the method to MINERvA medium-energy
νµ data from the open-data release [12]. The result is a
simultaneous charged-current inclusive cross section in
five observables: the transverse and longitudinal muon
momenta pT and p∥ , the available hadronic energy Eavail ,
the three-momentum transfer q3 , and the hadronic invariant mass W . It is reported in sixteen cells with its
covariance (Sec. V) and compared with five generator
predictions (Sec. V B). We first reproduce the published
double-differential cross section d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) [13] as an
end-to-end test of the unfolding, and we also give threeand four-observable central values. Reconstructed W is

fixed by the reconstructed (pT , p∥ , q3 ), so the five observables are simultaneous but not independent.
The validation studies assess the result through the twodimensional reproduction, background-subtraction tests,
recovery of generator-sized truth changes, a coverage test,
and a fake-data study of estimator bias. They examine whether the unfolding gives reliable multidimensional
cross sections when the simulation mismodels the truth by
generator-sized amounts. Interval coverage has not been
validated. The bias tests, the fake-data study, and the dependence of the generator χ2 on the flux correlations are
reported for the sixteen-cell result in Secs. IV H and V B.
The findings are those of one OmniFold implementation
on one data set, for the observables, configurations, and
deformations tested, rather than of multidimensional OmniFold in general.
Section II describes the data, the simulation and the
observables, and Sec. III the unfolding and the reporting
cells. Section IV gives the uncertainties and the validation studies, from the reproduction of the published
two-dimensional measurement to the fake-data tests of
the sixteen-cell result, and Sec. V gives the result and
its comparison with generator predictions. Section VI
discusses what limits the estimator, and Sec. VI A summarizes the principal limitations. The companion analysis
note [14], cited below by section, gives the full validation
record, the construction of the uncertainties, and the tests
with their assumptions.

III.

CROSS-SECTION EXTRACTION

Each OmniFold iteration has two classification steps. A
reconstruction-level classifier distinguishes data from simulated events and defines a likelihood-ratio weight. The
event pairing transfers that weight to truth, including
unmatched events, and a truth-level classifier propagates
a smooth reweighting to the next iteration. Cross sections are formed by histogramming the reweighted truth
events in the reporting binning and dividing by integrated
flux, target count, and bin width. Events that fail reconstruction enter as unmatched truth events, so no separate
efficiency correction is applied.
Backgrounds enter the reconstruction-level step through
the density the classifier learns to match. In the puritybased subtraction, every data event in reconstructed bin b
carries the weight wpur (b) = max(0, 1−Bb /Db ), where Db
and Bb are the data and predicted background counts in
that bin, so the classifier’s target density is D(x) wpur (b).

The unbinned negative-weight treatment instead adds
simulated background events with negative weight to the
data, so that the target is D(x) − B(x) pointwise. A
classifier refinement [22], clipped at zero, converts the
signed sample to non-negative weights before the first
iteration. The two targets share bin integrals wherever
Bb < Db but differ inside a bin wherever the background
fraction varies within it (Ref. [14], App. C).
Table I lists the two estimators and the results each
is used for. The two-observable central value uses
LightGBM [23] at seed 42, with negative-weight background subtraction and a refinement of 400 trees and 31
leaves. This reproducible central value is that of its own
systematic-universe ensemble, so its covariance is built
with the same estimator. The three- and four-observable
central values and the sixteen-cell five-observable result
(Sec. V) use the joint-test estimator, a deterministic LightGBM with the same background treatment and refinement capacity. This estimator removes most of the bias of
purity-based subtraction at nominal truth in the highestW cells (Sec. IV), and the note’s simulation-calibrated
tests use the same estimator. The other configurations
used below, and their results, are described in Ref. [14],
Sec. 3.2.
Both estimators use five OmniFold iterations with
gradient-boosted decision-tree classifiers [23, 24]. The
iteration count and classifier capacity regularize the unfolding. The nominal settings are the scikit-learn defaults,
with LightGBM matched to their capacity. The refinement capacity of the joint-test estimator was chosen in
simulation closure studies. No hyperparameter search
was performed on data. Each classifier is trained on the
full sample whose weights it then sets, so no held-out
or cross-fitted split is used; it is trained without early
stopping, and the two classes keep their physical weights
(Ref. [14], Sec. 3.1).
The joint-test estimator keeps five iterations because
none of the larger counts in the comparison of Ref. [14],
Sec. 9.4 reduced its model dependence, measured as the
largest residual proxy over the truth changes used in its
development; that section gives the counts compared and
the comparison.

A.

Reporting definitions and model dependence

the envelope of a cell as the largest shift of its data
result when the simulation’s prior is reweighted to each
of five generator-sized changes of the truth, and adds it
linearly to the probabilistic half-width. The envelope is a
sensitivity diagnostic that tracks the regularization bias,
not an interval. With the joint-test estimator, over the
reporting definitions of a five-observable measurement
tested in the study, the envelope has a median of about
10–13% per cell, comparable to the generator differences
that such a measurement would need to resolve. The
study and its precision targets are described in Ref. [14],

IV.

UNCERTAINTIES AND VALIDATION

A closure test measures how well a specified deformation
of the known simulated truth is recovered, with the prior,
the initial truth distribution, held fixed. Coverage, how
often intervals contain their target over repeated experiments, tests the estimator and the interval construction
together, and the coverage tested here is pointwise, for
each reported quantity separately, not the stronger simultaneous coverage of all of them. The closure, deformation,
and coverage studies reweight the true signal of the simulated events used as pseudo-data, which changes their
reconstructed and true distributions together and keeps
the detector response at fixed truth. Throughout, the
prior used to unfold them stays at Tune v1. The envelope
of Sec. III A instead reweights the prior used to unfold the
data. Every study uses the analysis simulation’s detector
response.

A.

The reported covariance

The reported covariance of the sixteen-cell result is
Ctotal + Cresp . Unlike the envelope of Sec. III A, its model
term is a covariance built from three generator shifts. The
term Ctotal sums the statistical, flux, interaction, detector,
lateral and normalization uncertainties of the joint-test
estimator and Cmodel . The model term Cmodel is built
from the change in the unfolded data when the prior
is reweighted to GENIE + MEC, NuWro and GiBUU
in turn. It represents unscaled generator-shape dependence, not a calibrated bound, and its coverage at truths
held out of its construction has not been validated. The
response term Cresp is the response of the result to a
one-standard-deviation scale of the reconstructed recoil
energy in the simulation. The scale is applied to proton,
meson, electromagnetic and other deposits. Cresp does
not cover neutron or muon deposits, nor species, energydependence or resolution effects beyond a scale. It enters
only this result, its fake-data tests and its comparison
in Sec. V B. The median per-cell relative uncertainty is
7.29%, and 6.74% without Cresp . The note gives the cells,
the central values, the per-group uncertainties and the
correlation matrix of the reported covariance.

B.

Two-dimensional benchmark

The two-dimensional result is an end-to-end test of the
unfolding against a measurement from the same data. Its
integrated cross section is 3.069×10−38 cm2 /nucleon, compared with 3.039 × 10−38 cm2 /nucleon in the published
analysis, a ratio of 1.010. The standardized bin differences
formed with the published uncertainties have an RMS of
0.535. Against the full published covariance, the descriptive covariance-weighted distance is χ2 /ndf = 2.27. It is
computed with the pseudo-inverse over the 205 bins, which
keeps all 205 eigenmodes (rank 205, condition number
1.5 × 1012 ), and ndf is that rank. It indicates a correlated
shape difference, not a normalization difference, spread
over many eigenmodes of the published covariance. The
source of the shape difference has not been identified.
Figure 1 shows the principal one-dimensional projections.
We quote the LightGBM result as the central value
because its covariance uses the same estimator. The distance depends on the estimator (Ref. [14], Sec. 6.4). The
learner was selected after the LightGBM and exact-split
comparisons with the published result, so the benchmark
is a reproduction rather than an independent validation.
The purity-subtracted exact-split result differs from the
central value by a median of 1.03 % per bin (maximum
17.6 %), as detailed in Ref. [14], Sec. 3.2.

C.

Two-dimensional covariance

The two-dimensional covariance is the sum of four
blocks, each from LightGBM unfoldings with the central

value’s estimator: systematic universes (187 universes,
including the normalization), statistical (300 bootstrap
replicas), run period (three period groups), and training
seed (five seed settings, so that its own size is poorly determined). The resulting relative uncertainty has a median
of 6.41 % per bin and is 4.76 % on the integrated cross section, compared with 6.85 % and 4.61 % for the publication.
The published flux uncertainty (median 4.01 %) is larger
than ours (3.71 %), for a reason the released inputs do not
let us determine. The covariance omits the approximation
in the systematic-universe unfoldings for events that fail
reconstruction, the learner dependence of Sec. IV B, and
the dependence on the regularization, while the dependence on the prior’s generator shape is reported beside
it (below). The covariance has not been independently
validated and provides no calibrated significance for generator comparisons. The block sizes, the assumptions of
the block sum, and the tests of the approximation are
given in Ref. [14], Secs. 6.3, 4.5, and 4.6.
The statistical block, with a median statistical uncertainty of 0.676 %, is a bootstrap of 300 replicas drawn at
the central value’s training seed, in which the background
templates are also resampled. The coverage of this block
and of the total covariance has not been tested. We make
no coverage claim on independent simulated samples or
simultaneously over all reported bins and projections.
Data from different run periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties. One period group’s
unfolded p∥ distribution, taken relative to its own simulation, differs from that of the earliest periods by a step
of about 7 % near 8 GeV/c. The cause is not known, and
these data cannot establish which period is correct. The
run-period block is built from double ratios among the
three period groups, each group’s unfolded result over its
own simulated truth, with no group chosen as the reference. It includes this inconsistency in the combined result
and amounts to 2.0 % on the integrated cross section.
Because the block is built from the observed differences
among the periods, its size is not validated and its own
consistency check cannot fail.

Normalization and closure

Of the joint-test estimator’s results, only the threeobservable marginal has been compared with the lowerdimensional result it extends. Its area-weighted total
agrees with the two-dimensional result to 0.141 %, a comparison across the two estimators. The normalization
checks of the earlier purity-subtracted five-observable central value are described in Ref. [14], Sec. 8.1, and the
closure checks of its estimator in three and four observables in Ref. [14], Sec. 5.1.
Figure 2 shows that, with purity-based background
subtraction in a deterministic LightGBM estimator, endto-end pseudo-experiments at nominal truth reproduce the

input except in the highest-W cells, which are biased by
up to 4 % in simulation. In the same estimator, unbinned
negative-weight subtraction with a refinement of 100 trees
and 8 leaves reduces it to below 0.3 % in those cells and at
most 0.4 % elsewhere in the projection, leaving up to 1 %
in some joint cells. On data, the two treatments differ by
at most 1.4 %. Both kinds of closure study are described
in Ref. [14], Sec. 5.1.
In two observables, a closure test with backgrounds (20
pseudo-experiments at nominal truth) finds the purity
and refined estimates more than one statistical standard
deviation apart in 14 of the 205 cells. The purity estimate
lies up to 5.9 statistical standard deviations from the
truth, and more than one total standard deviation in
one cell, while the refined one lies within 0.44 in every
cell. The two-dimensional result therefore uses the refined
estimator (Ref. [14], App. C.6).

E.

Generator-sized truth changes

Figure 3 shows that the unfolding recovers only part of
a change of the Eavail shape as large as the difference between generators. When the true shape is reweighted by
the GiBUU-to-GENIE ratio, the (Eavail , W ) cells unfolded
with a development variant of the joint-test estimator using a 100-tree, 8-leaf refinement deviate from it by several
percent in the largest cells and by up to 74 % at high W ,
in the cell 0.8 ≤ Eavail < 1.5 GeV, W ≥ 3 GeV. These
deviations occur with or without background, so background subtraction alone does not explain the residual.
We interpret the residual as regularization bias, because
it persists over the tested iteration numbers and classifier
capacities, although no single cause is established. For
other fixed generator shapes tested with the joint-test estimator, it is 16 % to 31 % in the most affected (Eavail , W )
cell and lies within the quoted total uncertainty in about
half of those cells. Those uncertainties come from an
earlier purity-subtracted five-observable covariance that
fails its specified acceptance criteria (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.6).
For the sixteen-cell result, Sec. IV H reports the fake-data
tests of the estimator’s bias. The deformation studies, the
iteration and capacity tests, the exact binned comparison
and the joint-test-estimator shapes are given in Ref. [14],
Secs. 9.2 and 9.3.
A binned, signal-only and noise-free diagnostic on the
109 joint cells and the truth changes used to develop

the estimator, with the detector response fixed at the
simulation’s, shows that exact binned iterative unfolding
with five iterations leaves about the same median residual
as the boosted-tree estimator. It covers only the residuals
whose diagnosis does not change with the resolution of
the truth binning (38 % of the joint-cell residuals above
2 %). Within that scope, the residual is consistent with
the regularization of the inverse problem at this binning
and prior, rather than with the tree approximation of the
classifiers (Ref. [14], Sec. 9.3). The diagnostic does not
address what unbinned information could recover, the
miscoverage of Sec. IV F, or the validity of any interval.

G.

Sensitivity to input rounding

The joint-test estimator is unstable under perturbations
of its inputs within floating-point rounding. Roundinglevel changes of its inputs move its results by up to 2.6
statistical standard deviations, and those of the lowercapacity development variant by up to 2.5, so the estimator fails a data-stability requirement of at most 0.3
(median) and 1.0 (maximum) statistical standard deviations. This spread lies inside the data-bootstrap statistical
uncertainty but limits how exactly the central values can
be reproduced.

H.

To compare the estimator’s bias with the reported
covariance when the truth differs from the simulation, we
unfold pseudo-data at 12 truths and compare the mean
unfolded result with each truth on the 16 cells through
χ2 with 16 degrees of freedom. The truths are not held
out: they are the simulation’s prior; five hybrid truths,
each a generator prediction on the fine grid with the
MINERvA Tune v1 composition below it; NuWro shape

changes in Eavail and in (pT , p∥ , Eavail ); a GENIE + MEC
shape change in (Eavail , W ); a GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail
ratio; a noise-free closure at the NuWro shape; and a
synthetic tilt of the q3 distribution at fixed (Eavail , W ).

column of Table II). A truth is listed when p < 0.01 or
when a cell has |z| > 2. Table II gives the results under
the reported covariance and under Ctotal alone, and 6
further truths, given in the note, are descriptive and not
judged against these criteria.

Weakening only the flux correlations by 10 % lowers the
χ2 of the 16-cell predictions by 24–48 %. The same change
to the detector or lateral correlations lowers it by at most
18 %. With the correlations of the interaction, flux, detector and lateral groups removed, every 16-cell prediction
keeps χ2 ≥ 45 for 16 degrees of freedom. The flux correlations are estimated from 100 hadron-production (PPFX)
flux universes [16], weighted by the neutrino–electron
and inverse-muon-decay constraint. Bootstrapping these
universes, with the flux variances kept, gives 16th–84th
percentile bands for each 16-cell χ2 , with edges within
6.2 % of the reported value and a median at most 1.0 %
above it. These bands describe the scatter of the χ2 from
estimating the correlations with these universes, not its
sensitivity to incorrect correlations, which the weakening
probes. In particular, they are not intervals for the χ2
under the true correlations.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

The principal result is the sixteen-cell cross section of
Sec. V, with its covariance and generator comparison.
Two validation findings point to the unfolded result
retaining part of its prior. The first is the residual under
a generator-sized change of the truth in Sec. IV E, which
we interpret as regularization bias. The second is the
envelope of Sec. III A, which tracks the same bias and
alone exceeds the precision target of Ref. [14], Sec. 9.4
in two of the three tested reporting definitions. The
note’s calibrated tests show the same bias toward the
prior: it displaces their null distributions for the external
predictions upward, and the calibration, which simulates
the whole analysis chain, reproduces that displacement
(Ref. [14], Sec. 11.5). The miscoverage of Sec. IV F is also
attributed to a residual bias, but whether that bias has
the same origin is not established.

Limitations

Apart from the low stability of four of the sixteen cells
(below), the limitations of these results are stated with
the results they qualify, in Secs. III–V, and those of the
calibrated tests in the companion note. The principal
ones are these.
1. The two-dimensional covariance has not been independently validated, and its coverage has not been
tested (Sec. IV C); the three- and four-observable
central values lack intervals of validated coverage
(Sec. V C); and the coverage of the sixteen-cell total
uncertainties is not validated (the fake-data tests of
Sec. IV H assess the estimator’s bias).
2. Four of the sixteen cells, R02, R06, R10 and R15,
have a reconstruction stability of 0.16–0.25: of the
simulated signal events generated in such a cell and
reconstructed, only that fraction is reconstructed in
the same cell.
3. The note’s calibrated tests reject hybrid nulls, conditionally on the simulation’s hadronic response.
However, a rejection extends to the generator prediction itself only if the residual below the fine grid,
where each null takes the simulation’s event composition, is bounded, and no such bound has been
shown. Their calibration is valid to the extent that

DATA AVAILABILITY

The input data and simulation are the MINERvA opendata AnaTuples, released under CC0 [12]. The sixteen-cell
cross section, its covariance matrix and the cell definitions
are provided as machine-readable files in the Supplemental
Material. This analysis used an earlier production of that
release; its 2,374 input files are identified by SHA256
checksum in the records cited in Ref. [14], App. G. The
analysis note’s figures of the joint-test null distributions
and the (Eavail , W ) map are regenerated by a candidate
release package of about 16 MB, not yet deposited, that

requires only numpy, scipy, and matplotlib. The paper’s
quoted numbers and the event-level processing require
the archived analysis products, the open-data AnaTuples,
and the generator samples produced for this analysis;

Ref. [14], App. G states what each part of the release
does and does not reproduce.
