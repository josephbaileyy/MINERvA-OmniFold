transfer

Unbinned OmniFold adds an observable by adding an input to the step-1 and step-2 classifiers,
without an explicit migration matrix or per-bin response tensor. It avoids the combinatorial
binning penalty of matrix unfolding but still has a dimensional cost: classifier training, acceptance

and support coverage, sample sparsity and uncertainty quantification become more demanding as
observables are added. The primary methodological motivation is the absence of a practical analog of
D’Agostini iterative Bayesian unfolding (IBU). We extend the measurement to d3 σ/(dpT dp∥ dEavail )
with available energy as a third axis.
MINERvA has published binned triple-differential results (Fig. 15, Table 29), including recent
comparisons between low- and medium-energy beams [44]. The latter measure pT , p∥ , and available
recoil energy for a quasielastic-like signal, for which the available energy is the sum of proton
kinetic energies. This differs from the inclusive signal and species-dependent energy definition used
here. What distinguishes this analysis is not the number of dimensions by itself but the unbinned,
simultaneous CC-inclusive unfolding of three, four and five recorded high-level observables.

7.4

Closure: the one injected Eavail deformation is recovered

A reweighting closure tests the 3D unfolding of configuration (a) only, on one injected deformation.
The unfolding recovers a known +30 % Gaussian bump injected into pseudo-data in truth Eavail
(center 0.3 GeV, width 0.15 GeV). The recovered step-2 mean weight is 1.048 against the injected
1.0485. Residuals against reweighted truth on the Eavail -marginal have median 0.9999 and standard
deviation 0.0006 (0.06 %); the per-3D-bin residual standard deviation is 3.2 % from MC statistics
in sparse high-recoil corners. The closure’s marginal scatter is far below the 3.7 % data-versus-2D
scatter. Because the closure uses configuration (a) and the data comparison sets (e) against (f),
the ratio of the two scatters is not quoted. Total normalization matches to better than 1 %. In
the metric of §5 (Eavail projection) the residual is 1.84 % against 18.1 % injected (ratio 0.102). No
acceptance threshold set before the test is documented, so it has no pass or fail verdict.
The test covers one shape, this Gaussian, injected into the pseudo-data and unfolded with the same
detector response that the unfolding uses. For this injection the configuration-(a) estimator neither
removes the injected feature nor creates a spurious one. That result is shown for configuration (a) only
and does not extend to (e); since the data-versus-2D scatter sets (e) against (f), it is not interpreted as
data–simulation structure resolved by the available-energy axis. The test does not exclude estimator
bias for other deformations (§5.1). The hidden-variable closure exceeds its threshold at the largest
amplitude (§5, App. D).

Our gradient-boosted decision trees differ from the neural-network classifiers used in published

7.6

Generator comparison in 3D

The published ancillary files provide only binned 2D model histograms. Quantitative 3D comparisons,
or comparisons in arbitrary projections, require generating truth-level events and histogramming
them as d3 σ in the same fiducial phase space with the same truth Eavail definition. Because the
unfolded result is at truth level, the comparisons need no detector simulation. The GENIE, NuWro
and GiBUU samples were generated and processed for this analysis, and MINERvA Tune v1 was
extracted from the analysis simulation’s truth with its full tune weight; no spectra were digitized or
externally supplied.
• GENIE 2.12.10 central value, generated with gevgen on a CH target with the MINERvA
ME FHC flux, using the DefaultPlusValenciaMEC cross-section splines [4] but gevgen’s default
event-generator list, which excludes MEC, so the sample has no 2p2h events (§7.7). It carries
no MINERvA reweights, and its events are reweighted to the analysis flux as described below.
• MINERvA Tune v1, the analysis tune (base GENIE + RPA, low-recoil 2p2h, and nonresonant-pion reweights), taken directly from the truth of the analysis simulation, which
reproduces the normalization of the published ancillary curve to 0.01 %.

• NuWro 21.09 [6], an independent generator on C with the same flux (after reweighting below)
and phase space.
• GiBUU 2019 [7], a transport-theory generator on C, built and run natively, without a

Flux repair and normalization. The GENIE (CV and +Valencia 2p2h) and NuWro samples
were generated from a flux that had no PPFX weights, was capped at 50 GeV, and stored flux
densities in variable-width bins that were sampled and normalized as contents, which under-weighted
every wide high-energy bin and suppressed the high-p∥ predictions and the integrated rates (Ref. [37],
R1.3). The repair reweights each event exactly to the analysis PPFX central-value flux on 0–100 GeV,
with correctly sampled 50–100 GeV supplements. The repaired GENIE and NuWro predictions are
used in §§7–8, except in three studies, flagged where they appear, that keep the unrepaired samples:
the FSI-dial studies, the departure ratio of §9.2 and the NuWro-shaped prior of §12. GiBUU reads
its own flux and receives an energy-dependent per-event reweight to the same convention, which
changes its integrated cross section by a factor 0.9966. Its sample stops at Eν ≈ 20 GeV, so it is
empty above p∥ ≈ 20 GeV/c. The analysis simulation, MINERvA Tune v1, is not affected. Each
prediction is normalized by its own flux-averaged total CC cross section per nucleon.

7.7

The low-available-energy excess and the known low-recoil deficit

We compare the low-Eavail excess with candidate model changes, both by reweighting and by
generating the relevant physics. MINERvA first isolated the rate enhancement between quasielastic
and ∆ peaks, with multi-nucleon final states, in a low-three-momentum-transfer subsample [5]. The
empirical tune was built to absorb it; our central values in muon kinematics and Eavail show the
predictions falling short in the same region.
The tested pion-FSI variations do not account for it. Reweighting 2M central-value GENIE
events with GENIE’s grwght1p changes dσ/dEavail by less than a percent for both tested pionFSI dials. FrInel_pi shifts the spectrum by ≤ 0.74 % (peak in 0.10–0.20 GeV) and FrAbs_pi by
≤ 0.82 % (peak in the lowest bin). Both are ratios within the unrepaired GENIE sample (§7.6), in
which the flux defect cancels to first order. Neither was recomputed. The integrated rate moves
by ±0.02–0.03 % at ±1σ. 1 A dial that moves the spectrum by less than 1 % cannot account
for an excess of 8 % to 36 % per bin over the GENIE central value below Eavail = 0.4 GeV. This
conclusion is limited to these dials and their range; other transport models or proton-FSI effects are
not excluded. Recent MINERvA quasielastic-like measurements point toward overestimated proton
and charged-pion FSI in their model comparisons [44]. Their different signal definition prevents
directly identifying that effect with our inclusive discrepancy.
Comparison with a missing 2p2h component. The base GENIE central value uses the default
event-generator list and contains no meson-exchange-current (2p2h/MEC) events. None of the
1.48 × 106 CC events of the generated sample is a MEC event, and the 50–100 GeV supplement is
MEC-free as well. Before the flux reweight of §7.6, the sample’s unweighted modes are QE 11 %,
RES 22 %, DIS 66 % and COH 0.9 %. 2p2h must therefore be added ; reweighting cannot supply
it. The mode decomposition multiplies each bin of the central-value dσ/dEavail by the fraction of
its events in each mode. Overlaid with data, it places the largest positive data−CV gaps in the
quasielastic–∆ dip, [0.10, 0.20) and [0.20, 0.40). The CV is much closer to data in the QE-dominated
[0, 0.10) and [0.40, 1.50) regions. At central value, 69 % of the positive deficit lies at Eavail ≤ 0.4 GeV,
where 2p2h events populate. Closing the integrated deficit requires 2p2h at ≈ 45 % of the QE rate
(68 % to 71 % in the two dip bins). This is the standard magnitude of the empirical and Valencia
MEC models; the FSI shifts are below one percent (Fig. 20).

Fourth axis: three-momentum transfer q3

The fourth axis adds one feature (§7), three-momentum transfer q3 = |⃗q |, chosen because its
(Eavail , q3 ) plane separates quasielastic, 2p2h and resonance processes and permits bin-compatible

Truth q3 comes from the generator (CVUniverse::Getq3True()); reconstructed q3 uses the muon Q2
and the calorimetric recoil (CVUniverse::RecoQ3()). Both are evaluated for all twelve playlists, as

for Eavail . Lateral systematic shifts change q3 but leave Eavail unchanged, so a shifted q3 is stored for
each lateral universe instead of reusing the central value. The classifier inputs are (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 ),
with deliberately coarse q3 binning [0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1.2, 2.0, 100] GeV (seven bins including a final
catch bin), giving 14 × 16 × 7 × 7 = 10 976 four-dimensional bins (4830 populated).

No 4D uncertainty. Neither a 4D uncertainty nor a ratio of the covariance from simultaneous
variation of every reweightable band and the flux multisim (the unified throw of §4.5) to the block
covariance is reported. A corrected 4D covariance would use the actual minus and plus variation
endpoints; MAT’s mean-centered covariance blocks, normalized by 1/N ; one fixed estimator seed;
and mean-centered joint throws, with the mean shift reported separately. It would not subtract a
scalar jitter floor. Because the single-pass shifted observables exist only for events selected at the
central value, re-selected event loops (§4.7) would be needed to bound migration effects.
Bin-identical comparison to the published low-recoil measurement. Supplemental data
from Ref. [8] (44-cell d2 σ/(dEavail dq3 ) with its full covariance, distributed inside the public arXiv
source package) permit a direct quantitative cross-check. Both measurements are merged onto their
maximal common grid, the edges present in both binnings. Our (pT , p∥ ) marginalization is restricted
to p∥ < 20 GeV/c to mirror the Ascencio muon selection (θµ < 20◦ , 1.5 < pµ < 20 GeV). Together,
these give two super-cells, Eavail < 0.4 GeV in q3 ∈ [0.4, 0.6) and [0.6, 1.2) GeV: the low-recoil 2p2h
region. Our central result lies 9.2 % and 6.0 % above Ascencio. No pulls or full-covariance χ2 are
reported (§7; Fig. 22). Both share MINERvA flux/detector systematics, treated here as independent.
The pµ and p∥ cuts differ by ≲ 6 % kinematic smearing at the 20 GeV boundary. The two analyses
also define true Eavail differently (§7.1): in this analysis’s simulation, Ascencio’s broader definition
moves 11.0 % of the population of our lowest true-Eavail bin (Eavail < 0.1 GeV) out of it. That shift
has the sign and location of the residual. The two common cells, however, span our three lowest bins
(Eavail < 0.4 GeV), and the aggregate shift over those bins has not been computed. The definition
difference therefore remains an alternative explanation that is not excluded. At the 10 % scale of
the residual, the comparison neither refutes nor validates the result. A comparison on all 44 cells
has not been made; it requires re-unfolding on the Ascencio edges, which is straightforward for the
unbinned method, and a systematic sweep on that binning.
