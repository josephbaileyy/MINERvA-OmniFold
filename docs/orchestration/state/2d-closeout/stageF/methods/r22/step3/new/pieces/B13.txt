Statistical methods for the multidimensional results and inference

This appendix gives the conventions for covariances and comparisons beyond the 2D result: rank,
ndf and finite-ensemble declarations for N-D covariances, the scalar five-dimensional diagnostic
reductions and interval definitions, the extended-fiducial band and pull fractions, and inference from
unfolded events and selected projections. The 2D methods and their worked examples are in App. A.

B.1

Rank, ndf and finite-ensemble declarations for N-D covariances

The 2D justification in §A.3.2 does not transfer to N-D. An earlier 5D covariance from the standard
production chain has 10 694 reported bins, effective positive rank 263 and infinite numerical condition
number. It is distinct from CZ , the five-axis covariance of the configuration-(a) central value, and
was built before CZ was chosen for use. Its 4825-bin 5D→4D projection retains rank 263 exactly.
The covariance is a sum of about 45 rank-one outer products, one for each two-endpoint MAT
systematic band, and of the statistical and machine-learning (ML) sample blocks; such a sum cannot
span 10 694 directions, so the rank deficiency follows from the construction. Quoting χ2 /nreported
would understate tension by roughly 40× in 5D or 18× in 4D.
For an N-D covariance, ndf is the retained rank, not nreported . Every N-D χ2 must state, where
it is quoted, (i) the inverse actually used, including the pseudo-inverse rcond or the truncation rank;
(ii) the retained rank, as ndf; (iii) the rank-truncation scan of that covariance, which is expected
to lack the smoothness that supports the 2D choice in §A.3.2; (iv) the identity of the covariance,
since the earlier 5D covariance, its 4D projection and the published 2D block differ in rank; and
(v) for each sample block in the sum, the ensemble size N , the biased 1/N or unbiased 1/(N − 1)
normalization, the effective inverted dimension p after truncation, and the finite-ensemble treatment,
or that none is applied. The measurements, the derivation and the requirement that N-D difference
statistics use a rank cut fixed in advance are in Ref. [37] (R1.15).
Finite-ensemble bias of the inverse. Item (v) records the ensemble size of each block; without it,
an uncorrected finite-ensemble bias could enter unnoticed. The inverse of a noisy sample covariance
is biased upward. For an unbiased 1/(N − 1) estimator over N independent Gaussian realizations,
the standard debiasing factor is (N − p − 2)/(N − 1). For this analysis’s production convention,


universe-mean centered with biased 1/N normalization, it is (N − p − 2)/N . The superseded
unified-throw 5D covariance has N = 160, truncated p = 100, giving 58/160 ≃ 0.363: uncorrected
precision is too large by ≃ 2.76. For fixed residuals this inflates χ2 and overstates tension; confidence
regions from the same over-tight covariance become over-optimistic.
No generic correction is prescribed. A sum of deterministic rank-one band terms and statistical
and ML sample blocks, such as the earlier 5D covariance from the standard production chain, has
no single debiasing factor. The standard factor also assumes independent Gaussian realizations
and a data-independent truncation dimension, neither established for a data-dependent cut. The
appropriate treatment depends on the covariance and inverse actually used, and is to be determined
from the quantities recorded under item (v).

B.4

Inference from unfolded events and selected projections

Unfolding correlates weighted events; a downstream likelihood treating them as independent can
underestimate parameter uncertainties [63]. Nominal weights do not specify uncertainty. Coherent
unfolding replicas could propagate it through the fit; resampling unfolded rows independently is not
equivalent to resampling inputs and repeating the learned estimator. Classifier and permutation
goodness-of-fit tests require null calibration for fitting and data reuse. No new validation result
accompanies these requirements.
The uncertainty target matters: separate bin or projection coverage does not imply simultaneous
coverage. A fixed linear projection y = P x propagates covariance as Cy = P Cx P ⊤ , with integrated
bin widths in P for densities. The identity neither calibrates that covariance nor accounts for regions
chosen after inspecting data.
Recent work constructs confidence intervals for selected functionals in constrained inverse problems [64] and joint confidence regions for multiple functionals [65], which could provide independent
benchmarks for specified integrals or contrasts. Their guarantees depend on the forward model, noise
law, and parameter constraints and do not transfer automatically to a learned response with finite
simulation and detector nuisances. A rank-deficient forward operator is also distinct from a rankdeficient sample covariance; neither truncating the latter nor validating a few projections certifies
the full distribution. Such a benchmark would supplement, not replace, the scalar five-dimensional
uncertainty construction that a measurement requires.

C

Unbinned background subtraction by negative-weight injection

Binned purity subtraction (§3.1) is the only step that uses analysis binning before final histogramming.
Each data event in reconstructed bin b receives the purity weight wpur (b) = max(0, Ndata (b) −
Nbkg (b))/Ndata (b). Injecting background simulation on the measured side with negative weight gives
a fully unbinned correction with the same bin-integrated estimand. Its agreement with the binned

correction is quantified on the 2D measurement below. It is the continuous version of the purity
weight recommended for nontrivial backgrounds in Ref. [18], and the natural treatment for the larger
backgrounds of the extended-fiducial analysis.
Scope of the numbers. The numbers in this appendix are not quoted uncertainties, and the
analytic derivation, the estimand identity and the displacement argument do not depend on any.
Which background treatment each quoted result uses is stated in §3.1, and by configuration in
§3.2 (Table 2). Negative-weight injection is studied here for its equivalence to binned purity and
as the extended-fiducial treatment; this appendix establishes no supported production path and
changes no default. The two covariance ratios are agreement diagnostics between two realizations of
one subtraction. Two provenance limits apply to the production numbers: the driver revision that
produced them is corroborated but not pinned, and no output records the job that produced them
(Ref. [37], R1.7).

C.3

Closed-form toy

A one-dimensional toy uses signal simulation N (0, 1), equally normalized data signal N (0.3, 1.1),
and background N (2, 0.7) contributing 16.7 % of pseudo-data. The known target (D − B)/S =
ϕ(x; 0.3, 1.1)/ϕ(x; 0, 1) is smooth and positive. A signed-weight gradient-boosted classifier recovers
it with median 6.8 % relative error, matching binned purity’s 6.7 % and reproducing it bin-for-bin
on the same problem. Omitting subtraction leaves 10.9 % residual-background bias. Results are
stable across classifier seeds (±0.3 %), with no numerical instability from negative weights. Moving
background into the sparse signal tail and deliberately over-subtracting makes the learned reweight
floor cleanly at zero where B > D, as anticipated.

C.4

Comparison on the 2D measurement

With identical unfolding settings, the histogram backend and a common classifier seed, the ME FHC
comparison isolates background treatment (Table 28). Total cross sections agree to −0.13 %, an
order of magnitude inside the ∼3 % purity correction. The per-bin ratio has median 1.000 and 1.4 %
RMS scatter across the plane (Fig. 35). Only one low-pT , high-background edge bin differs by more
than a few percent (−12.6 %); background concentrates there and subtraction is largest. Unbinned
subtraction reproduces the binned correction wherever it is well determined. Departures occur only
where the binned max(0, ·) and structural floor of §C.1 govern the result.

C.5

C.6

Which background treatment each production chain uses

Agreement between the two subtractions does not make the choice between them arbitrary, and the
production chains choose differently.
The standard N -dimensional chain uses purity. The entire standard five-dimensional chain,
that of the configuration-(a) central value and its five-axis covariance, uses binned purity (§3.1):
the central unfold, 169 vertical-band unfolds, 18 detector unfolds and 10 endpoint unfolds of the
re-selected lateral bands.
The existing endpoint unfolds, produced in July 2026, used purity, as established by elimination
from their job scripts and logs (Ref. [37], R1.17). Current production fixes the background treatment
in a configuration object that rejects every other mode. The standard job script passes the driver’s
background-mode option (--bkg-mode) explicitly from that configuration, and the value is written
into the record of every endpoint unfold as it is produced, so the background treatment of each new
component can be read from the component itself; this changes how the treatment is recorded, not
the treatment itself.

The standard chain uses purity by choice, on two grounds (Ref. [37], R1.8). First, a covariance
that mixes background treatments is inferior to one that uses either treatment consistently: a
lateral-band block built with negative-weight subtraction would be inconsistent with central and
vertical-band unfolds built with purity subtraction. Second, switching would have required rerunning
every unfold above and would have invalidated the flux-corrected covariance then just chosen for use;
this consequence, rather than the computing cost, decided the choice. The background treatment
has been revisited in two dimensions: the closure test below is its result, and the 2D result and its
covariance use the refined estimator. For the configuration-(a) five-dimensional central value, which
still uses purity, the revisit is open; the sixteen-cell result (§10) uses the refined estimator.

treatments. For publication, the extended-fiducial analysis designates negative-weight injection
with Stay-Positive refinement as its background treatment. The code fixes it, and a check stops
the run if any endpoint’s recorded mode differs or is missing; the number quoted in §12 was not
produced this way. The standard chain uses purity throughout. Each side of the measurement and
matched-control pair therefore has an internally consistent background treatment; uniformity across
the two sides is not claimed.


C.7

Displacement argument and scope of the agreement

Displacement across universes. The displacement argument rests on a measured input; it
assumes no identity of continuous estimands. The constructions share bin masses in the binned
limit (§C.2) but differ by within-bin shape terms (§C.1). Switching background treatment produces
a measured net per-universe displacement of order 0.1 %, including residual and shape effects, at
this measurement’s sub-percent genuine-background scale. Systematic covariance measures spread
across universes about their own mean. Similar small shifts of each universe leave that spread
nearly unchanged, explaining the 187-universe ratio 0.986, far from the purity correction’s ∼3 %.
This argument connects a handful-of-universes comparison to the 187-universe covariance; the full
187-universe rebuild in §C.5 tests it at 2D and passes.
Scope. The measured agreement concerns the 2D systematic and statistical covariances of §C.5 at
the stated configurations. It is not a theorem that the two background treatments give the same
covariance, because the underlying estimands are not pointwise identical, and covariance equivalence
for the continuous estimator or in five dimensions is not established. The five-dimensional evidence
is limited to the following two-universe spot check.
Limits of the five-dimensional evidence. There is no full five-dimensional 187-universe comparison of the two background treatments at the publication configuration (5 iterations, LightGBM). The
spot check uses one iteration and the histogram backend on the stored five-dimensional universe file:
under both treatments 2p2h:0 gives 3.014 × 10−38 with per-bin median 1.0005, and MaCCQE:0 differs
by −0.03 % with median 0.9990. Together with the displacement argument and the full-statistics
2D result, this supports quoting the standard chain with purity subtraction, with its background
treatment stated. It does not show that the background treatment is irrelevant in five dimensions,
which would require the full comparison. Raw negative weights destabilize the exact-split backend
(§C.1), but the standard chain uses LightGBM, so neither treatment is exposed to that instability
here.

C.8

Five-dimensional pseudo-experiments with both background treatments

The five-dimensional validation (§§9.1–9.3) reports end-to-end tests of both background treatments.
They cover the purity bias in the highest-W column at nominal truth, its reduction with refined
negative-weight subtraction, the data difference, and the estimator with higher refinement capacity.
That estimator is configuration (e), which gives the 3D and 4D central values and the sixteen-cell
result (§10) but not the configuration-(a) 5D central value; the quoted 2D central value uses the
same refinement capacity, in configuration (f).
Purity bias. Purity bias is 8–12 statistical standard deviations; signal-only experiments close.
Subtraction is exact per reconstructed bin, but the widest bin (W ≥ 3 GeV, background 13 % of
signal) contains background shape resolved by the unbinned classifier and missed by bin-uniform
purity. The within-bin shape term of §C.1 is therefore measurable, and it is direct evidence for
revisiting the background treatment of the configuration-(a) five-dimensional central value (§C.6).
The difference between five-dimensional purity and refined negative-weight subtraction on data is
the least computationally expensive estimate of its size on data.
Refined negative-weight subtraction. Refinement used the unfold’s deterministic GBDT
configuration, independent injection and subtraction halves of background simulation, and bootstrap

replicas that individually resampled every pseudo-event and refit refinement. It preserves the signed
total to 3 × 10−6 and clips 0.01 % to 0.05 % of events. Corner bias falls from 1.66 % to 0.24 %,
but residuals in some joint cells fail the nominal closure criterion. The criterion allows none of
the 153 reported quantities a mean residual beyond 3.6 standard errors of the mean (|t| > 3.6,
the two-sided 5% level, Bonferroni-corrected over the 153), or beyond half its bootstrap standard
deviation with |t| > 3. The data difference’s sign pattern matches simulated purity bias at an order
of magnitude smaller size; the difference is method sensitivity, not a measured bias. With either
treatment, the estimator does not follow generator-sized Eavail shape changes. No coverage test was
run. Reducing one bias does not itself qualify an uncertainty, so this result does not close the revisit
of the background treatment of the configuration-(a) five-dimensional central value (§C.6).
Refinement capacity. Stay-Positive classifier underfitting causes the residual nominal bias of
the refined subtraction. At the unfold’s GBDT setting (100 trees, 8 leaves), the positive-weight
probability is underfit, leaving up to 4 % reco-level subtraction error per (Eavail , W ) reco cell. With
400 trees and 31 leaves, error falls to 1.7 %, paired truth-level bias falls by more than 80 % in
every affected cell, and nominal background-inclusive closure passes on 20 development and 40 fresh
pseudo-experiments. Splitting background simulation into independent injection and subtraction
halves is not the cause.
Background treatment and choice of covariance. The choice of background treatment is
independent of the choice of covariance, and this appendix selects no five-dimensional covariance
for use. The lateral-band component from re-selected event loops is built, with five bands and ten
endpoint unfolds whose distinct checksums are listed in the record of the five-axis covariance of
the configuration-(a) central value. The status of that five-axis covariance and the scope of its use
are stated in §9.6. The choice of background treatment settles one of the conditions for using a
component; every other condition still applies.
