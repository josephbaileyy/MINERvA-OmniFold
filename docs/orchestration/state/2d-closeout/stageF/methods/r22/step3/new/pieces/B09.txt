The sixteen-cell five-observable measurement

This section gives a five-dimensional cross-section result with total uncertainties: the data result of
configuration (e) (§3.2) on 16 cells, R00–R15, with the reported covariance Ctotal + Cresp . It is a
separate product from the configuration-(a) five-dimensional central value of Secs. 8 and 9 and from
the five-axis covariance used with that central value (§9.6). Statements in those sections about that
central value and that covariance do not describe this result, and statements here do not describe
them. The section gives the cells and the result (§10.1), the covariance and what it is built from
(§10.2), the fake-data tests of the estimator’s bias against the covariance (§10.3) and the comparison
with generator predictions (§10.4).

10.2

The reported covariance

The reported covariance is Ctotal + Cresp , with Ctotal = Ce + Cmodel . Ce sums the statistical bootstrap
of configuration (e); the PPFX flux universes, weighted by the neutrino–electron and inverse-muondecay constraint; the interaction-model and detector bands, which reweight simulated events; the
lateral bands, which shift reconstructed kinematics (§4); the normalization; and the training-seed
block. With full-sample binning and deterministic training, the unfolds at the other seeds are bitwise
equal to the central one, so the measured training-seed block is zero in every cell. Table 13 gives
each group’s per-cell relative σ.
For Cmodel , the prior is reweighted in turn by the ratio of GENIE + MEC, NuWro or GiBUU to
GENIE CV, and the data are unfolded again. Each generator’s shift is the relative change of the
data result, and Cmodel is the unscaled sum of their outer products. It is generator-shape model
dependence, not a calibrated bound; its coverage at truths held out of its construction has not
been validated. The GiBUU reweighting is applied only below p∥ = 10 GeV/c; above it the prior
is unchanged. Between p∥ = 6 and 10 GeV/c, the GiBUU sample lacks the contribution from
Eν > 20 GeV, which is at least 4.9–5.3% of the cross section there in the GENIE CV and NuWro
samples (which stop at Eν = 50 GeV). The GiBUU shift contributes 0.890 of the trace of Cmodel , the
NuWro shift 0.095 and the GENIE + MEC shift 0.014. In every cell, 77–100% of the prior weight
lies next to a discontinuity larger than 0.1 in the reweighting ratio, created by the ratio’s statistical
gate or by a cut; the shifts are used unmodified.
For Cresp , the simulation’s reconstructed recoil energy is scaled by one standard deviation
separately for four deposit categories: protons, mesons, electromagnetic deposits and other deposits.
Cresp sums the covariance contributions of the four resulting shifts of configuration (e)’s data result.
It does not cover neutron or muon deposits, the effects of particle species, energy dependence and
resolution beyond a scale, the null distributions of the joint tests, the configuration-(a) products or
the two-, three- and four-dimensional results. The median per-cell relative σ of Cresp is 2.14%, and
adding Cresp to Ctotal raises the per-cell σ by a factor of 1.011–1.220 (median 1.048). A cross-talk
term, reported beside Cresp and not summed into it, has a median per-cell relative σ of 0.36%. Of
the trace of (Ctotal + Cresp )/f f T , Ctotal contributes 0.9262 and the four deposit categories 0.0168,
0.0436, 0.0071 and 0.0062.
Table 14 gives the correlation matrix of Ctotal + Cresp ; with the central values and the relative
uncertainties of Table 12, it gives the reported covariance to the printed precision.

Fake-data tests of the estimator’s bias

The fake-data tests compare the estimator’s bias at truths other than the simulation with the
reported covariance. Pseudo-data are generated at 12 truths and unfolded with configuration (e).
The mean unfolded result is compared with each truth on the 16 cells, through χ2 with 16 degrees of
freedom and the per-cell pull z. The truths are the simulation’s prior; the five hybrid truths of the
generator tests (Sec. 11), each a prediction on the fine grid; the historical shapes W1, W2 and W3

tilt of the q3 distribution at fixed (Eavail , W ). A truth from a Cmodel generator is compared with the
covariance without that generator’s shift. A truth is listed as failing when p < 0.01 or when any cell
has |z| > 2. These are not held-out truths, and the detector response is not probed. Table 15 gives

10.4

Comparison with generator predictions

Table 17 compares the result with five generator predictions. Each gives the χ2 against Ctotal +
Cresp + Vg , where Vg is the prediction’s finite-sample (diagonal) covariance, with its number of
degrees of freedom, and the shape-only χ2 , with the normalization free. No diagonal-only χ2 is
formed. GiBUU’s sample has no events above Eν = 20 GeV, so it is compared on the 8 cells below
p∥ = 4.5 GeV/c only, and no value is formed on the other 8. In the GENIE CV and NuWro samples,
at least 11.71% and 12.59%, respectively, of the cross section above p∥ = 4.5 GeV/c comes from

and NuWro fractions, not GiBUU values, are what a sample stopping at Eν = 20 GeV would miss.

Tune v1 is the simulation’s prior, not an independent prediction. Because Cmodel holds the GENIE
+ MEC, NuWro and GiBUU shifts, those comparisons each use a covariance built in part from the
compared generator, and the GENIE CV comparison does so through the reference of the ratios.
