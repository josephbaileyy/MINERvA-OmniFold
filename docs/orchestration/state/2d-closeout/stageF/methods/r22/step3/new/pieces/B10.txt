Calibrated joint tests of generator predictions

This section tests five generator predictions jointly in the five observables (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ), each
in total and in shape. Each test evaluates a hybrid null hypothesis about one prediction (§11.1)
against a null distribution obtained by simulating the full analysis chain. The tests are hypothesis
tests, not a measured cross section, and they add no reportable uncertainty. They neither use nor
validate the five-axis covariance of the five-dimensional central value unfolded with purity-based
background subtraction (configuration (a), §9.6). The section gives the design, the six conditions
of the declared model (§11.4), the decisions (§11.5; lost pseudo-experiments: §11.6) and the later
sensitivity studies. Reference [52] gives the specification and the decisions, and each later study’s
own reference is cited where the study is described.
Two properties must be distinguished. The coverage of unfolded intervals is the rate at which
they contain the true cross section. The calibration of a test is the rate at which it rejects a
true hypothesis. Calibration can be obtained without validated intervals by simulating the whole
analysis chain at the hypothesis, so that the estimator’s bias enters the simulated null distribution.
A calibration obtained in this way is valid to the extent that the simulated chain reproduces the
analysis of the data. Unfolded results can be compared with any prediction at truth level without
simulating each generator through the detector. For hypothesis tests the advantage is smaller. A
test at reconstruction level would avoid the regularization bias, but it would still need the same
response model. Each external prediction would have to be folded through the analysis simulation’s
response, which is the hybrid construction of §11.1, with the same composition below the fine grid.
The tests of this section instead calibrate the unfolded statistic in simulation, so that the bias enters
the null distribution rather than the decision. We have not compared their power with that of a
reconstruction-level test.

11.1

Hypotheses, grid and statistics

For each of five predictions G (the MINERvA Tune v1, GENIE 2.12.10 without and with MEC,
NuWro 21.09 and GiBUU 2019; §8.5), the null H0 (G) is that the true cross section equals G on
every cell of a fine five-dimensional grid. Below that grid, the null truth has the MINERvA Tune v1
simulation’s event composition, varied in each pseudo-experiment by the drawn interaction-model
universes. Every fine-cell integral is held fixed in that variation. The background is the Tune v1
simulation’s, with its uncertainty bands. This hybrid null is the hypothesis the calibration simulates.
For the Tune v1, which is the analysis simulation itself, no external prediction is combined with the
Tune v1 composition. Three requirements are distinct. The hybrid null is defined exactly on the
finite grid, and no convergence is needed to define it. Whether it represents the physical generator
prediction depends on condition (i) (§11.4). Whether the calibration reproduces the analysis of the
data is calibration fidelity (conditions (ii)–(iv)). Each prediction is tested in total (rate and shape

together) and in shape alone: 10 tests. The fine grid is the analysis binning of §3.5, 14 × 16 × 7 × 7 × 6
bins in (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ); the test cells below are its integrals.
The tests are evaluated on a coarse 35 grid in (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 , W ) with edges pT ∈
[0, 0.55, 0.85, 4.5] GeV/c, p∥ ∈ [1.5, 3.5, 6, 60] GeV/c, Eavail ∈ [0, 0.4, 1.5, 100] GeV, q3 ∈
[0, 1.2, 2, 100] GeV and W ∈ [0, 1.4, 2.2, 100] GeV. Each inner edge is the fine-grid edge
nearest a tertile of the weighted simulated truth distribution of the signal sample in that coordinate,
so the cells keep physical bin widths. That distribution is taken from the development simulation.
The tests are restricted to the 109 supported cells (J). For GiBUU, whose sample lacks high-energy
neutrinos, the domain is the 72 cells with p∥ < 6 GeV/c. The grid specifies a finite hybrid hypothesis
and a reporting statistic. It does not turn OmniFold’s event-level training into binned unfolding.
Two statistics are formed from the unfolded cell values F and the prediction µG . The total
(rate-and-shape) statistic is Ttot = r⊤ Σ−1 r with r = F − µG . The shape statistic Tsh has the same
form on the normalized cell fractions, with the normalization Jacobian applied to Σ and one cell
dropped. The metric Σ = V + diag Var(µG ) adds the prediction’s own finite-sample variance to V .
Here V is a Ledoit–Wolf-shrunk covariance [53] of 194 pilot pseudo-experiments at the Tune v1 null.
The metric defines how hypotheses are compared, and so it affects power. The null calibration below,
not the metric, sets each test’s size.
The prediction variance is diagonal because each simulated event of a prediction falls in one cell.
The pilot’s seeds are disjoint from those of the calibration, but it reuses the same simulated events
and omitted the MINOS-efficiency band. The diagonal of V matches the calibration’s null variances
(median ratio 1.00). The shrinkage, of intensity 0.015, floors about 80 of the 109 nearly degenerate
correlation directions. At the Tune v1 null the statistic therefore has a whitened trace of about 47
rather than 109. Since V was fixed before the calibration, this affects power only (§11.5). The power
at other shrinkage intensities has not been measured.

11.2

Calibration within the implemented simulation model

The null distribution of each statistic is obtained from pseudo-experiments that run the full analysis
chain at the null truth, using the joint-test estimator (§9.3). That estimator is deterministic
LightGBM OmniFold with five iterations and the negative-weight background treatment, whose
refinement classifier has 400 trees and 31 leaves. Each pseudo-experiment
• draws Poisson pseudo-data from one half of the simulation together with an independent
background source, refits the background-refinement classifier, and unfolds with the estimator
trained on the other half;
• draws one of 100 flux universes and every one of the 34 interaction-model bands at one of its
universes;
• draws the weight-based detector bands (MINOS muon-matching efficiency and the GEANT4
neutron, pion and proton interaction reweights) and the five muon and beam-angle lateral
bands through a linear surrogate;
• draws a 1.4 % normalization, the data’s rounding noise (taken from 20 real-data re-unfolds
with rounding-level input changes), and the prediction’s finite-sample residual.
The data are unfolded once, with the full simulation. The Monte Carlo p value of a test is
(k + 1)/(B + 1), where k of B null pseudo-experiments reach the observed statistic. This p value
is never zero. The p value on which each decision rests, the claim p value, is the largest over the
declared claim variants, which shift the null. The variants move it by an upper bound on the
simulation-size difference between the pseudo-experiments and the data unfold (condition (iv) below),
and by ±2 δfine (condition (i)). The external nulls are strongly non-central, with non-centralities λ

of 318–6344 in these units. They are non-central because the calibration reproduces the estimator’s
pull toward its prior (§9.3). Each observed statistic is compared with that displaced distribution
(Fig. 31).
Generative model of one pseudo-experiment. Each calibration pseudo-experiment splits the
simulation at random into two halves (Table 21) and refits the background refinement. Its truth
equals G on every fine cell that the source half populates. Cells it leaves empty lower a coarse cell
by at most 0.2 %, or about 4 % of the null standard deviation (the per-experiment spread is below
1.5 % of it). On the signal, each interaction-model band is renormalized within each fine truth cell,
so it moves only the composition within the cell, and with it the efficiency and the migrations. The
flux universe and the band universes are drawn independently, and their P
weight ratios multiply.
The muon-energy, muon-resolution, and beam-angle lateral bands enter as b zb ∆b , with ∆b from
endpoint unfoldings of the data. The detector weight bands include the GEANT4 hadron-interaction
reweights. Because the ∆b come from the observed data, the null distributions are conditional on
these data-derived inputs, which would differ in a repeated experiment. That conditioning has not
been validated.

11.3

Multiplicity, stopping rule and design

The ten tests are carried out with the Holm procedure [54] at familywise level α = 0.05. A determinacy
requirement also applies: a step rejects only when the 95% Clopper–Pearson interval [55] of k/B
lies entirely below its Holm threshold. Calibration proceeded in batches of 200 pseudo-experiments
per null, to at most 1999, under a sequential rule that stopped a null once both of its decisions
were determined. A null stopped when a 99.5% Clopper–Pearson interval of k/B lay entirely on
one side of each of its thresholds and met a fixed precision requirement. The decision rule uses the
upper end of the two-sided 95% interval, a one-sided 97.5% upper bound. Two assumptions are
made: (A1) each true null’s calibrated tail probability is uniformly distributed or conservative, and
(A2) the pseudo-experiments’ exceedances are independent given the data. Under A1 and A2, the
probability that this upper end falls below the threshold, at any number of pseudo-experiments up
to 1999, is below the threshold. This holds whatever rule determines when sampling stops, including
the budget extensions. The decision rule therefore controls the familywise error rate of the ten
decisions at 5% under these assumptions. That rate is the probability of falsely rejecting at least
one true hypothesis among them. The error control applies to the decisions, not separately to the
point estimates (k + 1)/(B + 1). Under these assumptions, the bound FWER ≤ 0.8902 α < 0.9 α is
derived in §B.5. A2 requires the lost pseudo-experiments to have been lost independently of their
outcomes, which has not been shown. When rerun, however, none of them reaches the observed
statistic (§11.6). The calibrated size is averaged over the nuisance priors: at a fixed true value of the
nuisance parameters it can exceed α.
The joint-test estimator (§11.2) was chosen before the design was fixed. The hypotheses, statistics,
variants, multiplicity rule, stopping rule and every input were fixed before any production pseudoexperiment or observed statistic, with two data-informed elements. First, the design’s reviewers had
seen descriptive per-cell residuals of the data before it was fixed. Second, a bounded extension of the
production budget was decided during production, while the calibration’s monitoring output showed
observed claim p values, so that the sequential rule, rather than the budget, could set the stop. No
statistic, variant, threshold or stopping rule was changed, and the familywise bound above holds
whatever rule determined when sampling stopped. An independent recomputation from the stored
products, with separately written code, reproduces every p value and decision of the production
evaluation. It verifies the calculation, not the adequacy of the calibration. The calibration is valid for
the simulated null to the extent that the pseudo-experiment chain reproduces the analysis applied to
the data. No rejection rate has been measured for null pseudo-data processed like the data, which
would require an independent simulation sample.
Three studies followed the decisions: the scan of the shift multiplier (§11.5), the rerun of the lost
pseudo-experiments (§11.6), and the matched coarse-projection comparison (§11.7). They are for
reporting only, outside the decision rule. Each was specified before it ran; the projection comparison
was specified after the five-dimensional outcomes were known.

11.4

Conditions

Each decision holds under six conditions of the declared model. They are restated here in the terms
of this note, numbered (i)–(vi) as in the test specification, and fall into three kinds.
Numerical convergence. Condition (i) concerns the null truth below the fine grid. There it has
the Tune v1 composition of the hypothesis definition (§11.1), varied by the drawn interaction-model
universes, with every fine-cell integral held fixed at that of G, including the finite-sample fluctuations
of G’s simulation. The fine-grid convergence check has not shown convergence. In the L2 norm of the

per-cell relative changes, the last refinement, from a twofold-merged grid to the fine grid, is 0.50–0.86
of the whole change from the coarse test grid to the fine grid. In that norm the successive refinement
changes are therefore not shown to shrink, and the residual below the fine grid is unmeasured and
potentially material. The design assumes that each twofold refinement of the grid multiplies the
refinement change, measured in a proxy of the test metric, by a factor of 0.45–0.76. This factor
comes from the review of the design, and its derivation is not given. If the change kept shrinking
geometrically at that rate, the residual would be about one to three times the last refinement change,
δfine . Here δfine is the change of the noise-free unfolded result when the null truth is refined from the
twofold-merged grid to the fine grid. That estimate sets the claim variants at ±2 δfine , the central
extrapolated estimate, and the robustness variants, for reporting only, at ±3 δfine . The extrapolation
assumes geometric convergence. The measured L2 ratios also allow slower rates and larger residuals.
Measured with V fixed before any observed statistic, the claim variants move the external nulls
by 0.55–2.0 null standard deviations and the robustness variants by 1.07–3.6. For the hybrid null
as simulated, the residual plays no role. The variants carry the rejections over to G itself only if
replacing the Tune v1’s shape below the fine grid by G’s moves the noise-free unfolded result by no
more than ±2 δfine along δfine . Condition (vi) concerns the predictions’ own finite simulated samples,
which enter the metric as diag Var(µG ) and the calibration as a drawn residual term.
Calibration validity. The calibration’s validity, and what the independent recomputation verifies,
are stated in §11.3. Two known departures are bounded. Condition (iv) concerns the simulation size:
the pseudo-experiments unfold with half of the simulation and the data with all of it. The resulting
shift D, estimated from 16 pairs, is at noise level per cell, with a median |D|/f of 0.02–0.075%.
Along the null’s bias direction it is not always consistent with zero (proxy a/se of +4.7 for the
Tune v1 and −3.4 for NuWro), but it is at most 0.068 null standard deviations. That bound was
measured with V fixed before any observed statistic, and it supersedes the specification’s estimate of
about 0.05. The shift enters as the bias-aligned upper-bound variant. That shift was measured for
the mean only, in a process without noise or nuisance draws. A separate check reran the first 200
calibration pseudo-experiments of the NuWro, GENIE 2.12.10, and GENIE 2.12.10 + MEC nulls
with a quarter of the simulation. Between half and quarter simulation, it resolved no change of the
null distributions’ location, width, or tails: the medians agree within 0.074 null standard deviations,
and the widths lie within 0.975–1.010 of the half-simulation values. The only change it resolved is a
mean paired shift of 0.006–0.034 null standard deviations. Under an assumed extrapolation from
half to full simulation that is conservative by design, all three shape decisions, and so all ten, remain
rejected. The check concerns the simulation size only and is not a test of the data process. Every
pseudo-experiment resamples the same simulated events, so the null distributions are conditional
on that simulation. The decisions closest to their boundary are the shape tests of NuWro and
GENIE 2.12.10. With the Holm order held fixed, their observed statistics exceed the value at which
the decision would change by 1.11 and 1.30 null standard deviations, and by 1.01 and 0.67 with the
robustness variants at ±3 δfine . The other eight tests lie 2.7 to 203 null standard deviations from
that value, and 2.0 to 203 with the robustness variants. Crossing that value would make a test
undetermined, its Clopper–Pearson interval straddling the threshold, rather than not rejected. The
data unfold’s rounding-level nondeterminism, measured over the 20 re-unfolds, leaves every observed
p value unchanged except NuWro’s shape test (2/1752 to 3/1752).
Physical-model adequacy. Condition (ii) concerns the detector model. It is the simulation’s per
event, varied only by the drawn detector bands (MINOS muon-matching efficiency and the GEANT4
neutron, pion and proton reweights, each at ±1σ) and by the linear surrogate for the five lateral

bands. The surrogate has been checked only for the symmetry of its endpoints. The analysis’s
systematic set (App. A) has no recoil-energy-scale band, so the completeness of the hadronic response
is not established (§11.8). Condition (iii) concerns the interaction-model prior, the analysis’s set
of 34 interaction-model reweighting bands, with every band drawn in every pseudo-experiment.
Negative weights in two of them, LowQ2_1 and HighQ2_1, are clipped per universe. Measured
on the pilot, the effect of clipping them rather than keeping them is at most 0.35% of a band’s
shift in whitened norm. Condition (v) concerns the neutrino-energy range. The GiBUU sample
contains no events with Eν > 20 GeV (§8.5), and its truth outside the test domain (p∥ ≥ 6 GeV/c,
with truncated Eν ) enters through the migrations into the domain cells. No prediction contains
events with Eν > 100 GeV; only the data’s flux integral stops there, and the omission is negligible.
The use of the Tune v1 composition below the fine grid in (i) is also a modelling choice of this kind.

11.6

Lost pseudo-experiments and their recovery

Computing time limits interrupted or prevented 224 of the submitted calibration pseudo-experiments
(111 interrupted, 113 never started) and 53 power pseudo-experiments. The decisions in Table 22 were
evaluated on the completed draws. A sensitivity analysis computed before the lost outcomes were
known showed that every decision could change under a realizable assignment of them. Had every
interrupted draw exceeded the observed statistic, no test would have been rejected (p 0.013–0.019).
Among completed draws, runtime is not associated with the statistic (|ρ| ≤ 0.06), which supports
but does not certify the decisions.
The lost seeds were rerun under a procedure that was fixed, and reviewed independently, before
the rerun [57]. A determinism check fixed in advance (16 of 16 completed seeds rerun bitwise
identically) shows that each recovered draw is the lost draw, so every submitted draw has been
observed. No recovered draw reaches the observed statistic in any test, claim variant or robustness
variant, so no assignment of the lost draws changes any decision. The closest margin is 4.6 units of
T below the observed value of 868.6 (GENIE 2.12.10 + MEC shape, ±3 δfine variant). The next is
49.8 (GENIE 2.12.10 shape, same variant). With complete batches the predeclared rule would have
stopped the Tune v1, GENIE 2.12.10, GENIE 2.12.10 + MEC and GiBUU nulls one batch earlier
(B = 1200), with the same decisions.
The recovered draws were evaluated in two ways: with the variant shifts of the primary evaluation
held fixed (the fixed-shift evaluation), and with the shifts recomputed on all draws (the recomputedshift evaluation). The recovery’s checks are narrower in scope than the main recomputation. Only
the fixed-shift evaluation was cross-checked independently. The recomputed-shift evaluation was
reproduced only by a standalone rerun of the same code, a consistency check rather than an
independent recomputation. The earlier stops rest on the calibration run’s own re-application of
the sequential rule and have no independent check. The decisions of Table 22 remain those of the
evaluation before recovery, at the B at which its rule stopped. The observed outcomes of the lost
draws supersede the finding of the sensitivity analysis described above.

11.7

Matched coarse projections

The same calibrated tests are restricted to matched 3 × 3 coarse projections of the same cells,
onto (pT , p∥ ) and onto (Eavail , W ). The comparison uses the same calibration ensembles, with the
recovered draws included, and the same variants and claim rule. Its p values were reproduced
independently [58]. The projected tests do not enter the Holm family and change no decision of
Table 22. The comparison and its criterion were specified after the five-dimensional outcomes were
known but before the comparison was run. Under the criterion, a test would count as evidence of

additional joint discrimination only if both of its projected claim p values were at least 0.05 in both
the fixed-shift and the recomputed-shift evaluations. No test meets it, and the values are identical in
the two evaluations. Every test has p < 0.05, without a multiplicity correction, in at least one of the
two projections. This study therefore provides no evidence of additional discrimination from the joint
five-observable information. Table 23 gives every projected claim p value. In the muon-only (pT , p∥ )
projection, GENIE 2.12.10 is not rejected (p = 0.48 in total, 0.63 in shape; a post hoc observation),
whereas its (Eavail , W ) projection is. Hadronic information therefore participates in that rejection.
This does not establish added power of the joint test over all matched projections, or accept GENIE
as true. The powers of the joint and projected tests have not been compared.

11.8

Hadronic-response condition and recoil-energy sensitivity

MINERvA’s recoil-energy analyses assign a hadronic-energy response uncertainty derived from
test-beam calorimetry [59], separate from the GEANT hadron-interaction reweights [3, 8]. In
the low-recoil measurement the resulting hadronic-energy uncertainty rises to 10 % at its highest
three-momentum transfers, 0.9 < q3 < 1.2 GeV [8]. The declared nuisance model here includes
the reweights but no calorimetric response band. Such a band would act on all five nulls together,
through the shared detector simulation and unfolding. A response mismodelling could move each
observed statistic in either direction, and a band drawn in the calibration would also broaden and
could shift each null distribution. The net effect on each p value is unmeasured. The published
comparison in muon kinematics alone, whose observables do not use the reconstructed recoil energy,
also disfavours the same generator families [3]. It provides context for a disagreement involving
muon observables, but the hypotheses tested here use a different estimator and calibration. The
GENIE 2.12.10 projection result of §11.7 shows that the information rejecting that prediction involves
the hadronic axes, on which a response band would act.
In an exploratory sensitivity study of the response [60], the data are re-unfolded with the
simulation’s reconstructed recoil energy scaled by ±4 %, and the ten tests are re-evaluated against
the null distributions used for the decisions. This scale is the level of data–simulation agreement
reported for test-beam protons, pions and electrons of 0.35–2.0 GeV/c [59]. Neutrons and highermomentum hadrons, which matter at high W , lie outside that sample. The ±4% scale is not a
validated uncertainty prescription. A re-unfold with unscaled recoil reproduces the decisions of

Table 22, with every claim p value inside the range spanned by the data’s rounding variations. At
both −4% and +4% every one of the ten rejections holds. The observed statistics move by up to
about 11 % from their recorded values. Nine tests keep k = 0. NuWro’s shape test, the closest to its
null, changes from k = 1 to k = 3 of 1751 at +4% (p = 0.0023, against a Holm threshold of 0.05).

The study measures the sensitivity of the observed statistics and decisions to one coherent scale
of reconstructed available energy and recoil energy, applied after the energy calibration at a single
magnitude. It leaves untested (a) the response of the null distributions, which were not regenerated
with the variation although they are simulated through the same detector model, so the size of this
omission is unmeasured for these strongly non-central nulls; (b) particle-species, energy-dependent

Generation checks. For GiBUU, the sampling-closure check failed on the hydrogen sample. A
bias check of that check’s premise was added post hoc. Its criterion, a change of at most 0.1 σc in
every cell, was declared before the check was computed. The check met the criterion: it moved the
CH prediction by at most 5.1 × 10−4 σc . Here σc is the standard deviation of the cell’s result over

is flagged rather than assumed negligible.

11.10

What the tests establish

The comparisons recover the generator disagreements already established by the published twodimensional measurement on the same data [3]. Their methodological contribution is calibration
within the implemented simulation model, which puts the estimator’s bias toward its prior and
the declared nuisances into each null distribution. The rejections are global assessments of the five
specified predictions under the declared model. Assigning a discrepancy to a kinematic region or
nuclear mechanism would require separate tests. The rejections apply to the generator versions listed
in §8.5, and they retain all six conditions of §11.4. Extending the inference would require a response
band drawn through the calibration and a demonstrated bound on the residual below the fine grid.
