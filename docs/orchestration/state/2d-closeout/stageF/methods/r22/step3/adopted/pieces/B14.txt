Detector-response-mismatch closure test

The truth-shape and hidden-variable closures in §5 test recovery of deliberately modified event
populations. They do not directly test truth-distribution bias from a mismatch between the pseudodata detector response and OmniFold’s nominal response. Kevin McFarland suggested this distinction
and proposed stressing the hadronic record, especially for quasielastic events, to inspect low-W bias
(private communication). This appendix gives a validation protocol for such a test, not a result or
an uncertainty component. Its first stage has been implemented but not yet run: no output has
been produced and no pass threshold has been set.
Reconstruction-only test. The test uses the ordinary five-dimensional closure sample. After the
pseudo-data arrays are copied, and before background mixing or classifier training, it changes only

Outputs include the full five-dimensional unfolded/reference residual, one-dimensional Eavail and
W projections, and an (Eavail , W ) ratio map. The primary observable is the signed bias versus W ,
with W < 1.1 GeV reported separately from the high-W tail. The global integral is a control rather
than the decision statistic. The implementation runs only as a pseudo-data closure and only on the
five observables, and cannot be combined with truth-reweight injection or bootstrapping. It labels
every output as a diagnostic that must not be quoted.
Physically correlated follow-up. The reconstruction-only test is by design a computationally
inexpensive adversarial stress test; it is not a detector model. A more faithful follow-up would select
quasielastic events, shift the leading-nucleon energy up and down, and recompute reconstructed
Eavail , q3 , W , and any affected selection from the altered low-level record. The current per-event
outputs hold only scalar observables, not that low-level record, so the follow-up requires dedicated
diagnostic output from the event processing. It is deferred unless the reconstruction-only test shows
material bias or the collaboration asks for the stronger response model.
Interpretation. The test leaves the quoted central value and the current covariance construction
unchanged. A small observed bias would support stability of the (Eavail , W ) interpretation only
within the tested response envelope. A large bias would require a decision on the response model or
an uncertainty component, and would not be absorbed silently into a non-closure error.

Additional recent measurements. The low-/medium-energy comparison of quasielastic-like
cross sections unfolds three variables simultaneously, pT , p∥ , and available recoil energy [44]; it
has the dimensionality of our three-dimensional result, with a different signal from our inclusive
one. The shallow-inelastic measurement reports one-dimensional neutrino and antineutrino cross
sections [49]. Other measurements report single-differential charged-pion production down to zero
pion kinetic energy [95] and inclusive antineutrino cross sections versus pT on several targets [96].
These channel-specific additions are not plotted in Fig. 15.

Future work: point-cloud representations

We are developing unfolding with point-cloud transformer (PET) representations of the full event,
which could use low-level detector and particle information that the scalar observables of this note
summarize. That work, in progress, is documented separately. No central value, uncertainty or
comparison from it is used or claimed in this note.
