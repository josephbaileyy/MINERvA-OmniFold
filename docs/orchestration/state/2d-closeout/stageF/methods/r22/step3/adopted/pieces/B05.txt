Comparison to the published measurement

The published and unbinned results share the data and the flux, detector and generator systematics,
and their cross-covariance is not available. Every χ2 and standardized difference here is therefore
descriptive, not a calibrated goodness-of-fit test.
Over the 205 reported bins, the total cross section reproduces the published result [3] to 1.0 %
(ratio 1.010; Table 6). The median per-bin ratio is 1.005; 86.8% of bins agree within 5 % and 95.1%
within 10 %. Indicative values of χ2 per reported bin are 2.27 with the published covariance alone
(analysed in §6.4) and 0.929 with the sum of the published covariance and that of this measurement.
The latter, χ2 /ndf = 0.929, double-counts the flux, GENIE and MINOS systematics that the
two results share. With the covariance of this measurement alone, χ2 is computed only in the
covariance’s well-constrained subspace, with a truncated spectral pseudo-inverse. The per-bin pulls
and the truncated result are therefore quoted together, and an unconstrained pseudo-inverse is not
used as a goodness-of-fit test on its own. The regularization scan (App. A.4) was made for the
exact-split cross-check on an earlier covariance and was not repeated for the quoted one.
Per-bin differences standardized by the published uncertainty (Fig. 8) are centered (mean 0.060),
with RMS 0.54, well inside the published uncertainties. Figures 9–10 compare the unfolded result
with simulated truth, the MC prior, in the published panel format.

6.4

Eigenmode analysis of the published-covariance tension, χ2 = 2.27

With the published uncertainties as a descriptive scale, the central values have a pull RMS of 0.535
(a diagonal-only χ2 /ndf would be 0.29), whereas χ2 per bin with the full published covariance is 2.27.
The eightfold increase lies entirely in the off-diagonal structure of the published covariance, and
it is a difference in shape, not normalization (χ2norm = 0.04 against χ2shape = 465). The eigenmode
decomposition (Fig. 11) spreads it over many modes: the largest has a paper-covariance-standardized
component of magnitude 4.8, and the next nine 3.4–3.8. The 10 smallest-λ modes carry 9.6 %
of the total χ2 . Of the five largest contributors, two lie in the smallest-λ tenth of the spectrum.
Restricted to the 73 best-measured directions, χ2 /ndf is 0.66. Of the six leading modes, the three
in the highest-p∥ bins are all poorly determined directions (λ/λmax ≲ 4 × 10−9 ); the other three
are moderate-λ modes (λ/λmax between 4 × 10−6 and 6 × 10−5 ) and lie below the highest p∥ bins
(Fig. 12). No single pT or p∥ band carries more than 15 % of the total.
With the purity-based subtraction, the choice of GBDT estimator spreads the distance over
about one unit, a methodological band: the exact-split cross-check gives 3.66, HistGBT 2.70 ± 0.04,
and LightGBM with seeds 1–10 gives 2.64–2.75. Beyond this estimator dependence, the source of
the shape difference has not been identified. At the central value’s seed, the refined negative-weight
subtraction of the quoted result lowers the LightGBM χ2 per bin from 2.65 to 2.27. For the
exact-split cross-check, χ2 is larger and distributed differently: its components of magnitude 5–6 lie
in moderate-λ modes on the low-pT ridge of the cross-section peak. Its 10 smallest-λ modes carry
3 % of the χ2 , and the 73 best-measured directions give χ2 /ndf = 1.42 (App. A.5). Going from 5 to
10 iterations changes the LightGBM (seed 1) value by a negligible +0.01. App. A.5 gives the full
treatment and Figs. 11–12 the eigenmode-localization maps.

Comparison to GENIE MINERvA Tune v1

GENIE 2.12.6 [4] with MINERvA Tune v1 under-predicts the total cross section by ∼ 9 %

the prediction from the published ancillary files with both the published data and this measurement.

outside that space requires a new unfolding, as in the 3D extension (§7), which adds Eavail as a
classifier feature.
