Uncertainty quantification: constructing the covariance blocks

The uncertainty budget sums four blocks: systematic universes, statistical bootstraps, ML trainingseed variation, and a run-period block (§4.5). Their independence is an assumption: distinct random
streams and physics sources do not by themselves establish it after unfolding. The universe, statistical
and training-seed blocks are computed with the LightGBM estimator of the quoted 2D central value
(§3.2); the universe and statistical blocks use its training seed (seed 42), and the training-seed block
uses five seed settings that include it. A 3D budget built with the analogous 3D grid construction is
not quoted, and no 4D uncertainty magnitude is reported (§7.8). App. A gives the full mathematics
and worked examples using the reported numbers (N-D declarations in App. B). The resulting
budget and its agreement with the published total are in §6.3; what the covariance includes and
omits, and the conventions it follows, are in §4.6.


4.1

Systematic universes

Detector and model systematics use the July universe sweep: the 44 error bands and 187 universes
of the MINERvA Analysis Toolkit (MAT), identical to the standard systematics set of the published
analysis. Each universe is re-unfolded with LightGBM at seed 42 and compared with the quoted
2D central value, unfolded with the same estimator and seed. The five lateral bands, which
shift reconstructed kinematics rather than event weights, are taken from re-unfolds of re-selected
universes (§4.7) at the same seed. The band sum omits the nonresonant-pion band Rvp1pi, which
encodes the same shift as Rvn1pi (§4.6); it holds 43 bands plus the flat normalization. Per-band
covariances follow MAT’s universe-mean centering and biased 1/N normalization. For a ±1σ pair
the covariance is the outer product of the half-difference of the two shifts, excluding any common
endpoint displacement from the central value (CV). The 100 PPFX hadron-production flux universes
use the same mean-centered 1/N formula.
Ranked by per-bin median fractional standard deviation, the largest bands of the 2D universe block
are the flux (3.71 %, with MAT’s ν–e and inverse-muon-decay constraint weights), the MINOS muon
energy scale (Muon_Energy_MINOS, 2.36 %), the MINOS muon-match efficiency (MinosEfficiency,
1.50 %) and the MINERvA muon energy scale (Muon_Energy_MINERvA, 1.44 %). Figure 4 shows

4.2

Statistical uncertainty: Poisson bootstrap

The statistical block jointly bootstraps data and simulation with 300 per-event Poisson(1) weight
replicas, each fully re-unfolded with the LightGBM estimator at the central value’s training seed
(seed 42), and is their sample covariance about the replica mean (Fig. 5). Each replica draws its
weights in three independent streams, for data, signal simulation and background simulation; possible
shared events between the two simulations were not assessed. Because the background simulation
is resampled in every replica, the separable background-only term is contained in the statistical
block. The reconstructed-level and truth-level weights of a simulated event share one draw, which
preserves within-event migration correlations. Fixing the GBDT seed per p
replica separates bootstrap
and training-seed variances. With 300 replicas the Gaussian formula 1/ 2(N − 1) gives a relative
standard error of about 4.1 % on each per-bin σ, and the per-bin σ from a nested half (150) of the
replicas is a median 1.000 times that from all 300. The replica
√ mean differs from the central value by
a per-bin median of 4.37 standard errors of the mean (σ/ 300), or 0.25 of the statistical standard
deviation. Because the block is the covariance about the replica mean, it does not include this offset.
The bootstrap block is smaller than the published statistical block (ratio of square-root traces 0.407).
This observed covariance difference does not demonstrate a causal efficiency advantage (App. A.4).
The statistical block quoted here has had no coverage test: the fixed-truth coverage studies of
§5.2 scored two earlier statistical bands, not this block. No coverage test on held-out simulation has
been run, and no design for one has been registered in advance. Because the fixed-truth test’s truth
equals the prior, it also cannot decide whether the smaller statistical block reflects an efficiency gain,
regularization bias or undercoverage.

4.3

ML/optimization uncertainty: training-seed variation

The training-seed block is the sample covariance, over five training-seed settings, of the mean of
20 bootstrap replicas at each setting. Each setting is a triplet (s, s+1, s+2) of seeds for the step-1

classifier, step-2 classifier and miss regressor, with s ∈ {42, 45, 48, 51, 54}; the block has rank 4
(§A.2.1). It is the smallest block, with a per-bin median fractional standard deviation of 0.048 %.
With four degrees of freedom, its variance has a Gaussian relative standard error of about 71 %.
Its per-bin σ is a median 0.27 of that of the earlier ten-seed block, because the bootstrap × seed
interaction, which dominated the earlier scan, is already inside the fixed-seed statistical block; the
training-seed block carries it only at its 1/20 share, a median 0.763 of its diagonal. The interaction
itself and the universe × retraining term are reported and not added (§4.6).
No block contains the dependence on the learner. The central value and the exact-split cross-check
differ by a per-bin median of 1.03 % (maximum 17.6 %; §3.2); they differ in background treatment as
well as in learner. One reseeded exact-split run differs from the unseeded one by a per-bin median of
0.18 % (maximum 1.6 %). The spread of the exact-split estimator over seeds is unmeasured (§3.2).

4.4

Further checks of the 2D covariance

Seven universes of the July sweep (§4.1) are reproduced to ≤ 1.4 × 10−11 by the committed driver;
the others are accepted by inference, because the same code produced them (Ref. [37], R1.16).
For two bands whose universes barely change the unfolding inputs, EtaNCEL and NormNCRES,
the responses are consistent with instability of the GBDT training contributing at the scale of the
training-seed variation (§4.3). They neither establish the mechanism for every small band nor justify
subtracting an overlap from the budget.
Each lateral band of the quoted universe block uses a single unfold at the central training seed,
so it includes that seed’s training noise. The flux-universe index identity behind the 1/Φu rescale is
established by the code chain and by an exact test: 900 events × 100 universes match to 5.5 × 10−14 ,
and a control shifted by one universe fails at ≥ 0.245 (Ref. [37], R1.13). In all 12 playlists the
production flux weights equal those of the constraint file and of the earlier 2D budget, with which
§4.6 compares the quoted construction.

4.5

Combination and the unified-throw test

The nominal covariance is the block sum C = Csyst + Cstat + CML + CPL , which assumes the blocks
are independent and the unfolding responds linearly to simultaneous perturbations, so that per-band
covariances add. The unified throw tests linearity directly (App. A.2.4; §9.6) by perturbing all
reweightable bands and the flux universes simultaneously in each event and re-unfolding jointly. The
procedure interpolates asymmetrically between actual −1σ and +1σ event-weight endpoints, fixes
the estimator seed for every throw, and centers the joint covariance on the throw mean. That mean’s
displacement from CV is stored separately as a per-bin vector and its norm; per-band endpoint
displacements are not stored. The matched block-sum comparator re-unfolds both endpoints of
each band using MAT’s universe-mean centering and biased 1/N normalization. A single-throw
superposition probe is insufficient to test nonlinear response. No scalar run-to-run jitter floor is
subtracted. The configuration-(a) 5D result uses the strict joint ensemble and matched-endpoint
comparator.
In the five-axis covariance of the configuration-(a) central value, CZ , the per-source decomposition
is retained for attribution and the joint throws enter through the inflation of Eq. (5) (§9.6).
The run-period block CPL is an uncertainty assigned for an observed inconsistency between run
periods whose cause is unknown; it is not a measured systematic uncertainty from an identified
source. Data taken in different run periods are not mutually consistent within the other uncertainties.
After unfolding, the double ratio (data over simulated truth) of the data-taking periods (playlists)
1G–1M to the earliest periods, 1A–1F, has a p∥ step of (+6.89 ± 0.46)% at 8 GeV/c. Diluted into

the combined result, the raw shift of those playlists is small in each bin: every marginal z is below
0.7. Its p∥ χ2 against the earlier 2D budget is nevertheless 34.4, where noise would give 2.0. These
data cannot establish which period, if any, is correct.
The block is built from separate seed-42 unfolds of three run periods, the playlist groups 1A–1F,
1G–1M and 1N–1P, each unfolded through its own simulated detector response. The block uses each
period’s unfolded result over that period’s own simulated truth. Each period is compared with the
exposure-weighted mean of the three, so no reference period is needed; the spread, applied to the
central value, gives the block. It is added after the other blocks. It has rank two, and with three
period groups its own size is poorly determined. Its per-bin median fractional standard deviation
is 2.09 % (84th percentile 3.34 %, maximum 6.48 %); it is 1.17 % to 3.36 % on the p∥ projections
and 1.97 % on the integrated cross section. Added to the other blocks, it raises the per-bin σ by a
median of 5.8 % (at most 39.1 %), the integral by 9.9 % and the p∥ projections by at most 21 %; it
carries a median 10.6 % of the per-bin variance and 17.1 % of the integral’s. Taking 1A–1F as the
reference would give a smaller block (per-bin median 1.38 %, integral 1.09 %).
The block’s interpretation is limited by the small number of period groups, by possible shrinkage
from the regularization, and by the statistical and training noise it retains. Because the block is
built from the observed differences among the periods, it contains them by construction; this carries
no information about its coverage, that is, about whether its size is right. For the same reason, the
double-ratio χ2 against any total that contains the block is bounded whatever the data, so it does
not test the block. The raw 1G–1M p∥ χ2 is 34.8 against the other blocks alone and 2.59 with the
block added; these values indicate sensitivity only.

4.6

Scope, conventions and limitations of the 2D covariance

Status of the 2D result. We report a reproducible two-dimensional central value, with a
covariance computed with the central value’s own estimator, under the conventions stated below.
The result’s intervals, bias and covariance have not been independently validated. In particular, the
coverage of its intervals has not been validated on independent simulated samples, nor simultaneously
over all reported bins and projections. The covariance also gives no calibrated significance for
comparisons with generator predictions. Such validation would need an independent simulation
sample of production size, a validated generator of pseudo-data with continuously varied nuisance
parameters, and samples from independent event generators beyond the truth-level ones already
examined; none is available. We therefore make no claim about coverage, and goodness-of-fit values
computed with this covariance are indicative only. The following are not included in the covariance:
• the approximation in how the systematic-universe unfolds weight events that fail reconstruction,
whose effect has not been measured beyond the low-sensitivity test below;
• the dependence on the machine-learning estimator (§4.3);
• the dependence on the regularization, and departures of the detector response from its
parametrized family, which we assume absent; we assign no uncertainty for either;
• the dependence on the generator shape of the prior, which is reported beside the covariance as
Cmodel (below).
Generator-shape model dependence. The dependence of the result on the generator

dependence either.
Cmodel measures generator-shape model dependence; it is not a calibrated bound on the bias in
each cell. A closure test with NuWro-shaped pseudo-data and the configuration-(a) estimator has a
bias above 1 % in 127 cells. In 67 of them the NuWro shift of the quoted result reaches the bias, and
in 72 the quadrature sum of the other two generators’ shifts reaches it; these counts are descriptive.
Where a pooled cell, a noise-gated cell, or an unreported cell meets another cell, the construction
can draw a step of more than 0.1 in a generator’s reweighting ratio; the cells beside such steps enter
with their measured shifts. Identified after the measurement, these 52 cells hold 59.4 % of the trace
of Cmodel and carry most of its large shifts.
Conventions. Each systematic band’s covariance is computed about the mean of its universes
(constraint-weighted for the flux), with 1/N normalization, not about the central value. This follows
the MAT treatment of universes (§4.1), which we use as a stated convention. Across bands, the
per-bin median offset of a band’s center from the central value is 0.10 % to 0.55 % of the central
value, or 0.09–2.85 times the band’s σ. The statistical block is the covariance about the replica mean
(§4.2).
Approximations and omitted terms. The systematic-universe unfolds approximate the weighting of events that fail reconstruction, which enters through each universe’s completeness; the
approximation is not corrected. It was tested in two of the 187 universes, MaRES_0 and the flux
universe with the largest median completeness change (Flux:23), each at seeds 42 and 43. In neither
was an effect resolved above the training-seed noise. The tests compared the difference with the σ of
the earlier ten-seed block, which is about 3.7 times that of the training-seed block used here. They
exclude only a median effect above twice that σ, so an effect below the training noise is not excluded.
Separately, retraining moves these two universes’ shifts by a median 1.13–1.23 times that σ.
One systematic category provided in the input products, NormDISCC, is identically zero; we carry
it as zero, which we have not been able to confirm is intended. No separate uncertainty is assigned
to the counting of protons on target: the NuMI flux prediction carries a 2 % counting term by
convention, so a separate band would risk counting it twice. Two links in that argument, that the
flux universes carry the term and that the neutrino–electron constraint absorbs it, are inferred, not
verified. The twelve playlists are combined with one global exposure ratio rather than per-period
ratios, and the resulting bias is not corrected. At reconstruction level the pooled result is high, by
0.18 % in total and by at most 0.32 % in a marginal bin. After unfolding, the pooled result is 0.076 %
above the exposure-weighted combination of the period results on the integral, the same sign as
at reconstruction level. It differs from that combination by at most 0.39 % in a marginal bin (the
highest p∥ bin) and by at most 0.33 % in pT . In single cells the combination differs from the pooled
result by −1.83 % to 3.78 %, each with |z| at most 0.31.
Other limitations. Both programs that use the covariance for the reported results, the χ2
comparison (§A.3.2) and the program that draws Fig. 4, check that its stored set of cells equals

the published set, and the check passes in every run of both on the quoted covariance. Limitations
specific to one block are stated with that block (§4.2, §4.3, §4.4, §6.3) and, for the duplicated pion
band, below.
Seed-related terms reported, not added. The variance of the bootstrap × seed interaction is a
per-bin median 0.072 of the statistical block’s variance. For the universe × retraining term, in the
six tested universes (of 187) with a non-null shift, the standard deviation of each universe’s shift
over training seeds, with four degrees of freedom, is a median 1.08–1.36 times the σ of the earlier
ten-seed block. In those universes the universe block, built at one training seed, therefore carries
single-seed training noise of about the earlier seed block’s size; it is part of the block as built and
is not separated from the physical variation. In two of them the noise is comparable to or larger
than the shift: in 2p2h:0 the shift is 0.097 % against a seed standard deviation of 0.192 %, and
in MaCCQE:0 0.239 % against 0.210 % (per-bin medians). We infer that in expectation such noise
adds variance to a band rather than removing it, and, from the medians, that these two bands are
largely training noise. The universe × retraining values and the band-centering numbers cannot be
recomputed from the saved covariance products; they were therefore not recomputed independently,
and their verification is limited to the checksums of their inputs.
Comparison with the earlier 2D budget. The saved products and code of the earlier 2D
budget [42] show the following defects in its construction; the quoted budget corrects each. First, the
systematic sweep of the earlier budget (the May sweep) has a simulated truth input that differs from
that of its seed-42 reference: even an EtaNCEL universe that leaves the event weights essentially
unchanged differs in all 205 truth-input cells, while its saved reconstructed inputs agree. A common
displacement cancels to first order in the MAT mean-centered covariance. The mismatch does not
invalidate the entire covariance, and it supplies no correction to subtract. Because the May input
and code revision are not available, the mismatch cannot be attributed more specifically. The July
sweep, which the quoted universe block uses, does not have this mismatch. Second, the two bands
Rvn1pi and Rvp1pi encode the same shift under the configured nonresonant-pion tune, and the
earlier budget counted it twice; the quoted universe block drops Rvp1pi and counts it once (§4.1).
The band is small, 0.088 %, but the double count is a defect in the construction of the covariance;
no conclusion about the published analysis’s implementation follows. Third, the earlier budget did
not propagate background-template statistics. In the saved reconstructed histograms their error
exceeds one tenth of the data’s Poisson error in 60 of 205 bins, so neglecting them everywhere is
unsupported; this is a reconstructed-space diagnostic, not a measured unfolded uncertainty. The
quoted statistical block draws the background simulation as its own bootstrap stream (§4.2), and
the quoted universe block takes its lateral bands from re-unfolds of re-selected universes (§4.1). The
values printed for the earlier budget were not changed by these findings.

4.7

What changes for the higher-dimensional products

Two event-loop modes supply the lateral bands. The single-pass event loop, which writes every
universe in one pass, stores each lateral universe’s shifted observables only for events that pass
the selection at the central value, so it omits events that the shift moves into the selection. Reselected event loops re-run the selection with one lateral universe active at a time, so they include
these migrations. For the five kinematic bands (beam angle BeamAngleX/Y, muon resolution
MuonResolution, and the muon energy scales Muon_Energy_MINERvA/MINOS), the re-selected
event loops supply the lateral-band block of CZ ; higher-dimensional products built from the single-pass
shifted observables are not used for any quoted uncertainty. Replacing the single-pass lateral-band

block by the re-selected one moves that block’s square-root trace by −0.0288 %. This aggregate
change does not bound the change in any individual bin. Across the 10 694 reported bins the per-bin
ratio of re-selected to single-pass standard deviation runs 0.177 to 3.062; within the 1 % of bins with
the largest single-pass variance, which carry 74.11 % of the total variance, it runs 0.687 to 1.153. A
small share of the aggregate variance does not make a bin irrelevant to a quoted result.
Scope of the re-selected replacement. The replacement covers the five detector bands using
shifted reconstructed kinematics. MINOS efficiency and the three GEANT hadron-interaction bands
(GEANT_Neutron, GEANT_Pion, GEANT_Proton) keep their weight-based treatment and are
therefore outside the kinematic replacement. This classification does not independently validate
hadronic-response model completeness for the Eavail and W measurement.
The ML term CML of CZ (Eq. (4)) is the split-varied band: its ensemble fixes the estimator seed
at 42 and varies the train/test split seed. Each of its replicas refits every classifier on a random
training subset of the events and still reweights every event, the only use of a training subset in this
note (training details: §3.1). The band therefore measures split response and not the full estimator
stochasticity. A separate scan of √
twelve estimator seeds at fixed data and split covers the remaining
stochasticity. Its covariance has Tr C = 1.306 × 10−39 cm2 /nucleon, which is 87.5 % of that of the
split-varied band (1.493 × 10−39 cm2 /nucleon). Estimator stochasticity, not the particular train/test
partition, therefore dominates the split-varied band. The scan is an auxiliary robustness check, not
an independent covariance term, since split and estimator responses are not assumed
√ uncorrelated.
Both bands are minor components, about three percent of the combined budget in Tr C.
CZ is a separate construction in which the unified-throw covariance is centered on the central
value (the CV-centered variant, cv; §9.6). A 5D covariance construction that preceded it used both
mean-centered and CV-centered unified throws (§4.5); neither variant is used, and no magnitude
from either is quoted.
Subtracting the background separately in each of the 187 universe unfolds and in the CV changes
the square-root trace of the combined block sum by 0.28 % and CZ by 0.18 %, a numerically negligible
bound on the background-CV choice (Ref. [37], superseded values). The 4D and extended-fiducial
results have no covariance from this procedure, and CZ is not transferred to them.
