Detector-response-mismatch closure (protocol implemented; not
yet run)

The truth-shape and hidden-variable closures in §5 test recovery of deliberately modified event
populations. They do not directly test truth-distribution bias from a mismatch between the pseudodata detector response and OmniFold’s nominal response. Kevin McFarland suggested this distinction
and proposed stressing the hadronic record, especially for quasielastic events, to inspect low-W bias
(private communication).
Reconstruction-only test implemented in the pipeline. The first-stage diagnostic uses the
ordinary five-dimensional closure sample. After copying pseudo-data arrays, before background
mixing or classifier training, it changes only the reconstructed pseudo-data record

The nominal reconstructed simulation, all truth coordinates, event weights, and event membership stay fixed. The pseudo-data therefore have a deliberately incompatible conditional response;
OmniFold still uses the nominal response. The protocol requires both signs of ϵ, at an amplitude
fixed before the results are examined. A documented hadronic-response variation or a quantified
assumption should justify that amplitude; the inherited factor in reconstructed Eavail is not enough
on its own.
Outputs include the full five-dimensional unfolded/reference residual, one-dimensional Eavail
and W projections, and an (Eavail , W ) ratio map. The primary observable is signed bias versus W ,
reporting W < 1.1 GeV separately from the high-W tail. The global integral is a control rather than
the decision statistic. The implementation runs only as a closure on pseudo-data and only on the
five observables, and it cannot be combined with truth-reweight injection or bootstrapping. It labels
every output as a diagnostic that must not be quoted.
Physically correlated follow-up. The reconstruction-only arm is intentionally cheap and adversarial; it is not a detector model. A more faithful follow-up would select quasielastic events,
shift leading-nucleon energy up and down, and recompute reconstructed Eavail , q3 , W , and any
affected selection from the altered low-level record. The current scalar event-loop outputs lack the
information for that recomputation, so this arm needs dedicated diagnostic output from the event
loop. It is deferred unless the reconstruction-only test shows material bias or the collaboration asks
for the stronger response model.
Status and interpretation. This appendix records a validation protocol, not a result or an
uncertainty component. No response-mismatch output has been produced and no pass threshold has

been set. The test leaves the quoted central value and the current covariance construction unchanged.
A small observed bias would support stability of the (Eavail , W ) interpretation only within the tested
response envelope. A large bias would require a decision on the response model or an uncertainty
component, and would not be absorbed silently into a non-closure error.
