Statistical methods and the 2D comparison

This appendix gives the statistical methods of this note’s measurements, centered on the 2D
comparison: covariance inversion; the training-seed, bootstrap and universe estimators, with the
Poisson limit and the ±1σ pair covariance; their MAT-conformant construction and combination; the
χ2 , pull and shape-only conventions; the rank and conditioning of the covariances; the anatomy of
the tension in the 2D comparison; the closure procedures; and the provenance of the 2D comparison
numbers. It covers analysis-specific conventions, not general statistics. The χ2 convention used
throughout is Eq. (17) in §A.3.2. The N-D declarations, scalar-5D reductions, extended-fiducial
fractions and unfolded-event inference are in App. B; the 2D fixed-truth coverage test is in §5.2 and
the same-ensemble pull diagnostic in §5.3.

A.2.3

Multinomial → independent Poisson bootstrap limit

Drawing N events with replacement from an
P N -event sample gives counts (n1 , . . . , nN ) ∼
Multinomial(N, p) with uniform pi = 1/N and i ni = N . Each marginal ni ∼ Binomial(N, 1/N )
has mean 1, variance (N − 1)/N → 1, and converges
P to Poisson(1) as N grows (many trials, small
success probability, fixed expected count). The
ni = N constraint induces O(1/N ) correlations
that vanish at large N . The multinomial is an independent Poisson process conditioned on N counts;
removing that constraint gives independent Poisson(1) variables (Poissonization). We draw one
np.random.default_rng().poisson(1.0)
factor per event. For N ≳ 106 , our regime, the relative
√
total-count error is O(1/ N ), far smaller than the per-bin Poisson variance.

How MINERvA builds its published covariance

MAT’s C++ framework defines the covariance construction that the published paper leaves unspecified. It is the mean-centered 1/N per-band sample covariance and element-wise band sum used
here.
Ruterbories 2021, Section V. Three uncertainty categories — flux, detector response, neutrinointeraction model — evaluated by “re-extracting the cross section using modified simulations”, i.e.
the universe method, but with no covariance formula given. Specifics quoted: flux below 4% across
most of phase space thanks to the ν-e-scattering constraint (Valencia, arXiv:1906.00111); 1.4%
target-nucleon normalization; in-situ test-beam hadron response with per-particle GEANT inelastic

measurement covariance matrix”), with 205 degrees of freedom (Table I), plus a log-normal χ2 variant
motivated by the highly-correlated flux normalization (Peelle’s Pertinent Puzzle). What the paper
does not say. It leaves to MAT, or does not state, the per-band covariance construction, any ±1σ

External statistical floor of the quoted combined χ2 . The quoted 0.929 inverts the sum of
the paper’s covariance and ours, not our blocks alone. That sum contains the paper’s StatOnlyCov,
which has rank 205 and is near-diagonal (§A.4), so it places a variance floor under every eigendirection
of the combined matrix. It is the larger of the two statistical contributions. Our statistical block is
0.407 of it in square-root trace, and the square of that factor in trace (operands in §A.4). Each ratio
states which power of the trace it uses, because the two ratios describe the same comparison and
differ by more than a factor of two. An external, published block, rather than any ensemble of ours,
therefore sets the small-eigenvalue directions on which a finite-N debiasing factor would act. This
bounds the quoted number’s exposure to declaration (v) of §B.1.
Trace share alone cannot justify this protection: trace weights eigenvalues by λ, precision by 1/λ,
so even a negligible-trace block can dominate an inverse. Without the external floor, our finite-N
blocks fill Csyst ’s null space (rank 138 → 205, eigenvalues above 10−12 λmax ) and, inverted directly,
yield χ2 /ndf = 22.29 (§A.4). Protection depends on the source of the smallest retained eigenvalues.

Closure tests: detecting bias

The variance methods of §A.2 quantify the scatter of the unfolded result. Closure tests measure its
bias, a consistent offset from a known injected truth, which the variance methods cannot show. Each
of three tests injects a known MC deformation, unfolds, and compares the median per-bin |residual|
of the recovered answer with a threshold. The thresholds lie comfortably above the 0.166% ML floor
of the earlier ten-seed scan, which was in force when the tests were graded (Ref. [37], superseded
values: retired material). All use playlist-1A LightGBM 5-iteration unfolds. Thresholds and results
are in §5; procedures and otherwise unreported values follow.

Standing assumptions
1. Pulls and χ2 approximate bins as Gaussian: weighted sums of O(104 ) MC events justify the
central-limit approximation.
2. Independence of error sources. Eq. (16) requires no cross-correlations between ML,
statistical, systematic and run-period blocks. The bootstrap pins the GBDT seed (so ML noise
cancels at first order); universes pin the seed too; the training-seed block averages the same
20 bootstrap replicas at each seed setting and includes the bootstrap × seed interaction at its
1/20 share. Among the first three, cross-terms are higher-order. The run-period block is built
from separate unfolds of the same data, and its noise share is not subtracted (§4.5).
3. Same-ensemble 68.71% (§5.3) checks Gaussianity; it does not certify Cstat at the half-percent
level. The fixed-truth test (§5.2) found the diagonal of the first-built Cstat undercovering in
its tails (C2 = 0.912, unevenly across bins); the band was rebuilt in response, and the rebuilt
band has not been independently re-tested. The statistical block quoted here is a different
ensemble and has not been tested either.
4. Band independence in Csyst . Different physics sources are taken as uncorrelated; the one
known exception, the Bashyal flux ↔ muon-E joint block, is treated in §A.4.
5. Combined-χ2 double-count. The combined distance shares systematics with the paper’s
V and describes “what OmniFold UQ adds on top of V ”; it is no independent ours-only test
