The MINERvA experiment, data and simulation samples

2.1

Detector and beam

MINERvA [9] was a fine-grained scintillator-tracker neutrino experiment on Fermilab’s NuMI
beamline. Its central tracker comprises hexagonal planes of triangular polystyrene scintillator
strips in three stereo orientations, surrounded by electromagnetic and hadronic calorimetry. The
magnetized MINOS near detector, 2 m downstream, serves as the muon spectrometer, measuring
momentum and charge for MINOS-matched tracks. We use the medium-energy (ME) neutrino-mode
(FHC) exposure with ⟨Eν ⟩ ∼ 6 GeV. The flux is the a posteriori constrained NuMI ME prediction
of Ref. [10], combining PPFX hadron-production weights and the neutrino–electron-scattering
constraint, identical to the published measurement’s flux [3].

2.2

Data and simulation samples

The twelve ME FHC playlists (ME1A–ME1G, ME1L–ME1P) contain 1.057 × 1021 protons on target,
the same exposure as Ref. [3]. The selection below leaves 4 119 797 reconstructed data events.
The reference simulation uses GENIE 2.12.6 [4] and MINERvA Tune v1, applied as per-event
weights through MAT (MINERvA Analysis Toolkit) reweighters. These are the flux-and-CV and
GENIE dial weights, the low-recoil 2p2h enhancement fit, and the non-resonant-pion reduction. The
full Geant4-based [34] MINERvA detector simulation underlies the production AnaTuples. The
simulated sample contains 32 849 103 true signal events, POT-normalized event-by-event to the data
exposure and processed with identical reconstruction and selection.

2.3

Signal definition and event selection

The signal definition and phase space follow Ref. [3] exactly. Truth-level signal events are chargedcurrent νµ interactions in the central-tracker fiducial volume with muon phase space
θµ < 20◦ ,

1.5 < p∥ < 60 GeV/c,

pT < 4.5 GeV/c.

The fiducial volume spans 5980 < z < 8422 mm within an 850 mm apothem and contains 3.2353×1030
nucleons, the fixed tracker-geometry constant.
Reconstructed events require a vertex in the same fiducial volume and apothem, a muon track
with θµ < 20◦ matched to a MINOS track with a passing fit (isMinosMatchTrack==1 && minos_trk_is_ok==1), no dead-time overlap, and a neutrino (rather than antineutrino) candidate.
Genuine backgrounds are reconstructed events whose true interaction fails the signal definition:
charged-current events of other flavors or wrong sign, neutral-current interactions, and out-of-fiducialvertex events. Their predicted rate is 0.35 % of selected events, evaluated on playlist 1A. This
is the same sub-percent scale as the 8655 (0.2 %) predicted background events of Ref. [3], which
counts only the wrong-flavor, wrong-sign and neutral-current components; exact agreement is not
expected given the differences in definition and selection, and neither prediction carries an uncertainty.
Out-of-phase-space signal (fakes: reconstructed in phase space, true muon outside it) is treated
separately. Both are POT-scaled and subtracted from the reconstructed-data target before unfolding
(§3).
Event selection determines which observations enter the analysis and which truth population is
being estimated. Classifier features determine what the learner can use within that population. A
muon can therefore be required for selection even when its momentum is absent from the classifier.

2.4

Per-event analysis trees

Unbinned unfolding needs per-event records rather than histograms. A dedicated event loop (App. H)
built on MAT’s systematic-universe machinery writes four flat trees per playlist: all true signal events
(the efficiency denominator), reconstructed truth-matched simulated events, simulated background,
and data. Entries carry truth and reconstructed observables (pT , p∥ , and in extended runs Eavail , q3 ,

W ) and the MINERvA Tune v1 central-value weight. The systematic-universe trees also carry every
universe’s weight and each lateral band’s shifted observables (in MAT’s terms, a lateral band shifts
reconstructed kinematics and a vertical band reweights events; §4). Two conditions make the truth
tree the complete reference for the simulated signal:
• A reconstructed signal event is filled only if its (run, subrun, event) key is in the truth denominator. Truth-only events enter as native OmniFold “miss” entries.
• Completeness is c = 1.000000 by construction at ME FHC (32 849 103 entries; §3.4).
The loop reads one playlist-wide flux and calibration from the first event, so it is run separately
for each playlist. The twelve outputs are then merged, with a tree-size-aware merger for the trees
with per-universe content, which exceed 100 GB. The corresponding baseline histogram event loop
supplies the flux histogram, POT-weighted across playlists; playlist-to-playlist flux differences are a
∼ 0.2 % effect.

2.5

Reco-level control distributions

Selected data exceed the POT-scaled MINERvA Tune v1 prediction (signal + background) by
∼ 12 % in overall normalization. Figure 1 compares all five reconstructed observables. The data/MC
ratio has a clear pT trend, rising from 0.88 in the lowest bin to ∼ 1.2 above 1 GeV/c. Excess and
trend together reflect the truth-level normalization deficit of §6.5 and §7.6. The simulation describes
the shapes well, which is what the unfolding relies on; the data pull corrects the rate offset.
The beam and acceptance set the shapes. The p∥ peak at 4–6 GeV/c reflects the ⟨Eν ⟩ ≈ 6 GeV
NuMI medium-energy peak; spectrometer matching removes the lowest-p∥ muons. The pT spectrum
peaks near 0.7 GeV/c and falls by over an order of magnitude across the measured range. Low-recoil
(quasi-elastic and 2p2h) events dominate the sample. Available energy Eavail concentrates below
∼0.5 GeV, with a long inelastic tail in the wide upper bins; q3 tracks this population’s momentum
transfer. Reconstructed W rises through the smeared quasi-elastic and ∆(1232) region before the
deep-inelastic continuum takes over above ∼2 GeV. Absolute background is at the few-percent level
under the muon projections and concentrates at high recoil (highest Eavail , q3 and W bins). Beyond
the pT trend noted above, the ratio panels also show a mild enhancement in the lowest Eavail bins,
reflecting the low-recoil excess examined at truth level in §7.7.

The unbinned OmniFold procedure

3.1

Algorithm

OmniFold [1, 2] unfolds by iterated reweighting with machine-learned likelihood ratios, operating on
per-event observables rather than on a fixed histogram binning. Each iteration has two steps:
1. Step 1 (detector space). A classifier separates measured data from reconstructed simulation
weighted by the current push-weights. Its calibrated output p gives the likelihood-ratio reweight

w = p/(1 − p) for each reconstructed simulated event. Each simulated event is a (truth, reco)
pair, allowing the weight to be pulled back directly to its truth record. Truth events without
a reconstructed partner (“misses”) receive step-1 weights from a regressor trained on the
reconstructed subsample, w(truth features). This native OmniFold acceptance treatment
requires no separate efficiency correction.
2. Step 2 (truth space). A second classifier separates the original truth-level simulation from
the same sample carrying the pulled-back step-1 weights. Its smooth truth-space reweight
becomes the next iteration’s push-weight.
After n iterations, the push-weights define an efficiency-corrected truth-level reweighted simulation
ensemble. Histogramming this ensemble in any binning chosen afterwards gives the unfolded spectrum.
The iteration count and learner capacity regularize the unfold.
Training details. Each classifier is trained on the full sample whose weights it then sets, with no
held-out or cross-fitted split and no early stopping. The two classes keep their physical weights, with
no class balancing and no renormalization between iterations, so the learned ratio p/(1 − p), with p
restricted to [10−6 , 1 − 10−6 ], carries the normalization. Truth events without a reconstructed partner
receive the prediction of an unweighted regressor of the step-1 weight on the truth observables. In
the refined configurations, a classifier separating positive from negative weights, trained with |w|,
gives each event the weight |w| max(0, 2g − 1). No effective sample size of the unfolded ensemble is
computed.
Background subtraction. In the binned purity subtraction, all selected data events enter step 1;
none is discarded and no negative-weight events are injected. Each receives a reco-bin purity weight.
The POT-scaled background prediction includes genuine backgrounds and out-of-phase-space signal
(§2.3) and is binned on the reconstructed analysis grid. A data event in reco bin b receives

the predicted signal purity, floored at zero in the rare bins where the background prediction exceeds
the data. The step-1 classifier retains the full unbinned kinematics. Binning enters only through this
multiplicative correction, an ∼3 % effect overall, largest where backgrounds concentrate (§2.3).
This correction is, by design, the one place the analysis binning enters before the final histogramming; other experiments’ treatments are summarized in §1. At the sub-percent genuine-background
scale, a per-reco-bin weight is a conservative middle ground. A fully unbinned alternative injects
negatively weighted background simulation on the measured side, targeting the identical backgroundsubtracted density (D − B)/S. It reproduces the binned correction to −0.13 % on the total 2D cross
section and its systematic and statistical covariances to within 2 % (App. C). It removes residual
binning dependence and suits the extended-fiducial analysis, where backgrounds are larger. The
quoted 2D result and its covariance, the 3D and 4D central values and the sixteen-cell 5D result and
its covariance (§10) use its refined form; every other quoted cross section and uncertainty, including
the configuration-(a) 5D central value and the extended-fiducial value, uses the binned correction.
The refined form is the background treatment of configurations (d), (e) and (f) (§3.2): (d) in the
background-bias and generator-sized-change studies of §9.2; (e), the joint-test estimator, in §§9.3–9.4,
the generator tests, the 3D and 4D central values and the sixteen-cell result; and (f) for the 2D
result and its covariance (App. C, §7, §6).


3.2

Estimators and configuration

Gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs) replace the original OmniFold neural networks for the scalar
unfolds. With at most five high-level features per event, tree ensembles typically match or outperform
neural networks [35] and train deterministically and far faster on CPU. Ref. [36] demonstrates their
effectiveness for unbinned unfolding with few high-level features, complementing deep models for lowlevel event representations. We use GBDTs for all unfolds in this note (point-cloud representations:
App. F). Calibration tests and MLP/transformer cross-checks test the implementation in §7.5.
The unfolding code supports interchangeable learners (component terminology and code paths in
App. H). The quoted 2D central value and all of its uncertainty ensembles (the systematic-universe
sweep, the Poisson bootstrap, the training-seed block and the run-period unfolds) use LightGBM
with 100 trees, 8 leaves and learning rate 0.1, with the same settings for the step-1 classifier, the
step-2 classifier and the miss regressor. They use refined negative-weight background subtraction
(App. C), whose refinement classifier has 400 trees and 31 leaves and takes the estimator seed plus
three as its seed. This is configuration (f) below. The central value is the universe sweep’s own
central value at seed 42, so the systematic block shares its estimator and seed (§4.1). The quoted
statistical block, a bootstrap drawn in three independent streams (data, signal and background
simulation), also uses seed 42, and the training-seed block uses five seed settings that include seed 42
(§4.2, §4.3). A central unfold at seed 45 differs from the seed-42 one by at most 1.4 % in a cell. With
the purity-based subtraction (configuration (a)), the seed-42 central value differs from the seed-1
one by a per-bin median of 0.18 % (maximum 1.79 %). That is 0.30 (maximum 1.28) of the per-bin
standard deviation of the earlier statistical band, which was built at seed 1. Both of those central
values are regenerated to ≲ 2 × 10−11 by runs with one driver and one input that differ only in the
seed, so the difference is attributable to the seed, as inferred from the two regenerations. The quoted
central value records its run configuration; it repeats to 9 × 10−12 and equals the refined 2D data
unfold of App. C.6 to 7 × 10−12 . The purity-subtracted seed-42 central value has no recorded run
configuration. An independent rerun reproduces it to 1.4 × 10−11 , and so does a regeneration whose
run configuration is recorded (Ref. [37], R1.12). Against the ten purity-subtracted LightGBM central
values at seeds 1–10 it is mildly over-dispersed (RMS standardized difference 1.28, maximum 4.25).
Seed 42 was chosen on computing cost: building the statistical band at seed 42 was the cheaper of
the two costed ways to give the central value and the statistical band a common seed.
The exact-split cross-check. The exact-split configuration (exact) uses scikit-learn GradientBoosting with single-threaded exact-split CART. Its seed was not fixed: the driver version it ran
had no seed option, and its production job script passed no seed. The run’s own log was not found.
Two driver revisions regenerate it to 7 × 10−15 . It is not invariant to the estimator seed, however:
one reseeded run (random_state 1/2/3) moves every bin, by a median of 0.18 % (maximum 1.6 %),
and gives χ2 /ndf = 3.57. The spread over seeds is unmeasured. The agreement of that median
with the LightGBM seed-1 to seed-42 median above is a coincidence. The exact-split central value
differs from the purity-subtracted LightGBM seed-42 central value by a per-bin median of 0.98 %
(maximum 12.2 %), 1.32 statistical standard deviations. It differs from the quoted central value,
which also differs from it in background treatment, by a per-bin median of 1.03 % (maximum 17.6 %).
It gives χ2 /ndf = 3.66 (unseeded, reproducible) against the published covariance, against 2.65 for
the purity-subtracted LightGBM central value and 2.27 for the quoted one (§6.4). It has no matched
covariance: no exact-split bootstrap, universe sweep or seed scan exists. LightGBM was chosen for
the central value after both comparisons with the publication were known, so that the central value
and its covariance share an estimator. A third learner, the scikit-learn histogram backend (hist,
HistGradientBoosting, matched 8-leaf configuration), is retained for method-dependence studies

(§6.4).

3.3

MINERvA driver and pipeline conventions

The higher-dimensional MINERvA driver (App. H) extends the 2D driver using an axis list and the
same 2D helpers. The first two axes are always (pT , p∥ ), carrying the fiducial phase-space cut and
the per-pT flux. Higher-dimensional runs extend the muon observables (pT , p∥ ) with Eavail , q3 , W in
the phase space of §2.3. Every production, universe-sweep, and closure run follows these conventions:
• Reco-space masking. The measured events entering step-1 training are exactly those passing
the reconstructed-level selection; nothing outside the reconstructed phase space reaches the
classifier.
• Fake-free step-1 training. Step 1 targets the background-subtracted data of §3.1, purityweighted or with background simulation injected at negative weight (refined to non-negative
weights in the refined configurations). Genuine backgrounds and out-of-phase-space signal
fakes are removed from the data side. The simulated reco sample is fake-free by construction,


so neither side of the step-1 training contains events whose truth lies outside the measurement
phase space.
• At histogramming, each simulated truth event carries its final OmniFold push-weight times
its central-value simulation weight, including MINERvA Tune v1 reweighting. No additional
efficiency-histogram division is applied. Step-1 miss regression already gives an efficiencycorrected truth spectrum; dividing again would double-correct.
• Closure mirrors production. In closure tests the pseudo-data are simulated events passing
both the reconstructed and truth selections, weighted identically to the production data path,
so closure exercises the same code path as the measurement rather than an idealized shortcut.

Truth→reco migration on the analysis binning (Fig. 3) summarizes the smearing OmniFold must

3.6

Related developments and estimator scope

Profile OmniFold [38] extends classifier-based EM to forward-model nuisance parameters. Its distinct
algorithmic construction complements earlier unbinned profiled unfolding [19]. Profiling nuisances
with observed data would change our inference model, which propagates an external nuisance
ensemble through a fixed procedure. Neither a profiled estimator nor its uncertainty is used here.
SPINUP learns a forward mapping [39]; AUSSIE replaces OmniFold’s second step with a different
loss and avoids the outer iteration [40]. Both offer possible future cross-checks with different
algorithmic assumptions. Reduced simulation-prior dependence does not establish detector-model
independence, adequate phase-space support, or coverage for this measurement. Comparisons would
require matched signal, selection, normalization, and uncertainty definitions.
