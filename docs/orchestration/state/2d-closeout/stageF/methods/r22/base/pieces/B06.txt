transfer

Unbinned OmniFold adds an observable by adding an input to the step-1 and step-2 classifiers,
without an explicit migration matrix or per-bin response tensor. It avoids the combinatorial
binning penalty of matrix unfolding but still has a dimensional cost: classifier training, acceptance

and support coverage, sample sparsity and uncertainty quantification become more demanding as
observables are added. The primary methodological motivation is the absence of a practical analog of

with available energy as a third axis.
MINERvA has published binned triple-differential results (Fig. 15, Table 27), including recent

recoil energy for a quasielastic-like signal, for which the available energy is the sum of proton kinetic
energies. This differs from the inclusive signal and species-dependent energy definition used here.
What distinguishes this analysis is the unbinned, simultaneous CC-inclusive scalar formulation
extended to three, four and five recorded observables, not the number of dimensions by itself.

7.4

Closure: the one injected Eavail deformation is recovered

A reweighting closure tests the 3D unfolding of configuration (a) only, on one injected deformation.
The unfolding recovers a known +30 % Gaussian bump injected into pseudo-data in truth Eavail
(center 0.3 GeV, width 0.15 GeV). The recovered step-2 mean weight is 1.048 against the injected
1.0485. Residuals against reweighted truth on the Eavail -marginal have median 0.9999 and standard
deviation 0.0006 (0.06 %); the per-3D-bin residual standard deviation is 3.2 % from MC statistics
in sparse high-recoil corners. The closure’s marginal scatter is far below the 3.7 % data-versus-2D
scatter. Because the closure and the data comparison belong to different configurations, (a) and (e)
against (f), their ratio is not quoted. Total normalization matches to better than 1 %. In the metric
of §5 (Eavail projection) the residual is 1.84 % vs 18.1 % injected (ratio 0.102) and is not graded,
because no acceptance threshold fixed before the test is on record.
The test covers one shape, this Gaussian, injected into the pseudo-data and unfolded with the
same detector response that the unfolding uses. On this injection the configuration-(a) estimator
neither manufactures nor destroys the feature. That result does not extend to configuration (e),
and the data-versus-2D scatter compares (e) with (f). The scatter is therefore not read here as
data ↔ MC structure resolved by the available-energy axis. The test does not exclude estimator
bias for other deformations (§5.1). The hidden-variable closure exceeds its threshold at the largest
amplitude (§5, App. D).

7.6

Generator comparison in 3D

The published ancillary files provide only binned 2D model histograms. Quantitative 3D comparisons,
or comparisons in arbitrary projections, require generating truth-level events and histogramming
them as d3 σ in the same fiducial phase space with the same truth Eavail definition. Because the
unfolded result is at truth level, the comparisons need no detector simulation. We generated and
processed GENIE, NuWro and GiBUU samples, and extracted MINERvA Tune v1 from analysis
MC truth with its full tune weight. No spectra were digitized or externally supplied.
• GENIE 2.12.10 central value, generated with gevgen on a CH target with the MINERvA
ME FHC flux, using the DefaultPlusValenciaMEC cross-section splines [4] but gevgen’s default
event-generator list, which excludes MEC, so the sample has no 2p2h events (§7.7). It carries
no MINERvA reweights, and its events are reweighted to the analysis flux as described below.
• MINERvA Tune v1, the analysis tune (base GENIE + RPA, low-recoil 2p2h, and nonresonant-pion reweights), taken directly from the truth of the analysis simulation, which
reproduces the normalization of the published ancillary curve to 0.01 %.
• NuWro 21.09 [6], an independent generator on C with the same flux (after reweighting below)
and phase space.
• GiBUU 2019 [7], a transport-theory generator on C, built and run natively, without a
container, with the same flux and phase space.

Flux repair of the GENIE and NuWro predictions. Except in three studies that keep
the unrepaired samples, the GENIE and NuWro predictions in §§7–8 are repaired by exact perevent reweighting to the analysis PPFX central-value flux on 0–100 GeV, with correctly sampled

The low-available-energy excess and the known low-recoil deficit

We compare the low-Eavail excess with candidate model changes, both by reweighting and by
generating the relevant physics. MINERvA first isolated the rate enhancement between quasielastic
and ∆ peaks, with multi-nucleon final states, in a low-three-momentum-transfer subsample [5].
The empirical tune was built to absorb it; our muon-kinematics × Eavail central values show the
predictions falling short in the same region.
The tested pion-FSI variations do not account for it. Reweighting 2M central-value GENIE
events with GENIE’s grwght1p changes dσ/dEavail by less than a percent for both tested pion-FSI
dials. FrInel_pi shifts the spectrum by ≤ 0.74 % (peak in 0.10–0.20 GeV) and FrAbs_pi by ≤ 0.82 %
(peak in the lowest bin). Both are ratios within the unrepaired GENIE sample (§7.6), in which the flux
defect cancels to first order. Neither was recomputed. The integrated rate moves only ±0.02–0.03 %
at ±1σ. 1 A dial that moves the spectrum by less than 1 % cannot account for an excess of 8 % to
36 % per bin over the GENIE central value below Eavail = 0.4 GeV, so the tested variations do not
account for the excess. This conclusion is limited to these dials and their range; other transport
models or proton-FSI effects are not excluded. Recent MINERvA quasielastic-like measurements
point toward overestimated proton and charged-pion FSI in their model comparisons [44]. Their
different signal definition prevents directly identifying that effect with our inclusive discrepancy.
Comparison with a missing 2p2h component. The base GENIE central value uses the default
event-generator list and contains no meson-exchange-current (2p2h/MEC) events. None of the
1.48 × 106 CC events of the generated sample is a MEC event, and the 50–100 GeV supplement is
MEC-free as well. Before the flux reweight of §7.6, the sample’s unweighted modes are QE 11 %, RES
22 %, DIS 66 % and COH 0.9 %. 2p2h must therefore be added ; reweighting cannot supply it. The
mode decomposition of central-value dσ/dEavail (per-bin mode count-fractions × bin value), overlaid
with data, locates the largest positive gaps in the quasielastic–∆ dip, [0.10, 0.20) and [0.20, 0.40),
while central values are much closer in the QE-dominated [0, 0.10) and [0.40, 1.50) regions. At central
value, 69 % of the positive deficit lies at Eavail ≤ 0.4 GeV, where 2p2h events populate. Closing the
integrated deficit requires 2p2h at ≈ 45 % of the QE rate (68 % to 71 % in the two dip bins), the
standard magnitude of the empirical and Valencia MEC models, compared with sub-percent FSI
shifts (Fig. 20).
GENIE with Valencia 2p2h enabled. We regenerated the GENIE central value with Valencia
2p2h enabled (–event-generator-list Default+CCMEC, 2M events). The Nieves–Simo–Vacas MEC
channel [46, 47] is present in the splines but absent from the total-CC cross-section graph written by
GENIE’s gspl2root, so we normalize by the non-MEC CC count: QE+RES+DIS+COH is fixed to
the known total, and MEC is added through 1/(1 − fMEC ) with fMEC = 2.87 %. 2p2h falls in the
dip and brings all three low-Eavail bins toward data (Fig. 21). In central values, it closes 52 % of
the data−CV gap at Eavail ≤ 0.4 GeV and 63 % of the integrated deficit (CV −7.2 % → −2.7 %).
The prediction still falls short in the dip, especially [0.10, 0.20), but its integral over the reported
Eavail range is closer to data than Tune v1 (−2.7 % against −9.5 %). Tune v1 layers MINERvA’s
empirical low-recoil 2p2h enhancement and RPA on top of the stock Valencia MEC. The central
values are consistent with the known need for that enhancement, and with NuWro and GiBUU
(native, un-enhanced 2p2h) sitting low in the dip as well, but they do not establish it.

Fourth axis: three-momentum transfer q3

The fourth axis adds one feature (§7), three-momentum transfer q3 = |⃗q |, chosen because its
(Eavail , q3 ) plane separates quasielastic, 2p2h and resonance processes and permits bin-compatible
comparison to MINERvA’s dedicated low-recoil d2 σ/dq3 dEavail measurement (Ascencio et al. [8]).
Truth q3 comes from the generator (CVUniverse::Getq3True()); reconstructed q3 uses the muon Q2
and the calorimetric recoil (CVUniverse::RecoQ3()). Both are evaluated for all twelve playlists, as
for Eavail . Lateral systematic shifts change q3 but leave Eavail unchanged, so a shifted q3 is stored for
each lateral universe instead of reusing the central value. The classifier inputs are (pT , p∥ , Eavail , q3 ),
with deliberately coarse q3 binning [0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1.2, 2.0, 100] GeV (seven bins including a final
catch bin), giving 14 × 16 × 7 × 7 = 10 976 four-dimensional bins (4830 populated).

3D Eavail spectrum and low-recoil excess (Fig. 23, right). The Eavail -marginal data/Tune v1 ratio is
1.21 in the lowest bin, dips to 1.01 across the quasielastic–∆ region, and rises to 1.16–1.23 in the
high-Eavail DIS tail (integrated data/Tune v1 = 1.13). These ratios are to Tune v1, whereas §7.7
uses untuned GENIE. Recovering the 3D result as a by-product of the higher-dimensional unfolding
is the 4D→3D analog of the 3D→2D marginalization check (§7.3).
What q3 adds. The resolved (Eavail , q3 ) plane (Fig. 23, left) reveals structure beneath the mild q3 marginal discrepancy integrated over Eavail (1.03–1.19, center). At fixed Eavail in the quasielastic–∆
dip, data/Tune v1 rises with q3 . The central value over-predicts low-q3 , moderate-Eavail cells (ratio
down to 0.66); data excess concentrates at low Eavail (all q3 ) and toward high q3 . The muon-only or
fixed-q3 projections considered here do not resolve this correlation, which is a natural input to the
bin-compatible comparison with Ascencio et al. The map’s comparator is the MINERvA Tune v1
prior, distinct from the untuned GENIE sample of §7.7.
No 4D uncertainty is reported. Nor is any ratio of the unified-throw to the block covariance.
A corrected 4D covariance would use the actual minus and plus variation endpoints; MAT’s meancentered covariance blocks, normalized by 1/N ; one fixed estimator seed; and mean-centered joint
throws, with the mean shift reported separately. It would not subtract a scalar jitter floor. Because
the single-pass shifted observables exist only for events selected at the central value, re-selected event
loops (§4.7) would be needed to bound migration effects.
Bin-identical comparison to the published low-recoil measurement. Supplemental data
from Ref. [8] (44-cell d2 σ/(dEavail dq3 ) with its full covariance, distributed inside the public arXiv
source package) permit a direct quantitative cross-check. Both measurements are merged onto their
maximal common grid, the edges present in both binnings. Our (pT , p∥ ) marginalization is restricted
to p∥ < 20 GeV/c to mirror the Ascencio muon selection (θµ < 20◦ , 1.5 < pµ < 20 GeV). Together,
these give two super-cells, Eavail < 0.4 GeV in q3 ∈ [0.4, 0.6) and [0.6, 1.2) GeV: the low-recoil 2p2h
region. Our central result lies 9.2 % and 6.0 % above Ascencio. No pulls or full-covariance χ2 are
reported (§7; Fig. 22). Both share MINERvA flux/detector systematics, treated here as independent.
The pµ and p∥ cuts differ by ≲ 6 % kinematic smearing at the 20 GeV boundary. The two analyses
also define true Eavail differently (§7.1): in this analysis’s simulation, Ascencio’s broader definition
moves 11.0 % of the population of our lowest true-Eavail bin (Eavail < 0.1 GeV) out of it. That shift
has the sign and location of the residual. The two common cells, however, span our three lowest bins
(Eavail < 0.4 GeV), and the aggregate shift over those bins has not been computed. The definition
difference therefore remains an alternative explanation that is not excluded. At the 10 % scale of the
residual, the comparison neither refutes nor validates the result. A finer comparison on all 44 cells
remains future work; it requires re-unfolding on the Ascencio edges, which is straightforward for the
unbinned method, and a systematic sweep on that binning.
