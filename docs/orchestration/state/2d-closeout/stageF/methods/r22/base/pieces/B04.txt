Validation of the two-dimensional measurement

In a simulated experiment, truth is the known target distribution, the prior is the starting simulated
truth distribution, and the response describes reconstruction and selection conditional on truth. A
closure test compares the unfolded estimate with its known target under a specified deformation.
Bias is the mean estimate minus that target over repeated experiments; a single closure residual also
contains sampling fluctuations. Coverage is the fraction of repeated intervals containing the specified
truth, and tests the estimator and interval procedure together. Table 3 separates these questions
before the results. Repeating a numerical calculation checks neither the coverage and width of the
intervals nor the adequacy of the physical model.

What the remaining validation needs. The follow-up assessment [42] distinguishes a proposed
split-sample width check from repeated-sampling coverage. With the MC sample, binning maps and
background subtraction held fixed, such a check could establish only the precision conditional on
them, at its tested exposure. Nonlinear unfolding does not guarantee a simple scaling from half to
full exposure. It cannot supply the variation across independent MC populations. Validated total
uncertainties for all 205 bins, the integral and the projections therefore cannot be obtained from
the current inputs. They would require independent populations or a validated generative law, a
continuous nuisance generator for tests of total intervals, and an untouched generator-truth domain
for bias checks (§4.6). This is a limit of the available inputs, not a general impossibility result for 2D
unfolding.

5.1

Closure tests across dimensions: what each test changes

Only the MINERvA simulation supplies a detector response, the conditional distribution of reconstructed events given truth. Other generators (GiBUU, NuWro, GENIE variants) supply truth
only. Every closure test here therefore keeps the simulated MINERvA response and changes the
truth distribution, by reweighting the simulated events, or changes the analysis chain around it.
Reweighting to another generator’s shape is a valid known-truth test of the unfolding; it is not a
test of the detector model. A closure test is only as informative as what it changes: an ordinary
closure, whose pseudo-data are the simulation itself, is an identity check that a constant estimator
would pass. Table 4 collects the tests by what they change.

Why injected bumps close while generator-shaped tests do not. The closing tests and the
failing tests measure different things, so they do not contradict each other.
• Deformation. The closing injections are single smooth bumps in one variable the classifiers
see directly. A generator ratio changes correlated structure across Eavail , W and q3 , including directions the reconstructed observables constrain weakly (§9.5, response-information
diagnostic).
• Metric. The 3D, 4D and 5D closures are judged on one-dimensional marginals, where per-cell
errors average out; the 3D test’s per-bin spread is already 3.2 %, and the 5D test’s is 3.7 %.
The generator-shaped failures are measured in joint (Eavail , W ) cells.
• Iterations. At five iterations the estimator retains part of the prior wherever the data pull
weakly. Thirty iterations reduce the Eavail residual by 26 % while the q3 residual grows (§9.3).
• The five-dimensional W test passes only in the marginal, and narrowly. A W bump at the
∆ peak is recovered to a ratio of 0.180 against a threshold of 0.20. The same unfold leaves
joint (Eavail , W ) cells in the peak column up to 9.4 % low and moves part of the bump into the
neighbouring W bins. A single smooth bump in a marginal is therefore weaker evidence than
the generator-shaped tests, which remain the main five-dimensional recovery evidence of §9.

5.2

Fixed-truth coverage of the 2D statistical band

This test asks whether the 2D statistical band quoted when it ran covers a fixed truth at its nominal
rate. That band is an earlier one, not the quoted statistical block. The design, criteria and positive
control of the test were specified before any toy was generated (Ref. [37], R1.1).
Each toy draws pseudo-data event by event as Poisson counts with means equal to the simulated
reconstructed weights. It resamples the simulation with the production Poisson(1) bootstrap and
unfolds with the statistical band’s estimator (LightGBM, five iterations, fixed training seed): the
learner of the quoted central value, at a different seed (§3.2). The reference T is the unfluctuated
simulated truth, identical in every toy. The no-fluctuation configuration reproduces both the
production closure output and T exactly.
In reported bin b of toy t, ztb = (Utb − Tb )/(rb Tb ), where rb is the production band’s relative
spread. Coverage Ck is the fraction of all (toy, bin) pairs with |z| ≤ k, with intervals from resampling
whole toys. The band counts as nominal if its ratio to the true scatter lies in [0.9, 1.1], which gives
the windows C1 ∈ [0.632, 0.729] and C2 ∈ [0.928, 0.972]. A failure at the interim analysis of 100 toys,
with 99.5% intervals, stops the study.
The band fails: it undercovers in its tails. At the interim analysis (Ntoy = 100, 205 bins):

• C1 = 0.679 [0.669, 0.690] is nominal (Gaussian 0.683).
• C2 = 0.912 [0.907, 0.918] lies entirely below its window (Gaussian 0.954).
• The pull RMS is 1.60, and the mean pull is consistent with zero.
By the criteria above this is a failure by undercoverage, and the study stopped at the interim analysis.
An independent recomputation with its own code reproduces the pooled coverages, their intervals,
the controls and the secondary analysis below.
The positive control rescales the band on the same toys. At ×0.7 and ×1.3, C2 moves to 0.809
and 0.955, and the test flags both directions.
The failure is one of width, not bias. The per-bin pattern below is descriptive and post hoc.
• Uneven in both directions. The band is too wide in 82 bins (pull RMS below 0.9) and
too narrow in 71 (above 1.1). The pooled C1 is nominal partly through this cancellation; the
median bin’s pull RMS is 0.97.
• Where it is too narrow. 35 bins have a per-bin C2 below 0.85. Of these, 21 form a block
at p∥ ≥ 15 GeV/c and pT < 0.55 GeV/c. Six more lie in the highest-pT row (2.5 GeV/c to
4.5 GeV/c) at p∥ 6 GeV/c to 20 GeV/c. The 14 bins with pull RMS above 2 carry 72 % of the
C2 deficit.
The band’s replicas divide by a bootstrapped completeness, whereas the central value’s completeness is exactly 1. A secondary analysis, added by amendment after the pilot, applies the same
division to the toys. Scored against the same band, it overcovers: C1 = 0.833, with its interval above
the window. The attribution rule was fixed before the full run, but its clause for the undercoverage
outcome was written after the pilot pulls had been seen. Under it, the primary undercoverage is
attributed to this term of the band. The attribution is partial: in the replica form, the p∥ 40 GeV/c
to 60 GeV/c column still undercovers.
The band was then rebuilt without this term. The quoted statistical block is a third, different
ensemble, 300 replicas at the central value’s training seed (§4.2). The rebuilt band was only rescored
post hoc (below), and the quoted block has not been tested.
Post hoc rescoring against the rebuilt (since superseded) band. Scoring the same
100 toys against the rebuilt band removes the tail undercoverage: C2 = 0.977 [0.974, 0.980] and
C1 = 0.781 [0.771, 0.790], both above their nominal windows, so the rebuilt band is wider than the
toy scatter (pull RMS 0.84, mean pull consistent with zero). The rescoring is post hoc and partly
circular, because the band’s MC part reuses the toys’ resampling mechanism.
The excess width lies in the data-statistics component. The per-bin pull RMS falls as that
component’s share of the band’s variance rises, which corresponds to a data-statistics part about
1.6× wider in σ than the toys produce. Background purity does not explain it, and its cause is
not established. A post hoc diagnostic that bootstraps a single toy, under a decision rule recorded
before it ran, is consistent with the closure toys scattering too little, because their pseudo-data are
built on the training MC events, rather than with the data bootstrap scattering too much. It is
neither an independent-population coverage test nor a calibration on real data. A re-test on held-out
simulation would separate the two explanations; it has not been run.
Scope. The result concerns the statistical band only.
• It changes no central value or quoted uncertainty.
• It tests the LightGBM statistical estimator at the band’s training seed (seed 1), not at the
quoted central value’s seed 42.
• It says nothing about systematic, total or five-dimensional coverage.
• Its simulation-side scatter reuses the resampling that built the band, so only the data-side
fluctuation is tested by an independent mechanism.
• Because T equals the prior, it cannot detect regularization bias toward the prior.


5.3

Other validation diagnostics

Unbinned diagnostic (C2ST). In the classifier two-sample test (C2ST), a classifier is trained
to separate the data from the (reweighted) simulation in the full unbinned feature space, and its
held-out AUC or accuracy is compared with the chance value 0.5. The AUC drops from ≈ 0.535
before unfolding to ≈ 0.501 after, indicating removal of the reconstructed data ↔ MC mismatch.
This binning-free check is descriptive, and no calibrated p-value is quoted. As run, the C2ST trains
and evaluates on the same sample without cross-fitting; a valid null would require re-running the full
cross-fitted OmniFold pipeline per pseudoexperiment. Calibrated unbinned goodness-of-fit remains
open [18].
Same-ensemble pull diagnostic (not a coverage test). An earlier ensemble of Ntoy = 200 2D
closure unfolds (Ref. [37], R1.2) uses weighted MC reco pseudo-data and stores the truth marginals
in truth histograms. Each toy has its own bootstrap seed k and per-event Poisson(1) draws, as in
production, with seeds chosen not to collide with the production replicas. Toy seeds are independent;
bins within a toy are correlated. The MC bootstrap multiplies the truth weight, so it fluctuates
both the unfolded result and the stored truth. The stored truth varies in all 205 reported bins, and
its all-toy mean X̄ truth is therefore not an independent fixed truth for a split-sample coverage claim;
it only centers a same-ensemble diagnostic.
