Figure 7 shows the unfolded d2 σ/(dpT dp∥ ) and its 1D projections. The total cross section is

Comparison to the published measurement

The published and unbinned results share the data and the flux, detector and generator systematics,
and their cross-covariance is not available. Every χ2 and standardized difference here is therefore
descriptive, not a calibrated goodness-of-fit test.
Over the 205 reported bins, the total cross section reproduces the published result [3] to 1.0 %
(ratio 1.010; Table 5). The median per-bin ratio is 1.005; 86.8% of bins agree within 5 % and 95.1%
within 10 %. Indicative values of χ2 per reported bin are 2.27 with the published covariance alone
(analysed in §6.4) and 0.929 with the combined covariance, which includes our uncertainty.
The combined (published plus ours) χ2 /ndf = 0.929 double-counts the flux, GENIE and MINOS
systematics that the two results share. With our covariance alone, χ2 is computed only in the
covariance’s well-constrained subspace, with a truncated spectral pseudo-inverse. We therefore
quote the per-bin pulls and the truncated result together, with a regularization scan made for the
exact-split cross-check on an earlier covariance and not repeated for the quoted one, rather than
treating an unconstrained pseudo-inverse as a goodness-of-fit test on its own (App. A.4).
Per-bin differences standardized by the published uncertainty (Fig. 8) are centered (mean 0.060),
with RMS 0.54, well inside the published uncertainties. Figures 9–10 compare our unfolded result
with simulated truth in the published panel format; published data do not enter this data-versus-prior
comparison.

6.4

Anatomy of the χ2 = 2.27 tension

With the published uncertainties as a descriptive scale, the central values have a pull RMS of 0.535 (a
diagonal-only χ2 /ndf would be 0.29), whereas the distance with the full published covariance is 2.27
per bin. The eightfold increase lies entirely in the off-diagonal structure of the published covariance,
and it is a difference in shape, not normalization (χ2norm = 0.04 against χ2shape = 465). The eigenmode
decomposition (Fig. 11) spreads it over many modes: the largest has a paper-covariance-standardized
component of magnitude 4.8, and the next nine 3.4–3.8. The 10 smallest-λ modes carry 9.6 %, two of
the five largest contributors lie in the smallest-λ tenth, and keeping the 73 best-measured directions
gives 0.66. Of the six leading modes, the three in the highest-p∥ bins are all poorly determined
directions (λ/λmax ≲ 4 × 10−9 ); the other three are moderate-λ modes (λ/λmax between 4 × 10−6
and 6 × 10−5 ) and lie below the highest p∥ bins (Fig. 12). No single pT or p∥ band carries more than
15 % of the total.
With the purity-based subtraction, the choice of GBDT estimator spreads the distance over
about one unit, a methodological band: the exact-split cross-check gives 3.66, HistGBT 2.70 ± 0.04,
and LightGBM with seeds 1–10 gives 2.64–2.75. Beyond this methodological band, the source of
the shape difference has not been identified. At the central value’s seed, the refined negative-weight
subtraction of the quoted result lowers the LightGBM distance from 2.65 to 2.27. For the exact-split
cross-check the distance is larger and distributed differently, with magnitude-5–6 components in
moderate-λ modes on the low-pT ridge of the cross-section peak; the 10 smallest-λ modes carry 3 %
of it, and the 73 best-measured directions give 1.42 (App. A.5). The number of iterations has a
negligible effect: going from 5 to 10 iterations moves the LightGBM (seed 1) distance by +0.01. See
App. A.5 for the full treatment and Figs. 11–12 for eigenmode-localization maps.

the published data (right), on a common colour scale of 0.7–1.3; the panels have no printed titles.
Neither map is flat near unity: both show the same tune deficit, with ratios above unity over most
of the plane and below it at the lowest pT . The two maps are close because our result departs from
the tune in the same places and by similar amounts as the published data. Neither map has the
published data as its denominator; the direct comparison with the published data is the median bin
