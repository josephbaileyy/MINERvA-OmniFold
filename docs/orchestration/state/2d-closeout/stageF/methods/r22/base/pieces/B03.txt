Uncertainty quantification: constructing the covariance blocks

The uncertainty budget sums four blocks: systematic universes, statistical bootstraps, ML trainingseed variation, and a run-period block (§4.5). Their independence is an assumption: distinct random
streams and physics sources do not by themselves establish it after unfolding. The universe, statistical
and training-seed blocks are computed with the LightGBM estimator of the quoted 2D central value
(§3.2); the universe and statistical blocks use its training seed (seed 42), and the training-seed block
uses five seed settings that include it. A 3D budget built with the analogous 3D grid construction is
not quoted, and no 4D uncertainty magnitude is reported (§7.8). App. A gives the full mathematics
and worked examples using the reported numbers (N-D declarations in App. B). The resulting
budget and its agreement with the published total are in §6.3; what the covariance includes and
omits, and the conventions it follows, are in §4.6.

4.1

Systematic universes

Detector and model systematics use the July universe sweep: MAT-MINERvA’s 44 error bands and
187 universes, identical to the standard systematics set of the published analysis. Each universe is
re-unfolded with LightGBM at a fixed seed (seed 42), against a reference central-value unfold with
the same estimator and seed, which is the quoted 2D central value. The five lateral bands are taken
from re-unfolds of re-selected universes (event loops that re-apply the selection to each universe’s
shifted kinematics; §4.7) at the same seed. The band sum drops Rvp1pi, which encodes the same
shift as Rvn1pi (§4.6), and holds 43 bands plus the flat normalization. Per-band covariances follow
MAT’s universe-mean centering and biased 1/N . For a ±1σ pair, the covariance is the half-difference
outer product, excluding any common endpoint displacement from CV. The 100 PPFX universes
use the same mean-centered 1/N formula.

4.2

Statistical uncertainty: Poisson bootstrap

The statistical block jointly bootstraps data and simulation with 300 per-event Poisson(1) weight
replicas, each fully re-unfolded with the LightGBM estimator at the central value’s training seed
(seed 42), and is their sample covariance about the replica mean (Fig. 5). The central value and its
statistical block are therefore matched in estimator and seed. Each replica draws its weights in three
independent streams: data, signal simulation and background simulation. Statistical fluctuations of
the background simulation are thus drawn independently of those of the signal simulation; possible
shared events between the two were not assessed. Because the background simulation is resampled
in every replica, the separable background-only term is contained in the statistical block. MC reco
and truth weights share a single event draw, which preserves within-event migration correlations,
and pinning the GBDT seed per p
replica separates bootstrap and training-seed variances. With 300
replicas the Gaussian formula 1/ 2(N − 1) gives a relative standard error of about 4.1 % on each
per-bin σ, and the per-bin σ from a nested half (150) of the replicas is a median 1.000 times that
from all 300. The replica

errors of the mean (σ/ 300), that is 0.25 of the statistical standard deviation; because the block
is the covariance about the replica mean, this offset is not included in it. The bootstrap block is
genuinely smaller than the published statistical block (square-root-trace ratio 0.407). This observed
covariance difference does not demonstrate a causal efficiency advantage (App. A.4).
The statistical block quoted here has had no coverage test: the fixed-truth coverage studies of
§5.2 scored two earlier statistical bands, not this block. No coverage test on held-out simulation has
been run, and no design for one has been registered in advance. Because the fixed-truth test’s truth

4.3

ML/optimization uncertainty: training-seed variation

The ML block is the sample covariance, over five training-seed settings, of the mean of 20 bootstrap
replicas at each setting. Each setting is a triplet (s, s+1, s+2) of seeds for the step-1 classifier, step-2
classifier and miss regressor, with s ∈ {42, 45, 48, 51, 54}; the block has rank 4 (§A.2.1). It is the
smallest block, with median 0.048 % per bin. The block measures LightGBM seed variation, with
the bootstrap × seed interaction at its 1/20 share. With four degrees of freedom, its variance has a
Gaussian relative standard error of about 71 %. Its per-bin σ is a median 0.27 of that of the earlier
ten-seed block, because the bootstrap × seed interaction, which dominated the earlier scan, is already
inside the fixed-seed statistical block; the training-seed block carries it only at its 1/20 share, a
median 0.763 of its diagonal. The interaction itself and the universe × retraining term are reported
and not added (§4.6).
No block contains the dependence on the learner. The central value and the exact-split cross-check
differ by a per-bin median of 1.03 % (maximum 17.6 %; §3.2); they differ in background treatment as
well as in learner. One reseeded exact-split run differs from the unseeded one by a per-bin median of
0.18 % (maximum 1.6 %). The spread of the exact-split estimator over seeds is unmeasured (§3.2).

4.4

Further checks of the 2D covariance

Seven universes of the July sweep (§4.1) are reproduced to ≤ 1.4 × 10−11 by the committed driver;
the others are accepted by inference, because the same code produced them (Ref. [37], R1.16).
The two examined small-input responses, EtaNCEL and NormNCRES, are consistent with trainer
instability contributing at the ML-noise scale. They do not establish a mechanism for every small
band or justify subtracting an overlap from the budget.
Each lateral band of the quoted universe block uses a single unfold at the central training seed,
so it includes that seed’s training noise. The flux-universe index identity behind the 1/Φu rescale is
established by the code chain and by an exact test: 900 events × 100 universes match to 5.5 × 10−14 ,
and a control shifted by one universe fails at ≥ 0.245 (Ref. [37], R1.13). The production flux weights

equal the constraint file and the earlier budget’s in all 12 playlists. The corrections the quoted
budget makes to the earlier 2D budget are stated in §4.6.

4.5

Combination and the unified-throw test

The nominal covariance is the block sum C = Csyst + Cstat + CML + CPL , which assumes the
blocks are independent and the unfolding responds linearly to simultaneous perturbations, so that
per-band covariances add. The unified throw tests linearity directly (App. A.2.4; §9.6) by jointly
re-unfolding all reweightable knob bands and the flux multisim, perturbed simultaneously per event.
The procedure interpolates asymmetrically between actual −1σ and +1σ event-weight endpoints,
fixes the estimator seed for every throw, and centers the joint covariance on the throw mean. That
mean’s displacement from CV is stored separately as a per-bin vector and its norm; per-band
endpoint displacements are not stored. The matched block comparator re-unfolds both endpoints
using MAT’s universe-mean centering and biased 1/N normalization. A single-throw superposition
probe is insufficient to test nonlinear response. No scalar run-to-run jitter floor is subtracted. The
configuration-(a) 5D result uses the strict joint ensemble and matched-endpoint comparator.
The block sum is an approximation, valid only where the unfolding responds linearly. In the
five-axis covariance of the configuration-(a) central value the per-source decomposition is retained
for attribution, and the joint throws enter through the inflation of Eq. (5) (§9.6).
The run-period block CPL is an uncertainty assigned for an observed inconsistency between run
periods whose cause is unknown; it is not a measured systematic uncertainty from an identified
source. Subsets of the data taken in different run periods are not mutually consistent within the
other uncertainties: after unfolding, the double ratio (data over simulated truth) of playlists 1G–1M
to the earliest periods, 1A–1F, has a p∥ step of (+6.89 ± 0.46)% at 8 GeV/c. Diluted into the
combined result, the raw shift of those playlists is small bin by bin, with every marginal z below
0.7, but its p∥ χ2 against the earlier budget is 34.4, where noise would give 2.0. These data cannot
establish which period, if any, is correct.
The block is built from separate seed-42 unfolds of three run periods, the playlist groups 1A–1F,
1G–1M and 1N–1P, each unfolded through its own simulated detector response. The block uses
each period’s unfolded result over that period’s own simulated truth. Each period is compared with
the exposure-weighted mean of the three, so the block needs no reference period, and the spread,
applied to the central value, gives the block. It is added after the other blocks. It has rank two:
with three period groups, its own size is poorly determined. Per bin its median is 2.09 % (84th
percentile 3.34 %, maximum 6.48 %), and it is 1.17 % to 3.36 % on the p∥ projections and 1.97 % on
the integrated cross section. Added to the other blocks, it raises the per-bin σ by a median of 5.8 %
(at most 39.1 %), the integral by 9.9 % and the p∥ projections by at most 21 %; it carries a median
10.6 % of the per-bin variance and 17.1 % of the integral’s. Taking 1A–1F as the reference would
give a smaller block (median 1.38 %, integral 1.09 %).
The block does not identify which period is correct. Its interpretation is limited by the small
number of period groups, by possible shrinkage from the regularization, and by the statistical and
training noise it retains. Because the block is built from the observed differences among the periods,
it contains them automatically. That says nothing about its coverage, that is, about whether its size
is right. For the same reason, the block’s own double-ratio χ2 against any total that contains it is
bounded whatever the data, so it is not a test. The raw 1G–1M p∥ χ2 , 34.8 against the other blocks
alone and 2.59 with the block added, is a sensitivity reading only.


4.6

Scope, conventions and limitations of the 2D covariance

Status of the 2D result. We report a reproducible two-dimensional central value, with a
covariance computed with the central value’s own estimator, under the conventions stated below.
The result’s intervals, bias and covariance have not been independently validated. In particular, the
coverage of its intervals has not been validated on independent simulated samples, nor simultaneously
over all reported bins and projections. The covariance also gives no calibrated significance for
comparisons with generator predictions. Such validation would need an independent simulation
sample of production size, a validated generator of pseudo-data with continuously varied nuisance
parameters, and samples from independent event generators beyond the truth-level ones already
examined; none is available. We therefore make no claim about coverage, and goodness-of-fit values
computed with this covariance are indicative only. The following are not included in the covariance:
• the approximation in how the systematic-universe unfolds weight events that fail reconstruction
(below); beyond the weak test described below, its effect has not been measured;
• the dependence on the machine-learning estimator (§4.3);
• the dependence on the regularization, and departures of the detector response from its
parametrized family, which we assume absent; we assign no uncertainty for either;
• the dependence on the generator shape of the prior, which is reported beside the covariance as
Cmodel (below).

the measurement, these 52 cells hold 59.4 % of the trace of Cmodel and carry most of its large shifts.
Conventions. Each systematic band’s covariance is computed about the mean of its universes
(constraint-weighted for the flux), with 1/N normalization, not about the central value. This follows
the MAT treatment of universes (§4.1), which we use as a stated convention. The band centers lie a
median 0.10 % to 0.55 % of the central value from it, or 0.09–2.85 of the band’s σ. The statistical
block is the covariance about the replica mean (§4.2). A 1.4 % normalization uncertainty is included
as a rank-one block.

Flux:23 (the flux universe with the largest median completeness change), each at seeds 42 and 43.
In neither was an effect resolved above the training-seed noise. The tests compared the difference
with the σ of the earlier ten-seed block, which is about 3.7 times that of the training-seed block used
here, and they exclude only a median effect above twice that σ; the test is not tight, and an effect

One systematic category provided in the input products, NormDISCC, is identically zero; we carry
it as zero, which we have not been able to confirm is intended. No separate uncertainty is assigned

flux universes carry the term and that the neutrino–electron constraint absorbs it, are inferred, not
verified. The twelve playlists are combined with one global exposure ratio rather than per-period
ratios, and the resulting bias is not corrected. At reconstruction level the pooled result is high, by
0.18 % in total and by at most 0.32 % in a marginal bin. After unfolding, the pooled result is 0.076 %
above the exposure-weighted combination of the period results on the integral, the same sign as
at reconstruction level. It differs from that combination by at most 0.39 % in a marginal bin (the
highest p∥ bin) and by at most 0.33 % in pT . In single cells the combination differs from the pooled
result by −1.83 % to 3.78 %, each with |z| at most 0.31.
Other limitations. Both programs that use the covariance for the reported results, the χ2
comparison (§A.3.2) and the program that draws Fig. 4, check that the covariance’s stored set of cells
equals the published set; the check passes in every run of both on the quoted covariance. Limitations
specific to one block are stated with it: the matching of estimator and seed, the background stream
and the statistical ensemble’s precision (§4.2); the training-seed block’s size and precision (§4.3);
the universe sweep’s provenance, the lateral bands and the flux-universe index identity (§4.4); the
duplicated pion band (below); and the flux block (§6.3).
Seed-related terms reported, not added. Two seed-related terms are reported and not added
to the covariance. The bootstrap × seed interaction is a median 0.072 of the statistical variance;
the training-seed block includes it at its 1/20 share. For the universe × retraining term, in the six
non-null universes tested (of 187), the seed standard deviation of each universe’s shift is a median
1.08–1.36 times the σ of the earlier ten-seed block, with four degrees of freedom each. In those
universes the universe block, built at one training seed, therefore carries single-seed training noise
of about the earlier seed block’s size; it is part of the block as built and is not separated from the
physical variation. In two of them the noise is comparable to or larger than the shift: in 2p2h:0
the shift is 0.097 % against a seed standard deviation of 0.192 %, and in MaCCQE:0 0.239 % against
0.210 % (per-bin medians). We infer that in expectation such noise adds variance to a band rather
than removing it, and, from the medians, that these two bands are largely training noise. The
universe × retraining values and the band-centering numbers cannot be recomputed from the saved
covariance products, so they were not recomputed independently; the verification compared only the
checksums of their inputs.
Changes from the earlier 2D budget. The saved products and code of the earlier 2D budget [42]
show the following defects in its construction, each of which the quoted budget corrects. First,

the May systematic sweep that the earlier budget used has a different simulated truth input from
its seed-42 reference: even a no-op EtaNCEL universe differs in all 205 truth-input cells, while
its saved reconstructed inputs agree. A common displacement cancels to first order in the MAT
mean-centered covariance. The mismatch does not invalidate the entire covariance, and it supplies
no correction to subtract. Because the May input and code revision are not available, the mismatch
cannot be attributed more specifically. The July sweep, which the quoted universe block uses,
does not have this mismatch. Second, the Rvn1pi and Rvp1pi bands encode the same shift under
the configured nonresonant-pion tune, and the earlier budget counted it twice. The band is small,
0.088 %, but this is a real defect in how the covariance was built; no conclusion about the published
analysis’s implementation follows. The quoted universe block drops Rvp1pi and counts the shift
once (§4.1). Third, the earlier budget did not propagate background-template statistics. In the
saved reconstructed histograms their error exceeds one tenth of the data’s Poisson error in 60 of 205
bins, so neglecting them everywhere is unsupported; this is a reconstructed-space diagnostic, not a
measured unfolded uncertainty. The quoted statistical block draws the background simulation as its
own bootstrap stream (§4.2), and the quoted universe block takes its lateral bands from re-unfolds
of re-selected universes (§4.1). These findings left the earlier printed budget unchanged.

4.7

What changes for the higher-dimensional products

Two event-loop modes supply the lateral bands. The single-pass event loop, which writes every
universe in one pass, stores each lateral universe’s shifted observables only for events that pass
the selection at the central value, so it omits events that the shift moves into the selection. Reselected event loops re-run the selection with one lateral universe active at a time, so they include
these migrations. For the five kinematic bands (BeamAngleX/Y, MuonResolution, and Muon_Energy_MINERvA/MINOS), the re-selected event loops supply the lateral-band block of the
five-axis covariance of the configuration-(a) central value; higher-dimensional products built from the
single-pass shifted observables are not used for any quoted uncertainty. Replacing the single-pass
lateral-band block by the re-selected one moves that block’s square-root trace by −0.0288 %. That
is a change in a square-root trace, not a bound on any individual bin. Across the 10 694 reported
bins the per-bin ratio of re-selected to single-pass standard deviation runs 0.177 to 3.062; within the
top 1 % of bins by single-pass variance, which carry 74.11 % of the total, it runs 0.687 to 1.153. A
small share of the aggregate variance does not make a bin irrelevant to a quoted result.
Scope of the re-selected replacement. The replacement covers the five detector bands using
shifted reconstructed kinematics. MINOS efficiency and the three GEANT hadron-interaction bands
(GEANT_Neutron, GEANT_Pion, GEANT_Proton) keep their weight-based treatment and are
therefore outside the kinematic replacement. This classification does not independently validate
hadronic-response model completeness for the Eavail and W measurement.
