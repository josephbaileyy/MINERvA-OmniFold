Five-dimensional estimator: validation and uncertainty

This section gives the validation of the five-dimensional estimator behind the central values of §§7–8:
what each validation test isolates (§9.1), background subtraction and generator-sized truth changes
(§9.2), why the estimator does not follow such changes (§9.3), model dependence and the reporting
definitions it rules out (§9.4), the five-dimensional coverage test (§9.5), and the status of the five-axis
covariance of the configuration-(a) central value (§9.6). The sixteen-cell result of configuration (e)
follows in §10, and the calibrated generator tests built on a variant of this estimator in §11. The
unbinned negative-weight background method itself is derived in App. C.

9.1

Which source of error is being tested

Prior reweighting changes the frequencies of existing simulated truth events while retaining their
detector-response simulation; a response mismatch instead changes how a given truth is reconstructed.
Finite classifier capacity and iteration count approximate the inverse and can leave residuals in either
case. Coverage asks whether the resulting intervals contain their specified target at the stated rate
(§9.5). These mechanisms are separate: an incorrect generator prediction alone does not diagnose an
interval failure. Each test below isolates one intervention.
All 2D closures in §5 unfold reweighted simulated signal without background, so they do not test
background subtraction.

9.2

Background subtraction and generator-sized truth changes

Background subtraction. End-to-end five-dimensional pseudo-experiments add backgroundsimulation events and rebuild per-bin purity weights as in the measurement. At nominal truth they
return the highest-W column of (Eavail , W ) 3.5 % to 4.1 % low and the highest-Eavail , highest-W cell
1.7 % high; without background, the same experiments close to 0.22 %. Relative to these cells’ total
uncertainty in the configuration-(a) covariance, the bias is 0.36–1.07 standard deviations. It comes
from the purity-based subtraction (§C), enters no quoted covariance, and has no established size on
data. With a deterministic variant of the five-dimensional LightGBM production estimator, refined
unbinned negative-weight subtraction (negweight-refined, App. C) brings the highest-W column
to 0.11 % to 0.24 % and the corner cell to 0.24 % over sixty nominal-truth experiments. Some joint
cells remain up to 1.0 % high, up to 14 standard errors, against −0.2 % without background; the
mechanism is not established. On data, the two treatments differ by a median of 0.36 % and at
most 1.4 % over the reported functionals. These differences measure method sensitivity, not bias.
Negative-weight subtraction is not used for the configuration-(a) five-dimensional central value; the
sixteen-cell result uses it (§10).
Generator-sized truth changes. The unfolding does not follow a GiBUU-to-GENIE Eavail shape
reweighting of the truth (0.70–1.33 per Eavail bin, total preserved). The GENIE prediction behind
the ratio uses a mis-sampled flux (§7.6), so the ratio is a valid known-truth deformation of generator
size but not a measured GiBUU–GENIE difference. Residuals in (Eavail , W ) are median 9.7 %, up
to 74 % in the highest-W column, and several percent in the largest cells. The 74 % residual is in
the cell 0.8 ≤ Eavail < 1.5 GeV, W ≥ 3 GeV, whose share of the cross section the deformation study
did not record. A q3 deformation preserving the (Eavail , W ) truth shifts these cells by up to 37 %.
Signal-only experiments with the same seeds reproduce both results, so they are a property of the
estimator (five iterations, the GBDT configuration of §3), not of the background treatment (status
in the quoted uncertainties: §9.3).

9.3

Why the estimator does not follow a generator-sized change

Both OmniFold steps behave as intended: the first matches the reco pseudo-data within statistical
noise, and the second reproduces the truth-level weight sums to 0.5 %. Yet the unfolded result folds
back onto the data less well than the true model does. With noise-free pseudo-data it recovers more
than 99 % of the departure’s reco signal, while (Eavail , W ) residuals remain median 5 % to 11 % and
up to 74 % in the worst cell. The remaining reco imprint is small relative to the departure but
statistically significant at the analysis exposure.
With noise-free pseudo-data, thirty iterations reduce the Eavail residual by 26 %; it continues
falling slowly, while the q3 residual grows with iterations. A larger classifier improves the reco-level
fit without improving the Eavail truth residual, and removing the regression for events that fail
reconstruction does not help it. No single cause is established, and no estimator change within the
scope of the study meets the recovery requirement, set before the test, for the GiBUU-to-GENIE
departure. The Eavail and q3 residuals, a regularization (unfolding-model) bias of the estimator, enter
no uncertainty quoted for the configuration-(a) central value. For the sixteen-cell result (§10), the
model term of its reported covariance is the change in the unfolded data when the prior is reweighted
to each generator (GENIE + MEC, NuWro, GiBUU; §10.2), unscaled and not a calibrated bound;
§10.3 gives the fake-data study’s measured comparison of the estimator’s bias with the reported
covariance.

9.4

Model dependence, variability and reporting resolution

The scalar five-dimensional estimator studied here uses production LightGBM.
These studies measure three different quantities: truth-dependent responses to iterations and
capacity, small repeat variability at one setting, and appreciable prior sensitivity. Unfolding
benchmarks also assess these quantities separately [50]. The studies do not establish a bias–variance
tradeoff across estimator settings, and regularization bias can invalidate intervals based solely on
estimator variability [51]; neither reference supplies the variance measurement that is missing for
this estimator.
The noise-free study unfolds the full simulation’s reweighted reco events without background.
Baseline classifiers and the missed-event regressor use 100 trees and 8 leaves; the capacity probe
uses 400 trees and 31 leaves. Repeated experiments use estimator R with five iterations, baseline
OmniFold estimators and a separate 400-tree, 31-leaf background-refinement classifier. With seed 42,
its 40 nominal experiments and 20 at each of five departures include Poisson pseudo-data, changing
MC splits and background refinement. Because these samples were used during development, they
provide no independent validation here. Their assessment seeds are separate but also entered the
estimator’s later development.

assessment keeps all 42 EW cells, including the three that the support rule excludes (definitions and

Reporting definitions for a five-dimensional measurement. The five-dimensional precision
study assessed three reporting definitions at the development resolution: the 109 J cells (RD1),
the 27 H2 cells (RD2) and the 39 EW cells (RD3). Its bounded unfolding-model component, the
largest shift over five generator-sized truth departures at the smallest envelope the vertices allow, has
median 12.0 %, 9.6 % and 13.4 % respectively (90th percentile 38.6, 22.5 and 39.2 %), with GiBUU
the dominant vertex in most cells. The study set its total-width target, a median 68% half-width of
at most 10 %, and its selection procedure before it was run. It adds the model component linearly to
the probabilistic half-width, cell by cell, so the total-width target cannot be met in RD1 and RD3
from the model component alone, nor in RD2 once the flat 1.4 % normalization is added (median
at least 11.0 %). The model-component and recovery targets (median at most 5 %; recovery of
at least 75% of a generator-sized departure, that is, a residual proxy of Fig. 26 of at most 0.25,
against 0.31–0.63 measured) fail in every definition. The selection procedure then takes RD3 as the
fallback, and RD3 fails the total-width target. None of the three reporting definitions can meet these
precision targets with any estimator configuration the study’s selection procedure permits within its
computing budget. The limitation is the measured prior dependence, about 10–13% per coarse joint
cell, which is comparable to the generator differences a user would need to resolve. It motivates the
calibrated joint tests of §11, a separate inference that reports no measured cross section.
Variability and interval behavior at five iterations. Figure 27 compares ensemble residual
means and repeat standard deviations at the same setting. At historical W3 truth, EW29 has mean
residual 23.67 % and repeat SD 0.56 %; EW7 has −6.81 % and 0.30 %. Small spread does not imply
small bias. Coarser cells can have smaller residuals and spread, but they estimate different integrals;
this is not an improvement in precision on an unchanged quantity. No matching repeat ensembles
exist at more iterations or larger OmniFold capacity, so no tradeoff can be inferred.

explains much of the apparent variation among the six estimates.

Prior sensitivity is not a measured real-data bias. Real-data prior shifts correlate with
minus the noise-free residual at the same historical truth (Fig. 29). W3’s J correlation is 0.92, yet
|shift| ≥ |residual| holds in only 71% of supported cells (52% on H2, 54% on EW). Maximum shifts
over five historical vertices have medians 12.0%, 9.6% and 13.4% on J, H2 and EW; excluding
D1 gives 11.2%, 4.3% and 9.9%. These envelopes are sensitivities: they provide no probabilities,
covariance components or guaranteed bias bounds, and the truth of the data has not been shown to
lie within their span.
Deciding the tradeoff would require sharing pseudo-data and MC splits across a small set of
iteration and capacity settings and measuring bias, variance and mean squared error for common
functionals. That comparison would not validate intervals, which also requires an explicit treatment
of physical nuisances and model dependence. No new ensemble enters these studies.

9.5

Five-dimensional coverage test

An end-to-end independent-truth test assessed statistical intervals of a deterministic five-dimensional
estimator variant with nominal-truth bias correction (1,200 pseudo-experiments over three truths).
It failed its futility criterion, set before the test was run, at the nominal truth and under a 10%
reduction of the signal rate with a weak Eavail slope (below). No frequentist coverage is therefore
claimed for any five-dimensional interval. Figure 36 shows the coverage of each reported integral
under the rate reduction. The intervals divide each estimate by 1 + β, where β is the relative closure
bias at nominal truth, and add the standard error of β times the corrected estimate in quadrature to
the statistical standard deviation. A quantity fails when the upper confidence bound on its coverage
lies below 0.66 for 68% intervals or below 0.94 for 95% intervals. The second truth (the Eavail
tilt) multiplies the true signal by 1 + 0.2 (Eavail /100 GeV − 12 ) without renormalization, with the
background unchanged; below Eavail = 3 GeV the weight is 0.900–0.906, so it is in effect a 10 % lower

signal rate with a weak Eavail slope. Under that change, 51 of the 153 reported cell and projection
integrals have 68% coverage confidently below nominal, with coverage as low as 0.060, and 42 fail at
95%. At nominal truth two fail at 95%, the lowest at 0.850.
Why the intervals miscover under the rate reduction. A diagnosis from the stored outputs
and the code accounts for the failure under the rate reduction. The rate change itself is recovered:
in the one locally stored pseudo-experiment the total changes by −9.58 % against −9.56 % injected,
and over the 400 the total covers at 0.74 (68%), so this is not a normalization failure. Nor are the
intervals too narrow: they use one nominal bootstrap σ at every truth, which for 10% fewer events
is slightly too wide apart from the background not being redrawn (inferred spread about 1.15σ at
W ≥ 3 GeV), and setting the spread equal to the half-width leaves the failing quantities’ coverage
at 0.06–0.22. The purity subtraction does not apply a nominal signal fraction to the reduced data:
it removes the absolute nominal background expectation, which is unchanged, and in the stored
pseudo-experiment the subtracted total agrees with the pseudo-signal to 60 of 3.2 million events.
The miscoverage is a residual bias that the nominal-truth bias correction does not remove. The
rate change adds a positive bias that grows with W , about 0.2 % for 1.4 ≤ W < 2.2 GeV, 0.4 %
for 2.2 ≤ W < 3 GeV, and 0.7–1.1 % for W ≥ 3 GeV, which is 1.3–2.6 standard deviations against
intervals of 0.16–0.43%, whatever the sign of the nominal bias β; removing that bias restores coverage
to 0.62–0.82 at 68% and 0.92–0.99 at 95%. These per-quantity biases and spreads are inferred,
not measured: the stored output records only hit counts, so each quantity’s mean shift and spread
come from a Gaussian fit to its 68% and 95% coverage, checked against one recorded value (EW17:
+1.04% fitted against +1.05%). The source of the W -graded term is not established: its size and
ordering roughly match, under an approximate assignment of background fractions to W bins, the
rise of the background fraction by 0.111 fbkg , but it may also belong to the estimator alone. A rerun
of five of the same pseudo-experiments, without background and with the background scaled by
0.9 so that the background-to-signal ratio is restored, did not decide between the two: restoring
the ratio raised the residual for W ≥ 3 GeV rather than removing it, and without background the
residual persists.

The projection to (Eavail , W ). The quoted 42-cell covariance is CEW = M CZ M ⊤ , with M of
shape 42 × 10 694 over kept axes (Eavail , W ). Each entry of M is the product of the dropped axes’
bin widths, giving a differential density in cm2 /GeV2 /nucleon. Integrating a destination cell requires
its own ∆Eavail ∆W . Rows follow the 7 × 6 (Eavail , W ) grid in measured C order. No source cell
is dropped, and the result is exactly symmetric. Support and pinned masks and row-index arrays
accompany the covariance and are byte-identical in the two variants. The covariance is paired with
the central values of Fig. 24 by checksum identity: the projected source’s central-value array and the
figure’s are the same bytes, and their numerical agreement is only a corroborating check.
Conditioning, and why no rank is quoted. Measured extremes λmin = +4.359104 × 10−92
and λmax = 1.488215 × 10−77 give λmin /λmax = 2.9 × 10−15 and condition number ≈ 3.4 × 1014 , at
double precision’s edge. No eigenvalue is negative, but the smallest lie at the floating-point noise
floor of a 10 694-term weighted sum. Their sign carries no information: nnegative = 0 describes this
computation and does not show that the source’s negative directions were cured. Rank is a property
of the cutoff, not of the matrix. The 42 eigenvalues decay smoothly across about fifteen orders of
magnitude with no plateau. Retained counts are 26 at relative cutoff 10−6 , 36 at the code default
10−12 , 41 at numpy.linalg.matrix_rank’s default nϵ = 9.3 × 10−15 , and 42 without a cutoff. No
rank is therefore quoted for this covariance; in particular, 36 is the count at one cutoff, not a number
of degrees of freedom. An inverse requires a declared regularization, and none is applied implicitly.
What the covariance supports. Four measurements limit what the covariance supports. Items
1, 2 and 4 use two companion covariances built at two estimator-seed settings; only item 3 describes
this file.


1. Estimator-seed sensitivity exceeds its limit. Companion covariances at seed offsets {0, 1200}
give the correlation-sensitive measure sproj = 6.145 %, against the 5 % movement limit set before
production, on 2026-09-18, as a direct movement tolerance at the scale of the ensemble’s own
precision. Ap160-throw ensemble determines a standard deviation only to a relative precision
of about 1/ 2(N − 1) ≈ 5.6 % for independent Gaussian throws, so a smaller seed-induced
drift could not be resolved against the width it would change. The limit is a declared stability
tolerance, not a derived confidence threshold for a maximum over correlated projections. The
diagonal-only measures pass (sagg = 0.471 %, smed = 0.485 %); the failing one probes the
off-diagonal structure used by projections.
2. The sensitivity is not the statistic’s own resampling noise. With the throw ensemble fixed
and only the seed changed, it does not fall over the tested ensemble sizes: mean 6.02 % over
four disjoint 40-throw subsets, 6.02 % over two 80-throw halves, and 6.145 % over all N = 160
throws. The same-seed resampling floor falls steeply from 20.91 % at N = 40 to 7.57 % at
N = 80, with two-point exponent 1.467; no floor measurement exists at N = 160. Statistical
noise must fall with N ; seed sensitivity did not fall between N = 40 and N = 160. Three
limits rule out a statement about infinite ensembles. First, the 40- and 80-throw points are
nested subsets of the same 160 throws, not independent ensembles. The flatness therefore
holds within one ensemble, over a factor of four in N ; the behaviour at larger N is unmeasured.
Second, the quoted ±0.39 % is subset scatter at a single seed pair (offsets {0, 1200}); the width
of the distribution over seed pairs is unmeasured. Third, the statistic is a maximum over the
declared offset set, so additional pairs can only raise it. Within these limits, the failure of the
5 % limit is not the statistic’s own resampling noise: it shows no reduction over N = 40–160.
