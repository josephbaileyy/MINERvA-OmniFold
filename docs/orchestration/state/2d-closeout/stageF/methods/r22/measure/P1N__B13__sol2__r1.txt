1. QUOTE: "Here negative-weight injection is an equivalence study and the natural extended-fiducial treatment;
this appendix establishes no supported production path and changes no default. The two covariance
ratios are agreement diagnostics between two realizations of one subtraction, not quoted uncertainties.
Two provenance limits apply to the production numbers: the driver revision that produced them is
corroborated but not pinned, and no output records the job that produced them (Ref. [37], R1.7)."
   REWRITE: This appendix evaluates the equivalence of the subtraction methods and their applicability to the extended-fiducial analysis; it does not validate a production implementation. The two covariance ratios quantify agreement between the methods and are not uncertainty estimates. The provenance of the numerical results is incomplete: the driver revision is corroborated but not uniquely established, and the outputs do not identify the generating jobs (Ref. [37], R1.7).
2. QUOTE: "The standard chain uses purity by choice, on two grounds (Ref. [37], R1.8). First, mixing
background treatments inside one covariance is worse than either consistent choice. A lateral-band
block built with negative-weight subtraction, inserted into a central and vertical set built with purity
subtraction, is a mismatch, not an upgrade. Second, switching would have meant rerunning every
unfold above and would have invalidated the flux-corrected covariance just chosen for use. That
consequence, not the computing cost, decided the choice."
   REWRITE: The standard chain retains purity subtraction to ensure consistent background treatment across the central value and all covariance components (Ref. [37], R1.8). Adopting negative-weight subtraction would require repeating all constituent unfolds and rebuilding the selected flux-corrected covariance; replacing only the lateral-band component would introduce inconsistent background treatments.

condition still applies."
