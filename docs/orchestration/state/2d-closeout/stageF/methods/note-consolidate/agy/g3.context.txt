ROW R06b (passage sec_5d:8): fact: Correcting the nominal closure bias does not establish coverage under other truths
  home sec_5d.tex: "The miscoverage is a residual bias that the nominal-truth bias correction does not remove."
    in its paragraph: \paragraph{Why the intervals miscover under the rate reduction.} A diagnosis from the stored outputs and the code accounts for the failure under the rate reduction. The rate change itself is recovered: in the one locally stored pseudo-experiment the total changes by \SI{-9.58}{\percent} against \SI{-9.56}{\percent} injected, and over the 400 the total covers at 0.74 (68\%), so this is not a normalization failure. Nor are the intervals too narrow: they use one nominal bootstrap $\sigma$ at every truth, which for 10\% fewer events is slightly too wide apart from the background not being redrawn (inferred spread about $1.15\sigma$ at $W\ge\SI{3}{GeV}$), and setting the spread equal to the half-width leaves the failing quantities' coverage at 0.06--0.22. The purity subtraction does not apply a nominal signal fraction to the reduced data: it removes the absolute nominal background expectation, which is unchanged, and in the stored pseudo-experiment the subtracted total agrees with the pseudo-signal to 60 of 3.2 million events. The miscoverage is a residual bias that the nominal-truth bias correction does not remove. The rate change adds a positive bias that grows with $W$, about \SI{0.2}{\percent} for $1.4\le W<\SI{2.2}{GeV}$, \SI{0.4}{\percent} for $2.2\le W<\SI{3}{GeV}$, and 0.7--\SI{1.1}{\percent} for $W\ge\SI{3}{GeV}$, which is 1.3--2.6 standard deviations against intervals of 0.16--0.43\%, whatever the sign of the nominal bias $\beta$; removing that bias restores coverage to 0.62--0.82 at 68\% and 0.92--0.99 at 95\%. These per-quantity biases and spreads are inferred, not measured: the stored output records only hit counts, so each quantity's mean shift and spread come from a Gaussian fit to its 68\% and 95\% coverage, checked against one recorded value (EW17: $+1.04\%$ fitted against $+1.05\%$). The source of the $W$-graded term is not established: its size and ordering roughly match, under an approximate assignment of background fractions to $W$ bins, the rise of the background fraction by $0.111\,f_{\mathrm{bkg}}$, but it may also belong to the estimator alone. A rerun of five of the same pseudo-experiments, without background and with the background scaled by 0.9 so that the background-to-signal ratio is restored, did not decide between the two: restoring the ratio raised the residual for $W\ge\SI{3}{GeV}$ rather than removing it, and without background the residual persists.
