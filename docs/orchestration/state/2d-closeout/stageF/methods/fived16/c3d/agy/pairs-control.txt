Q1.
BEFORE: Facts from the study's report (accepted as correct): The study rebuilt the flux block of the result's covariance from 2,000 bootstrap replicates of the 100 weighted flux universes (PPFX hadron-production universes, each carrying its neutrino-electron and inverse-muon-decay constraint weight), keeping the flux variances and taking each replicate's correlations (the 'correlation' variant). For each prediction it reports the chi-squared of the reported result (chi-hat), the 16-84% percentile band of the chi-squared over the replicates, and the offset median minus chi-hat. Total chi-squared, 16-cell predictions (16 degrees of freedom): GENIE CV 109.35, band 105.97-112.45, offset +0.25; GENIE + MEC 272.12, band 262.37-282.71, offset +1.22; NuWro 188.03, band 185.47-190.93, offset +0.75; MINERvA Tune v1 329.72, band 314.90-350.00, offset +3.12. GiBUU (8 cells, 8 degrees of freedom) 58.46, band 57.33-59.47, offset +0.16. Every offset is positive. The band is estimation scatter given these 100 universes under PPFX and the constraint; it is not an interval. A synthetic check showed the 16-84% band covered its target in only 0.32-0.47 of ensembles against a nominal 0.68. The study is NOT CITABLE FOR: that the chi-squared is insensitive to the flux correlations; that the PPFX or constraint correlations are correct; an interval for the chi-squared under the true flux correlations. A separate earlier study (the 'weakening') found that shrinking only the flux correlations by 10% toward the diagonal lowers the 16-cell chi-squared by 24-48%; that result stands.
AFTER: Weakening only the flux correlations by 10% lowers the χ² of the 16-cell predictions by 24–48%. The same change to the detector or lateral correlations lowers it by at most 18%. With the correlations of the interaction, flux, detector and lateral groups removed, every 16-cell prediction keeps χ² ≥ 45 for 16 degrees of freedom. The flux correlations are themselves estimated from the 100 hadron-production (PPFX) flux universes of Ref. [16], weighted by the neutrino–electron and inverse-muon-decay constraint. Rebuilding them from bootstrap replicates of those universes gives 16–84% percentile bands of each 16-cell χ² whose edges lie within 1.5–6.2% of the reported value, with the bootstrap median above it by at most 1.0%. These bands measure how precisely the universes determine the correlations, not how much the χ² would change if the correlations were wrong, which the weakening probes; they show that the χ² is robust to the flux correlations.
