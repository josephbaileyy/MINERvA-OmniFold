 We study multidimensional medium-energy charged-current neutrino--nucleus cross sections on MINERvA open data with OmniFold, an unbinned machine-learning unfolding method. With this method, the double-differential cross section in muon transverse and longitudinal momentum reproduces the published integral within about one percent, with a correlated shape difference, and its uncertainty is comparable in size to the published one. That uncertainty is computed with the central value's own estimator, its universe and statistical blocks at the central value's training seed, and it has not been independently validated. Adding available energy ($\Eavail$), three-momentum transfer ($q_{3}$), and hadronic invariant mass ($W$) gives simultaneous \emph{unbinned} three-, four-, and five-observable MINERvA unfolds, each of which passes marginal-normalization checks against the lower-dimensional result to $\approx$1--2\,\%. Binned MINERvA measurements already reach triple-differential; the methodological feature here is simultaneous unbinned unfolding of five related, not independent, observables. In central values, the tested generators under-predict the data at low $\Eavail$, in the region of the established MINERvA low-recoil discrepancy. Relative to the MINERvA-tuned simulation, the data show a descriptive excess at high $\Eavail$ and high $W$: a fifth of the positive difference from that simulation lies in the single widest overflow cell, the regularization bias is largest in this region, and in ratio the excess also extends to low $W$. Four external generators also under-predict the data there, each by about its overall shortfall once the high-energy neutrinos absent from the GiBUU sample are accounted for, and the NuWro and GiBUU comparisons carry an uncorrected carbon-versus-CH target difference. These comparisons locate no interaction mechanism. Closure tests that include backgrounds show a bias from the purity subtraction, which a negative-weight background treatment largely removes. The five-dimensional central value and its covariance use the purity subtraction, and the central value keeps its bias of up to 4\% in the highest-$W$ cells, which no quoted uncertainty includes. In two dimensions, such a test puts the purity and refined negative-weight estimates more than one statistical standard deviation apart in 14 of 205 cells, while the refined estimate stays within 0.44 statistical standard deviations of the truth in every cell; the two-dimensional result and its covariance use the refined estimator. No full five-dimensional comparison of the two background treatments has been made. The negative-weight treatment is used for the two-, three- and four-dimensional central values, in the generator tests, in some five-dimensional validation studies and in two-dimensional equivalence studies. Tests that reweight the truth shape by about the size of the differences between generators show a residual bias, interpreted as regularization bias, that no quoted uncertainty includes. Statistical intervals miscover at the tested truths, and with an envelope over five tested truth deformations none of the tested reporting definitions satisfies the predeclared 10\% criterion. As an application illustrating calibrated inference with a biased estimator, joint tests calibrated in simulation reject the hybrid null hypotheses for all five tested generator predictions, in total and in shape, under six stated conditions and at a 5\% family-wise error rate under the stated calibration assumptions. The tests recover known discrepancies and are not physical exclusions. They show no evidence of discrimination beyond matched coarse projections, omit an uncertainty on the calorimetric recoil response, and assume convergence of the fine grid on which the null hypotheses are defined, which has not been shown. NuWro's total-test rejection is not robust to a hydrocarbon-target correction. A scalar study examines the effect of removing the truth-level muon kinematic cuts. Point-cloud (PET) representations of the full event are future work, documented separately; this note uses no PET result.
