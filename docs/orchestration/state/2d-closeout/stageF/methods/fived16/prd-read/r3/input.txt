BACKGROUND. The paper is being reframed from a methods study to a measurement led by its sixteen-cell five-observable cross section, and it now presents only the two final estimators (a two-observable estimator and a joint-test estimator); earlier estimator variants and the results resting on them moved to the companion note, with pointers, except findings the paper's argument needs, which stay described in words.

PART A. The paper abstract.
OLD (117 words): Applied to MINERvA open data for medium-energy ν_μ charged-current inclusive interactions, OmniFold reproduces the published muon-kinematic cross section's integral within about 1%, with a correlated shape difference. With five simultaneous observables (adding available energy, three-momentum transfer, and hadronic invariant mass), it yields central values only, whose normalizations agree with lower-dimensional results. Pseudo-experiments measure a background-subtraction bias, a partial recovery of generator-sized truth changes, and statistical undercoverage at the tested truths. With a model-dependence envelope added linearly to the uncertainty, no tested reporting definition meets our 10% precision criterion. As an illustration, simulation-calibrated tests of hybrid null hypotheses for five generator predictions all reject at the 5% familywise level, with no evidence of gain over matched coarse projections.
NEW (204 words): We report a simultaneous measurement of the ν_μ charged-current inclusive cross section in five observables, the muon transverse and longitudinal momenta, available hadronic energy, three-momentum transfer and hadronic invariant mass, unfolded without binning with OmniFold from MINERvA medium-energy open data and reported in sixteen cells with a covariance whose median per-cell uncertainty is 7.29%. Three external generator predictions compared on all sixteen cells give χ² of 109–272 for 16 degrees of freedom, and GiBUU, on 8 cells, 58. The coverage of the intervals has not been validated; in a fake-data study, the reported uncertainty does not cover the estimator's bias at GiBUU-like truths or at a synthetic q_3 tilt; and the sixteen-cell χ² values fall by 24–48% when only the flux correlations are weakened by 10%. The same unfolding reproduces the integral of the published muon-kinematic cross section within about 1%, with a correlated shape difference. Pseudo-experiments measure a background-subtraction bias that negative-weight subtraction largely removes, a partial recovery of generator-sized truth changes, and statistical undercoverage of a deterministic purity-subtracted variant at the tested truths. As an illustration, simulation-calibrated tests of hybrid null hypotheses for five generator predictions all reject at the 5% familywise level, with no evidence of gain over matched coarse projections.

PART B. The companion note's abstract.
OLD (584 words): We study multidimensional medium-energy charged-current neutrino–nucleus cross sections on MINERvA open data with OmniFold, an unbinned machine-learning unfolding method. With this method, the double-differential cross section in muon transverse and longitudinal momentum reproduces the published integral within about one percent, with a correlated shape difference, and its uncertainty is comparable in size to the published one. That uncertainty is computed with the central value's own estimator, its universe and statistical blocks at the central value's training seed, and it has not been independently validated. Adding available energy (E_avail), three-momentum transfer (q_3), and hadronic invariant mass (W) gives simultaneous unbinned three-, four-, and five-observable MINERvA unfolds, each of which passes marginal-normalization checks against the lower-dimensional result to ≈1–2 %. Binned MINERvA measurements already reach triple-differential; the methodological feature here is simultaneous unbinned unfolding of five related, not independent, observables. In central values, the tested generators under-predict the data at low E_avail, in the region of the established MINERvA low-recoil discrepancy. Relative to the MINERvA-tuned simulation, the data show a descriptive excess at high E_avail and high W: a fifth of the positive difference from that simulation lies in the single widest overflow cell, the regularization bias is largest in this region, and in ratio the excess also extends to low W. Four external generators also under-predict the data there, each by about its overall shortfall once the high-energy neutrinos absent from the GiBUU sample are accounted for, and the NuWro and GiBUU comparisons carry an uncorrected carbon-versus-CH target difference. These comparisons locate no interaction mechanism. Closure tests that include backgrounds show a bias from the purity subtraction, which a negative-weight background treatment largely removes. The five-dimensional central value and its covariance use the purity subtraction, and the central value keeps its bias of up to 4% in the highest-W cells, which no quoted uncertainty includes. In two dimensions, such a test puts the purity and refined negative-weight estimates more than one statistical standard deviation apart in 14 of 205 cells, while the refined estimate stays within 0.44 statistical standard deviations of the truth in every cell; the two-dimensional result and its covariance use the refined estimator. No full five-dimensional comparison of the two background treatments has been made. The negative-weight treatment is used for the two-, three- and four-dimensional central values, in the generator tests, in some five-dimensional validation studies and in two-dimensional equivalence studies. Tests that reweight the truth shape by about the size of the differences between generators show a residual bias, interpreted as regularization bias, that no quoted uncertainty includes. Statistical intervals miscover at the tested truths, and with an envelope over five tested truth deformations none of the tested reporting definitions satisfies the predeclared 10% criterion. As an application illustrating calibrated inference with a biased estimator, joint tests calibrated in simulation reject the hybrid null hypotheses for all five tested generator predictions, in total and in shape, under six stated conditions and at a 5% family-wise error rate under the stated calibration assumptions. The tests recover known discrepancies and are not physical exclusions. They show no evidence of discrimination beyond matched coarse projections, omit an uncertainty on the calorimetric recoil response, and assume convergence of the fine grid on which the null hypotheses are defined, which has not been shown. NuWro's total-test rejection is not robust to a hydrocarbon-target correction. A scalar study examines the effect of removing the truth-level muon kinematic cuts. Point-cloud (PET) representations of the full event are future work, documented separately; this note uses no PET result.
NEW (349 words): This note documents an unbinned, simultaneous unfolding of MINERvA medium-energy ν_μ charged-current inclusive open data with OmniFold, and its validation. Its principal result is a cross section in five observables, muon transverse and longitudinal momentum, available energy (E_avail), three-momentum transfer (q_3) and hadronic invariant mass (W), in sixteen cells, with a covariance of statistical, flux, interaction, detector, lateral, normalization, generator-shape and calorimetric-response terms; its median per-cell uncertainty is 7.29%. Three external generator predictions compared on all sixteen cells give χ² between 109 and 272 for 16 degrees of freedom, and GiBUU, on its 8 cells, 58. The coverage of the result's intervals has not been validated; in a fake-data study, the reported uncertainty does not cover the estimator's bias at GiBUU-like truths or at a synthetic q_3 tilt; and the sixteen-cell χ² values fall by 24–48% when only the flux correlations are weakened by 10%. The note also gives the reproduction of the published double-differential cross section in muon momentum, whose integral agrees within about one percent, with a correlated shape difference and a covariance that has not been independently validated; three- and four-observable central values; and an earlier purity-subtracted five-observable central value on a finer grid, whose covariance does not meet its specification's acceptance criteria and whose quoted uncertainties omit that estimator's background bias of up to 4% at high W and its regularization bias. The validation studies show that negative-weight background subtraction removes most of the purity method's bias, that generator-sized truth changes are recovered only in part, a residual interpreted as regularization bias, that statistical intervals of a deterministic purity-subtracted variant miscover at the tested truths, and that no tested reporting definition meets a predeclared 10% precision criterion. Joint tests calibrated in simulation reject hybrid null hypotheses for five generator predictions at a 5% family-wise error rate; they are not physical exclusions, show no evidence of discrimination beyond matched coarse projections, omit an uncertainty on the calorimetric recoil response and assume an unshown convergence of the fine grid on which the null hypotheses are defined, and NuWro's total-test rejection is not robust to a hydrocarbon-target correction.
