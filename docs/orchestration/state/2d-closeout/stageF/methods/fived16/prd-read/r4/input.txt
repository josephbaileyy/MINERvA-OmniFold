BACKGROUND. The paper reports a sixteen-cell, five-observable neutrino cross section with a covariance and compares five generator predictions with it through chi-squared. A new study rebuilt the covariance's flux block from bootstrap replicates of the 100 flux universes from which the flux correlations are estimated; the passages below add its result after an existing sensitivity paragraph. D1 is the paper's paragraph and D2 the note's; P02 is an earlier rewording of a sentence of the paper's introduction.

D1 (paper). OLD: Weakening only the flux correlations by 10% lowers the χ² of the 16-cell predictions by 24–48%. The same change to the detector or lateral correlations lowers it by at most 18%. With the correlations of the interaction, flux, detector and lateral groups removed, every 16-cell prediction keeps χ² ≥ 45 for 16 degrees of freedom.
NEW: Weakening only the flux correlations by 10% lowers the χ² of the 16-cell predictions by 24–48%. The same change to the detector or lateral correlations lowers it by at most 18%. With the correlations of the interaction, flux, detector and lateral groups removed, every 16-cell prediction keeps χ² ≥ 45 for 16 degrees of freedom. The flux correlations are themselves estimated from the 100 hadron-production (PPFX) flux universes of Ref. [16], weighted by the neutrino–electron and inverse-muon-decay constraint. Rebuilding them from bootstrap replicates of those universes gives 16–84% percentile bands of each 16-cell χ² whose edges lie within 1.5–6.2% of the reported value, with the bootstrap median above it by at most 1.0%. These bands measure how precisely the universes determine the correlations, not how much the χ² would change if the correlations were wrong, which the weakening probes; they are not intervals for the χ² under the true correlations.

NEW: Weakening only the flux correlations by 10% lowers the χ² of the 16-cell predictions by 24–48%. The same change to the detector or lateral correlations lowers it by at most 18%. With the correlations of the interaction, flux, detector and lateral groups removed, every 16-cell prediction keeps χ² ≥ 45 for 16 degrees of freedom. Table 18 gives each group's sensitivity, GiBUU's eight-cell values included. These are descriptive sensitivities of the reported χ², not alternative results: one group's correlations are shrunk toward the diagonal by a fraction α of the way, with its variances kept. The flux correlations are themselves estimated from the 100 PPFX flux universes, weighted by the neutrino–electron and inverse-muon-decay constraint (§10.2). Rebuilding the flux block from 2000 bootstrap replicates of those universes, with its variances kept, gives 16–84% percentile bands of the total χ²: GENIE 2.12.10 CV 106–112 around 109, GENIE 2.12.10 + Valencia MEC 262–283 around 272, NuWro 21.09 185–191 around 188, MINERvA Tune v1 315–350 around 330, and GiBUU 2019, on its 8 cells, 57–59 around 58. In each, the bootstrap median lies above the reported value, by 0.25, 1.22, 0.75, 3.12 and 0.16 in the same order. These bands measure the estimation scatter of the reported χ² given these universes, not how much it would change if the flux correlations were wrong, which the weakening above probes; they are not intervals for the χ² under the true correlations.

P02 (paper). OLD: The validation studies show how far the result can be trusted: the two-dimensional reproduction, the background subtraction, the recovery of generator-sized truth changes, a coverage test and a fake-data study of the estimator's bias. They ask whether the unfolding gives reliable multidimensional cross sections when the simulation mismodels the truth by generator-sized amounts.
NEW: The validation studies assess the result through the two-dimensional reproduction, background-subtraction tests, recovery of generator-sized truth changes, a coverage test and a fake-data study of estimator bias. They examine whether the unfolding gives reliable multidimensional cross sections when the simulation mismodels the truth by generator-sized amounts.
