Here, true \(q_3=\sqrt{Q^2+(E_\nu-E_\mu)^2}\), with exact four-vector \(Q^2\); it is not calculated by substituting \(E_{\rm avail}\) for energy transfer (`docs/analysis-note/sec_eavailw.tex:50–65`). For confirmation, the tilts are useful controlled challenges, but insufficient proxies for generator diversity. The proposed amplitude is 0.3 multiplying a standardized coordinate, not a uniform 30% change (A:215–221). Algebraically, it means roughly 30% per standard deviation before clipping and recentering. I regard that as a substantial, defensible stress amplitude, **not a demonstrated realistic uncertainty envelope**. Smooth coordinate tilts can expose retained-prior bias, but they do not deliberately challenge localized peaks, changes of interaction mixture, or joint correlations characteristic of generator differences.

One definition needs tightening before binding: **what population defines each standardization and recentering?** The cited q3 implementation standardizes within every fine \((E_{\rm avail},W)\) cell and preserves that cell’s weighted total (`nd-unfolding/s5c_pseudo.py:54–60,80–95`). Global tilts and analogous conditional tilts ask different questions. Freeze that choice, normalization, and treatment of the domain before generating anything. In particular, pT, pparallel and W tilts challenge C7 through correlations; they do not independently span arbitrary changes across its two axes.
